# 09.04 — Fresh checks

Save your answers before opening the separate key. All data are fictional. Attribute each claim and show denominators rather than relying on a trust label.

## Check A — Two websites, one survey

An event organizer advertises: “Our layout causes people to stay twice as long.” The only original record is survey P: twelve people chose the new room and twelve chose the old room. Six in the new room and all twelve in the old room returned a form. All six new-room respondents and six old-room respondents said, “I would like to stay longer.” Nobody timed actual stays. There is one session per room and no baseline. Two websites repeat P; neither collects data. The organizer sells layout consulting. A reviewer with no financial link recalculates P but collects no new data.

Calculate the yes rates among respondents. Bound the new-room yes fraction among all twelve selected people when the six missing answers are unknown. Explain the difference between a missing-response bound and a statistical confidence interval. State how many underlying datasets exist. Address expertise, incentive, method, sample, replication, uncertainty, and at least two alternatives. Write one justified descriptive statement and one reason not to repeat the causal headline. Does the financial interest prove that the claim is false?

## Check B — Better evidence is not a guarantee

Team Q, which sells a meeting timer, uses a documented random-number procedure to allocate forty volunteers to each of two formats. A preregistered outcome is “at least one recorded contribution in the session.” Every participant has a recorded outcome. Counts are 24/40 for the new format and 16/40 for the old. Team R, with no connection to Q, recruits new volunteers, uses the same protocol, and records 22/40 versus 18/40. Observers can see the format. Each team studies one club and measures one session per participant; no durable change or other population is measured. No uncertainty analysis beyond the counts is supplied.

Compute the within-team percentage-point differences. Identify what improves relative to Check A and which limitations remain. Is R only another publication or a new investigation? Does preregistration or random allocation prove a universal benefit? Is Q's incentive alone a reason to discard the counts? Give a claim-specific confidence judgment and a next evidence request without asserting statistical significance that you have not calculated.
