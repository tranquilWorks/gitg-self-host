# 09.04 — Answer guidance and repairs

Open after making your attempts. These answers concern the supplied fictional evidence, not validation of an intervention or independent acceptance of your work.

## Main practice

Among returned forms, the new group has 10/10 = 100% yes and the old group 20/40 = 50% yes. The ratio of those response proportions is 2. It is not a doubling in the number of actual contributions. Among all twelve selected new-format members, the possible yes fraction ranges from 10/12 (about 83.33%) to 12/12 (100%). These are bounds from two missing binary responses. They do not describe sampling uncertainty, a population parameter, or causation. Relative to the old group's 50%, the possible selected-member ratio ranges from 5/3 to 2.

There is one underlying dataset, R1. The newsletter republishes it; the volunteer independently checks its arithmetic. Neither is a new study. Relevant facilitation experience and a financial incentive are separate facts, not automatic acceptance or rejection rules.

Self-selection could produce groups that differed before the sessions. Session-specific events or expectations could affect answers. The outcome measures a reported sense of opportunity, not observed participation. These alternatives are plausible, not demonstrated causes. The club sample and absence of follow-up limit population and durability claims. The single session per format also prevents treating the individual questionnaires as repeated tests of the format across sessions.

A defensible statement is: “In this report, 10 of 10 new-format respondents and 20 of 40 old-format respondents said they felt able to contribute; two new-format forms were missing.” The causal claim about actual participation for all members is unsupported as stated. That conclusion does not prove the format has no benefit.

A recovered form would narrow missing-response bounds. A behavior measure would address the outcome mismatch. Comparable allocation, repeated sessions, independent new data, and follow-up address other questions. Avoid promising that any one addition fixes all of them.

## Check A

Respondent yes rates are 6/6 = 100% and 6/12 = 50%. The new-room selected-member yes fraction can be anywhere from 6/12 = 50% to 12/12 = 100%. The six missing answers cannot be silently removed or set to yes. The bounds simply enumerate their possible values; they are not a confidence interval.

There is one dataset, P, despite two websites and a reanalysis. Expertise beyond running the event is not established. Consulting revenue warrants examination but does not prove dishonesty or a false result. Self-selection, missing responses, session differences, and expectations can affect the reported preference. Actual duration was not measured at all.

A justified statement describes the returned-form responses and missingness. The headline about caused duration changes is not supported. Better reporting might recover forms, but timed comparable observations and an appropriate comparison design would be needed for the stronger claim.

**Repair:** If you wrote “twice as long,” replace it with the actual survey outcome and its denominator. If you wrote “three independent sources,” draw all publications back to P. If you rejected the evidence solely for the incentive, identify the methodological limitation instead.

## Check B

Q's rates are 60% versus 40%, a 20-percentage-point difference. R's are 55% versus 45%, a 10-percentage-point difference. “Percentage points” refers to subtraction of percentages, not a relative percent increase.

Documented random allocation, a specified recorded behavior, complete outcome capture, and a separately recruited new investigation address important weaknesses in A. R supplies new evidence, not just another report of Q. A shared protocol does not make the participants the same. Q's commercial interest remains worth disclosing; it does not erase the design or settle the truth.

Both datasets favor the new format descriptively. Their uncertainty, observer awareness, limited club populations, single-session measurement, and absence of durability evidence remain relevant. Preregistration and random allocation do not guarantee flawless execution, measurement, or generalization. A judgment can be more favorable than for A without claiming a universal or statistically established effect.

A reasonable next request is the prespecified analysis with an appropriate uncertainty estimate and details of how observers recorded contributions, followed by evidence in additional contexts or later sessions if the claim includes those contexts or durability. Do not manufacture a p-value, call the difference “significant” without analysis, or demand certainty before acknowledging stronger evidence.

**Repair:** Keep improved design, observed differences, and remaining uncertainty in separate sentences. Neither reflexive dismissal nor unqualified acceptance is the target.
