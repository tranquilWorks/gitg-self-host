# Background sources and claim limits

Consulted 12 September 2026. The curriculum scope comes from the repository's canonical YAML at commit `d93532696d3ea9d04e50f47350b90fa255208511`, blob `9ba2a9991f46cddb8ff11249de1800f605cf664c`, entries 09.04–09.06. The existing exercise source was inspected at blob `7ed508d16f06e801b4bccf303cc32744cb757f06`.

All people, meetings, rooms, datasets, response forms, prompts, keys, and fixture calculations in this cohort are original synthetic constructions. None is presented as an observed study or participant result. The following sources supply limited background, not endorsement of the exercises.

## S1 — Contextual authority

Association of College and Research Libraries, *Framework for Information Literacy for Higher Education*, “Authority Is Constructed and Contextual.”
https://www.ala.org/acrl/standards/ilframework

The inspected official framework supports judging authority in relation to the information need and examining origins, expertise, context, and evidence. It does not supply a numerical trust score or validate this source-audit exercise. The cohort's meeting/lamp cases and answer guidance are original.

## S2 — Study limitations

Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 25, *Assessing risk of bias in a non-randomized study*, particularly sections on confounding, selection, and missing data.
https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-25

The inspected chapter supports the distinction between an observed association and a causal effect, and the need to examine allocation, measurement, and missing observations. Its clinical risk-of-bias tool is not implemented or scored here. The nonclinical examples teach bounded reasoning, not competence to conduct a Cochrane review. The arithmetic bounds simply enumerate possible missing binary answers; they are not inferential confidence intervals.

## S3 — Intellectual humility: abstract-level consultation

Leary and colleagues (2017), *Cognitive and Interpersonal Features of Intellectual Humility*, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 43(6), 793–813; DOI 10.1177/0146167217697695.
https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/dspace/handle/10161/23967
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28903672/

Bibliographic/abstract-level search material was retrieved. The concept of acknowledging the possible limitations of one's beliefs is relevant background. No full-text analysis, effect estimate, intervention efficacy, or measurement validation is claimed. This cohort neither reproduces nor administers the paper's scale. The versioned-belief procedure is an original instructional design, not an established treatment.

## S4 — Forecast verification

Joint Working Group on Forecast Verification Research, *Forecast verification — methods, issues and FAQ*.
https://jwgfvr.github.io/forecastverification/

The inspected technical guidance distinguishes probability accuracy, reliability/calibration, resolution, and reference-based skill, including Brier verification. This cohort uses the binary mean squared probability error and direct finite-group frequency comparisons. It does not infer global calibration from one score or claim that the synthetic groups validate a real forecaster. All numerical meeting and audit examples are original and separately checked with exact arithmetic.

## S5 — Probability and decisions

Met Office, *Using ensemble forecasts in decision-making*.
https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/research/weather/ensemble-forecasting/decision-making

The inspected official guidance connects uncertain forecasts with decisions and notes that modeled probabilities are imperfect. The cohort's cost/benefit examples are deliberately simplified original time models, not weather recommendations or empirically estimated effects. Access, duty, risk, and omitted costs remain outside their arithmetic.

## Originality and remaining evidence

No source validates this curriculum as a whole, the three exercises, causal developmental gains, psychometric properties, independent learner usability, or cross-cultural suitability. Source links and mechanical tests must not be recorded as formal human review acceptance. Numerical correctness is checked separately from claims about learning or behavior.
