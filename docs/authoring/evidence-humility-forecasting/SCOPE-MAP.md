## 09.05 — Epistemic humility

| Canonical element | Concrete teaching and practice | Fresh check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confidence levels | Low/moderate/high confidence is tied to an exact proposition and reasons, not presented as a calibrated score. | B: high confidence in a notice's contents differs from certainty about future operation. |
| Assumptions | One Main-room attempt does not survey every room or day. | A: keep an original universal claim distinct from a later frequency claim. |
| Unknowns | Other rooms, later availability, representativeness, and the original interruption cause. | A/B: identify unresolved future conditions without denying current evidence. |
| Limitations | Same reader/task and observed contexts, with reports distinguished from observations. | A: successful visit supplies a counterexample, not population frequency. |
| What would change a belief | Preserved revision conditions precede the separately revealed evidence packet. | B: unsupported pressure versus a newer relevant official correction. |
| Progress indicator | Append V2 without erasing V0/V1; acknowledge the prior view without humiliation or evasion. | Both: keep the historic claim, update the appropriate proposition, choose a proportionate action. |

Fictional updating establishes procedure practice only. A personally meaningful low-stakes belief and actual relevant new evidence are needed to demonstrate the real-world progress indicator. Retaining a belief under unsupported pressure can be appropriate.

## 09.06 — Probabilistic and scenario thinking

| Canonical element | Concrete teaching and practice | Fresh check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ranges | Historical delay range versus a probability-judgment range; neither is automatically a confidence interval. | Main key and A: keep meanings and point probabilities fixed before resolution. |
| Likelihoods | Probabilities as uncertain event judgments rather than guarantees. | A: a 0.25 event may occur without making its prior probability impossible. |
| Base rates | Ten complete historical records, explicit event threshold, denominator and context boundary. | Main: exactly five minutes counts; future conditions are not guaranteed identical. |
| Scenarios | Three stipulated probabilities and two possible realized net consequences. | A: a second cost/benefit case transfers the method. |
| Sensitivity | Solve a break-even threshold and propagate the probability range. | A: changed cost/benefit inputs produce a different threshold. |
| Expected value | Time-only formula, certain cost, contingent benefit, and model exclusions. | A: expected saving differs from single-case realized saving. |
| Forecast recording | Three prospective records; separate outcome file; void and unresolved rules set first. | A: retain exclusions and use only the two resolved cases in the score denominator. |
| Calibration evaluation | Distinguish Brier accuracy from probability-versus-frequency agreement with counts. | B: exact group comparisons, finite-sample boundary, and low-score rare-event counterexample. |
| Progress indicator | Forecast, preserve, resolve later, and inspect calibration rather than only retrospective success. | B is synthetic; actual prospective forecasts and later observations remain necessary for live evidence. |

Brier calculations are instructional arithmetic. They do not change application completion or scoring. A standalone reader's reveal behavior is not the application's reveal behavior.
