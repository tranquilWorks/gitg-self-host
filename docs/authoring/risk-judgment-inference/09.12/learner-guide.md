# 09.12 — Decision records and updating

## The skill and its boundary

Preserve what you knew before choosing, then append what happened without rewriting the first record. Review both the result and the process that produced the choice. A reasonable decision can have a disappointing outcome; a weak process can be lucky.

**Canonical scope:** Document important choices, predictions, assumptions, dissent, review dates, and outcomes.

**Deliverable:** A prospective-style decision record, a preserved choice-time copy, a separate review, and one specific improvement to the next decision process. Allow roughly 35 minutes for the fictional route. A real route uses a genuinely undecided low-stakes choice and an actual later review within 30 days. Writing a suggested review date in this lesson does not create a reminder or schedule anything in your accounts.

**Compatibility boundary:** 09.12 already has retained typed-evidence rules. This source does not alter its action IDs, observation allowlist, completion logic, scoring, or replay. Keep private record contents out of application evidence. Do not upload names, narratives, quotes from others, secrets, or the record itself merely to demonstrate this exercise.

## A compact record can retain disagreement without retaining identities

Separate four layers. **Before choice:** the objective, live options, assumptions, prediction, uncertainty, and a counterargument. **At choice:** the selected option and date. **After choice:** any separately labeled clerical correction. **At review:** the observed outcome, assumption dispositions, and a future process change.

Encode dissent as a neutral counterargument or assumption code, not someone else's identity or words. For example, A2 can mean “the session may be interrupted.” That concern deserves consideration whether it came from your own reflection or another person. Do not flatten disagreement into an unexplained yes/no approval field.

A preserved copy supplies comparison, not magical proof of authenticity. A dated paper copy or ordinary local file version can help. A hash can detect changed bytes when compared with a trusted earlier hash, but a person controlling both files can replace both. Do not call a freshly recomputed hash an independent timestamp or tamper-proof human review.

The general distinction between a prospective plan and later deviations is supported as background by [R3](../SOURCES.md). This private practice is not a public research registration, an evidence upload, or a validated decision-improvement intervention.

## A different worked example: two storage plans

Before choosing a personal note-storage arrangement, someone records that retrieval matters more than appearance, that one location may be unavailable during cleaning, and that the next check is on Day 5. They choose the simpler location with moderate confidence. A one-off closure later makes retrieval inconvenient.

The review should inspect what was knowable at choice time, not simply say “bad outcome, bad decision.” If the closure was announced before choice and ignored, that suggests a missed information check. If it was first announced afterward, it is a different finding. Both outcomes can prompt a useful contingency without pretending the original information was the same.

## Complete fictional choice packet — stop before the later outcome file

At **Day 0, 10:00**, the decision-maker has not selected a reading session. The neutral objective **O1** is to complete one uninterrupted 30-minute section within a 45-minute available block on Day 3.

| Option | Choice-time facts |
| --- | --- |
| A | A room is confirmed bookable for the block; a current notice designates it quiet; no closure notice appears in the supplied choice-time material |
| B | A home space is available; the same block overlaps a known ordinary interruption window; uninterrupted time is uncertain |

The person prefers A for the uninterrupted-reading objective, while acknowledging that a designation is not a guarantee of silence. Both options are optional and reversible. There is no purchase, duty to attend, or effect on another person's access.

The maximum three assumption codes are **A1: room remains available**, **A2: interruptions do not prevent O1**, and **A3: the 45-minute block remains available**. Counterargument **A2** points to a vulnerability of even the preferred option. Use confidence words such as low, moderate, or high, with a reason kept only in your private working notes. The planned review is **Day 7, 10:00**, earlier if the outcome becomes impossible to determine or a material assumption changes.

The later result is in **outcome-packet.md**. Do not open it until you have saved the first record and choice. These files are separately stored, not access-controlled. Opening them early does not make you a bad learner; label the exercise retrospective rather than claiming a prospective prediction.

### Action 1 — Write the minimum useful record before selecting

Use this private template:

`Record code | neutral category | objective code | live option codes | up to three assumption codes | counterargument code | predicted status | confidence band | record date | review date or trigger`

For the supplied packet, category is reading, options are A/B, and the allowed prediction statuses are completed, partly completed, or not completed. Unresolved is a possible review status, not a claim that a completed outcome was observed. Record what would count as the objective being met: an uninterrupted 30-minute section in the defined block.

Add a short private reason for your confidence only where your chosen local record method permits it; do not copy that reason into any application observation field. The machine fixture supplied with this lesson uses neutral codes only. It is a teaching fixture, not a substitute or extension for production typed evidence.

Before proceeding, explain how A2 could matter. A record that lists only supporting assumptions can conceal a known weakness even when its final choice is reasonable. Do not invent a probability unsupported by the packet.

### Action 2 — Preserve the choice-time version and establish the review

At the actual choice point, add the selected code and choice date without changing the original expectation. Preserve that version separately using a dated paper copy or an appropriate local snapshot. For the fictional route, write “Day 0, 10:05; A selected; rehearsal.” That is simulated chronology, not evidence of actual elapsed time.

For a real route, use an actual permitted calendar or local reminder method and verify it exists. For the fictional route, record only the intended Day 7 review. The application has not been instructed by this lesson to schedule it.

A clerical correction should identify the original field, corrected value, reason code, and date in a separate layer. If “A chosen” was accidentally transcribed as B, preserve both entries and identify it as a correction. Do not use a clerical correction to change a prediction because the outcome became known. A late reconstruction must be labeled late.

### Action 3 — Append an evidence-bounded review

Now open the later packet. Make a separate record:

`Original record reference | review date | outcome status | assumption-code dispositions: supported / contradicted / unknown | known-before versus learned-after distinction | next-process-change code | next check`

Keep the initial copy intact. Supported means the later evidence supports that assumption in the stated trial; it is not a universal truth. Contradicted means the assumed condition did not hold for the trial; it does not prove someone lied at choice time. Unknown is an honest result where evidence is missing.

Evaluate process quality before congratulating or blaming the outcome. Did the original record include relevant available evidence, a feasible alternative, a counterargument, and a review? What information could reasonably have been checked before choosing? Which new fact could not have been obtained then from this packet?

Choose one concrete change such as “recheck room availability on the session day,” not “be smarter.” A contingency can be worthwhile without proving that the earlier decision was irrational. Do not announce that this change improves every decision; it addresses one observed vulnerability.

## Privacy and evidence are part of the task

Use neutral labels; keep third-party content, identity, and consequential matters outside this exercise. A simple assumption code can preserve a concern without exposing a person. The action is preserving and reviewing the record, not collecting more private detail.

Only the application's existing reviewed observation fields may be submitted when this material is eventually integrated. The supplied JSON, local validator, and snapshot digest are not production observations and must not be imported as if they were. They test the example's structure. Do not infer that passing them grants completion credit or changes any historical score.

## Accessibility and progression

Use large-print cards or dictated neutral codes. A helper may transcribe under your privacy constraints without choosing or revising the substance. Record assistance where appropriate. A spoken note transcribed later is retrospective unless the original recording was actually preserved before choice.

For a harder route, compare two records with identical choice-time information and opposite outcomes. Ask whether their original process ratings should change merely because of the outcome. Then introduce one relevant pre-choice warning into only one record; explain why that changes the evaluation. Hold the information set fixed when testing outcome bias.

For later transfer, use another real low-stakes undecided choice, preserve it before acting, and perform its actual review. A fictional sequence or reconstructed memory cannot establish that prospective behavior. Do not select a sensitive decision merely to make the exercise feel more important.

## Interpret the result

**Supportive:** Initial uncertainty and the counterargument survive, the outcome is appended, and a specific process change follows the evidence. **Mixed:** The record is preserved but outcome quality is mistaken for proof of process quality. **Contradictory:** The prediction is rewritten after the result or private content is uploaded into evidence. **Inconclusive:** There is a retrospective story but no actual pre-choice version or clearly labeled rehearsal.

**Final review:** What did you know at choice time, what became known later, and what would a future reviewer need to distinguish a learning update from rewritten history?
