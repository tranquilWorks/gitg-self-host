# 09.10 — Fresh checks

Save your attempts before opening check-answers.md. Both cases are fictional.

## Check A — A control that moves the problem

A club's paper display can be placed on Table X or Table Y. X is stable but blocks the only shared access aisle when the display is open. Y leaves the aisle clear but has not been checked for stability. You are allowed to use either table if suitable, but not to close the aisle. A volunteer says, “Put it on Y; nobody has complained, and we had no incidents last month.”

Identify the hazard and affected people for both choices. Give separate entries for severity, likelihood, exposure, detectability, controllability, and mitigation. Select the next step without declaring unknown stability safe or treating the blocked aisle as acceptable. Specify who must verify what, a hold threshold, and what finding would permit a different decision. Is the month's no-incident history enough to establish zero risk?

## Check B — Exposure and a failed warning

For a harmless fictional sorting process only, each pass has independent mismatch probability 1/10, constant across passes. Compare the chance of at least one mismatch across three passes and six passes. Show the complement calculation, not 3/10 or 6/10 as an exact answer.

A proposed detector notices a mismatch only after all passes finish. Explain whether that detector prevents the first mismatch, supports later correction, or establishes both. What changes if passes share one common labeling fault and are not independent? What would be wrong with using these invented probabilities to certify the physical route in the guide?
