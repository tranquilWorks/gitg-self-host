# Sources and claim boundaries

Consulted 12 September 2026. The eight references below are background, not validation of this curriculum. No source exercise, numerical case, table, or substantial passage is reproduced. All learner cases, prompts, keys, fixtures, and demonstrations are original. A source being available does not establish specialist review, effectiveness, or actual learner acceptance.

## R1 — Risk identification, controls, and review

UK Health and Safety Executive, *Steps needed to manage risk*.
https://www.hse.gov.uk/simple-health-safety/risk/steps-needed-to-manage-risk.htm

The page was read. It supports distinguishing hazards and possible harm, considering likelihood and severity, implementing controls, recording responsibility, and reviewing changing conditions. The lesson does not import UK employment-law requirements, claim compliance, prescribe safe loads, or turn toy probabilities into measured physical risk. Exposure, detection, and permission in the fictional packet remain explicitly bounded design questions.

## R2 — Practical judgment and particulars

Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Book VI, W. D. Ross translation, Internet Classics Archive at MIT.
https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.6.vi.html

Book VI's account of practical judgment attending to particulars was inspected. It provides a limited historical framing, not authority for the modern privacy, consent, access, or cultural cases. The lesson does not endorse every claim in the text or portray one tradition as a universal moral test. The modern response rubric is author-prepared and permits multiple justified responses.

## R3 — Prospective plans and transparent changes

Center for Open Science, *Preregistration*.
https://www.cos.io/initiatives/prereg

The initiative page was read for the distinction between specifying a plan before results and making later work transparent. The private decision record is not an OSF registration and does not claim an independent timestamp. No claim is made that recording or preregistration alone guarantees reliable decisions, eliminates bias, or makes deviations invalid. A preserved plan and a later update remain distinguishable evidence layers.

## R4 — Descriptive location

NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, section 1.3.5.1, *Measures of Location*.
https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda351.htm

The section was read for mean/median distinctions. The dataset summaries, outlier comparison, and numerical illustrations are independently calculated from the supplied fictional values. Describing location is not a causal or population inference.

## R5 — Fitted regression

NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, section 4.1.4.1, *Linear Least Squares Regression*.
https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmd/section1/pmd141.htm

The section was read for least-squares model framing. The slope formulas, fitted values, residuals, and extrapolation boundaries in the lesson are checked using exact rational arithmetic. No confidence or prediction interval is asserted, and a fitted slope is not called an intervention effect. Regression toward the mean is demonstrated separately by an explicitly stipulated independent-noise model.

## R6 — Sampling variability and spread

Barbara Illowsky and Susan Dean, *Introductory Statistics 2e*, OpenStax, section 2.7, *Measures of the Spread of the Data*.
https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/2-7-measures-of-the-spread-of-the-data

The displayed section was consulted for the distinction between observed spread and sampling variability. No book case, figure, or paragraph is reproduced. The lesson's finite-population enumeration is original; it does not apply that toy sampling distribution to convenience samples or invent a confidence interval. This is not a reproduction or adaptation of the textbook's teaching sequence.

## R7 — Blocking and experimental comparisons

NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, section 5.3.3.2, *Randomized block designs*.
https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section3/pri332.htm

The section was read for treatment comparisons within blocks and attention to nuisance factors. The personal four-run layout exercise is not claimed to be a fully balanced population experiment. Its reversed order, different decks, practice, and repeated-person limits remain visible. A coin choice or reversed order does not establish elimination of every confound.

## R8 — Statistical versus causal quantities

National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, *Advancing the Framework for Assessing Causality of Health and Welfare Effects to Inform National Ambient Air Quality Standard Reviews*, chapter 3, *Introduction to Causal Inference Principles*, NCBI Bookshelf edition.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK588337/

The HTML chapter was read for the difference between observed statistical parameters and intervention-defined causal effects, and for assumptions connecting the two. No environmental or health inference is made here. The selection-reversal table, four-person counterfactual worlds, and randomized-offer packet are original mathematical examples, not empirical studies or conclusions about real learners.

## Sources not claimed as fully inspected

Pearl's introductory causal paper was located, but its PMC page returned a browser challenge. It is not used as a fully read source. No full-text Baron–Hershey outcome-bias paper was retrieved; no finding or effect size from it is asserted. The decision-record contrast is an explicit logical comparison of identical information sets, not an attributed empirical effect estimate.
