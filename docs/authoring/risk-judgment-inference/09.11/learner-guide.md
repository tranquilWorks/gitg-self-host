# 09.11 — Practical wisdom and context

## The skill and its boundary

Preserve a principle's purpose while changing its application for a materially different situation. “Always do the same thing” and “anything goes in context” both avoid the actual judgment.

**Canonical scope:** Apply principles with sensitivity to timing, role, relationship, capability, culture, and exceptional circumstances.

**Deliverable:** Six short context-to-action records, a justified exception with a remaining duty, and two fresh case responses. Allow roughly 40 minutes on paper or in a spoken rehearsal. No actual contact or personal disclosure is required. This is not legal, employment, safeguarding, or clinical advice.

[R2](../SOURCES.md) supplies a limited historical background: practical judgment involves particulars as well as general principles. The six modern cases and their pluralist constraints are original materials, not an assertion that one philosopher settles every cultural or moral disagreement.

## What makes a difference relevant?

Start with the good a principle protects. In this lesson, **give affected people usable notice when a commitment changes** protects their ability to plan, consent to alternatives, and avoid unnecessary waiting. It does not demand unlimited disclosure, unsafe communication, or certainty about an estimate you do not have.

**Timing** changes what information can still help. **Role** changes the responsibilities and authority attached to the task. **Relationship** changes what was agreed and what communication is appropriate, without changing anyone's dignity. **Capability** changes which action is feasible, not whether someone deserves consideration. **Culture** can shape a person's preferred channel or form of respect; ask or use an explicitly stated preference rather than assigning one from a group label. **Exceptional circumstances** can make the ordinary procedure harmful or impossible while leaving some responsibilities intact.

A relevant distinction connects a fact to the principle's purpose. “This recipient has no text access, so use the agreed phone relay” does that. “This recipient is less important, so no update is needed” substitutes a status judgment. A principle may also conflict with another legitimate good: a useful update must not disclose another person's private situation unnecessarily.

## A different worked example: returning borrowed cards

You promised to return a friend's game cards on Friday. One card remains at a second agreed storage location. A truthful update is useful: identify what can be returned now, when the rest can be returned, and whether the friend needs another arrangement. It is not necessary to disclose someone else's private reason for a storage change.

If direct speech is inaccessible, the friend's agreed text channel can carry the same substance. If the card is urgently needed before your proposed time, that new fact changes the repair needed. “I had a good intention” does not restore the friend's ability to plan. The principle survives while timing and channel change.

## Complete case packet

All cases are invented. Use only the facts stated; do not infer diagnoses, ethnic norms, employment rules, or unrecorded permissions. The shared commitment is to help with a low-stakes community display. Each case is independent.

| Case | Supplied situation and constraints |
| --- | --- |
| A — Partner | You and one willing friend agreed to start at 14:00. At 13:50 you learn you will arrive at 14:10. The agreed text channel works; the friend may decline to wait. |
| B — Coordinator | You coordinate three consenting roles: setup, labels, and photographs. Setup cannot begin until your materials arrive. The latest estimate is 14:20, with uncertainty of ten minutes. A shared update channel is agreed; you may revise the plan but not promise other people's extra time. |
| C — Access | A participant has explicitly said that small text is inaccessible and prefers an agreed voice relay. You can reach that relay safely. They have not consented to having access details discussed in the group. |
| D — Preference | Another participant requests a private message through an agreed coordinator rather than public correction. The coordinator can relay an explicit revised time and ask whether the new plan is acceptable. The person has not asked you to disguise uncertainty. |
| E — Limited role | You are a volunteer, not the schedule owner. You discover that a proposed delay affects the display opening. You may alert the owner with known facts but cannot approve a new opening time. |
| F — Protected channel | A communication channel is no longer safe to use, according to the case's stipulated safety restriction. An already agreed, safe support contact exists. No immediate public danger needs an emergency response in this fictional case. You must not use the unsafe channel or disclose private details to the group. |

### Action 1 — Explain the principle and compare the contexts

For each case, complete:

`Purpose protected | relevant facts | timing | role/authority | relationship/agreement | capability/channel | stated preference | exception, if any | information still missing`

Not every dimension must create a different action in every case. Mark “no distinct difference supplied” rather than inventing one. The six labels are prompts for inspection, not six required excuses. Name at least one fact whose removal would change your recommended action.

For B, distinguish a time range from a firm promise. For C and D, describe the actual preference in the packet, not what people from some culture allegedly prefer. For E, notice the boundary between informing and authorizing. For F, the safety restriction is given; this exercise is not asking you to investigate or prove someone's account of harm.

### Action 2 — Write an executable response

Write one or two sentences of message content and one next action for each case. Supply the information the affected person needs: what changed, what is known or uncertain, what options remain, and when the next update will occur. Do not promise a response or time you cannot control.

A good message is not necessarily maximally direct, long, or emotionally expressive. It must be understandable in the agreed channel. An indirect relay that omits the revised time fails the purpose; a concise private relay that conveys it can satisfy the purpose. A public disclosure that invades privacy is not better just because it is faster.

For any requested exception, fill:

`Ordinary action | material constraint | narrower substitute | duty that remains | review point | authority required`

Where a safe substitute is unavailable, state that limitation. Do not invent a trusted intermediary or compel a person to make contact. The supplied F case includes one already agreed support contact; a different real case may not.

### Action 3 — Challenge your own exception

Apply a **same-relevant-facts test**. Replace “my friend” with “a person I find difficult,” holding agreements, capability, risk, and role fixed. Would your responsibility change? If so, identify a relevant reason rather than preference alone. Then change one genuinely relevant fact, such as the recipient's channel access or who owns the schedule. Explain why a different action is now justified.

Check the practical outcome of your rehearsal: could the recipient know what to do next? Could a coordinator distinguish an estimate from an authorization? Was private information protected? Did a remaining duty disappear into the phrase “special circumstances”?

Complete both fresh checks, then read their separate answer guidance. Unlike an arithmetic problem, more than one response can meet the criteria. The key states required boundaries and representative responses; it is not a culture-ranking rubric or a machine score for wisdom.

## Conflicting goods without a magic formula

Suppose a delay update would be more understandable with someone's personal medical details. The default is not to share those details. You can state the practical constraint and revised plan without its private cause. Ask what the recipient needs for the decision, what you have permission to share, and whether a narrower disclosure is sufficient. Do not treat privacy as a reason to withhold every useful logistical fact.

Likewise, flexibility should not automatically transfer all inconvenience to the least powerful person. Offer a real option to decline or revise the arrangement. A power difference can affect how freely someone agrees, so a reply of “fine” may not establish every preference you hope it does. This lesson records the stated agreement, not a hidden psychological inference.

## Accessibility, progression, and actual transfer

Use a spoken comparison, six cards, large print, or a communication aid. A helper may read and transcribe; the reasons and decisions remain yours. Do not make fast social performance a prerequisite for careful judgment.

For a more demanding route, write two defensible responses to B that trade speed of notice against precision. Explain which initial notice is useful now and what later update it requires. Retain a stable principle rather than grading only rhetorical sophistication.

For later transfer, use an actual ordinary commitment change within your authority. Preserve only the minimum information needed for your private reflection. Record whether your message was actually sent, merely rehearsed, or blocked by missing access. No live interaction, reconciliation, or broad growth in wisdom is inferred from the fictional exercise. Escalate consequential legal or safeguarding questions to appropriate local support rather than applying a classroom exception rule.

## Interpret the result

**Supportive:** Actions differ for stated relevant reasons while usable notice, privacy, consent, and remaining responsibilities survive. **Mixed:** Channel choice improves but the revised time, uncertainty, or next owner remains unclear. **Contradictory:** Status or convenience is used to deny another person's consideration, or a safety restriction is overridden in the name of duty. **Inconclusive:** Every case receives the same slogan, with no connection from facts to action.

**Final review:** Which difference justified your adaptation, what duty remained, and could someone else use the same reasoning in the same circumstances?
