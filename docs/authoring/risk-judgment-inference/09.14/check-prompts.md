# 09.14 — Fresh checks

## Check A — A polished but confounded protocol

A person files eight familiar slips in layout U first, then twelve unfamiliar slips in layout L. The second run uses larger labels and a helper who chooses the boxes. The report discards two mistakes as “practice,” reports only completion time, and says forty slip movements are forty independent participants. A second analyst reruns the saved calculation and calls that an independent experimental replication.

Identify at least five separate design or reporting defects. Propose a feasible next comparison with complete material counts, consistent assistance, predeclared errors/missing-data rules, and a condition-order plan. Explain the actual unit and the distinction between reproducing analysis and collecting a new replication. State what peer review could inspect without claiming it guarantees truth.

## Check B — Apply the criterion without changing it

A four-run fictional pilot planned the same criterion as the guide: L at least five seconds faster on mean completed-run time with no lower correct-placement proportion. All runs assign ten slips.

| Run | Layout | Seconds | Correct | Incorrect | Unplaced | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | L | 18 | 10 | 0 | 0 | complete |
| 2 | U | 24 | 10 | 0 | 0 | complete |
| 3 | U | 22 | 10 | 0 | 0 | complete |
| 4 | L | 20 | 9 | 1 | 0 | complete |

Calculate the two mean times and accuracy fractions, then decide whether the criterion is met. Can the last mistake be excluded because L looked faster? Name a useful next design step without claiming the method is universally ineffective.

In a separate reporting exercise, replace run 2 with **interrupted; duration unknown; correct 6, incorrect 0, unplaced 4**. Explain how to preserve its assigned denominator and missing duration. Is silently retrying only that run until a favorable time appears acceptable?
