# 09.14 — Scientific reasoning and research design

## The skill and its boundary

Turn a claim into a test whose materials, measurements, comparisons, and limits another person could inspect. A small pilot can expose a bad design without establishing a universal effect.

**Canonical scope:** Understand hypotheses, measurement, controls, confounding, replication, peer review, and limits of inference.

**Deliverable:** A short pre-result protocol, four supplied pilot-run interpretations, a design critique, and a better replication plan. Use a paper-only analysis route or an optional harmless personal filing task. The default fictional route needs no participant, stopwatch, or real experiment. Allow two unrushed sessions. Never experiment on other people, essential services, health, deprivation, or hazardous activities under this lesson.

[R7](../SOURCES.md) supports the limited design background on blocking and treatment comparison; [R3](../SOURCES.md) supports making plans and deviations visible. The materials below are original, not a validated study or a claim that preregistration guarantees truth.

## From an interesting claim to an interpretable test

A **hypothesis** predicts an observable pattern under specified conditions. A **measurement rule** states what is counted, with units, start/end conditions, and missing-data handling. A **control comparison** keeps the relevant contrast visible. A **confound** offers another explanation for a contrast because factors change together.

**Replication** obtains new observations under a stated procedure; reusing the same saved results checks reproducibility of arithmetic, not a new empirical replication. **Peer review** can scrutinize methods and reasoning, but neither a reviewer nor a publication label guarantees correctness. State what a reviewer actually inspected, rather than filling in imagined approval.

Random assignment of condition order can help avoid choosing a favorable order. It does not guarantee balance in a tiny run, erase practice, or make a single person representative. Blocking can make comparisons within similar periods more interpretable, but all remaining differences and carryover still need consideration. More measurements on the same person do not automatically become independent participants.

## A different worked example: a comparison with two things changed

Suppose someone tries one label layout on six slips, then another on twelve slips, and reports that the first is faster. Layout and workload changed together. Even exact timing cannot isolate layout. A better next comparison fixes slip count and content, records errors, and varies layout with an order plan. If the faster condition made more filing mistakes, speed alone does not establish a better method.

The repair is not to declare experimentation useless. It is to identify which change would make the next observation answer the actual question.

## Complete filing materials

Draw five equal boxes in one row. Label them **A, B, C, D, E** for layout **L**, or **C, A, E, B, D** for layout **U**. Use the same label size, available reference, response method, and comfortable position in both layouts. The outcome is placing a slip in the box bearing its letter, not remembering the alphabet unaided.

Four complete decks are supplied. Each has ten slips, two of each letter. “Matched” here means matched count and frequency, not proof that every order has identical human difficulty.

| Deck | Slip order |
| --- | --- |
| X | A, C, E, B, D, B, A, D, E, C |
| Y | D, B, A, E, C, A, D, C, B, E |
| Z | C, E, B, A, D, E, C, D, A, B |
| W | B, D, C, E, A, D, B, A, E, C |

For optional physical use, write these on ordinary paper or point to the target boxes instead of moving slips. Do not cut materials with hazardous tools or work through strain. A helper may operate a timer or record your choices without selecting boxes. For the default, inspect the supplied results rather than manufacturing observations.

## The example protocol — adopt or revise before opening the results

**Question:** For this person, these materials, and this access method, does L reduce mean time compared with U without reducing correct placements?

**Example pilot criterion:** L's mean completed-run time at least **5 seconds lower** than U's, with no lower overall correct-placement proportion. This is a chosen workflow threshold, not a validated meaningful difference or statistical significance test. State a different justified threshold before results if you use one; never choose it afterward to favor the outcome.

**Units:** A run is ten assigned slips. A person is the experimental participant. In the four-run example there is one simulated person, not forty independent participants.

**Measurement:** Start when the first slip is made available; finish when the last assigned slip is placed. Count correct, incorrectly placed, and unplaced slips so their sum equals ten. Do not include a warm-up in the measured four runs. Keep the same available instructions and supports across layouts. An interrupted or incomplete run has no completed-run duration in the primary comparison; retain its assigned count and partial outcomes separately.

**Limits:** Optional real runs stop at 120 seconds or earlier for strain, access difficulty, or an interruption that breaks comparability. Do not continue to secure a favorable number. Do not record an interrupted run as zero seconds, delete it, or repeat only an unfavorable result without preserving the deviation.

**Order:** Use two blocks of two runs. A fair preselected choice determines the first block's order; the second reverses it. The supplied simulated sequence is **L then U; U then L**, with decks X, Y, Z, W respectively. The reversed second block is deliberate counterbalancing, not another independent random draw. State the sequence before seeing results. Rest or reset consistently between runs; record any difference rather than claiming all learning or fatigue vanished.

### Action 1 — Identify what is controlled and what remains confounded

Write the hypothesis, primary criterion, outcome definitions, count identity, order, exclusion rules, and fixed number of runs. Then list at least four influences: layout, practice/order, slip sequence, interruption, fatigue, measurement method, or support use. For each, state whether it is held fixed, varied deliberately, balanced imperfectly, or merely recorded.

Critique the supplied protocol. Different deck orders may differ in difficulty; practice can be nonlinear; a person may remember the prior layout; timing can include helper delay. Holding frequencies equal helps one comparison but does not prove equivalence. Name a result that would challenge the claim before reading any results.

### Action 2 — Inspect a complete pilot without discarding awkward results

Save your protocol. Open **pilot-results.md** only afterward. Label the route **supplied simulation**. Calculate mean time by layout, mean time by first/second position within a block, and overall correct placements by layout. Show the denominator for each.

Apply the chosen criterion exactly. Compare the within-block contrasts as well as the pooled contrast; a single favorable average may hide an order effect. Do not report repeated slips as independent people or infer a population effect from one simulated participant. A criterion not met is not proof of no possible benefit under every design.

For an optional real route, follow your saved protocol, use the provided decks, and record actual results with their limitations. Do not mix the supplied simulation with your real results as if all observations came from one trial.

### Action 3 — Design a more informative replication and review

Write a next protocol that addresses the strongest remaining confound while preserving the question. For example, use further matched decks, a preselected order schedule, consistent reset and timing rules, and clear interruption handling. A new condition order and new observations are a repeat; recomputing the same four times is only analysis reproducibility.

Describe exactly what a willing independent reviewer could inspect: material identity, saved plan, raw run records, count conservation, deviations, arithmetic, and claim wording. An actual reviewer would need to supply their own feedback. Do not create an “approved” receipt from your description of what they might say.

State scope limits in the conclusion: the person, task, response method, setting, and sampled occasions. Testing a different access method may be worthwhile, but it changes the target claim. A non-timed route can investigate accuracy or usability, not establish the same speed claim. Retain necessary supports rather than removing them for a supposedly pure test.

## An error-aware reporting template

`Question | planned comparison | hypothesis and threshold | unit and assigned count | raw outcomes | missing/interrupted runs | deviations | descriptive contrast | alternative explanation | next design | unsupported stronger claim`

A useful report says which evidence would discriminate between the explanations. “More research is needed” without specifying a changed measurement or comparison adds little. A negative or ambiguous pilot can be successful design work if it identifies a tractable flaw.

## Accessibility, progression, and transfer

Use large-print letters, tactile identifiers, spoken selection, or a screen-reader-friendly list. Keep the chosen response method stable across the comparison. Record a helper's role and preserve the learner's decisions. Stop when the task itself is unsuitable.

For a harder route, distinguish random sampling from randomized order, and identify the experimental unit separately from the number of rows. Ask whether an independently repeated personal result generalizes to other learners. It does not do so automatically.

For later transfer, critique an ordinary public experiment using its actual methods, not merely its headline. Missing methods remain a limitation. Designing a better study is not evidence that it was performed, and human research may require consent and oversight beyond this lesson.

## Interpret the result

**Supportive:** Materials and measures are complete, all assigned work is accounted for, and the conclusion separates a descriptive result from causal and population claims. **Mixed:** Both conditions are present but sequence or assistance is not recorded. **Contradictory:** Unfavorable runs disappear or a post-result criterion is represented as preplanned. **Inconclusive:** A hypothesis is stated but no implementable protocol or interpretable result record exists.

**Final review:** Which remaining confound could explain the contrast, and what exact design change would make the next comparison more informative?
