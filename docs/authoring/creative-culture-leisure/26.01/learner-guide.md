# 26.01 — Play for its own sake

Choose an ordinary, freely available form of play and return to it once. This companion explains the existing ten-day reserve–play–return protocol: reserve at least thirty minutes within the next three days, use the window without an output goal, and return within seven days. About fifty to seventy-five minutes overall is the expected burden; the existing shorter or split accessible variants remain legitimate.

## Remove the requirement to produce something

Play can include imagination, games, movement, music, humor or friendly competition. A game may have rules and an ending without becoming a test of your worth. What matters here is that improvement, winning, publication and measurable output are not conditions for the activity to count as worthwhile. An absurd unfinished story is allowed to remain unfinished.

The abstract and introduction of [Pressman and colleagues' leisure study, S11](../SOURCES.md#S11) describe voluntarily chosen leisure and report associations with well-being. They do not validate this ten-day play protocol, prescribe a happiness target or establish that these windows cause an individual's health to improve. No questionnaire from that study is administered here.

A brief note afterward can distinguish what happened from what was planned. Monitoring every moment during play would instead turn the window into assessment. You may use an ordinary end reminder to protect responsibilities without timing creative performance, counting ideas or grading enjoyment. The activity can be quiet, solo, seated, digital or supported; social participation is optional and requires willingness.

## Supplied play plan P9

On day one, the fictional participant Niko reserves day two, 18:30–19:00, at home for a playful story about a household object. Essential evening care is already covered by an agreed arrangement, the activity costs nothing, and no other person is expected to join. This is a supplied circumstance, not an assumption that every learner has uninterrupted time or someone else available for care.

The story starter is complete enough to begin:

> A teaspoon applies to become the moon's librarian. At the interview it is asked where it would shelve the tides. It asks whether the moon has a drawer for overdue clouds.

Follow any harmless direction that is amusing or intriguing. The spoon can misunderstand the job, open a soup library or abandon the interview. There is no word count, polished ending, skill target, audience or post. These three lines are an invitation to play, not a prompt requiring three successful inventions.

Niko places an output checklist out of reach and decides not to save a final story. The booked window is preparation. At this point no play has occurred, and an intention must not be reported as a completed activity.

## First window: an unfinished story stays unfinished

The supplied day-two window occurs for thirty minutes. Niko imagines the teaspoon interviewing a cloud, changes the librarian into a sleepy kettle and loses track of the initial plot. A thought appears that the story might make a good public post. Niko puts that idea aside and continues without editing for an audience. The window ends with the kettle still looking for a library card.

Those details are a fictional after-the-fact account, not live measurements of spontaneity. There is no count of laughs or rating of happiness. The unfinished plot is not a failure requiring another work session. The useful distinction is between an actual freely chosen activity and an imagined future output.

The temptation to optimize is kept in the record. Its appearance does not automatically mean the whole period became work; ignoring a sustained shift into performance would also be inaccurate. Afterward, Niko can describe what happened in ordinary terms without making every playful choice prove improvement.

## Return once without escalating the task

Niko chooses a second window on day six for fifteen minutes, four days after the first play and within the existing seven-day return window. The second activity is an absurd weather forecast delivered by a sock. A friend declines an invitation to join; Niko accepts the decline without asking for a reason and uses the solo route. The friend contributes no observation or relationship evidence.

Before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md), distinguish a reserved return from an actual return. A second plan does not count as activity. A more ambitious story, public audience or longer duration is not required. If necessary responsibilities, discomfort or pressure make the window unsuitable, adapt or defer honestly instead of manufacturing a completed record.

With five minutes to reserve the activity, thirty for the first window, fifteen for the return and a brief five-minute final review, the supplied overall plan is fifty-five minutes within ten days. The ordinary time windows protect availability; they are not performance targets inside play. Shorter accessible practice can still be legitimate under the existing variant, with the actual duration stated instead of pretending it was thirty minutes.

## Keep the existing protocol intact

The app's existing sequence remains reserve, play and return. Its completion criteria remain: all three actions attempted, at least two actions completed, at least one period of genuinely non-instrumental engagement, and a final review submitted. Reading this companion, answering its questions or following the supplied fiction does not submit that review or complete runtime actions.

Use only the app's existing available observation fields for an actual check-in. The source companion adds no field, no enjoyment intensity score and no new completion rule. The frozen package contains a documented marker that the existing check-in does not collect; do not invent a missing answer to fill it. Exact compatibility details are preserved in the canonical map for author review, not added to the learner's play window.

## Make a small actual opportunity

1. Choose a safe available activity, a realistic place and a specific window. Check essential responsibilities, cost and any other person's consent. Remove the output contract before beginning.
2. Use the reserved time for play. Let harmless changes and unfinished activity remain possible; stop or adapt if the activity becomes pressured, unsafe or compulsive.
3. Return once within seven days for a short period or another playful activity. Record a missed return as missed rather than counting the reservation.
4. Compare only afterward: what supported free play, what constrained it, and whether the second period supported or contradicted the first. Do not grade enjoyment.
5. Complete any actual app review through the existing rules, keeping the reading checks separate and completion distinct from mastery.

For support, begin with the teaspoon starter or a five-minute variation under the established accessible route. Split time when needed without concealing the split. For a harder reflection, distinguish an ordinary game rule from a demand to improve, publish or prove yourself; do not make the play itself harder to earn credit. Gambling, substances, costly risks, unsafe movement and neglect of essential care are outside this activity. Rest or deferral may take priority without implying a deficit.

Two windows can show only what occurred in those circumstances. They do not prove a stable capacity for spontaneity, a joyful identity or a particular amount of connection. No actual learner experience or runtime evidence has been produced by reading this example.
