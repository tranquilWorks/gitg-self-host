# 25.13 — Cultural appropriation, exchange, and attribution

Compare three proposed cultural uses for thirty minutes, then spend twenty minutes writing one exact revised plan. Work from the complete fictional cards below. No actual motif, restricted recording or protected expression needs to be copied, and no publication or sale occurs during this exercise.

## Permission belongs to a particular use

Viewing a resource, learning privately, adapting it, teaching it and selling something based on it are different acts. Credit identifies a contribution; permission concerns what may be done. Payment may fairly compensate work but does not automatically buy every use or establish community endorsement. A respectful decision can be to proceed within a clear invitation, ask a narrower question, or choose an original alternative.

[Local Contexts, S09](../SOURCES.md#S09) describes Indigenous community-defined provenance, protocol and permission labels. The relevant lesson is to attend to the appropriate holders and actual conditions, not to apply a label yourself or treat every community as having one rule. [WIPO's overview, S10](../SOURCES.md#S10) describes traditional cultural expressions and the variety of related protection questions. It is not a legal clearance service for these fictional cases.

Keep three questions separate. Legal rights concern applicable permissions and law; customary restrictions concern the practice's own authorities and protocols; ethical judgment concerns representation, consent, benefit and power. They can overlap without being identical. A claim that a use is legally possible would not itself settle the other two, and this exercise makes no jurisdiction-specific legal determination.

## Three complete proposed-use cards

**Case A — Public beginner lesson.** A fictional community teacher, Ren, openly offers an ordinary beginner pattern for private practice and one unrecorded noncommercial class demonstration. The written offer requires the credit “Beginner pattern taught by Ren through the community workshop” and prohibits copying the lesson diagrams. The teacher agrees the listed workshop fee covers this scope; all learners may decline the demonstration. Adaptation, recorded teaching and product sales are outside the offer and require a new discussion. No actual pattern or diagram is supplied here.

**Case B — Unverified ceremony recording.** A clip is publicly reachable on an unrelated account. Its caption calls it a sacred community ceremony, but the uploader's identity, authority, original audience and consent to recording are unknown. A viewer proposes to download it, edit it into a public presentation and teach its movements. No authorized community contact or use conditions have been established. Do not reproduce the clip while investigating whether it is restricted.

**Case C — Commercial representation.** A fictional seller plans thirty cards using a community-associated motif found online and the phrase “Official community design, authentically endorsed.” There is no permission, endorsement, documented source or benefit agreement. No cards have been produced, no orders taken and no money collected. The seller would receive sales income and control the description; the community would receive neither stated credit nor compensation and might bear misrepresentation risk. The facts concern an unsupported claim, not an assumption about the seller's ethnicity.

## Distinguish the five uses in each case

| Use | A: teacher's offer | B: ceremony clip | C: proposed motif cards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| View | Included in the public lesson invitation | Online access exists; legitimate recording and audience remain unknown | Online access does not establish source or authority |
| Learn privately | Explicitly allowed for this beginner pattern | Not established; defer copying or practicing the ceremony | Not established by the supplied facts |
| Adapt | Outside the stated offer; ask separately | Not authorized; defer | Not authorized; do not use motif in the proposed design |
| Teach | One unrecorded noncommercial demonstration with required credit | Not authorized; defer presentation and movement teaching | No teaching permission or representative authority established |
| Sell | Outside the offer; a new agreement would be needed | No sale permission established | No permission or endorsement; current plan cannot proceed as described |

The table does not declare that merely encountering a public page is a moral offense or that cultural exchange is generally forbidden. It distinguishes what is known from what is missing for a proposed action. Nor does it turn one willing practitioner into a universal representative who can settle every disagreement.

## Trace who gets what and who decides

In A, the teacher receives the agreed workshop fee and explicit credit, retains limits on diagrams and wider reuse, and has authorized a bounded learning exchange. Those conditions do not prove that all possible community members agree, but the learner can identify the invitation being relied on. A request to expand the use should include new audience, recording and compensation details without demanding unpaid cultural education.

In B, an unknown uploader controls circulation while the depicted people may face exposure outside the intended setting. Those people receive no established benefit in the supplied facts. A suitable authorized practitioner, institution or rights holder may help clarify access, but choosing any person who identifies with the community would not automatically resolve authority. Until a legitimate route and scope are established, defer the proposed reuse. A refusal or inability to establish permission is a valid stopping point.

In C, the unsupported endorsement helps market the seller's product while moving representation risk to people who have not agreed. A donation made afterward would not repair missing consent or make the authenticity claim true. Remove the claim and the unverified motif. If a future collaboration is desired, negotiate actual scope, credit, control and fair compensation with appropriately authorized partners before using their contribution.

## Exact revised plan for Case C

“I will not use the unverified motif or the phrases ‘official,’ ‘authentic community design’ or ‘endorsed.’ I will make an original arrangement of ordinary lines and circles without copying this motif, and describe it as my own geometric card design. No community name will market the alternative. No cards will be offered during this exercise. If I later seek a community collaboration, I will first establish the appropriate authority and agree the contribution, permitted uses, review control, credit and compensation; declining collaboration remains acceptable.”

The alternative removes the unsupported material rather than merely adding a credit to it. It does not prove that any future design is legally cleared or free from every resemblance. If a collaborator contributes, their actual contribution must be credited accurately; the seller cannot keep calling the entire result solely their own. The original plan had no existing stock or paid orders, so no invented recall or refund is reported.

## Make a decision someone can inspect

1. Identify source, context, proposed use, audience and possible authority for all three cards. Keep unknowns visible.
2. Compare the five uses and separate legal, customary and ethical questions. Do not let credit stand in for permission.
3. Trace money, visibility, control and risk. Name misrepresentation only where the facts support it.
4. Write one plan specifying material used and excluded, required consent, attribution and benefit. A decision not to use the material is complete work.
5. Open the [later packet](later-packet.md) and examine what happens when the audience or representation changes.

For support, use the cards without investigating real restricted material. For a harder pass, explain how two community members could reasonably disagree and what their actual authority would allow you to conclude. An accessible conversation or authorized description may inform a real case, but neither pressure nor unnecessary disclosure is justified to finish the exercise. The product is a bounded permission analysis, not a universal rule about who may create.
