## Check A — Feasibility, dominance, and preference sensitivity

A person may use at most 100 future minutes for one complete plan. All plans are free, permitted, and meet the same access requirements. Their risk, reversibility, and effects on others are equal for this toy comparison. The only varying relevant quantities are:

| Plan | Future minutes | Benefit units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P | 50 | 5 |
| Q | 70 | 8 |
| R | 80 | 6 |
| S | 130 | 10 |

First screen feasibility and identify any dominated feasible plan. Then use the explicitly stipulated preference score **benefit minus lambda times future minutes**. Lambda is how many benefit units this fictional person trades for a minute; it is not a probability or a universal human value. Evaluate at lambda = 0.10 and lambda = 0.20.

For each value, identify the preferred feasible plan, its best foregone feasible alternative, and the score difference. Find the lambda at which P and Q tie. Explain why changing lambda changes a preference, not the empirical time data. Would an arbitrarily high benefit score make S feasible? What omitted real-world difference could invalidate the claimed dominance outside this packet?

## Check B — A good-looking plan is not necessarily available

You have 60 minutes before an existing commitment. Plan F is a 45-minute shared activity with a friend who has **not replied** to the invitation. Plan G is 50 minutes of a solo activity you control. Plan H is 70 minutes of another solo activity. Travel, setup, access, and cleanup are already included in these totals. No deadline or other duty compels any optional activity.

Classify each as confirmed feasible, infeasible, or contingent on missing agreement. Explain the difference between treating F as impossible forever and treating it as not yet confirmed. Name a useful, proportionate next step that preserves the existing commitment without repeatedly contacting the friend.

A later message confirms F, with enough time still remaining for its complete 45-minute plan. Compare F and G under your stated priorities, naming the best foregone confirmed alternative for whichever you choose. Explain why today's unconfirmed invitation must not be recorded as a completed shared activity or an already scheduled replacement next week.
