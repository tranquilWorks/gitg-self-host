# 09.09 — Answers and corrective guidance

## Check A — Key

P, Q, and R meet the 100-minute constraint; S does not. Q dominates R on the stipulated relevant criteria: it uses 70 rather than 80 minutes and provides 8 rather than 6 benefit units, with all other relevant conditions equal. P and Q are not dominated by one another: P uses less time, Q provides more benefit.

At lambda 0.10, scores are P = 0, Q = 1, R = -2. Q wins; P is its best foregone feasible alternative. The difference is 1 benefit unit. The opportunity cost is the value of P under that stated preference, not the sum of P, R, and the infeasible S. A zero net score in this toy scale does not mean the alternative has no human worth.

At lambda 0.20, scores are P = -5, Q = -6, R = -10. P wins; Q is the best foregone feasible alternative. The difference is again 1. Negative net scores do not represent moral failure, and the prompt compares one of these complete plans; adding a new no-action alternative would require specifying its benefit and conditions rather than silently changing the option set.

Set 5 - 50 lambda = 8 - 70 lambda. Thus 20 lambda = 3, or lambda = 0.15. Both scores then equal -2.5. A tie does not authorize claiming a unique winner. Any tie-break must be named separately.

Changing lambda changes how this fictional person values future time relative to the stipulated benefit scale. It does not alter the measured or given minutes. S stays infeasible regardless of its score because the time constraint is mandatory. In a real comparison, an omitted access, consent, risk, timing, or obligation difference could defeat the apparent Q-over-R dominance.

**Repair:** Screen the constraint before optimizing. Preserve the two score tables and identify the exact preference change. Do not average raw minutes with benefit units unless the conversion rule is explicitly supplied and appropriate.

## Check B — Key

G is confirmed feasible within the supplied conditions. H is infeasible because 70 exceeds 60. F is contingent on missing agreement: 45 minutes would fit, but willingness is not established by silence. It is not necessarily impossible forever, and it is not a confirmed alternative yet.

A proportionate step is one ordinary clarification through the agreed channel, with a cutoff that still preserves the existing commitment, or choosing G without waiting. Rest or no new activity can also be considered explicitly because no optional activity is required. Do not repeatedly message the friend or treat missing agreement as consent.

After the timely confirmation, F and G are both feasible. Either may be selected with a defensible preference and an explicit sacrifice; the supplied times alone do not establish that the shorter shared option is universally better. If choosing F, G can be the best foregone confirmed alternative under a stated ranking; if choosing G, the reverse can apply. Keep any explicitly introduced rest alternative in that ranking too.

A future invitation, a confirmed plan, an attempted action, and a completed activity are different states. A replacement next week needs its own agreement. Do not use an imagined future replacement to pretend the current sacrifice is costless.

**Repair:** Give missing permission its own status. Do not turn an uncertainty into either an assumed yes or an unsupported permanent no.

An acceptable response preserves constraints, states priorities, identifies the strongest unchosen feasible plan, and performs a consistent sensitivity check.
