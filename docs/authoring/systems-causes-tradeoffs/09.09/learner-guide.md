# 09.09 — Tradeoffs and opportunity cost

## What you are practicing

Choose among worthwhile alternatives while keeping the sacrificed good visible. First protect real constraints; then compare benefits, costs, time, risk, reversibility, and effects on other people. A table helps expose a choice. It does not make your values objective or entitle you to override someone else's needs.

**Canonical scope:** Compare alternatives across benefits, costs, reversibility, time, risk, affected parties, and foregone options.

**Deliverable:** A feasibility screen, a reasoned comparison of three complete plans, the best foregone alternative under your stated priorities, one sensitivity check, and a next-step/review record. Allow about 30 minutes. Paper, speech, a text editor, or option cards are sufficient. All required facts for the fictional route are supplied; no spending, booking, or actual social contact is required.

This is educational source, not personal financial advice or a general scoring algorithm. The decision-analysis background in SOURCES.md supports documenting criteria, alternatives, uncertainty, and rationale. The plans and numerical checks below are original examples, not a validated measure of human flourishing.

## A reference for comparing without pretending everything is equivalent

A **constraint** determines whether an option is acceptable at all: an existing promise, essential access, a time limit, or a required permission. A **preference** distinguishes acceptable options. Do not allow enough preference points to compensate for violating a genuine non-negotiable constraint. When a constraint is uncertain, mark the uncertainty and obtain the required information instead of silently treating it as satisfied.

A **direct cost** is something consumed by the chosen plan, including future effort and time. An **opportunity cost** is the value of the best feasible alternative you forgo under the same decision boundary and priorities. It is not the sum of every appealing activity you can imagine. Without a stated preference ordering, the best foregone alternative can remain unresolved; explain that rather than inventing precision.

**Reversibility** has stages. You may be able to cancel before starting, stop after a small trial, or switch later, but spent time and expired opportunities cannot always be recovered. Distinguish reversing a decision from fully recovering its consequences. A cancellable invitation can still impose inconvenience.

**Risk** concerns possible adverse outcomes and uncertainty about them. Do not assign a numerical probability without a basis. Some consequences should be discussed in words or treated as constraints. Affected people are stakeholders, not entries whose rights disappear when the total score is high.

A **dominated option** is no better on any included relevant criterion and worse on at least one, compared with another feasible option. That judgment depends on the criteria and evidence being complete enough. An omitted access need, obligation, or timing difference can invalidate an apparent dominance claim.

A **sensitivity check** changes one stated assumption or preference weight and asks whether the choice changes. This reveals dependence; it is not permission to change the rules solely to make your favorite win. Preserve the original comparison and identify whether the change is new evidence or a changed preference.

## A different worked example: a short reading block

A person has 40 minutes. One complete plan is a 30-minute article and 10 minutes of notes; another is a 40-minute conversation with a willing reading partner. Both are feasible. Reading the article and taking the conversation would require 70 minutes even before notes, so that combination is not a live alternative in the same block.

If the person's priority is understanding the article today, the first plan may be preferred. Its best foregone alternative can be the conversation, not the article plus the conversation plus every other good use of time. If the partner is available only today and reading can move without cost, the relevant opportunity changes. There is no universally correct ranking without the person's aims and actual constraints.

## Complete practice packet: one protected two-hour block

All people, arrangements, and values here are fictional. You have a block from **13:00 to 15:00**. You already promised a brief check-in from **14:50 to 15:00**. Keep those **10 minutes protected**, leaving **110 minutes** for an optional plan. There is no emergency, required medical action, or other existing duty in this packet.

You report enough capacity to attempt any of the three plans, while valuing recovery as a legitimate good. All options are free, use resources already available, and meet the stipulated access needs. A willing friend has confirmed availability for the specified visit today. A later visit is possible in principle but not confirmed. The archive task has no deadline today. No physical repair, deletion of originals, or hazardous tool use is involved.

### Complete option cards

| Plan | Optional block allocation | Main benefit | Foreseeable cost or uncertainty | Reversibility and affected people |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Archive | 90 minutes sorting duplicate copies; 20 minutes recovery | Progress on a personally useful archive | Less recovery than C; no live connection today; amount finished uncertain | Can stop after a small batch; spent time remains spent; no one else is waiting on completion |
| B — Visit | 20 minutes round-trip travel; 60 minutes visit; 10 minutes buffer; 20 minutes recovery | Time with the willing friend today | Less recovery than C; no archive progress; travel may overrun | Confirm timing before leaving; tell the friend promptly if changing plans; missed opportunity may not recur soon |
| C — Recover | 110 minutes freely chosen quiet recovery | More protected recovery time | No archive progress or visit today; restoration is not guaranteed | Can change the activity within the block; elapsed time and today's visit opportunity are not recoverable |

Each complete plan fills the optional 110-minute block and preserves the check-in. For B, schedule travel and the visit before recovery and protect the 14:50 return boundary. The buffer is included, not an extra ten minutes to add afterward. If travel uncertainty cannot be reconciled with the existing promise, modify or decline that plan rather than sacrificing the promise silently.

Two tempting alternatives are deliberately incomplete: **D**, doing both the full archive task and full visit, needs 90 + 20 + 60 + 10 = 180 minutes before any recovery; **E**, postponing the visit to next week, has no confirmed agreement. D is infeasible in this block. E is a possible future inquiry, not a guaranteed substitute for today's visit.

### Action 1 — Screen constraints before ranking benefits

Write the decision, time boundary, protected promise, access conditions, and whose agreement is needed. Calculate each complete plan's total. Keep rejected and unresolved alternatives in a small record with their reasons instead of removing them without explanation.

Then record your initial preference and priorities. You may prefer A, B, or C, but must explain what makes its benefit more important in this fictional situation. Do not invent an unmentioned emergency or call recovery morally superior to connection, or connection superior to useful work.

For every feasible plan, fill this comparison:

`Benefit | direct time/effort cost | possible harm or uncertainty | reversible step | consequence that cannot be recovered | affected people | information still needed`

Avoid double counting. The 90 archive minutes are already part of A's 110-minute plan, not an additional 90-minute opportunity cost to subtract from an arbitrarily assigned score. An opportunity cost is an alternative's forgone value; mixing raw minutes, feelings, and invented points can conceal assumptions rather than clarify them.

### Action 2 — Name the conflicting goods and test an assumption

Choose the two strongest feasible candidates under your priorities. Describe the benefit each protects and what the other would sacrifice. Do not make a rejected alternative look worthless to ease the choice. Identify the **best foregone feasible complete plan** for your provisional choice and state why it outranks the other unchosen plan for you.

Now perform one of these sensitivity checks, keeping the original record:

- The friend genuinely confirms an equally suitable visit next week. How does that affect the cost of forgoing today's visit? Does it change the ranking or only make the sacrifice easier to address?
- The archive task acquires an agreed deadline tomorrow, and no other adequate work block is available. Does that create a new constraint or change a preference? State which.
- The person reports that today's capacity has dropped so a trip is no longer manageable. Re-screen feasibility before scoring the options.

These are separate hypothetical cases, not facts to combine opportunistically. Pick one and write exactly what changed. Different conclusions can be defensible when their criteria are explicit and consistent. If the choice is close, a limited information check may be more valuable than another invented decimal place.

For an optional numerical extension, use the fully specified toy preference rule in Check A. Its numbers are stipulated units for that case, not measured values for friendship or rest. A weighted total never rescues an option that violates its mandatory constraints.

### Action 3 — Make a bounded choice and preserve the sacrifice

Write a short decision statement:

`I choose __ because __ under these constraints: __. The best feasible alternative I forgo is __, which matters because __. The assumption most likely to change this choice is __.`

Then record the next reversible step. For A, define the first small batch while preserving originals. For B, confirm the already discussed timing before leaving and keep the promised return boundary. For C, protect the block without claiming that a particular activity must restore you. For the fictional route, write the step as a **rehearsal**, not an action you actually performed.

Use this review form after a real appropriate attempt, or as a labeled plan for this fictional one:

`Expected benefit and burden | actual observation or not yet observed | effect on the protected promise | effect on others | what changed | whether the sacrificed good needs another opportunity`

Do not invent a successful outcome to close the exercise. A decision can be reasonable given the evidence and still have an inconvenient outcome. Conversely, a lucky outcome does not justify having ignored a promise. Record process quality and outcome separately.

A later place for the sacrificed good is a possibility, not a retroactive claim that the tradeoff cost nothing. Seek the relevant person's agreement before treating another visit as scheduled. Do not commit another person to your revised plan without consent.

## Fresh checks, Accessibility, and progression

Complete both checks in **check-prompts.md**, then inspect **check-answers.md**. Check A makes time, benefit, feasibility, dominance, and a preference switch numerically explicit. Check B tests the difference between an attractive plan and an available, permitted plan.

For Accessibility, use one plan per card or a spoken comparison of the strongest two after screening all candidates. A helper may record your priorities, but should not replace them with their own. Keep time and consent constraints visible. A calculator may support arithmetic; no speed requirement applies.

For a more demanding route, create a staged alternative such as a brief trial followed by a decision, then account for all setup and switching time. It is a new complete plan, not free flexibility. Include what information the trial could actually reveal and what opportunity would expire while waiting.

For later transfer, use one ordinary decision you control, record the initial priorities and alternatives before acting, and return after the chosen opportunity. Keep high-consequence legal, medical, fiduciary, or emergency choices outside this exercise and seek suitable expertise. No self-improvement target authorizes overriding another person's rights.

## How to interpret your result

**Supportive:** The choice preserves real constraints, compares worthwhile alternatives, names a best foregone plan under explicit priorities, tests an assumption, and distinguishes a planned step from an actual one.

**Mixed:** The comparison is thoughtful, but the later visit is treated as guaranteed or travel buffer is counted twice.

**Contradictory:** A preferred plan wins only by dropping the promise, presuming consent, or assigning enough points to outweigh a mandatory access condition.

**Inconclusive:** A table is filled but no conflict, sacrifice, sensitivity result, or next step is stated. A fictional decision does not establish actual follow-through or wise judgment across contexts.

**Final review:** What genuine good did the decision cost, why was that sacrifice acceptable under the stated priorities, and what would make you reconsider?
