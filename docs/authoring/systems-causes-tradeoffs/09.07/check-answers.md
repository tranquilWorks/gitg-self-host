There are 2 + 3 times 4 = 14 original legitimate requests across each three-day run. Baseline and hiding each have 9 permanently settled and 5 unfinished. Clarification has 11 settled and 3 unfinished. Twelve processing attempts under baseline do not equal twelve permanently settled originals; the three scheduled reopenings account for the difference in this model.

On Day 1, clarification's ready queue is 3 rather than baseline's 2. Its all-unfinished total is nevertheless the same: 3. By Day 3 it avoids two unfinished requests relative to baseline, conditional on the stipulated zero-reopening rule. Its remaining queue of 3 is stable, not eliminated. Hiding produces a ready queue of 1 but no reduction in all unfinished work relative to baseline.

The rework route reinforces load after a one-day delay. Processing opposes the ready accumulation but is capacity-limited. The dashboard incentive can make the hiding policy attractive without making it effective. A diagram should label that behavioral response as a hypothesis, while labeling the simulation's rules as stipulated.

A defensible decision is to investigate clarification with a reversible, authorized trial and measure reopens, setup effort, requester burden, missing requests, and access. Do not declare that zero rework is an established real outcome. Unequal request size, longer reopen delays, or an access barrier could alter the conclusion.

**Repair:** If your totals lose tomorrow's reopening or refused work, add separate stocks and rerun all three days. If your chosen policy changes because the boundary expanded, preserve that revision rather than rewriting the initial prediction.

## Check A — Key

Available work is 8 + 2 = 10. The ending ready queue is 10 - 3 = 7. One reopening is pending, so all unfinished work is 8. Only 3 - 1 = 2 requests are permanently settled in this packet. Conservation is 10 = 8 + 2.

Capacity 20 would still permit at most 10 attempts before any next-day reopening. Capacity is a limit, not a supply of work. The scheduled reopening's next-day addition follows the stipulated rule; anxiety causing additional errors needs actual evidence. One possible hypothesized reinforcing loop is queue growth, increased pressure, additional errors, and rework feeding the queue. The hypothesis requires more than a plausible story.

**Repair:** Keep units and timing beside each number. Today's ready work excludes tomorrow's reopening, but today's all-unfinished total includes it.

## Check B — Key

Policy A orders 6 boxes in Week 1 and another 6 in Week 2 because physical stock is still 4. Six arrive in Week 3 and six in Week 4. Final stock is 16, a surplus of 6.

Policy B orders 6 and then 0 because the Week 2 inventory position is 4 physical plus 6 outstanding. Six arrive in Week 3; none arrive in Week 4. Final stock is 10 with no surplus. The pipeline is part of the state; ignoring it repeats a correction before the original correction has arrived.

Outside this toy packet, consumption, cancelled orders, uncertain delivery, unusable stock, or a changed target could matter. A due date and reliability estimate may be necessary; subtracting an order that will never arrive can create a shortage. Do not invent those events in the stipulated calculation.

**Repair:** Separate physical inventory from outstanding orders. Check a delivery's arrival date before treating its boxes as usable, and check all outstanding commitments before issuing another correction.
