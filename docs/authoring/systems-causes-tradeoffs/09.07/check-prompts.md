## Check A — An outflow is not the same as a finished request

A different desk starts with 8 ready requests and no reopening or hidden stock. During one day, 2 new requests arrive, 3 processing attempts occur, and 1 of those processed requests is scheduled to reopen tomorrow. No other changes occur.

Calculate the ending ready queue, delayed reopening stock, all unfinished work, and permanently settled count for this day. Check conservation. Explain what is wrong with reporting “three requests permanently resolved.” Would increasing capacity to 20 allow 20 attempts in this same day? State the necessary limit.

Identify which statement is arithmetic and which needs additional evidence: “A scheduled reopening adds work tomorrow” and “The larger queue makes the workers anxious, causing more errors.” Name a feedback loop that the second hypothesis could form, without treating the hypothesis as established.

## Check B — Ordering without remembering what is already ordered

A fictional stationery cupboard needs 10 boxes. It starts Week 1 with 4. There is no consumption, loss, or other delivery during this exercise. An order placed at the end of Week 1 arrives at the start of Week 3; an order at the end of Week 2 arrives at the start of Week 4. No orders are placed after Week 2.

Policy A orders the gap between 10 and the boxes physically present, ignoring orders already in transit. Policy B orders the gap between 10 and the sum of boxes present plus outstanding orders. Neither policy orders a negative quantity. Both start with no outstanding orders.

For each policy, record the Week 1 and Week 2 orders, the deliveries in Weeks 3 and 4, the final physical stock, and any surplus above 10. Explain the delayed information that distinguishes the policies. Identify one situation outside these stipulated facts in which a pipeline-aware policy would need another input rather than blindly subtracting all outstanding orders.
