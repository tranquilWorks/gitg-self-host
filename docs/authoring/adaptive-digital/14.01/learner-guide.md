# 14.01 — Digital and media self-governance

## Decide what the session is for

Self-governance means choosing how media fits your purposes, including enjoyment, connection and rest. Minutes alone cannot decide whether a session was useful. The important distinction is between an endorsed activity and a continuation you did not mean to choose. A boundary makes the choice visible; friction makes an unwanted continuation less automatic while preserving access you need.

Allow 25 minutes for the complete fictional exercise. You need only a notes tool. An optional live version adds three ordinary sessions and a short manual review; it requires no monitoring software, account credentials or exposure to upsetting or sexual content. It is not a treatment for compulsive use or a test of moral worth.

## Locate the choice, not just the screen

A **cue** is what starts the action: a notification, boredom, habit or a deliberate plan. The **entry route** is how you arrive: a direct conversation link, a feed or a game lobby. The **stopping point** is tied to purpose: send the reply, finish a chosen round, or read one item. **Friction** changes one of those transitions, such as removing a feed shortcut. A total communication blackout may reduce interruptions while defeating care, work or accessibility needs.

Different media require different choices. For notifications, distinguish who or what must reach you from promotional prompts. For feeds and outrage, ask whether the next item serves the current question or simply continues reaction. For gaming, keep chosen play and social commitments visible. For pornography or other lawful adult media, a relevant adult can choose a private boundary consistent with consent and their values; this exercise requires no consumption, disclosure or moral verdict. Repeated checking needs a defined question or next check opportunity, not punishment. Mark categories not relevant to you as not applicable. Persistent loss of control or significant distress warrants appropriate support beyond a harsher app rule.

Real settings vary by version. For example, [Apple's Focus documentation](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/allow-or-silence-notifications-for-a-focus-iph21d43af5b/ios) describes people/app choices and exceptions. Use your provider's current instructions, confirm what a setting actually does, and test essential routes with an agreed ordinary message. A setting name does not guarantee every urgent communication can reach you.

## Worked example: fewer minutes can be a worse result

A person plans a 20-minute game with friends and finishes as agreed. Another spends six minutes repeatedly opening a news app without answering their original question, then misses an expected transport message. The longer session may better fit its purpose. Compare intended action, stopping point, necessary access and aftermath; do not award success simply to the shorter number.

## Complete case: Noor's message-to-feed detour

Noor opens the fictional Linkspace app at 18:00 to confirm a community meeting room. The goal is to send the reply and close it by 18:05. Noor also wants a separate chosen news-reading session after dinner. A household contact must be able to send a time-sensitive pickup message through Linkspace; ordinary phone calls and the device's accessibility alerts must remain available.

The current app opens its home feed. Promotional alerts lead to that feed. Direct conversation bookmarks are available. These are the complete stipulated control options:

| Option | What changes | Cost or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Disable every Linkspace notification | Pickup messages stop alerting; phone calls still work |
| B | Disable promotional alerts only; retain direct-message alerts; bookmark the meeting conversation | News must be opened deliberately; other messages can still interrupt |
| C | Give another person the device passcode and let them choose when access is allowed | Control of access and private information leaves Noor |

No actual app login or setting change is needed to solve the case. Treat the controls as fictional, not instructions for a real product.

### Action 1 — Write a purpose and route card

Record the cue, intended reply, detour, stopping point, desired later leisure and three access requirements. Select an option and explain its tradeoff. Write an undo instruction in plain language. A valid plan preserves voluntary control and the three requirements; it need not eliminate all interruptions.

### Action 2 — Rehearse installation and essential access

Describe the specific setting and bookmark change. List checks for direct messages, calls and accessibility alerts, identifying which are only planned tests. Write a one-sentence stop cue: “After __, I will __; news remains available at __.” Save this before [the later session records](later-packet.md). Do not report installed settings or delivered messages unless the supplied record says so.

### Action 3 — Judge three opportunities without moral arithmetic

For each record, note whether Noor sent the reply, used or bypassed the stopping point, retained necessary access, and experienced an unwanted cost or useful benefit. Separate an unavailable opportunity from a failed one. Calculate minutes only where start and end are given; do not invent missing durations or an average with missing data. Recommend keep, adjust or remove based on fit, not shame.

Attempt [the fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the key](check-answers.md). A complete output includes the route card, reversible change, access-test plan, three-session review and one revision. No real-media history needs to be stored or shared.

## Adaptations and development beyond the default

Use a paper cue, accessible shortcut or a supporter explaining settings without receiving credentials. On a shared or managed device, use only authorized controls and preserve other users' needs. If coercive control or stalking is a concern, do not make a surprise account change to prove autonomy; seek a safe, appropriate support route.

Harder task: Noor now needs live group updates during an event. The original “reply and close” boundary does not fit that purpose. Write a time-bounded exception, a natural ending and a return to the ordinary setting. Legitimate exceptions are not secret failures. For an experienced learner, repeat across a second media category and compare the actual entry mechanism; copying the same timer into every activity misses the skill.

At three later natural opportunities, implement one reversible change you control, manually record the observations above and revise it. Retain meaningful enjoyment and essential communication. A successful small experiment does not establish long-term control, diagnose addiction or validate this exercise as treatment. Unlike 08.02's attention experiment, this task audits media entry routes, permissions and voluntary boundaries across purposes.

**Supportive:** The chosen task and essential access work, and later leisure remains deliberate.

**Mixed:** The default route improves but a real exception needs an explicit revision.

**Contradictory:** Necessary messages are blocked or another person controls access to enforce compliance.

**Inconclusive:** A limit is selected but no session or access check is observed.
