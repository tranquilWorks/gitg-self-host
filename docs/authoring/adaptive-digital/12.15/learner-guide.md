# 12.15 — Adaptive movement and fitness

## Keep the purpose; change the fit

Adaptation changes how a person participates so movement fits their capacity, environment and chosen purpose. It is not necessarily an easier version of somebody else's activity. A familiar seated sequence, assistance, equipment, shorter bouts or a different location may preserve the intended benefit; replacing all movement with paperwork does not demonstrate physical participation. Equally, cancelling an unsuitable session is not a character failure.

This guide teaches how to carry an existing individualized plan into a changing setting. The default is a fictional planning and review exercise, requiring paper or an accessible notes tool, about 30 minutes, and no physical performance. Do not adopt the case's minutes as your own exercise prescription. Health and exercise content must be adapted to the person and does not replace licensed medical care. No diagnosis, pregnancy status or disability disclosure is required.

## What can change, and what must be checked

Separate **purpose** (what participation matters), **task** (the actual movement), **load** (duration, intensity, repetitions and frequency), and **conditions** (equipment, assistance, transport, temperature and recovery). Shorter duration alone does not make an unfamiliar task suitable. A helper can change physical demand as well as access. Write down which variable changed before comparing outcomes.

For disability or chronic illness, useful activity and progression depend on the person. The [CDC guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/chronic-health-conditions-and-disabilities.html) supports appropriate activity and discussion of type and amount with a qualified professional. It does not authorize a universal increase after a successful attempt. If an established plan calls for pacing, rest or symptom-contingent limits, retain them. Delayed effects matter; finishing a session does not settle its fit.

Pregnancy and postpartum changes require their own care discussion; [CDC's pregnancy guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/healthy-pregnant-or-postpartum-women.html) advises consulting the responsible clinician about adjustments. An older age alone does not define capacity. An injury may change a formerly familiar activity's suitability. For an unfamiliar restriction or changed symptoms, the useful next step is a precise question and an access plan, not an improvised movement test.

## Worked example: a location change is still a change

An established plan permits a familiar supported seated movement at a quiet indoor venue. Moving it outside adds heat and an uneven approach. Calling the task “the same three minutes” hides those changes. A useful adaptation first checks the approach, temperature, seating and assistance. If they cannot be confirmed, the original indoor option or deferral remains available. This reasoning establishes planning fit, not medical clearance or actual tolerance.

## Complete case: Morgan's studio session

Morgan wants to continue a familiar movement-to-music activity with their community group. The following is a **stipulated fictional plan**, already agreed with Morgan's qualified practitioner. It is case data only:

| Requirement | Supplied current status |
| --- | --- |
| Activity | The familiar seated sequence already taught to Morgan; no new movements |
| Amount | Two three-minute bouts, a two-minute seated rest between; no increase before review |
| Support | Usual seat and existing positioning aids; helper Lee present throughout |
| Setting | Quiet, cool indoor studio; accessible route confirmed open |
| Stop/review | Stop for new or changed symptoms; contact the existing care team; do not make up missed bouts |
| Follow-up | Record ordinary function later that day and next morning for the plan review |
| Transport | Arrival 09:00; pickup 09:20; same confirmed support for both journeys |
| Other session tasks | Four minutes setup; three minutes closing and preparing for pickup |

These facts are sufficient to make a paper plan. They do not teach a bodily sequence and therefore cannot be used as instructions to perform one.

### Action 1 — Build an adaptation card

Write `purpose | existing plan | task/load | required assistance | environment | transport | stop/help | later review`. Fill every field from the case. Arrange setup, first bout, rest, second bout and closing inside the transport window. Label active minutes, other session minutes and spare time separately. Do not count travel or waiting as performed exercise.

Draft this clarification request with the actual missing detail filled in when needed: “My purpose is __. My current plan is __. The changed condition is __. Which existing option remains appropriate, what should stop the activity, and what later effects should prompt review?” An unanswered request is not permission to progress.

### Action 2 — Rehearse the pre-session decision

Check all requirements, including the return journey, before classifying the session as planned. Use **confirmed**, **missing**, **changed** or **unknown**, rather than a single ready/not-ready feeling. Say what you would do if Lee were absent. Retain Morgan's choice to decline even when the conditions fit. Save the plan before opening [the later packet](later-packet.md).

### Action 3 — Reconcile attempts and changes

For each later record, enter `date | conditions actually present | bouts actually performed | change/stop | later function | contact sent/answered | next decision`. Count actual bouts only. Preserve an incomplete next-morning report as unknown. Explain why completing an earlier session cannot authorize a longer next session.

Then complete these alternative planning cards. They ask for useful preparation, not generic referral:

| Changed context | Supplied facts | Produce this concrete next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pregnancy | Familiar activity; clinician has not yet discussed the new pregnancy | A concise request naming the activity, current load, pregnancy change and questions about adjustment/stop signs; no case-dose adoption |
| Aging | Familiar plan still fits; transport now requires an extra transfer | Map who provides each transfer and where the person rests; confirm those supports before scheduling |
| Injury | Existing guidance excludes one movement used in the familiar sequence | Mark that movement unavailable and ask the practitioner for an appropriate substitute; do not simply reduce its repetitions |
| Variable capacity | Person's own plan requires pacing and records next-day effects | Preserve its limit and later observations; no automatic catch-up or calendar-based increase |

Attempt [the fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the answers](check-answers.md). A complete source exercise contains the adaptation card, feasible schedule, contingency, reconciled later records and four context cards.

## Supported, harder and later routes

Use dictated notes, a supporter reading one card at a time, or a large-print schedule. Keep aids and assistance; “independent” does not mean removing them. If no current plan exists, finish the fictional exercise and prepare the named-purpose request above. That is preparation, not exercise performed.

For a harder paper case, arrival changes to 09:07 while pickup stays 09:20. Preserve the existing activity and support limits. Identify the shortfall and request a revised transport slot or another already-agreed option; do not compress rest or invent a higher-intensity substitute.

At a later real opportunity, use your own familiar, appropriate plan and change only what it permits. Verify one access condition, record actual participation and subsequent effects, and bring a concrete question to the existing review. If changed symptoms or uncertainty make that unsuitable, use the care/support route. This extends 12.14's long-term participation planning into present adaptation; it does not replace qualified movement instruction.

**Supportive:** The chosen purpose remains possible with verified assistance and actual follow-up inside the person's plan.

**Mixed:** Access works, but later function changes; the record prompts review rather than progression.

**Contradictory:** Assistance or rest is removed to make the session look more capable, or missed movement is made up against the plan.

**Inconclusive:** A complete schedule exists, but no participation or later response has been observed.
