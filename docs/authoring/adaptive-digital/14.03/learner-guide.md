# 14.03 — Digital identity, privacy, and data rights

## Follow the information beyond the visible profile

Privacy choices concern what information is collected, how identities are linked, who can use it, for what purpose and for how long. Hiding a public profile field changes an audience; it may not change the provider's collection or erase a recipient's copy. A useful audit names the specific data flow that changed and the uncertainty that remains.

The default uses a complete fictional service packet and takes about 30 minutes. You need only a notes tool. No real account, legal request, private screenshot or contact upload is required. The optional real route is limited to an account you control and current official instructions. This is privacy literacy, not a legal determination or a guarantee of anonymity.

## A practical vocabulary

**Supplied data** are entered or uploaded by a person. **Observed data** come from activity or device interactions. **Inferences** are conclusions drawn from those records; they can be wrong. **Identity linkage** connects records through a login, address, device signal or shared identifier. A pseudonym does not necessarily break those links. **Audience** concerns who sees something; **purpose** concerns what may be done with it. Consent to one purpose should not be silently treated as agreement to every other use.

The [FTC's tracking explanation](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-websites-and-apps-collect-and-use-your-information) distinguishes collection and settings; private browsing does not make activity invisible to websites. For a real service, inspect current policy and settings, retain dates, and mark ambiguous language unknown. A privacy setting, a deletion request and verified processing are three different states.

Legal rights depend on applicable law and circumstances. The [UK ICO's erasure guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/your-right-to-get-your-data-deleted/) describes a right with conditions and exceptions. Do not import its rules or deadlines into another jurisdiction, or treat a fictional policy below as a real legal promise. Use the relevant regulator/service's verified process and appropriate qualified advice for a disputed case.

## Worked example: the consent changed, but a copy remains

Someone allowed a club to publish a picture on its own page, but did not agree to advertising use. Removing the club post can change future visibility without retrieving a copy already downloaded by a viewer. Record the request, the page result and the outside copy separately. A useful follow-up asks the responsible party about the additional use; it does not claim the person became anonymous after one toggle.

## Complete fictional packet: Mira's Sketchyard account

Mira's purpose is to show drawings to approved hobby contacts without publicly revealing a precise location or inviting automated contact matching. Mira needs email access for recovery. **Sketchyard is invented.** The following six records are the entire policy/control packet for this exercise, dated 1 September 2026:

| Record | Current fact or stipulated policy |
| --- | --- |
| P1 — Profile | Mira supplied nickname MiraDraws and location “Cedar House, Unit 4”; profile currently public. Location can be cleared without deleting the account. |
| P2 — Audience | Private mode limits future profile/post viewing to approved followers. It does not remove already downloaded copies. |
| P3 — Contacts | Contact discovery is on and a list was previously uploaded. Turning it off stops future uploads/matching; deleting the stored list requires a separate request. |
| P4 — Collection | Provider stores sign-in device/time records for its stated security purpose; the private-profile setting does not stop that collection. Optional personalized recommendations are a separate switch. |
| P5 — Deletion | The service accepts a specific stored-contact-list deletion request and reports its status. The packet supplies no processing deadline or promise to remove recipients' copies. |
| P6 — Identity | Recovery email is linked to the account; clearing public location does not alter recovery. An old public drawing included the same location in its caption. |

One approved follower has already downloaded that drawing. No statement about this follower deleting it is available. No real people, addresses or service terms are represented by the invented names.

### Action 1 — Draw a data-flow ledger

Use `item | supplied/observed/inferred | identity link | audience/recipient | purpose | available control | retention unknown`. Fill rows for profile location, uploaded contacts, sign-in records, recovery email and the old caption. Note which record supports each conclusion. No inference model is described in the packet, so do not claim one was inspected; the general concept does not establish a specific practice.

### Action 2 — Choose changes that match the purpose

Write a minimal sequence that removes the precise public location, limits future audience, stops contact discovery and addresses the old caption/stored list separately. Preserve recovery email. State the tradeoffs: new viewers will need approval and automatic contact finding is lost. Use this request template: “For this account, please explain the status and process for deleting the previously uploaded contact list. Please identify any retained categories, reasons and further steps.” Do not include actual contact details or secret values.

Mark each proposed change **planned** until the supplied record establishes it was performed. Save the ledger before [the later packet](later-packet.md). A request about contacts is not a request to delete the whole account.

### Action 3 — Verify each claim at its own boundary

Apply the later observations to the ledger. Distinguish the public preview, persisted settings, request acknowledgement, provider response and outside copy. Write a short truthful result using “changed,” “requested,” “confirmed by provider” and “unknown” precisely. Choose a future trigger such as adding a linked app or a policy change. Attempt [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the separate key](check-answers.md).

A complete output includes all five data rows, policy references, the change/request sequence, tradeoffs, later status and residual-copy statement. “Private now” without these distinctions is incomplete even if the public preview looks correct.

## Access, harder work and real transfer

Use an accessible dashboard or one setting at a time. A helper can read explanations without receiving credentials or seeing contacts. On a shared account, separate your authority from another person's choices. In a stalking or coercion situation, settings changes may have consequences; use a safe device and suitable specialist support instead of this ordinary-account exercise.

Harder case: a linked drawing app retains a copy under its own account. Add a separate recipient, control and policy row; revoking future access does not establish deletion of earlier transfers. If policy is vague, write a focused question rather than invent a retention period.

Later, choose one real privacy priority and one account you own. Inspect the current official settings/policy, make a reversible authorized change if appropriate, and verify through the service's own preview or another authorized view. Keep a private minimized receipt without names or identifiers. Record any rights request as pending until its response is known. This is distinct from 14.02's data recovery and 14.04's protection against unauthorized access: a service may securely retain information you prefer it not to collect.

**Supportive:** Specific flows change as intended; collection and copies outside the tested control remain explicit.

**Mixed:** Future sharing is reduced, but older material needs another request or recipient review.

**Contradictory:** Private mode is represented as anonymity or a request acknowledgement as universal erasure.

**Inconclusive:** A policy was skimmed without linking its statements to an item, control or observed result.
