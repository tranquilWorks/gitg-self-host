# 27.09 — Coherence without rigidity

## Keep the principle visible while the method changes

A principle tells you what good you are trying to protect; a method is one way of protecting it. Confusing the two makes every change feel like betrayal. But merely renaming an inconvenient duty as flexibility can excuse harm. Coherent revision needs both supporting and contrary evidence, a legitimate alternative and an honest record of the old view.

Allow twenty minutes to compare the fictional arrangement, ten for two later opportunities and ten for checks. [IHI's improvement overview, S03](../SOURCES.md#S03) supports testing a change and learning from its actual consequences. The principle/method distinction and messaging case are original curriculum-based work, not evidence that an organizational model settles ethical beliefs.

Worked example: someone values hospitality and believes every visit must include a cooked meal. A guest explains that a shorter no-food visit is more accessible. Changing the method may better serve welcome. Serving nothing without considering an already-promised meal would be a different case; the actual agreement matters.

## The nonurgent-message agreement

Fictional Jo manages voluntary planning for a small reading circle. Nobody relies on Jo for emergency, clinical or safety support. The principle is reliability: people can use timely, accurate information and know when to expect a reply. Jo's old method is answering every nonurgent message immediately.

Two prior observations are supplied. On Monday an immediate answer let another organizer finalize a room booking before 16:00; that supports promptness when a decision truly depends on it. On Tuesday five nonurgent interruptions broke Jo's preparation period and led to an incorrect room number in one answer; that challenges automatic immediacy. Neither observation establishes all future effects.

The organizers agree to a one-week trial. Ordinary questions received before 17:00 will be answered in the 17:00–17:15 window that day. If an actual decision is needed earlier, a sender may use the already agreed direct coordination route and Jo will address the real deadline or explain unavailability. This is ordinary low-stakes coordination, not an invented emergency service. Questions arriving after 17:00 receive a next-day expectation unless another agreement is made. Everyone explicitly accepts the trial; no colleague inherits hidden work.

The two natural opportunities to inspect are a Thursday question received at 14:00 with no earlier need, and a Friday question received at 16:00 with a 16:30 decision deadline. The second uses the direct route as agreed. Do not deliberately delay it to prove the new window works.

## Make a revision with a memory

1. Write four headings: principle, old method, evidence for/against, alternative. Include both prior observations. Name a fact that might require revisiting the principle's interpretation itself—for example, evidence that what Jo calls reliable service excludes members unable to use the agreed route. That is more consequential than irritation at notifications.
2. Save the trial agreement and read the [later packet](later-packet.md). Judge each opportunity against its own deadline, not only the generic window. Record information accuracy, any unmet need and whether the original interruption cost was actually measured.
3. Keep, restore or revise the method with a reason. State the stable good, the changed behavior, any necessary repair and what remains uncertain. Preserve “I used to answer immediately” rather than claiming the new view was always held.

## Revision is bounded by real duties

Use a written reminder, visual cue or a chosen planning helper. If consultation would be unsafe, choose an entirely self-controlled low-stakes habit or stay with the supplied case. Do not experiment with consent, medication, emergency response, required professional coverage or another person's rights.

A harder case reveals one member cannot access the direct channel. The agreement is not adequate merely because others accepted it; establish a workable route before relying on the trial. In later transfer, observe two ordinary opportunities and one genuine cost of the old method. Do not manufacture a crisis. A promising pair of replies can support continued testing while broad claims about efficiency and moral coherence remain unproven.
