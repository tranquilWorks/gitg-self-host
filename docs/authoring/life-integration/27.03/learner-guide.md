# 27.03 — Seasons and changing emphasis

## Give this season a boundary and a review

A season names what is asking more of you now; it does not explain your whole life or prescribe when you must recover. Building, maintenance, caregiving, recovery, exploration, grief and transition can overlap. A useful description changes a few arrangements while protecting what cannot simply wait.

Use the fictional fortnight below. Allow twenty minutes to prepare, fifteen to examine the later record and ten for checks. No personal care details are needed. [OpenLearn on changing priorities, S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) recognizes that work–life priorities can change. The seven season descriptions and this particular plan come from the curriculum and original exercise, not a diagnosis or a validated timeline.

Worked example: a person preparing to move pauses an optional reading group, but first tells the group and returns its borrowed materials. Calling the period “transition” does not itself release the agreement. Nor does a moving date establish when the person will feel settled.

## Complete fortnight file: October 5–18

In the fictional case, Rowan has two additional companion visits for a relative during each of the next two weeks. Each visit uses 45 minutes including travel. Necessary care arrangements are already established; this task changes no treatment, supervision or clinical duty. Rowan's regular work and essential personal needs remain protected. Available discretionary time was 240 minutes per week before these additional visits and is now 150. Their end date is unknown.

A willing relative, Jules, has agreed to receive a practical handoff note about arrival times and the existing contact route. Jules has not agreed to take over a visit. Rowan also co-owns an optional puzzle project with Kit, who is willing to pause it if informed by October 6. The project would otherwise take sixty minutes weekly. No financial or caregiving dependency relies on it.

| Disposition | Item and owner | Bounded arrangement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advance | Rowan: handoff note | 20 minutes once on October 5; Jules checks receipt |
| Protect | Rowan: existing essential work and care | Retain current arrangements; escalate actual gaps appropriately |
| Protect | Rowan: quiet rest opportunity | 30 minutes weekly already within discretionary time; nobody else's work is assumed |
| Wait | Rowan and Kit: puzzle project | Ask/confirm pause by October 6; keep unfinished file |

The week-one new allocation uses ninety minutes for additional visits, twenty for the note and thirty for the quiet period. This leaves one hundred of the original 240 minutes unallocated. Unallocated time is not proof of spare energy and need not be filled. Week two has no planned note-writing cost, so its accounting differs. The review is October 19, with an earlier check if visits, support, safety or capacity change.

## Make three decisions instead of labeling everything

1. Write two present demands, the one confirmed support and the uncertain duration. Consider all seven season descriptions, then choose caregiving with maintenance as the most useful description here. State why “building everything at once” does not fit the supplied capacity, without treating other seasons as inferior.
2. Copy the advance/protect/wait plan with owners and communications. Keep no more than two advance, two protect and three optional wait items if adapting it. Distinguish Jules receiving a note from Jules covering care. Save the plan before the [later packet](later-packet.md).
3. Compare both weeks with the plan. Name one carried-out advance, each actual protection and any failed protection. At review choose what stays paused, what may resume or what can end with notice. The calendar date opens a question; it does not end the season.

## Adapt the amount, not the responsibility away

Use symbols, spoken notes or a chosen helper. With very limited capacity, protecting one essential arrangement may be enough; label other areas unreviewed. A harder variation makes the visits sixty minutes each in week two. Recalculate discretionary capacity and reassess the optional work before adding pressure. Do not borrow another person's labor without agreement.

In a later real fortnight, make one advance action and use one feasible protection, keeping actual events separate from intentions. A blocked rest period may point to a support problem, not insufficient determination. Grief and recovery need no deadline imposed by this exercise, and serious symptoms or care disruption call for appropriate timely support. The output is a situated plan and review, not proof of successful caregiving or recovery.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Capacity, access and context govern actual attempts. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Human dignity is never scored.
