# 27.04 — Roles and responsibility portfolio

## Find the obligation beneath the loudest request

A role is a relationship to responsibilities, not a demand to spend equal time on eight identities. Some responsibilities come from a promise, a dependent or legitimate authority; others are self-imposed expectations. Work notifications can be loud without being urgent, while a quiet promise to a friend can still be binding.

Allow twenty minutes to map the fictional roles, ten for the follow-up and ten for checks. [OpenLearn's work–life discussion, S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) supplies context for changing priorities across life. The role inventory below is a curriculum-based planning aid, not legal advice about employment or family duties.

Worked example: a volunteer assumes that attending every optional committee discussion proves commitment, then misses a promised key return. Declining one optional discussion and returning the key serves an actual responsibility. It does not show that community membership is less valuable than other roles.

## Eight complete role cards for Alex

The fictional Alex works in a non-safety-critical administrative role. The manager's current agreement is that nonurgent evening messages may be answered by 10:00 the next working morning; there is no evening on-call duty. Urgent operational matters use a separate staffed route. Alex must not alter that route or use this exercise to test an emergency.

| Role | Current status | Actual responsibility or limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual | Held | Submit ordinary library renewal by October 8 |
| Partner | Not held | No partner role assumed or required |
| Relative | Held | Already agreed Saturday call with aunt |
| Friend | Held | Promised ten-minute check-in with Sam on Tuesday at 19:00 |
| Worker | Held | Complete assigned work and follow the agreed response window |
| Citizen | Held | Existing civic obligations remain; no current task is supplied |
| Caregiver | Not held | No dependent-care obligation in this case |
| Community member | Held | Return borrowed room key by Friday 17:00 |

At 18:58 on Tuesday, a nonurgent message asks Alex to correct a meeting title by 10:00 Wednesday. The correction needs twelve minutes. Alex feels it must be done immediately and misses Sam's check-in without notice. On Wednesday Alex has a suitable 09:30–09:50 work window. Sam later says a Thursday 19:00–19:10 replacement is welcome, but no other relationship outcome is supplied.

The quieter individual obligation is also at risk: the five-minute library renewal has not been submitted. It can be done Wednesday 18:00–18:05 through the existing library route. The room key and aunt call have not disappeared merely because the exercise focuses on friendship.

## Repair one collision and keep the other cards visible

1. For every card identify the source of responsibility and mark held, sought, not held or consciously unchosen where appropriate. Do not assign a partner or caregiver to make the portfolio look complete. Where the case supplies no actionable civic detail, preserve that limit rather than inventing a legal task.
2. Separate the manager's actual agreement from Alex's instant-response rule. Plan the twelve-minute work correction within its deadline, acknowledge the missed friend promise and use the welcome repair slot. Put the renewal in its specific window without silently claiming it is already done.
3. Save the map, then examine the [later packet](later-packet.md). Revisit all held roles: did work remain served, did Sam receive the agreed check-in, and did a new burden land on someone else? Remove one unsupported demand, not a real duty.

## A boundary needs authority and follow-through

Use culturally fitting role names, spoken cards or a trusted adviser. An adviser can help clarify a duty but cannot erase another person's rights. If a live workplace or care arrangement is uncertain, confirm it through an appropriate route before changing availability. Keep sensitive details in private notes; this source exercise requires no application entry.

A harder case gives Alex an actual on-call duty during the friend slot. The nonurgent assumption no longer applies: arrange coverage or renegotiate the personal commitment rather than ignoring duty. Later transfer is one low-stakes role repair with an actual response and a check that the louder role remains adequately served. Unequal time can be responsible; a tidy role map without action cannot demonstrate that.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Capacity, access and context govern actual attempts. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Human dignity is never scored.
