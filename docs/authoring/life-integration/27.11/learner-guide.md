# 27.11 — Legacy in people, institutions, and works

## Leave something that can live without your supervision

Legacy can be a cared-for relationship, a useful institution, a carried tradition, shared knowledge or repaired harm. Fame and ownership are not the test. Sometimes repairing an overdue obligation matters more than starting something with your name on it. A useful handoff includes a willing recipient, authority to change it and a way to retire it. A polished artifact nobody wants to maintain is not a completed legacy.

Use the complete fictional case below. Allow twenty minutes to choose the contribution, thirty to build it and fifteen for a handoff attempt. These are teaching allowances, not a demand for unpaid institutional labor. For real work, first check what users want and what responsibility you actually hold. NASA's distinction between status reporting and assessment that informs corrective action [S02](../SOURCES.md#S02) supports checking usefulness and maintenance. Applying it to a small community guide is original synthesis, not NASA certification.

## A guide with a willing user and an unfinished ownership question

Noor helps at a voluntary neighborhood room. Consider five possible legacies: a reliable relationship with a new helper; a booking process others can maintain; an optional closing tradition; a usable room-opening guide; and repair of an incorrect booking notice. The room coordinator actually wants the guide, but the wrong notice is due for correction first. The coordinator has authorized Noor to correct that notice and draft the guide; this does not authorize a new volunteer obligation for anyone else.

**Complete room rules, version 2, checked December 2:** The coordinator confirms each booking. A confirmed helper collects the key from the staffed desk at 09:00, reads the current booking card and opens the room for that booking only. A missing or inconsistent card goes back to the coordinator; do not guess a booking. After use, return the key to the same staffed desk before 12:00. Contact the coordinator through the room's existing message book. This guide does not cover emergencies, building access outside those hours or safeguarding responsibilities.

**Draft A:** “Get the key, check bookings, open up and put everything back. Ask Noor if unsure.” It omits the authorized desk, times, missing-card rule and durable question route. A willing new adult helper, Pat, has agreed to test a draft using the supplied case, not to work a shift. Coordinator Lee can accept maintenance responsibility but has not yet done so.

A worked improvement replaces “ask Noor” with the existing coordinator route and adds the actual key-return deadline. Its footer reads: “Room-opening guide, version 2, checked December 2; stored beside the booking cards. Intended for confirmed helpers during staffed hours. Proposed maintainer: coordinator; acceptance pending. Recheck after booking-rule changes; withdraw if no one can verify the rules.” Noor's name can appear in history without becoming a permission gate.

## Repair, test and transfer responsibility honestly

1. Record why this guide is wanted and why the notice repair comes first. Write the exact corrected notice from the supplied later facts only when they become available; do not pretend the correction was already delivered. Build Draft B from the complete rules, including question route, scope, location, last-checked date and retirement condition.
2. Give Pat this test: “Your booking is confirmed, but the booking card is missing at 09:00. What would you do? Where does the key go after use, and by when?” Save the draft and expected answers before reading the [later packet](later-packet.md). Observe use without explaining away unclear wording. Access aids are welcome; record instructional help if given.
3. Revise the actual confusion and ask Lee explicitly whether maintenance is accepted, with what review trigger. Save the acceptance or its absence. Remove unnecessary founder approval. An adopted correction, a usable guide and an agreed maintainer are separate results; none establishes future institutional survival.

## A small contribution is enough

For support, dictate a short guide or improve one existing paragraph with the user's agreement. Without a willing user, make a private rehearsal and mark handoff untested. Do not publish private names, contact details or community knowledge without authority. For a harder case, the proposed maintainer declines: agree another owner or keep the draft out of operational use. Never silently make Pat responsible because Pat tested it.

At a later real review, ask whether the guide is still used, corrected or properly retired. A maintained work can matter without broad recognition. This exercise cannot prove repaired relationships, durable institutions or effects on future generations.
