# 12.01 — Health literacy and preventive care

## Turn an uncertain record into a useful care question

Health literacy includes knowing what a record establishes, what remains unknown and which service can resolve it. Screening looks for a condition before symptoms; symptom evaluation investigates a concern; treatment addresses a condition. A reminder, an appointment and completed care are different events. An empty record proves none of them.

This source companion teaches navigation across screening, symptoms, medicines, vaccination, dental care and sexual health. Use the fictional packet below. You need paper or a text editor, no account, purchase, personal history or actual contact. Allow 15 minutes for the summary, 10 for the simulated exchange and 10 for checks. Completing this route produces a care-navigation artifact, not completed preventive care. Health and exercise content must be adapted to the person and does not replace licensed medical care.

## A small, trustworthy summary

Record the source and date alongside each status. “Due according to my existing care plan” has a basis; “everyone my age needs this” needs current local and individual guidance. Screening can have benefits and harms, so ask about the relevant options rather than maximizing tests. Symptoms deserve their own question even when preventive records look complete. A checklist cannot rule out illness.

For medicines, a useful private list includes prescription and nonprescription products, supplements, the written instructions and actual use, plus any discrepancy. A pharmacist or prescriber can clarify it; the worksheet cannot authorize starting, stopping or changing a medicine. For vaccination, seek records and applicable local advice before inferring missing doses. Dental reminders identify follow-up work; they do not prove either disease or completed treatment. Sexual-health questions may involve prevention, symptoms, testing, contraception or consent, depending on the person. No one must disclose sexual history to complete this learning exercise.

Use teach-back to check the explanation: restate what happens next, who does it, when, and what remains unresolved; invite correction. This tests shared understanding, not the patient's intelligence. See [AHRQ's communication tool](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/teachback.html). Qualified clinical advice and local emergency routes take priority over a worksheet when symptoms are concerning; do not wait for this exercise or a routine appointment in an emergency.

## Worked example: a dental reminder

A fictional letter says “contact us to arrange the review already discussed.” Sam writes “review requested by existing dental plan; not booked.” The dental service offers Tuesday, but Sam cannot travel then. Sam asks for an accessible slot and keeps “booking unresolved.” Copying Tuesday into a calendar would not establish an agreed appointment. Once the service confirms Thursday and transport is arranged, “booked” is justified; “attended” still is not.

## Complete materials: Morgan's Monday file

All facts are invented. Today is case day 1, Monday. Morgan is an adult with no current symptom concern in this packet. No ages, diagnoses, medicine doses or screening/vaccine schedules are supplied or needed. The clinic contact and messages below are simulated; do not send anything.

| Area | Information available on day 1 | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screening | Existing clinic note: discuss individual preventive options at the next routine review | No test is ordered and no due date is supplied |
| Symptoms | Morgan reports no current symptom concern | A report, not a health examination or guarantee |
| Medicines | Two prescribers appear in the private medicine folder; current instructions for product M are unclear; no list review has occurred | Do not guess use or dose; ask pharmacist/prescriber to reconcile actual use with instructions |
| Vaccination | Old clinic record is absent | Status uncertain; neither unvaccinated nor protected follows |
| Dental care | Reminder says book the review in the existing plan; no booking confirmation | Follow-up due according to that reminder, not completed |
| Sexual health | Morgan requests a private discussion of prevention options at routine review | No history, exposure or test indication is specified |

The clinic's fictional records service accepts a request from Morgan or a specifically authorized helper. Morgan consents to the learner drafting a records request only; no sending, medicine disclosure or broad record-sharing permission exists. Morgan can use text communication and needs a written reply; the clinic can provide it. The records service says allow two working days for a response, then contact the records desk on day 4 if nothing arrives. These times are toy service facts, not clinical waiting advice. The medicine uncertainty goes separately to the pharmacist/prescriber promptly; a vaccination-record request is not its resolution.

### Action 1 — Create the six-area summary

For every row write `status | supporting fact | unknown | appropriate next channel`. Include the medicine discrepancy and dental reminder as open work even while selecting vaccination records for the worked exchange. Add the written-reply access need and the exact consent boundary. Do not fill missing clinical facts with assumptions.

### Action 2 — Draft a bounded request and preserve it

Write two or three sentences requesting the existing vaccination record and the information required to release it securely. Ask who will interpret it, not which vaccine to administer. Example opening: “I want to check which vaccination records you hold. Please explain the secure request process and who can advise on my current individual needs.” Keep a separate line for the medicine clarification and dental booking routes.

Save your summary and draft before opening [later-packet.md](later-packet.md). Predict what a record acknowledgement, a partial record and a booked appointment would each permit you to claim. The default is finished by analyzing the supplied replies; a simulated message is never an actual contact.

### Action 3 — Reconcile the reply without closing the wrong gap

Apply each later branch separately. Preserve day 1 facts, add the dated event, write a teach-back and name the next actor and follow-up. Use `drafted → authorized → sent → acknowledged → record received → clinically interpreted` only when that exact event is supplied. These stages are evidence distinctions for this case, not a universal linear clinical workflow. Booking and attending care remain separate tracks. Missing answers stay open.

Attempt [check-prompts.md](check-prompts.md), then use the [corrective key](check-answers.md). The source artifact is complete with all six areas, the limited draft, both reply reconciliations and a next-step record. These reading outputs add no application action, evidence field or completion rule.

## Access, deeper work and transfer

Dictation, a reader, an interpreter or a consented support person can assist. Use category labels instead of medicine names in shared notes; private clinical details belong only in appropriate care channels. An inaccessible portal, cost or missing service is an access barrier: record the barrier and alternative channel, not personal failure. No live route is necessary to finish the default.

Harder case: the record received on day 3 belongs to a different person with a similar name. Do not use or redistribute it; contact the service through its verified privacy/correction route and keep Morgan's record uncertain. Describe only the mismatch category in the learning record. A correct identity is a prerequisite for interpretation, not something inferred from a matching first name.

At a later suitable real opportunity, prepare one private question, use an authorized care channel, and record what actually happened. Ask a qualified service to explain applicable options, access, benefits/harms and follow-up. Real care may take longer than the learning exercise. An author-checked simulation is not an independent observation or qualified review.

**Supportive:** The correct record arrives and its uncertainty is clarified by the relevant service, with unresolved care explicitly retained.

**Mixed:** A record is obtained but incomplete; navigation progresses while clinical status remains open.

**Contradictory:** Missing records become invented vaccination status, a medication is changed from a guess, or concerning symptoms are deferred for the checklist.

**Inconclusive:** Only a draft or acknowledgement exists; neither record receipt nor care completion is established.
