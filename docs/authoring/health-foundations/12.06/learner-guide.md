# 12.06 — Cardiovascular endurance

## Ask whether an effort is repeatable, including its aftermath

Cardiovascular endurance concerns sustaining activity and recovering appropriately for the person's health and goals. A faster time can come from greater effort, an easier route or changed assistance; it does not automatically show improved capacity. Easy aerobic participation, higher-intensity training and a maximal test are different tasks with different preparation needs.

This guide provides a complete fictional route-and-recovery comparison. Allow 10 minutes to choose the route, 10 for the later sessions and 10 for fresh checks. Paper, dictation and simple time arithmetic suffice. No exercise, wearable, purchase, fitness disclosure or physical challenge is required for the default. Its output is an evidence-based decision about supplied records, not a claim that the learner exercised. Health and exercise content must be adapted to the person and does not replace licensed medical care.

## Duration, intensity, frequency and recovery

Duration is how long an effort lasts; frequency is how often it occurs; intensity describes how demanding it is. A useful record also includes the activity type, environment, equipment, support and recovery afterward. Increasing several at once makes both tolerability and the explanation harder to assess. More exhaustion is not a required sign of a useful session.

The CDC describes [relative intensity and the talk test](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html). Conversational ease can be a rough description for someone able to use it; it is not medical clearance or a universal instrument. Speech, respiratory conditions and communication differences may make it unsuitable. Keep the person's established nonverbal effort cues or qualified plan instead of requiring speech. A numerical heart-rate formula is unnecessary here.

Base activity usually emphasizes repeatable sustainable effort. Higher-intensity goals may need different preparation, progression and recovery; this guide supplies no interval prescription. A previously tolerated activity may still be inappropriate today if health, symptoms or conditions change. Check the opportunity to stop and return before choosing a route. New chest discomfort, faintness or unusual breathlessness means stop and obtain appropriate urgent medical help; use local emergency services for severe symptoms or an emergency. Existing stop rules also apply. Do not wait for the next log entry.

Immediate recovery and later ordinary functioning are different observations. Delayed worsening remains relevant even after breathing settles. For conditions involving post-exertional worsening, use individualized clinical guidance; no automatic graded increase is required. [CDC's ME/CFS guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/me-cfs/hcp/clinical-care/treating-the-most-disruptive-symptoms-first-and-preventing-worsening-of-symptoms.html) supports that specific limitation, not a diagnosis from fatigue alone.

## Worked example: equal duration, changed conditions

Jamie completes an eight-minute familiar activity twice. The first is indoors with usual assistance; the second is outdoors in heat with less support and requires substantially more recovery. Equal minutes do not make the efforts comparable. The second record should preserve changed conditions and the recovery cost. Restore suitable conditions or seek relevant guidance before repeating; do not interpret the harder aftermath as proof of better training.

## Complete materials: Dev's route choice

Dev has an existing individualized activity plan for a familiar indoor accessible loop using their usual mobility aid. It permits up to 6 minutes total of easy movement, including the return, on suitable days. The present observation cycle makes no dose increase. Ordinary function and usual recovery must be restored before another session; calendar spacing alone does not establish this. The movement, support, equipment and stop rules are already familiar in the case. These are fictional personal-plan facts, not instructions for the reader to perform six minutes.

The loop takes about 2 minutes at Dev's planned pace; distance and speed are not goals. An accessible exit and seat are available after every loop. No public road crossing is involved. The alternative is a familiar level out-and-back path taking 4 minutes outbound and 4 minutes to return, with no earlier safe exit. Conditions today are suitable indoors. There is no current symptom concern reported, and the usual aid is available. If any important condition is unknown in a real setting, obtain appropriate information or defer the physical attempt.

### Action 1 — Choose and predeclare

Calculate which route fits total movement and return. For the loop, 3 × 2 = 6 minutes is the planned ceiling, not a reason to hurry or finish when a stop condition occurs. The alternative requires 8 minutes; “only four minutes out” omits the return. Write a plan containing type, duration, effort cue, aid, exit, stop rules and recovery review. Preserve it before [later-packet.md](later-packet.md).

Use `session | conditions | planned/actual minutes | effort | support | stop/interruption | immediate recovery | later function | next decision`. Missing recovery is unknown, not zero burden. If a real route cannot meet these requirements, a route/consultation brief completes preparation only, not physical practice.

### Action 2 — Read the two sessions honestly

Apply the later observations without changing the original limit. Compare effort, support, immediate recovery and later burden alongside duration. Distinguish an appropriately shortened effort from a violation such as adding an unplanned sprint. A stop can be a responsible action while evidence about sustainable participation remains limited.

### Action 3 — Choose a defensible next step

Choose repeat unchanged, reduce or adapt within existing guidance, seek qualified advice, or stop and use appropriate care. No disposition is a reward for finishing. Cite the observation that controls the decision and what remains uncertain. If the goal later requires higher intensity, name the individualized instruction/preparation needed; do not append intervals to this easy trial.

Complete [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the key](check-answers.md). The source artifact needs the route calculation, preserved plan, session comparison and next disposition. It does not replace the existing two-outing application exercise or create evidence of aerobic adaptation.

## Access, harder practice and later transfer

Use familiar indoor, seated, supported or shorter activity only within an already suitable plan. Assistance is a legitimate comparison condition, not a failed version. A reader may analyze the entire default without movement. If access, safety or recovery is uncertain, write the exact question for a qualified person: appropriate activity and cue, current tolerable dose, stop/return arrangement, recovery criteria and progression authority. An unanswered consultation is preparation, not clearance.

Harder case: someone completes the loop in 5 minutes by increasing pace, then claims a 1-minute gain at the same dose. Identify the intensity change, withhold a capacity-improvement claim and preserve the original easy-effort boundary. No sprint or maximal test is needed to answer.

At a later suitable real opportunity, use an already tolerated activity and existing limits, observe a first effort and its aftermath, and repeat comparably only after appropriate recovery. Preserve shortened attempts and naturally changed conditions. Longer-term evidence of endurance change would require a suitable comparable measure and adequate repeated observation; two outings, a self-review or this simulation do not supply it. Independent and qualified observations must actually occur and remain distinct from self-report.

**Supportive:** Both efforts remain within the existing plan and ordinary recovery supports repeating the same dose.

**Mixed:** Similar minutes conceal greater effort or delayed burden, prompting adaptation or review.

**Contradictory:** A warning sign is ignored, support is removed or intensity is added to produce a faster result.

**Inconclusive:** A route is planned but no activity or recovery is observed on the live route.
