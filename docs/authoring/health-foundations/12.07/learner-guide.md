# 12.07 — Mobility, flexibility, and joint capacity

## Make a useful movement accessible before asking for more range

A daily movement needs enough usable range, control, stability and tolerance for its task. Reaching farther is not automatically better. Moving an object closer can improve access immediately without changing the person's joint range. That distinction makes environmental adaptation a real success while keeping claims about physical capacity honest.

This guide teaches a task-specific comparison using complete fictional shelf records. Allow 10 minutes to analyze the arrangement, 10 for the later repeat and 10 for fresh checks. No reaching, stretching, balance test, measurement of your body, purchase or private disclosure is required for the default. Paper, labeled cards or dictation suffice. Its output is an access decision and a limited interpretation of supplied observations, not a physical examination. Health and exercise content must be adapted to the person and does not replace licensed medical care.

## What the task demands

**Range of motion** is the movement available at a joint; **flexibility** concerns the capacity of tissues to permit movement. **Usable mobility** also needs control for the actual task. **Stability** helps maintain a controlled position; **balance** concerns maintaining the body in relation to its support. **Tissue tolerance** concerns how the body's structures tolerate the demands placed on them, including repeated use and aftermath. These are working educational distinctions, not diagnoses or independent clinical measures.

The NHS connects [flexibility with movement needed for daily tasks](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-improve-strength-flexibility/). That does not mean every limitation needs stretching. A grip problem, inaccessible shelf, dizziness or fatigue can affect the same task. Observe the limiting feature and label its cause unknown when appropriate. A larger movement accompanied by pain, loss of control or removed support does not establish better usable capacity.

Keep ordinary supports and familiar limits. Do not force or bounce a joint, test an unstable stance or deliberately provoke pain. [Mayo Clinic's stretching guidance](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/basics/stretching-and-flexibility/hlv-20049447) supports avoiding painful or bouncing stretches; this case prescribes none. New limitations, pain, instability or dizziness need appropriate assessment rather than more force. A clinical mobility or rehabilitation plan governs any live exercise route.

## Worked example: the task became easier

A drawer handle is moved from a distant position to a closer one while the person uses the same seat and support. The person can now open it without leaning. That supports improved access in the changed environment. It does not show a larger shoulder range, stronger grip or a treatment effect. If grip fatigue persists, the adaptation can remain while the unresolved need is assessed.

## Complete materials: Noor's cup station

Noor uses a familiar seated position with usual back and foot supports. A light unbreakable empty cup is placed on a stable surface. Three shelf arrangements are described below. Distances are supplied task geometry, not recommended reach ranges for anyone. Noor's existing task plan allows only familiar comfortable arrangements with supports retained. No new clinical limitation is reported at baseline; that report is not clearance for an unfamiliar reach.

| Arrangement | Horizontal distance | Shelf above starting surface | Existing knowledge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A, usual shelf | 35 cm | 20 cm | Familiar and previously comfortable with usual support |
| B, proposed closer shelf | 25 cm | 20 cm | Familiar and previously comfortable with usual support |
| C, high display shelf | 45 cm | 35 cm | Unfamiliar; safe control and tolerance unknown |

B changes horizontal distance only. C changes two demands and has no established safe route. Do not physically test it. Noor's goal is to place and retrieve this cup during an ordinary break, not to maximize reach or become independent of aids.

The four predeclared criteria are: usual supports retained; cup placed and retrieved under control; no new pain, dizziness or instability; and tolerable grip/aftermath in ordinary use. Use yes/no/unknown for each; a missing observation is not a pass. Distance alone cannot satisfy a criterion. The planned comparison includes one placement/retrieval at A and one at B only if each remains appropriate that day, followed by another ordinary occasion using the selected arrangement. No repetitions to fatigue, forced end range or support removal are permitted.

### Action 1 — Define the demand and choose the comparison

Write the task, supports, four criteria and why A/B are the bounded candidates. Calculate the 10 cm horizontal reduction and record unchanged height; do not compute a joint-angle gain from shelf distances. State how range, balance, stability, control and tolerance might each matter without diagnosing the person. Preserve your plan before [later-packet.md](later-packet.md).

Use `arrangement | distance/height | support | control | symptoms/instability | grip/aftermath | actual task | unknowns | next disposition`.

### Action 2 — Compare the supplied observations

The later packet separates initial access from a second occasion. Identify which criteria are met, which remain unknown and what changed. Do not average away a warning sign or call an unobserved high-shelf attempt successful. If a real usual arrangement is already unsafe, adapt the environment or seek instruction without first performing a baseline challenge; safety does not require a paired physical test.

### Action 3 — Keep access and clarify the remaining need

Choose retain the useful arrangement, adapt another task feature within familiar safe limits, or seek appropriate qualified assessment. Preserve the accessible option while any training need is considered. Better access and persistent limitation may coexist. Ask a useful question: is the unresolved feature grip, range, balance, stability, tolerance or something that needs evaluation before choosing training? A worksheet cannot answer that clinical question from shelf geometry.

Complete [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the key](check-answers.md). A complete source artifact has the geometry comparison, observed criteria, repeat interpretation and an honest next step. These are companion reading outputs, not replacement application evidence or a flexibility score.

## Access, challenge and transfer

Analyze the case entirely through text or tactile labeled cards. A consented helper can arrange an already suitable work surface; assistance is part of the setup, not evidence against capability. Keep seated access, mobility aids and occupational/rehabilitation supports. If the ordinary task is not suitable, choose a familiar safe equivalent under the existing plan or produce a consultation brief only. Consultation preparation does not establish movement performed.

Harder case: B stays 25 cm away, but the usual chair support is removed and a heavier cup is introduced. A successful placement would not isolate the effect of shelf position, and known support conditions have been violated. Restore supports and an appropriate object rather than claiming that the person should adapt through effort.

At a later ordinary live opportunity, choose one already safe useful task, preserve support and compare a closer/easier arrangement only when both are appropriate. Retain the better arrangement on another ordinary occasion; never deliberately induce fatigue to test it. If training is needed, an appropriately qualified plan should specify the relevant capacity, technique, dose and review rather than withholding access aids. Longer-term range or tolerance evidence needs a suitable comparable measure; two cup placements do not provide it. Actual movement, self-review, a helper's report and qualified observation are separate evidence.

**Supportive:** Controlled task access remains useful on another occasion with supports and tolerable aftermath.

**Mixed:** Access improves but grip fatigue or another limitation remains unresolved.

**Contradictory:** Range is forced, support removed or pain ignored to claim a larger movement.

**Inconclusive:** Only shelf dimensions are known, or control and aftermath were not observed.
