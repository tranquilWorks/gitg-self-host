# 12.02 — Sleep and circadian health

## Protect sleep opportunity, then test one feasible change

Time available for sleep, time actually asleep and next-day functioning are related but different observations. A long window can contain awakenings; a brief morning note cannot identify sleep stages or diagnose a disorder. Regularity concerns timing across days, not perfect adherence to an arbitrary bedtime.

This guide teaches schedule, light, caffeine, environment, stress and recovery through a complete seven-night fictional comparison. Allow 10 minutes to plan, 10 to analyze the later packet and 10 for fresh checks. No wearable, clock-watching, purchase, personal sleep disclosure or deliberate sleep deprivation is needed. You produce a preserved prediction, comparison and next decision. The case does not demonstrate that you slept differently. Health and exercise content must be adapted to the person and does not replace licensed medical care.

## Six influences, one manageable experiment

Schedule creates an opportunity that must fit work, care and individual needs. Light can affect the body clock; caffeine can interfere with sleep. Noise, temperature and necessary sensory supports affect the environment. Stress may make settling harder, while extra monitoring can itself add burden. Recovery needs and existing clinical plans take priority over the experiment. NHLBI describes [healthy sleep habits](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-deprivation/healthy-sleep-habits); those general suggestions do not specify an individual's treatment or shift-work schedule.

Choose one controllable factor and record the others as unchanged, constrained or unknown. A quieter entertainment period can be tested while keeping a safety light and caregiving alert. Turning off every light is not a requirement. Do not postpone bedtime to finish a log, suppress essential care, change medicines or use sleep restriction to manufacture a difference.

Compare the same simple indicator across periods: for example, the person's description of ordinary daytime functioning. Keep exceptions, missing records and changed shifts visible. A short comparison can inform whether a habit is feasible; it cannot prove the habit caused a change. Persistent problems, breathing concerns during sleep or dangerous sleepiness need qualified evaluation. Do not drive or perform hazardous work while dangerously sleepy to complete a trial.

## Worked example: a long window is not measured sleep

Taylor has an opportunity from 22:45 to 06:45: 75 minutes before midnight plus 405 after it, or 480 minutes. They report a prolonged awakening but do not time it. The record can say “480-minute opportunity; actual sleep unknown.” Subtracting a guessed 60 minutes would create false precision. If the next day's functioning was not recorded, leave it missing rather than treating it as good.

## Complete materials: Lee's ordinary week

Lee has a stable daytime shift, an existing 23:00–07:00 sleep opportunity and a required dim safety light. No medicine changes or current dangerous sleepiness are reported. A care alert must remain audible. These are stipulated case facts, not medical clearance. Lee wants a less intrusive wind-down, not a sleep score.

Here “manageable” means Lee reported ordinary daytime duties felt manageable; “difficult” means they reported unusual difficulty staying engaged. These original descriptive categories are not a validated scale. Blank means missing. All windows cross midnight once.

| B3 | 23:00–07:00 | manageable | Bright entertainment screen until bed |

The proposed single change is to replace bright entertainment with familiar quiet audio from 22:30, keeping the dim safety light, care alert and 23:00–07:00 opportunity. Audio is already available. Caffeine routine, work shift and room setup stay unchanged unless a later record says otherwise. Lee will spend at most two minutes on a morning note; no overnight measurement is requested.

### Action 1 — Preserve the plan before outcomes

Compute each baseline opportunity and the mean; retain the care disruption. Write a six-factor map and the prediction “this may be easier to use and may support manageable daytime function; cause will remain uncertain.” Define a practical continuation rule before opening results: consider retaining the change if it is feasible on at least three of four trial nights, observed functioning is no worse in proportion than baseline, and no new safety concern or unwanted burden appears. Missing or substantially changed conditions can make this inconclusive. This is Lee's toy decision rule, not a health threshold.

Use `night | planned window | actual opportunity | change used yes/no/unknown | functioning | interruption | logging burden`. Save the plan before opening [later-packet.md](later-packet.md).

### Action 2 — Analyze all four trial nights

Separate exposure (whether the change occurred) from the outcome (functioning). Use the number of observed functioning reports as its denominator; also state how many nights are missing. Compare opportunity minutes separately. Do not add missing nights to the favorable count or omit inconvenient disruptions from the case narrative. Explain whether the predeclared rule is met, contradicted or uncertain.

### Action 3 — Make a bounded next decision

Choose retain provisionally, revise one method, reject an unwanted change or seek qualified help for a relevant concern. State exactly what happens next and what would change that choice. “Sleep improved” without a comparable observation is not enough. A real follow-up uses similar shifts and unchanged supports; if opportunity is constrained by care or work, address access/support rather than blame.

The source learning artifact includes the preserved rule, baseline and trial arithmetic, missingness, burden and next decision. ## Access, challenge and transfer

A reader can calculate elapsed windows; approximate morning descriptions are enough for live use. Use relative anchors for shift work and compare similar shifts. Keep assistive audio, dim lighting and clinical instructions. If recording becomes distressing, reduce or stop this optional observation; more tracking is not the goal.

Harder supplied variation: keep the same four trial records, but T2 and T3 are now after a newly changed night shift. Recalculate nothing falsely: the arithmetic is the same, but comparability is weaker. Plan a future comparison of similar shifts without forcing a return to a daytime schedule or cutting sleep.

For later real transfer, use three ordinary baseline mornings and four feasible trial nights under the existing source route, with a brief comparison. Record actual behavior only when it happens. Unsupported self-report is distinct from device measurement, qualified assessment and an independent observer; none may be invented to satisfy a score. A week cannot establish a general circadian-health outcome.

**Supportive:** A feasible change accompanies useful functioning under comparable conditions and can be retained provisionally.

**Mixed:** The change is feasible, but interruptions, burden or different shifts weaken the comparison.

**Contradictory:** Needed sleep or care is removed, worsening is hidden, or hazardous sleepiness is ignored.

**Inconclusive:** Missing observations or a plan without actual use cannot establish a benefit.
