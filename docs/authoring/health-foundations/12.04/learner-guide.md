# 12.04 — Food relationship and body image

## Keep ordinary care available when appearance feelings are difficult

An appearance preference, a health concern and a person's dignity are different things. Someone may want a different outfit, dislike a photo or have a legitimate medical question without making nourishment conditional on changing their body. This guide practices identifying a shame-based rule and replacing its effect with one concrete care choice. It does not require positive body feelings or denial of distress.

The complete default uses a fictional text vignette, with no images, mirrors, scales, calorie targets, private disclosure or exposure to triggering material. Allow 10 minutes for the fact/judgment/care distinction, 10 for an action card and later review, and 10 for checks. You may use dictation or have a reader omit the quoted judgment and refer simply to “the harmful rule.” If even the fictional case is unwelcome, use the garment-access variation below. The output is an educational care decision, not treatment or proof that your relationship with food changed.

Health and exercise content must be adapted to the person and does not replace licensed medical care. Eating disorders can occur across body sizes; appearance cannot establish whether someone needs help. Persistent restriction, bingeing, purging, compulsive exercise or serious distress calls for qualified support. [NIMH's overview](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/eating-disorders) supports those boundaries. An existing treatment plan remains authoritative; this reflection does not replace it or supply a diagnostic test.

## Separate four kinds of statement

An **observed fact** describes what happened: an image appeared, a garment pinches, a meal is scheduled. An **appearance judgment** evaluates looks, often through comparison. A **care need** remains relevant whatever the judgment: nourishment, comfort, access or appropriate evaluation. An **appearance preference** may guide ordinary choices without becoming a condition of worth or care.

The important change is practical. “I must feel attractive before eating” makes a necessary action depend on an unstable feeling. “I can follow my existing meal plan while feeling uncomfortable about the image” preserves care without requiring the feeling to vanish. A positive affirmation can be welcome, but copying one does not show that a meal, support contact or clothing change occurred.

Do not turn this exercise into food monitoring or a contest in resisting distress. Removing an unwanted comparison cue is optional and should not require revisiting it. Clothing choices can serve comfort, expression, culture or access; choosing a different fit does not concede a moral failure. A real health concern deserves qualified attention, not dismissal as “just body image.”

## Worked example: comfort without a body verdict

Casey notices a shirt's seam rubbing while seated. They prefer its color but also have a comfortable shirt already available. The fact is friction in that position, not a defective body. Casey changes shirts for comfort and keeps the preferred color as a future clothing preference. No purchase, body change or positive affirmation is required. If a painful skin problem persists, changing a shirt does not replace appropriate care.

## Complete materials: Rowan's lunchtime choice

All events are fictional. At 11:50 Rowan sees an idealized image accidentally in a feed. The exercise supplies no image and requires no search. Rowan thinks, “I should postpone lunch until I look different.” That is the rule to examine, not advice. Rowan's existing care plan includes a prepared lunch at 12:00. No change to that plan has been advised. They have a comfortable garment available and permission to mute their own unwanted feed cue. They need not change clothes or mute it to earn lunch.

Rowan has no present emergency described in this case. They prefer a different clothing style and want to discuss ongoing comfort concerns at their next existing care review. Their chosen support person, Jo, has agreed to receive a short message if the rule repeatedly interferes with eating. Jo is a trusted contact, not assumed to be a clinician. Rowan can also contact their existing qualified care service if concerning behavior is happening now; they need not wait for repetition to deserve help. No one has consented to sharing pictures, body measurements or meal details publicly.

### Action 1 — Make the distinction before offering reassurance

Write four lines: observed event, appearance judgment, valid care need and optional preference. Keep the planned lunch and comfort concern visible. Name what is not known: the image does not establish Rowan's health, other people's eating or whether Rowan has a disorder. Do not diagnose from this vignette.

### Action 2 — Produce a concrete care card

Write `next action | when | existing support | optional cue change | no compensation | help route`. For the default, the proposed action is following the existing lunch plan at 12:00. Add a sentence Rowan could use: “This feeling can be here while I keep the care already planned.” If desired, draft a brief message to Jo asking for support without sending it. A draft remains a draft.

Keep care independent of changing appearance, earning food, extra exercise or making the distress score fall. Preserve your card before [later-packet.md](later-packet.md), then use each branch's actual supplied behavior to assess it. The learner's real work is the written decision; Rowan's behavior is simulated.

### Action 3 — Review the action and what remains difficult

Record whether the care action occurred, whether distress remains, whether support was requested and whether any response arrived. These are separate facts. Use the branch to select one next step: retain the practical rule, ask the agreed support person, or use qualified care for current concerning behavior. Do not label a person noncompliant or morally better based on the case outcome.

Attempt [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before the [key](check-answers.md). The source artifact is complete with the four-way distinction, bounded care card, both branch reviews and a concrete support threshold. These are reading checks, not new application evidence or completion requirements.

## Access, harder practice and later transfer

For a less activating supplied alternative, use Casey's garment case alone: record friction, optional style preference, available comfortable shirt and the unknown cause of any persistent discomfort. Draft the care action, then analyze these two supplied outcomes: shirt changed and comfort improved; or shirt changed but discomfort persisted. The first supports practical fit, the second supports further attention, and neither establishes a medical cause. This is a complete supported route with the same fact/care distinctions and no food reflection.

Harder case: Rowan's friend says “you look fine, so you do not need help,” while the later record shows repeated interference with planned eating. Write a response that accepts the friend's care without accepting appearance as an assessment: “Thank you; the concern is what is happening with eating, and I want support for that.” Preserve the option of qualified help and do not require Rowan to persuade the friend before seeking it.

For later real transfer, choose a naturally occurring low-stakes comfort or nourishment choice within your existing care plan. No deliberate exposure is needed. Record the action and support used privately, with no body ranking. If behavior is currently concerning, move to appropriate support rather than using the exercise as self-treatment. A self-review, actual support contact and qualified assessment are distinct; no independent observer or improvement claim is supplied by this guide.

**Supportive:** Ordinary care occurs while appearance feelings remain, with no punishment or compensatory action.

**Mixed:** Care occurs but the rule continues to interfere, so a support step is needed.

**Contradictory:** Care is withheld, compensation added or a legitimate concern dismissed because of appearance.

**Inconclusive:** Reassuring words are copied but no action or follow-up is documented.
