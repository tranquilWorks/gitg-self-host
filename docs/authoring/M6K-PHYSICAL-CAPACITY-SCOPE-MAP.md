# Physical-capacity pathways: two distinct runtime rewrites

This two-ID cohort completes the remaining demonstrated M6K source repairs. The
competencies are related because both require capability-fit movement selection,
technique, recovery and bounded progression, but they are not interchangeable.
Strength concerns force against resistance; coordination, reaction, speed, power
and agility have distinct task demands and evidence requirements.

These are original educational exercises informed by current professional guidance.
Selection means authored runtime content, not medical clearance, a training
prescription, formal A/B/C acceptance or evidence that the exercise caused a health
or performance outcome. Runtime readers and separately labeled checks live in each
competency's quality folder; the canonical package YAML is the application source.

## 12.05 — Strength and muscular capability

Starting ability: select a movement already known to be safe and familiar, follow an
existing plan, or describe the uncertainty to an appropriate professional. The
stable-chair rise is an accessible entry, not a universal test or the entire
competency. Someone beyond that entry receives an established-plan route and a
concrete instruction-first brief rather than a fabricated generic program.

| Canonical facet | Teaching and practice | Observable evidence and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major movement patterns | `pattern-map` covers squat/rise, hinge, push, pull and carry | Every pattern gets a familiar equivalent, instruction need or honest not-currently-relevant status; only one is practiced |
| Progressive training | `route-card`, two-session comparison and `next-cycle` | Exactly one later variable may change only inside existing guidance; two sessions do not establish strength gain |
| Sound technique | Four predeclared observable criteria, stable equipment and retained support | Mixed control prevents automatic progression; the guide neither teaches an unfamiliar lift nor certifies technique |
| Recovery | Planned interval plus ordinary-function, delayed-symptom and unusual-fatigue check | Recovery is considered before a repeat; no clinical interpretation or universal interval is supplied |
| Capability fit | Entry/adapted, established-plan and instruction-first routes | A no or important unknown leads to instruction or defer; the screen is not medical clearance |
| Useful next step | Consultation brief and five explicit dispositions | Repeat, regress, plan-authorized progression, instruction or stop remains available without penalty |

The supportive example retains the same dose after two acceptable observations.
The mixed example changes course after delayed discomfort. The failure example
removes support and progresses after technique loss. These cases discriminate
reasonable execution from unsafe escalation without converting completion into a
strength score.

## 12.08 — Power, speed, coordination and agility

Starting ability: classify the intended capacity and choose a route whose movement,
environment, prerequisites, dose and stop rules are already known. The complete
default is a low-risk tabletop coordination/reaction exercise. Speed, power and
agility routes require an existing suitable plan or qualified instruction.

| Canonical facet | Teaching and practice | Observable evidence and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordination | L-C-R-C tabletop material fixes order, target and controlled start/stop criteria | Can show task-specific accuracy and cue response; it does not show running speed, force or agility |
| Reaction | Unpredictable-cue case distinguishes initiation after a signal | Reaction choice still depends on coordination and does not establish movement speed |
| Speed | Straight-distance comparison is defined separately | Only an unchanged existing-plan route is permitted; the guide supplies no sprint program |
| Power | Rapid force against resistance is distinguished from moving quickly | Requires known technique, prerequisites and an existing plan; no jump or loaded explosive task is prescribed |
| Agility | Whole-body direction change in response to a task or signal | Strength, balance, deceleration and force-absorption prerequisites remain explicit; tabletop work is not athletic agility |
| Progression and safety | Prerequisite screen, comparison record and separate changed-condition case/key | Changed shoes, surface and cueing plus a repeated deceleration defect block progression despite a faster result |

The supportive example shows fewer order errors with a self-spoken cue but mixed
transfer to an external cue. The mixed and failure paths preserve unresolved
prerequisites or changed conditions. No route converts desk-level coordination into
a claim of power, speed or athletic competence.

## Source and review boundaries

- ACSM's 2026 resistance-training update supports distinguishing outcomes and
  individualizing participation to goals, enjoyment and safety. It does not validate
  this product-authored exercise or provide clearance for an individual.
- The ACSM Certified Exercise Physiologist outline supports separating muscular and
  neuromotor components and considering status, goals, conditioning and access when
  progressing or modifying a program. It is a professional knowledge outline, not a
  learner prescription.
- The NSCA introductory manual supports distinguishing power, speed and agility and
  attending to preparation, force absorption, rest and volume. It is older, not
  individualized medical guidance and not authority to start demanding drills.
