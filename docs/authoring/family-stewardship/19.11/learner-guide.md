# 19.11 — Elder care, dementia, and later-life decision-making

Build a person-centered support map for one ordinary difficulty, then identify what requires qualified advice. Use two twenty-minute sessions and, only for an appropriate real role, an authorized inquiry. This is not a dementia screen, a capacity determination or a legal document. A person may choose a supporter; a family connection alone does not authorize control of their affairs.

## Distinguish the questions before choosing a solution

Changing memory, health, communication or mobility can affect daily tasks. An observed missed reminder is a fact about an event, not a diagnosis. Dementia concerns clinical conditions that require appropriate assessment; age or occasional forgetfulness cannot establish it. Sudden concerning changes, including acute confusion, need prompt qualified attention rather than this routine planning exercise. Do not use a calm-looking worksheet to dismiss a current urgent concern.

Capacity and authority are separate from diagnosis. A diagnosis does not automatically settle what a person can decide, and apparent difficulty with one task does not establish global inability. Relevant capacity questions and legal standards depend on the decision and jurisdiction. A relative's confidence, family majority vote or possession of a phone is not legal authority to take over finances or care.

Supported decision-making starts with the person's preferences and chosen assistance. Accessible language, a quiet discussion, an appropriate interpreter, communication aids or a trusted supporter can make participation more meaningful. Ask what the person wants help with and what they want to keep doing. Support should fit the task; its existence does not erase the person's choices.

Advance planning can make future wishes and authorized roles clearer while participation is possible. Existing documents need to be understood in the relevant local legal and care system. A financial authorization does not necessarily include health decisions; titles and powers vary. This lesson does not draft a directive, appoint a guardian or activate someone else's authority. Appropriate legal and clinical guidance addresses those matters.

Protection and autonomy are not resolved by choosing the most restrictive arrangement imaginable. Compare the actual risk, the person's preference, feasible support and the conditions that would require review. Sustainability matters too: an arrangement that relies indefinitely on an exhausted relative may leave care unreliable. Include willing backup, respite questions and a way to address disagreement without making the person disappear from the conversation.

## Worked example

An older adult misses an appointment reminder. One relative proposes taking over every message and bank account. Another suggests an agreed visible reminder plus a chosen reminder call. The narrower option may address the observed difficulty while preserving participation, but it still requires agreement and review. Neither proposal establishes diagnosis, capacity or legal authority. If the problem persists or new concerns arise, seek appropriate assessment rather than silently expanding control.

## Supplied fictional case

Ruth has missed two appointment reminders and wants to remain involved in arrangements. No diagnosis, capacity finding, emergency or legal document is supplied. Ruth chooses Alex to help discuss a reminder system. Bea worries about safety and proposes handling all communications. Alex can make one agreed call each week but cannot provide continuous oversight. A potentially available respite service has not been contacted or accepted.

The immediate proposal is a visible reminder in Ruth's chosen format plus Alex's call before the next ordinary appointment. Any information shared with Alex must be within Ruth's permission and the actual applicable authority. No medical appointment result or financial transaction is part of this exercise.

## Build the support and authority map

1. Separate Ruth's stated preference, the two observed events, unknown clinical questions and unknown legal powers. Output: a map that does not turn memory concerns into a diagnosis or family worry into authority.
2. Compare the limited reminder proposal with Bea's broad takeover proposal. Ask Ruth what assistance is welcome and make communication accessible. Output: the preferred bounded support, rejected assumptions and any unresolved safety concern needing qualified advice.
3. Identify the authorized route for clinical questions and the local professional route for advance-planning or legal-authority questions. Use existing documents only in the proper private channel. Output: questions and inquiry status, not an invented capacity finding or new directive.
4. Assign one accepted ordinary task, a clear limit, a backup question and a review trigger. Alex's weekly limit must remain visible. Output: who will do what, what remains uncovered and when the plan needs reconsideration. An uncontacted respite service is not backup coverage.
5. Save the plan before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Review the actual reminder attempt with Ruth's perspective. Output: one specific result and unresolved clinical, legal and sustainability questions.

## Support and transfer

For support, discuss only the next reminder with the person's chosen communication method and a trusted appropriate supporter. For a harder fictional version, relatives disagree about whether a missed call justifies broad control. Revisit the actual risk, authority and person-specific supports; do not resolve it with a family popularity vote. Real transfer requires a role the person has accepted or that is validly authorized, plus qualified help where the task exceeds it.

[S12, ACL](../SOURCES.md#S12), supports chosen, task-specific assistance while retaining control. [S13, Alzheimer's Society](../SOURCES.md#S13), explains advance planning and different powers in its England and Wales context; it is not the law for every location. One useful reminder is not evidence that dementia is absent, that capacity is established or that caregiver strain is solved. Keep medical, financial and legal records outside app evidence.


## Scope and evidence boundary

Medical, reproductive, child-safety, guardianship, and elder-care decisions require qualified local guidance.

Applicability and normative status are role-conditional. No reproductive, parenting or care role is compulsory. Fiction does not establish actual participant evidence. Keep identifying, clinical and private family information outside app records. Formal M6K A/B/C, independent, actual learner, qualified and owner content acceptance remain pending.
