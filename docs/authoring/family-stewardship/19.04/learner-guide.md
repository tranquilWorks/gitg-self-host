# 19.04 — Fertility and reproductive health literacy

Turn a reproductive question into a bounded, medically informed consultation question. Use two twenty-minute sessions and, only for a real current need, an appropriate qualified inquiry. This is literacy and decision preparation, not a fertility test or an instruction to pursue pregnancy. Use all three fictional cases without supplying personal sexual or medical history. A person can decide not to pursue a reproductive path.

## Learn what kind of question you have

Fertility concerns the possibility of reproduction; an individual's situation is not readable from appearance or a calendar estimate. Contraception concerns preventing pregnancy. Infection prevention is a separate question: an answer about pregnancy prevention does not automatically answer it. Pregnancy care concerns an existing pregnancy and appropriate professional support; prenatal or antenatal care is the care provided before birth. Preparing for a possible pregnancy is different from establishing that someone is pregnant.

Infertility and reproductive aging require attention to individual circumstances. Population patterns can identify useful questions but cannot supply a personal prognosis. Nor should a public-health definition be converted into a rule that someone must wait before asking for care. Information about one reproductive partner does not settle the other person's contribution or needs. Evaluation, diagnosis and treatment selection remain with qualified care, informed by the person's choices.

A decision includes values as well as facts. Privacy, reversibility, cost, access, timing, religious commitments and willingness to undergo procedures may matter differently to different people. Stating a value does not make a medical claim true; a medical fact does not determine which acceptable option someone must choose. Separate desired outcome, relevant value, medical unknown and next source of information.

Reliable sourcing requires a named authority, an identifiable topic and a scope. A national health page can explain categories while leaving personal suitability unanswered. Check its review date and intended health system. A commercial testimonial may describe one experience without establishing comparative effectiveness or suitability. A list of bookmarks is not an evaluated source record.

## Worked example

An adult wants to avoid pregnancy and values a reversible option. A general method overview shows that choices have different features. It does not establish which method fits that person's medical history or whether infection prevention is addressed. A good question is “Which options fit my health, preferences and access, and what separate infection-prevention questions should I discuss?” Choosing a method from the first appealing paragraph would skip the individual decision.

## Three supplied fictional cases

**A:** Mara wants to avoid pregnancy for now, values privacy and reversibility, and has not discussed personal medical suitability with a clinician. No method, health condition or sexual history is supplied.

**B:** Elise is considering pregnancy and wants to know how pre-pregnancy advice differs from care once pregnant. Elise needs an accessible appointment format and has not reported a pregnancy or urgent symptoms. Elise's partner offers to help arrange transport, not to choose care.

**C:** Omar and Ren have questions about difficulty conceiving and reproductive aging. No ages, duration, test results or diagnosis are supplied. They want to understand when their circumstances warrant evaluation and what information a licensed service needs. A friend claims a single birthday predicts the outcome; the case provides no support for that claim.

These cases deliberately leave medical variables unknown. The task is to form useful questions and preserve choice, not fill the gaps with imagined clinical facts.

## Make a source-to-question record

1. For each case write the desired outcome, whose decision and body are involved, one stated value or access need, and the unknowns. Mark pregnancy status unknown where appropriate. Output: three decision cards that do not turn Mara's avoidance into future parenthood or Elise's consideration into a pregnancy.
2. Read the general-choice portion of [CDC contraception guidance, S01](../SOURCES.md#S01), the definition and individual-care caveat in [CDC infertility guidance, S02](../SOURCES.md#S02), and the introductory account of [NHS antenatal care, S03](../SOURCES.md#S03). Record authority, country, date inspected, one question each helps answer and one it cannot answer. Do not import an NHS appointment schedule into another system. Output: three narrow source uses, in your own words.
3. Draft one consultation question per case. Ask about suitable options for A, preparation versus prenatal-care access for B, and individualized evaluation for C. Include confidentiality, appointment type, accessibility and what information to bring. Output: concise questions, with no medication change, diagnosis, personal deadline or fertility guarantee.
4. In fiction, rehearse an inquiry and label it uncontacted. In an authorized real situation, use the verified channel of an appropriate licensed service and record whether an inquiry actually occurred. Keep private details in the proper care channel. Output: actual status rather than a claim that reading resolved a clinical decision.
5. Save your cards before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Identify which questions received administrative information and which still require individualized advice. Output: a supported next step with uncertainty intact.

## Support and transfer

For support, begin with A, use a translated official page or have a trusted chosen supporter help formulate questions. A qualified interpreter may be needed for care; an app translation alone should not settle an important clinical misunderstanding. For a harder fictional attempt, add a confident statistical claim and explain why its population, outcome and limitations matter before applying it to an individual.

For real transfer, use a current question that belongs to you or one you are legitimately supporting with permission. Ask a professional what is appropriate rather than treating this guide as a referral-timing algorithm. Current concerning symptoms, coercion or urgent pregnancy concerns need the appropriate prompt help; they are not held for the second study session. An informed choice can include declining a procedure or pathway, while acknowledging what is still medically unknown.

## Source limits

S01 supports general informed choice and separate prevention questions; S02 explicitly distinguishes public-health definitions from individual care. S03 describes an NHS care system. No treatment, dosage, screening schedule or personal prediction is copied into this exercise. The three cases and decision-card method are original teaching design, not a validated clinical instrument.


## Scope and evidence boundary

Medical, reproductive, child-safety, guardianship, and elder-care decisions require qualified local guidance.

Applicability and normative status are role-conditional. No reproductive, parenting or care role is compulsory. Fiction does not establish actual participant evidence. Keep identifying, clinical and private family information outside app records. Formal M6K A/B/C, independent, actual learner, qualified and owner content acceptance remain pending.
