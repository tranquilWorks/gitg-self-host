# 19.05 — Pregnancy, birth, and postpartum responsibility

Build a support handoff that centers the pregnant or postpartum person's choices and recovery. Allow thirty minutes to plan, fifteen for a tabletop interruption, and a brief confirmed handoff. A real route requires permission and an appropriate existing support role. Otherwise use this fiction. Do not simulate symptoms, make a practice emergency call, manage a birth or seek private experiences to complete the task.

## Separate support from authority

Pregnancy, birth and postpartum recovery involve bodily demands and potential risks that ordinary helpers cannot assess through a worksheet. Consent to help with a meal is not consent to touch, attend a consultation, post a photograph or make a medical decision. A support person, an infant caregiver and a legally authorized decision-maker have different roles. Clarify the particular task and existing authority rather than treating family membership as permission for everything.

A birth preference or support plan helps people communicate what matters. It is not a guarantee that clinical circumstances will unfold as hoped or a reason to override the person's ongoing choices. Qualified care teams handle medical questions and changing clinical needs. A helper can make it easier to ask questions, obtain accessible communication and reach care without speaking over the person or deciding that their concern is exaggerated.

Recovery support may include food, household work, transport, visitor management and a real chance to rest. Infant care is a separate responsibility requiring an appropriate competent caregiver and established professional instructions. Do not improvise feeding, handling, sleep arrangements or treatment here. A meal delivery does not establish infant-care competence, and a willing visitor does not become a safe substitute merely because the schedule has a gap.

Postpartum mental health belongs in the plan alongside physical health. Ask what contact and support are welcome and how concerns reach qualified help. [CDC's warning-sign resource, S04](../SOURCES.md#S04) identifies urgent concerns during pregnancy and afterward, including serious physical symptoms and thoughts of harm. The list is not exhaustive. Actual concerning symptoms require prompt appropriate care, not a helper's reassurance or completion of this exercise.

## Worked example

A recovering parent requests a meal left at the door and no visitors. A helper cooks, enters without asking and invites relatives to see the baby. The food arrived, but the agreed support was not fulfilled as specified. A better handoff confirms the time, delivery location and completion signal, while respecting the visitor limit. Good intentions cannot substitute for the recipient's actual permission.

## Supplied fictional task

Tessa has recently returned home after birth and has an existing maternity-team plan and infant-care instructions. The task does not reproduce those clinical records. Tessa wants a meal delivered at 17:30 on 8 October, no visitors that evening, no photographs shared, and a rest opportunity from 18:00 to 19:00. Tessa can change these preferences.

Friend Ivo accepts meal delivery but has no infant-care role or medical decision authority. Co-parent Sal is an established competent infant caregiver under the existing instructions and accepts the 18:00–19:00 interval. Neighbor Dev offers to help but has not been accepted for any task. The qualified maternity contact and local urgent-care route are available in the private care plan; do not invent phone numbers. No actual symptom is supplied at the starting point.

For the tabletop disruption only, imagine Ivo becomes unavailable and Tessa says something feels unexpectedly wrong physically. This hypothetical branch does not say what condition exists. It asks who should be contacted and how support continuity is handled without delaying care.

## Create and challenge the handoff

1. Make seven headings: qualified contacts, Tessa's choices, recovery support, infant care, mental-health support, visitors/privacy and backup. Use the supplied roles and preferences. Output: a role map that leaves Dev's offer unassigned and retains the private professional instructions as authoritative.
2. Write the meal and rest arrangements with accepting owners, time windows, permission limits and completion signals. Identify any necessary task not covered. Do not silently assign it to Tessa. Output: two bounded handoffs, not a general “family will help” statement.
3. Read the warning-sign resource and identify the established route for contacting qualified care. Do not turn the resource into a checklist that rules out concern when a symptom is not listed. Output: a care-contact route and a separate practical backup question, without diagnosing or sharing clinical details.
4. Rehearse the tabletop disruption aloud or on paper. Pause ordinary planning and contact the appropriate qualified route in the scenario; use local emergency services for an actual emergency. Seek an accepted meal alternative and preserve safe infant coverage as appropriate. Output: an action order that does not wait for dinner or a family vote before seeking care. Make no real practice calls.
5. Save the plan and read the [later packet](later-packet.md). Separate the tabletop response from the ordinary handoff that actually occurs within the fiction. Output: support delivered, choices respected, burden remaining and the status of future coverage.

## Adapt and apply

For support, work only with the meal task and the existing care-contact route, using a translated or accessible note. No one must tell a birth story or disclose mental-health history. For a harder case, remove the accepted infant caregiver and explain why an available untrained visitor cannot simply fill that role. An unresolved safe-coverage gap needs appropriate help, not optimistic relabeling.

For an authorized real attempt, ask the person what would reduce burden, confirm one practical arrangement and follow the current professional plan. Check whether the help was welcome afterward without demanding gratitude. A successful meal or rest interval supports that limited claim; it does not certify medical recovery, infant safety across all settings or the absence of postpartum distress.

## Source use

[S03, NHS antenatal care](../SOURCES.md#S03), supports person-specific discussion with maternity professionals, within its own health system. S04 supports prompt attention to urgent concerns; no symptom list or diagnosis is used as a gate to help. The role map, times and fictional interruption are original and do not certify birth or infant-care skills.


## Scope and evidence boundary

Medical, reproductive, child-safety, guardianship, and elder-care decisions require qualified local guidance.

Applicability and normative status are role-conditional. No reproductive, parenting or care role is compulsory. Fiction does not establish actual participant evidence. Keep identifying, clinical and private family information outside app records. Formal M6K A/B/C, independent, actual learner, qualified and owner content acceptance remain pending.
