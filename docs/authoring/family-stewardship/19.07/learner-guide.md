# 19.07 — Adoption, fostering, stepfamilies, and chosen family

Make one small belonging practice that leaves room for more than one history. Spend twenty minutes preparing, hold a short willing conversation, and try the agreed change. Use the supplied family-shelf case unless you already have an appropriate family or care role. Do not recruit a child, ask for a placement story or seek sensitive family details for evidence.

## Belonging has several dimensions

Adoption involves a legal family relationship as well as emotional, social and identity questions. Fostering involves care within a particular arrangement whose authority and expectations must be checked. A stepfamily brings relationships and histories that do not become identical merely because adults form a partnership. Chosen family names meaningful commitments and belonging; it does not by itself grant custody, medical decision rights or access to private records. These forms can overlap, and each contains considerable variety.

Attachment is built through responsive, dependable relationships, not demonstrated by a required title or public declaration. Prior loss can coexist with present affection. Remembering a birth parent or former home does not necessarily reject a current caregiver. Nor can a quiet response establish trauma, attachment difficulty or disloyalty. Ask what support is welcome without requiring an explanation that the person cannot or does not want to give.

Identity includes origins, names, language, culture and a person's changing understanding of their story. Preserving those connections does not authorize unsafe or prohibited contact. Check the applicable arrangement and seek qualified help where contact or disclosure is uncertain. A family display is optional; a child does not owe adults photographs or a simplified account of how everyone belongs.

Loyalty conflicts grow when someone feels required to prove affection by denying another relationship. An adult can reduce that pressure by accepting different names, private memories and uneven readiness. Equal dignity does not require identical feelings, matching titles or the same disclosure from every person. Listening to a child's perspective is meaningful only if it can change the proposed activity.

## Worked example

An adult proposes a shelf labeled “our real family,” with a photograph from the new household and no room for earlier connections. Another person asks to keep a previous-home memory nearby. Replacing “real” with a chosen neutral label and offering a private keepsake option makes the practice less excluding. It does not solve legal contact questions or establish attachment security. The adult still needs permission before displaying another person's image.

## Supplied fictional case

Jo and Sam share a household with Kit and Rowan after family changes. The case does not disclose each person's legal relationship or private history. Existing care arrangements remain authoritative. They are considering a small shelf with names, a household image and one meaningful object. Kit wants a reminder of an earlier home but does not want to explain it publicly. Rowan dislikes the proposed parent title. A close chosen-family adult, Ari, may be included in a shared activity but has no stated legal powers.

Available choices are a private keepsake, a self-chosen label, an image used with appropriate permission, an empty space, a neutral symbol or no participation. No one must choose an option. Unverified image or contact permission means postponing that part, not persuading the child to authorize it on behalf of others.

## Build and revise the practice

1. Draw three separate headings: belonging, care responsibility and legal authority. Put each supplied fact under the appropriate heading and mark unknown legal details unknown. Output: a small map in which living together and loving someone do not create unstated powers.
2. Ask what each willing participant would like represented and what should stay private. Use an accessible choice or allow a later answer. Do not ask why an origin memory matters as a condition of including it. Output: preferences with refusals and unknowns intact; use fictional summaries in app evidence.
3. Audit the proposed display for forced titles, “real family” ranking, loyalty tests and unwanted disclosure. Check the existing arrangement before using images or facilitating contact. Output: one concrete revision and one permission question that remains with an authorized adult or qualified service.
4. Implement only the agreed, permitted part. A neutral symbol or no shared display can be a valid outcome. Output: what was actually placed or changed, who chose it and what was deferred. A plan to ask permission is not received permission.
5. Before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md), set a low-pressure way to revise or remove an item. After the activity, ask privately whether the option still works. Output: one retained or revised choice without interpreting it as a measure of love.

## Support and transfer

For support, start with two neutral options and a no-participation choice. A person can point, use an established communication aid or have a chosen supporter help convey preferences. For a harder fictional version, an adult insists that honoring an earlier home is ungrateful. Explain how to preserve the child's voice while moving the adult's feelings out of the child's responsibility. Do not turn the task into a confrontation when safety is uncertain.

In real transfer, use an ordinary low-stakes practice within your actual role: how people are introduced, how a holiday invitation is phrased or where a keepsake is stored. One successful revision shows a specific accommodation, not general belonging, secure attachment or an absence of grief. Contact disputes, placement decisions and legal changes need the appropriate qualified local route. Privacy protection may leave little sharable evidence; that is preferable to making someone else's history an assignment.

## Source limits

[Child Welfare Information Gateway, S06](../SOURCES.md#S06) describes legal, emotional and social dimensions of adoption, including loss, identity and connections. It does not make the shelf exercise a validated intervention or supply the powers of a particular foster, step or chosen-family adult. Those distinctions and the case are original teaching applications. No family form is treated as inherently superior.


## Scope and evidence boundary

Medical, reproductive, child-safety, guardianship, and elder-care decisions require qualified local guidance.

Applicability and normative status are role-conditional. No reproductive, parenting or care role is compulsory. Fiction does not establish actual participant evidence. Keep identifying, clinical and private family information outside app records. Formal M6K A/B/C, independent, actual learner, qualified and owner content acceptance remain pending.
