## Support costs change the decision

The supplied pilot review reveals that delivery labor would cost **6 per pack**, not the assumed 4. Materials remain 2 and handling 1, so variable cost becomes **9**. At the original price 12, contribution is **3** and fixed-cost-only break-even is **34 packs**. At 40 packs, provisional profit before the campaign is **20**; after the 30 campaign it is **minus 10**.

The originally proposed 10-pack pilot would now require **100 + 90 = 190** before receipts, leaving only **10** of the available 200. Even if all ten sold at 12, modeled loss would be **70**. No actual cost was incurred, customer recruited or pack sold. The owner chooses to keep the work analytical and redesign the support burden before deciding whether to spend.

Do not keep calling the offer profitable by using the old labor assumption. Nor does a 34-pack fixed-cost break-even establish demand or accommodate the campaign at the stated capacity: covering 130 at contribution 3 requires 44 packs. Expansion beyond 40 may require new fixed costs and staffing, so linear scaling is unverified.

Retain the first model, the new labor evidence supplied in the fiction and the stop decision. Retention and willingness to pay remain unknown; a prudent revision is a valid learning outcome without any commercial success claim.
