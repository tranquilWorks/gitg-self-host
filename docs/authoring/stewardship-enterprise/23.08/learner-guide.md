# 23.08 — Generosity and responsible provision

Make one useful offer whose limits are clear before anyone depends on it. Allow twenty minutes to inspect the example, then ten for the follow-up. The product is a need, capacity and delivery record showing what was provided and what remains the recipient's choice.

## Give in a way another person can actually use

Generosity can share money, knowledge, access or an opportunity. The face value of a gift says little about its usefulness if the recipient cannot use it, must pay unexpected costs or is expected to surrender privacy. Ask what help is wanted before selecting a resource. A requested explanation may be more useful than another unsolicited plan. A recipient may decline without needing to prove that the giver's idea was bad.

Provision also has a time dimension. A promise that can be kept once is different from recurring support. Account for verification, preparation, delivery and follow-up as well as the visible moment of giving. Check legitimate obligations such as essential bills, care and already agreed work before allocating what remains. A person with little discretionary capacity can make a modest offer, postpone it or decline. Scarcity is not a generosity defect.

The CFPB's [family lending discussion, S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) helps distinguish the terms and limits of financial help. This exercise applies that clarity to a knowledge gift. It does not treat a worksheet as a loan contract or turn an explanation into permission to manage someone's finances. Gifts with repayment expectations should not be described as unconditional gifts.

## Supplied request K3

All people and responses here are invented. Dev asks Rowan: “Do you know a free page that explains why bills can be difficult between paydays? I want to work on it privately. Ten minutes on the timing idea would help; please don't ask for my figures.” Dev can read an English PDF on an existing device and does not want an account or subscription. Rowan has **30 discretionary minutes this week, zero money to spend and no ongoing support capacity** after existing obligations.

Rowan identifies the CFPB's [four-page cash-flow tool, S02](../SOURCES.md#S02). The public PDF opened on 24 September 2026 without a login or charge. Its introductory explanation links beginning resources, dated inflows and outflows, and the balance carried forward. That check establishes public retrieval; it does not certify accessibility for every reader, provide a device or pay for connectivity. Dev's stated device and format preference fit this fictional request. If the format did not work, ask about an alternative before promising one.

Rowan's proposed offer is: “I can share this public PDF and explain one made-up timing example for up to ten minutes on Thursday. You can keep your own information private. There is no repayment, public acknowledgment or future session expected. You can skip the explanation.” Dev accepts those terms in the supplied case. Acceptance is specific to this offer; it does not authorize account access or further advice.

## Work out the burden before delivery

Rowan budgets **12 minutes to verify the source, 8 to prepare and 10 to explain: 30 total**. The resource costs zero to retrieve, but time is still a cost. The private toy example starts with 50 units, receives 100 on Friday and has an 80-unit bill on Wednesday. Before Friday, 50 minus 80 is **minus 30**; after Friday, the arithmetic balance becomes **70**. The monthly-looking total does not remove Wednesday's shortage. This is a timing demonstration, not a suggested borrowing or bill-priority decision.

The supplied delivery record says that Rowan shared the official link, used only these invented numbers, stopped the explanation at ten minutes and left Dev to decide whether to use the tool. No worksheet was collected. A screenshot of Dev's completed budget would add privacy cost without being necessary to show that the promised resource was delivered. A minimal record can say “official resource shared; agreed explanation completed; no private financial data requested.”

Do not infer benefit from the act of sending. A link could be unread, inaccessible or unhelpful. Equally, a recipient may have benefited without wishing to provide feedback. Those possibilities require an unknown status rather than a gratitude test. Separating delivery from benefit protects both the recipient's agency and the accuracy of the giver's account.

## Produce the bounded giving record

1. Restate Dev's requested help, preferred format and privacy boundary. Record what is known from the supplied request and what you would still need to ask in an actual situation.
2. Make a capacity ledger for money, minutes, existing obligations and future commitments. State the maximum before choosing the offer. Include the cost of checking a resource rather than treating that work as free.
3. Record the resource's publisher, public URL, retrieval date, cost, login requirement and fit with the request. Distinguish observed access from assumptions about usability. Do not describe the entire CFPB toolkit as inspected.
4. Write the offer in words a recipient can decline. Specify delivery, duration, ownership of any later decisions and whether repayment or continuing support exists. Use the supplied acceptance only within this fictional case.
5. Reproduce the timing example and the delivery record. Save an initial conclusion with separate fields for delivery, usefulness and burden before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md).

For an actual low-stakes attempt, choose a willing person's small request or a resource you already have legitimate permission to share. Do not make a purchase to complete the lesson. Share only what you can sustain, and ask permission before any optional feedback question. If no person wishes to participate, complete the supplied case and label actual transfer unattempted.

## Review without making generosity transactional

A useful follow-up question is whether the resource met the requested need or created an unexpected burden. Ask once only if welcome; silence can remain silence. Record your own effort separately from the recipient's response. If the offer was too large, a future offer can be smaller. That does not authorize abruptly withdrawing essential care or breaking an existing obligation: arrange an appropriate transition where someone already depends on agreed support.

For support, work just the three monetary dates and the time ledger, with a helper reading the PDF introduction aloud if desired. For a harder pass, imagine a request for weekly account reviews and decide which terms exceed the original offer. A responsible refusal can leave the public resource available without promising replacement professional support. Evidence of growth would combine an actual bounded offer or delivery artifact with a later burden or use observation. This supplied story provides neither actual adherence nor proof that Dev's financial position improved.

## Scope and evidence boundary

Canonical professional boundary: Financial education only; tax, investment, insurance, estate, and benefits decisions may require licensed or jurisdiction-specific advice. Applicability remains context_sensitive; normative status remains cross_tradition_core_or_broadly_recurrent. Use fiction or privately redacted records; keep credentials and sensitive identifiers outside app evidence. Human dignity is never scored.
