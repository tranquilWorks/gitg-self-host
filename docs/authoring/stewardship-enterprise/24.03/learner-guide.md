# 24.03 — Business, markets, and economics

Explain a small business as a connected set of incentives, operations and financial flows. Allow thirty minutes to reconstruct the supplied four-order month and ten to revise it. Your product is a business account that survives a rework cost and a late payment instead of relying on revenue alone.

## Follow value, promises and money separately

A business makes an offer to customers, arranges inputs, delivers work and receives payment under terms. Those events need not happen on the same date. Revenue describes sales recognized in the stated accounting example; profit subtracts the relevant costs; cash reflects money actually available. An unpaid invoice can be an asset and still be unusable for paying today's supplier. The SEC's [financial-statements guide, S12](../SOURCES.md#S12) supports this distinction. It is an educational explanation, not an accounting policy for a real enterprise.

Incentives matter at every stage. A customer wants a useful result at an acceptable cost. A worker needs manageable, compensated work. A supplier wants payment; the owner may seek margin and continuity; an approver may care about accuracy and reputation. Competition gives users alternatives, but switching can require effort. Pricing and marketing therefore need a credible account of the benefit, the alternative and the full delivery burden.

The SBA's [planning overview, S10](../SOURCES.md#S10) connects market questions with costs and competitors. This exercise adds original numbers and a complete transaction sequence. It does not treat a business slogan, a forecast or a break-even estimate as evidence of viability.

## Supplied notice-formatting operation E2

An owner produces formatted notices from customer-approved facts. The service does not invent event details or certify legal wording. Four customers each order one notice at **50 units**, all delivered and accepted within the fictional period. Variable cost is **25 per order** and period overhead is **60**. All those costs are paid during the period. Taxes, depreciation, financing, owner drawings and additional obligations are omitted and must stay explicitly outside the result.

Two customers pay their 50-unit invoices now. Two owe 50 each next period. Opening cash is **100**. No loan, grant, new owner contribution or other cash movement is supplied. The production process is inquiry, scope and fact approval, formatting, quality check, delivery, invoice, then collection. Invoicing and collecting are separate stages.

| Period calculation | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Revenue: 4 × 50 | 200 |
| Variable cost: 4 × 25 | 100 |
| Overhead | 60 |
| Provisional profit before omitted items: 200 − 100 − 60 | 40 |
| Cash receipts from two paid invoices | 100 |
| Cash paid for variable costs and overhead | 160 |
| Closing cash: opening 100 + receipts 100 − payments 160 | 40 |
| Unpaid customer invoices | 100 |

Profit and closing cash happen to equal 40 here. That numerical coincidence does not make them the same concept. Opening cash and unpaid invoices explain why the cash calculation needs its own inputs. The receivable is still exposed to delay or nonpayment, and cash 40 says nothing about costs not included in this simplified period.

## Explain the market beyond the price tag

The target user needs an accurate readable notice; the buyer approves the price and terms. A customer could instead use an existing template, a volunteer or another provider. Their total cost includes supplying facts, reviewing the draft and handling mistakes. Marketing should state the formatting scope and approval requirement, without promising guaranteed attendance or compliance.

The owner supplies coordination and some work; a worker or subcontractor may supply production. Their compensation and employment status require actual terms and qualified review where relevant. A facts approver controls source accuracy; the formatter does not gain that authority merely by editing text. Taxes, privacy, contract obligations and applicable accessibility or sector requirements must be checked through the relevant qualified routes before a real service operates.

Macroeconomic conditions can affect this small operation through specific channels. Broad input-price increases may raise printing or supplier costs. Lower customer income can reduce discretionary orders. Interest-rate changes can alter financing cost or customer spending. Exchange-rate changes matter if inputs or customers cross currencies. These are conditional mechanisms, not claims that any particular economic event is occurring or forecasts of its effect. Separate such economy-wide influences from a single customer's late payment or an internal quality failure.

For a simple cost sensitivity, if variable cost rose from 25 to **30** on each of four orders, provisional profit would fall from 40 to **20**, with all other assumptions held constant. That calculation does not show that the owner can raise price without losing demand. The relevant next questions are whether the cost is real, what alternatives customers have and whether capacity or quality changes at another volume.

## Produce the business explanation

1. Draw or list the seven operating stages and assign the customer, facts approver, producer and person responsible for collection. Mark which promises exist before payment and which facts need approval.
2. Explain each party's incentive and at least two substitutes for the service. Describe a truthful marketing claim and one tempting claim the evidence cannot support.
3. Reconcile revenue, costs, provisional profit, cash and receivables using separate lines. State the omitted items and why the result is not spendable owner income.
4. Identify one internal operational risk and one conditional macroeconomic channel. Work the 30-unit variable-cost sensitivity without assuming a price rise, a loan or more orders.
5. Save a short account of what would have to stay true for continuity: demand, delivery capacity, reliable records and payment timing. Open the [later packet](later-packet.md) and update the affected lines without reclassifying an unpaid invoice as cash.

## Use the model to ask better questions

For support, begin with the four customer invoices and mark paid or unpaid, then add costs. Use counters if the distinction between a sale and a receipt is unfamiliar. For a harder pass, move one payment another period later and ask which commitments can still be met from actual cash. Do not solve the problem with an unapproved credit line.

An optional actual transfer is a redacted explanation of a small operation you own or are authorized to inspect, or a public business model with missing figures labeled unknown. Do not obtain private books, investigate an employer or make legal determinations. A balanced toy ledger demonstrates reasoning about supplied inputs; it is not an audit, a tax filing, proof of customer demand or evidence that a real business can continue. Useful progress combines an inspectable account with a later check against authorized operating or financial evidence.
