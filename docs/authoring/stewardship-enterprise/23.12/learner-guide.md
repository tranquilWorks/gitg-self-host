# 23.12 — Financial relationships and shared money

Write a shared-money agreement whose contributions, privacy limits and decision rights can be reviewed without giving one person control over the other. Allow twenty-five minutes for the comparison and conversation, then ten for an income change. The supplied case involves consenting adults and contains no actual account data.

## Fairness needs an explicit basis

Equal amounts and equal proportions answer different questions. Equal contributions divide a bill evenly. Proportional contributions divide it according to an agreed income basis. Neither automatically accounts for disability costs, care, debt already owed, unequal unpaid work or access to resources. A couple or household must be able to discuss those differences without treating the higher earner as the owner of all decisions.

Secrecy, privacy and coercion also differ. An agreement may need enough information to calculate an accepted contribution; that does not entitle a partner to every transaction, password or private communication. Hidden borrowing against shared resources creates a different concern from retaining reasonable personal privacy. A rule allowing “discussion” is not permission to make a commitment first and explain it afterward.

The CFPB's [family financial-help questions, S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) support clarifying expectations, capacity and changes. They do not select a universally fair contribution formula or provide a valid household contract. If pressure, retaliation or financial control makes joint discussion unsafe, use private, appropriate support rather than a forced meeting. The fictional case below assumes willingness to discuss and freedom to decline.

## Supplied household card M4

Ari receives **1200 take-home units per month** and Bea receives **2400 take-home units** on the same agreed income basis. Shared monthly costs are **900**, made up of 600 housing contribution, 200 food and 100 utilities. These are sample household figures, not a complete personal budget. Ari also has necessary care costs outside this pot; their amount is private and not supplied. Neither has authorized the other to borrow, change ownership or access an individual account.

Both can consider two initial allocations:

| Method | Ari pays | Bea pays | Ari share of income | Bea share of income |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Equal amount | 450 | 450 | 37.5% | 18.75% |
| Proportional to supplied income | 300 | 600 | 25% | 25% |

The proportional calculation is 900 times 1200/3600 and 900 times 2400/3600. It leaves 900 and 1800 respectively before individual obligations. Those remainders are not established discretionary cash because necessary private costs are missing. The comparison supports a conversation; it does not prove affordability.

In the supplied dialogue, Ari says, “I need the care costs to remain possible, and I don't want account monitoring.” Bea says, “I need shared commitments to be visible before they become my responsibility.” Both agree to use 300/600 for one fictional month, subject to each confirming affordability privately. Confirmation is supplied for that initial month only. Their concerns must appear in the agreement, not disappear behind the formula.

## Distinguish a budget rule from authority

The sample agreement funds only the three named shared categories. Each person may make an ordinary purchase **up to 100 units** within those categories and within the remaining category budget, recording the amount in the shared ledger. Larger purchases require both people's prior agreement. A purchase outside the shared categories requires both people's prior agreement at any amount. Discussion itself does not authorize borrowing, pledging property, expanding the budget or taking money from an individual account.

With 90 still available in the food category, an ordinary **80-unit food purchase** fits the delegated rule and leaves 10. A **110-unit purchase** exceeds both the threshold and the available category balance; talking about it does not create funding. Splitting the 110 into smaller receipts would not make the same commitment legitimate. A proposed new item must identify whose funds and agreement would cover it.

A relative mentions **200 units of possible help**. Whether this would be a gift or a loan is unknown, and nothing is received or promised to the household on clear terms. Do not put it in available income. Clarify amount, recipient, repayment expectations, conditions and effects on the household before any acceptance. A gift can also introduce control if the giver expects a say in unrelated decisions.

## Produce the reviewable agreement

1. Recalculate both contribution methods and income percentages. Describe the fairness principle each uses and one important fact it does not capture. Do not infer that private necessary care costs can be cut.
2. Write each person's concern and privacy boundary in neutral language. State the limited information needed for the selected method, who may see it and what account access remains unauthorized.
3. Record the first-month 300/600 contributions, named shared categories and a contribution date before those shared costs fall due. In this fiction both choose day 1; actual households must use their real pay and bill dates.
4. Specify the 100-unit rule, available-budget condition and prior joint agreement for exceptions. Work the 80 and 110 examples. Keep possible relative help in an unresolved field outside resources.
5. Set a month-end review and an earlier review if income, care needs or shared costs change. Preserve the first agreement and open the [later packet](later-packet.md). Do not rewrite the original as though later agreement already existed.

A useful ledger records category, amount, date, permission where needed and remaining balance. It need not expose unrelated personal purchases. Contributions should be distinguishable from ownership: paying a larger share does not by itself settle property title or legal liability. Where rights matter, obtain qualified local advice rather than treating the exercise as an enforceable agreement.

## Practice disagreement without manufacturing consent

For support, begin with the two-row comparison and use a separate color for “discussed,” “agreed” and “paid.” A helper can check the percentages without seeing real account data. For a harder pass, change one income and explore why applying the same formula may still create an unaffordable burden. A smaller total, a different temporary share or a support request would each require explicit agreement and realistic consequences.

Optional actual transfer is a willing, low-stakes conversation about one shared expense. Do not send an agreement on someone else's behalf or demand private proof to finish a competency. If no shared arrangement applies, use the fictional case and mark real transfer unattempted. An actual agreement artifact plus a later record of a decision or revision could support progress; neither a hypothetical signature nor a balanced table proves trust, consent or a safe financial relationship.
