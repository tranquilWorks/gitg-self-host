# 24.02 — Entrepreneurship and value creation

Design and deliver one tiny offer, then compare the intended benefit with the user's result and the provider's effort. Allow thirty minutes for the supplied case and ten for its revision. The finished artifact should solve a bounded problem for someone who wants it; a persuasive description of a future business is not enough.

## Value is an effect on a person or task

A problem is economically interesting when someone experiences a cost and an offer could reduce it in a way they value. The user, buyer and person approving the work may be different. A free prototype can reveal usability, but it does not establish willingness to pay. A purchase can reveal demand without proving that the product helped. Keep these claims separate so an attractive idea does not become its own evidence.

Start with the current workaround and its burden. A proposed offer should specify what it does, who it is for, how the result will be checked and what it asks from the recipient. The work needed to supply accurate facts, maintain the artifact, answer questions and make corrections belongs in the cost of delivery. Moving that work onto an organizer without agreement can make a tidy product more burdensome than the original process.

The SBA's [business-planning material, S10](../SOURCES.md#S10) supports inspecting demand, alternatives and customer information. This companion's event and observations are original fiction. No market study, sales contact or real customer validation occurred. An actual attempt would need willing participation and an authorized, low-stakes problem.

## Supplied offer V7

A fictional organizer, Sol, runs a **Local History Table** event. In the previous event cycle, Sol answered six repeated logistics questions at two minutes each: **12 minutes** of recorded response time. This is a small baseline from one cycle, not a forecast of every future event. The existing workaround is a long email containing all the facts, including unrelated background.

Sol wants a one-page visitor guide. The approved source card says:

| Field | Approved fictional fact |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | Share and discuss copies of local historical photographs |
| Time | Saturday 2–3 p.m.; visitors may arrive during that hour |
| Location and access | Cedar Hall, East Room; use the East entrance, which has a step-free route |
| Bring | A printed copy of a photograph is optional; do not bring original irreplaceable items |
| Contact | Ask Sol through the event's existing organizer channel |

The place and event are invented. These facts are not advice about an actual venue's accessibility. Sol is the authorized approver of this source card within the case. The offer is one guide using those five fields, delivered for a simulated Thursday review, with one revision and a **40-minute provider ceiling**. No fee, live publication, mailing list or ongoing maintenance service is included.

## Deliver a complete first version

A workable first draft reads: “Local History Table: discuss copies of local historical photographs. Saturday, 2–3 p.m.; arrive during that hour. Cedar Hall, East Room; East entrance offers the step-free route. Bring a printed photo copy if you want; originals are unnecessary. Questions: use the existing organizer channel to reach Sol.” All five fields are present. Sol's supplied first review approves the facts but asks to make the entrance instruction easier to locate before release.

One fictional intended reader, N, attempts two information tasks without hints. N finds the start time correctly in 20 seconds. Asked which entrance provides the step-free route, N answers “the main entrance” after 35 seconds. Do not score the second answer as correct because the text technically included the right fact. The task revealed that one important fact was not found accurately under the supplied conditions.

The revised guide gives “Use the East entrance for the step-free route” its own clearly labeled access line. Sol approves the revision in the case before the simulated visitor use. A different invented reader, O, finds the East entrance in 18 seconds. This supports a limited usability observation, not proof that all access needs are met or that the event will receive fewer questions.

## Reconcile benefit and delivery effort

Provider effort is 10 minutes organizing the source, 18 drafting, 8 reviewing and revising, and 4 recording the outcome: **40 minutes**. The ceiling was met exactly; no spare capacity for ongoing updates has been established. Sol's review and future maintenance are additional burdens, not hidden inside the provider's time.

N's first two tasks show **one of two correct**. After revision, a different single reader succeeds at the previously failed access task. Do not combine that into “three successful tasks” or claim a controlled improvement from incomparable tiny samples. The useful conclusion is that a specific failure prompted an approved correction with one later successful check. Whether the guide reduces the organizer's total effort remains a question for the later packet.

## Produce the value-delivery record

1. Identify the recipient, user, facts approver and possible buyer. Record the six-question baseline and the current email workaround. Keep payment demand unknown because the offer is free.
2. Write a tiny offer with the five fields, delivery date, approval step, one-revision limit and 40-minute ceiling. Check consent and permissions before any actual delivery; the supplied appointments apply only to V7.
3. Create the full first guide from the source card. Do not invent missing venue facts or collect visitor details. Record approval status separately from drafting status.
4. Run the supplied reader tasks analytically, preserving the wrong entrance response. Revise the access line and use the supplied later reader result with its sample-size limitation. Keep original and revised artifacts findable.
5. Record provider effort and set a total-burden check for Sol after the event. Save the initial claim before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md). A reader's success and an organizer's saved time are different outcomes.

## Learn before expanding the promise

For support, create a five-row guide directly from the card and practice identifying who can approve each fact. A helper may assist with layout, with that assistance recorded. For a harder pass, have the organizer change the time after approval; define how the current version would be corrected without pretending old copies disappear.

Optional real transfer could be one small, authorized improvement for a willing club or colleague, with a complete artifact and a later task observation. No outreach, actual publication or purchase is required by the supplied case. Respect privacy, copyright and the owner's actual approval process. Do not advertise a measured saving until the record supports it.

Progress would include actual delivery and a measurable benefit to a real recipient, alongside the burdens or failures encountered. These invented records let a learner practice that reasoning while leaving actual value creation unproven. A narrower offer or a decision to stop can be the appropriate entrepreneurial response when the total burden exceeds the benefit.
