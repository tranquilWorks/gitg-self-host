# 23.11 — Financial hardship, poverty, and constrained choice

Build a seven-day stabilization plan for a shortfall that optional spending cuts cannot solve. Allow twenty-five minutes for the supplied budget and verified help route, then ten for the changed circumstances. The aim is feasible harm reduction with honest unknowns, not a promise to eliminate poverty through budgeting.

## Name the constraint before prescribing effort

Scarcity can limit time, transport, housing choices, access to support and the ability to absorb a fee. Irregular income makes dates matter as well as totals. Disability-related costs and caregiving may be necessary to keep someone safe or able to work; removing them from a spreadsheet does not remove the need. Unaffordable housing or a debt trap may require assistance, negotiation or a structural change unavailable within seven days. A plan should distinguish what the person can change from what depends on another party or a public system.

Optional spending deserves a check, but an essentials-only deficit remains a deficit. Borrowing can move the immediate shortage while adding repayments and fees later. Delaying a payment changes timing only if the provider agrees, and it does not erase the obligation. Prioritization should consider actual consequences and available qualified help; this lesson does not impose a universal legal ranking of bills.

The CFPB [cash-flow tool, S02](../SOURCES.md#S02) supports distinguishing resources available at different dates. The Atlanta Fed's [benefits-cliff explanation, S07](../SOURCES.md#S07) describes how increased earnings can coincide with reduced assistance. It does not establish anyone's eligibility or imply that earning more always reduces resources. A household needs its actual rules, costs and transition dates checked before relying on a change.

## Make seven days manageable

1. Reconcile income, essential costs and optional costs. Show both 480 and the residual 400. Mark dated shortfalls as unmet amounts instead of letting a spreadsheet silently spend nonexistent cash.
2. On day 1, the fictional resident prepares a concise official-service inquiry and a private document checklist. Limit the task to information necessary for the route. Record contact as planned, not completed.
3. On day 1, draft a request to the housing provider about the day-3 shortfall and any available arrangement. Ask about charges, consequences and written confirmation. Keep the original due date in force unless a valid agreement changes it.
4. On day 2, review food, necessary care and transport continuity with the real consequences visible. Identify where an appropriate support worker or qualified adviser is needed. Do not solve the gap by deleting disability or caregiving needs.
5. Set a day-4 check for actual responses and a day-7 revision. List owner, next date, status and unresolved amount for each of the three tasks above. Preserve the first plan before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md).

The tasks can be reduced or shared with permission if disability, language, care or access burdens make them unrealistic. A helper may assist with finding official information without taking account control. Existing dependents and support relationships matter; a lower spreadsheet total achieved by shifting uncompensated care onto someone else may simply move the harm.

## Judge stabilization with proportionate evidence

For support, calculate the monthly gap first, then just the day-3 housing problem. For a harder pass, introduce delayed income and ask which earlier arrangement would need rechecking; do not invent retrospective consent. In an optional actual attempt, use only your own or willingly shared redacted circumstances and an appropriate local route. A person need not disclose hardship to practice this competency.

Useful progress could include an accurately limited plan and a later verified response, changed burden or continuity observation. A request, an approval, a payment and a recurring improvement are different events. One relief payment may reduce this month's deficit without addressing unstable income, housing cost or a benefits cliff. This case treats partial stabilization as partial, and human worth as entirely separate from financial circumstances.
