# 27.02 — Balancing competing goods

## Admit the good your choice leaves out

Tradeoffs are hardest when more than one alternative is genuinely good. Calling the unchosen activity selfish, lazy or pointless makes a decision easier by distorting it. A transparent choice names what is protected, what is possible now, who is affected and which worthwhile thing will wait.

Allow twenty minutes for the fictional weekend decision, ten for its later outcome and ten for checks. No real care, medical, legal or financial decision is requested. [OpenLearn's wider work–life balance discussion, S09](../SOURCES.md#S09) supports considering commitments beyond employment. It supplies no universal ranking of family, work, pleasure or rest, and this exercise introduces none.

Worked example: a person chooses rest instead of an optional class after an unusually demanding week. The class remains worthwhile, and the person may later regret missing a useful lesson. That regret alone does not make the original decision unsound. Conversely, enjoying a class would not justify ignoring a foreseeable essential care obligation.

## Neri's genuinely discretionary three hours

On Saturday Neri has 14:00–17:00 free after work, essential care and necessary household tasks. The window is confirmed, not borrowed from sleep or someone else's responsibility. Neri wants low effort this week and values a rare invitation from a willing friend. There are no additional shared commitments within the window. The following four options are feasible individually under the initial facts; travel and transitions are included.

| Option | Time in minutes | Specific good | Cost or unserved good |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| F: visit a willing friend | 90 | Rare welcome conversation | Uses some quiet recovery time |
| G: use an available self-study lesson | 120 | Skill growth and interest | Demands sustained attention |
| S: already approved local service task | 150 | Requested contribution | Higher effort and less private rest |
| R: stay home with quiet leisure/rest | 180 | Low demand and ordinary pleasure | Misses this rare visit and today's service opportunity |

A mixed option F plus 90 minutes of quiet time also fits. F + G does not. Neri has promised no attendance at S and no class place at G; declining creates no cancellation debt in this case. If actual commitments existed, they would belong in the protected-obligations check before using this table.

Give all eight goods a disposition: work, family, health, service, rest, growth, pleasure and security. Here work and necessary family/health responsibilities are protected outside the window; service, rest, growth and pleasure are live possibilities; security is protected through essential resources with no new spending required. “Not an additional option now” does not mean an area is unimportant. Do not turn the dispositions into a moral ranking or equal-time requirement.

## Choose a rule you can later examine

1. Write three genuinely live alternatives, including doing less. For each name the benefit, cost, affected people and reversibility. You may compare F plus rest, G plus spare time, and R; preserve S as a considered but unselected possibility. Check the sum of any combination against 180 minutes.
2. Choose with two reasons and a fair account of the strongest sacrifice. The supplied starting decision is F plus rest: a rare welcome chance and room for low effort. It sacrifices today's learning session and service opportunity. Set a reversal condition before acting: if the friend cancels, do not treat the old invitation as continuing. A new care need also takes priority. The fallback is quiet time at home, with no claim that all missed goods will be made up.
3. Save the choice and reasons before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Compare the actual consequence with the prediction. Evaluate whether the decision was reasonable on the information available separately from whether the outcome felt pleasant. Retain, revise or abandon the rule with a reason.

## A sacrifice is not a debt to maximize later

The output is one decision record with an honest loss and an actual review. Saying “I will do everything next weekend” merely moves the impossible budget. A bounded revisit might be checking a future lesson opening; it is a proposal until time, energy and any affected person agree.

For support, compare two alternatives with spoken notes while keeping the unchosen good visible. A helper may clarify facts but should not decide the person's priorities. For a harder variation, a 45-minute new care obligation reduces the discretionary window to 135 minutes. Recheck which options fit and who must be told; do not preserve the old decision by relabeling care optional.

Later transfer concerns one low-stakes choice and a review within a week. Coercion, urgent care or major consequences call for the appropriate people and procedures, not this fictional table. A good outcome can follow poor reasoning, and a careful choice can disappoint. Learning depends on keeping both facts available rather than defending the original answer at any cost.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Capacity, access and context govern actual attempts. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Human dignity is never scored.
