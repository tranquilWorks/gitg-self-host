# 26.08 — Adventure and calculated challenge

## Choose uncertainty you can responsibly work with

A worthwhile challenge adds something unfamiliar while leaving a realistic way to stop, ask for help and return. Risk is not the ingredient that makes courage count. New navigation can be challenging without isolation, danger or a demand to prove independence.

Use the fictional library map below. Allow fifteen minutes for preparation, ten to trace the later attempt and ten for checks. The default is a paper task, not anxiety treatment or physical training. [NPS planning guidance, S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) distinguishes a suitable activity and preparation from enthusiasm. Here that general planning principle is adapted to an ordinary public building; no park procedure certifies the library.

Worked example: Lee chooses to find a new reading room while staff are available. When the lift is out, Lee asks for another suitable destination on the entrance level. Asking changes the route and preserves access; it does not invalidate the challenge. Taking stairs against an access need would add an unrelated hazard.

## Fictional library route L6

Rin knows the entrance desk D but has never located the map display M. Today's visitor information confirms the public building is open, the following level corridors are suitable for Rin's existing supports, and a staff member is at D throughout the planned attempt. The map display is open. No stairs or unlisted doors belong to the plan.

| Corridor | One-way minutes | Map instruction |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| D to junction J | 3 | Follow the blue line to the large J sign |
| J to display M | 4 | Turn toward the M sign |
| J to workroom W | 2 | Staff-only door; no visitor entry |

Rin starts at 14:00, may spend three minutes reading M, and must be back at D by 14:20. Rin sets a 14:10 turnaround, carrying the map in readable form and keeping existing communication support. If signs disagree, access changes, distress or effort becomes unsuitable, or staff support disappears, Rin pauses safely and returns or asks for help. No diagnosis or numerical distress threshold is invented.

The reason for trying is to use more of an already familiar public resource. The unfamiliar part is following the junction sign, not enduring discomfort without assistance. Rin predicts needing one map check. The destination is optional if the supported return becomes uncertain.

## Plan the bounded extension

1. Draw only the permitted route, calculate its round trip with reading time, and write the stop/return decision before seeing the outcome. Distinguish an unfamiliar sign from a locked door: the first invites clarification, the second restricts entry.
2. Save the plan and use the [later packet](later-packet.md). At each timestamp mark location, information available and action. Do not score a help request as failure or assume a projected arrival occurred.
3. Compare prediction, actual support and remaining uncertainty. Name what was practiced—checking and correcting a route—and what was not, such as navigating alone or using an unfamiliar building without access information. Choose repeat, a small appropriate variation, rest or stop with a reason.

The output is a route trace and one evidence-based next decision. A destination reached by violating a staff-only boundary contradicts the task. A planned route without a visit shows preparation; a safe return after changed conditions can show sound adaptation.

## Keep the challenge chosen

A reader can trace the whole case without leaving home. In a real setting, use the actual venue's current map, access information and available support; retain necessary aids. If the activity is unsuitable, choose a familiar destination or decline. A partner may accompany the attempt without pretending the resulting experience was solo.

For a harder paper case, the display closes while Rin is still at J. Propose a different permitted entrance-level destination only if its access, time and support are confirmed; otherwise return. Do not turn disappointment into permission to enter W.

At a later suitable opportunity, repeat the same route or locate one similarly supported destination. Record actual assistance, not an aspiration to need less. Specialized outdoor environments, dangerous equipment, clinical exposure and emergency situations require different expertise. A successful library visit establishes none of those capabilities.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Capacity, access and context govern actual attempts. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Human dignity is never scored.
