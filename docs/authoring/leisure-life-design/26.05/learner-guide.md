# 26.05 — Outdoor competence and responsible recreation

## Make the return part of the outing

An outdoor plan is an agreement between a particular route, current conditions and the people using it. Carrying equipment is useful only when it fits those requirements and someone knows how to use it. Reaching a destination while ignoring a closure or the return limit is not competent completion.

Use the complete fictional Alder Park file below. Allow twenty minutes to prepare, ten to review the later record and ten for checks. Paper or spoken notes are enough; no outdoor attempt is required. The output is a route decision, a return calculation and a review that separates preparation from participation. [NPS trip planning, S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) supports checking current site information, matching capability and supplies, and maintaining a return contact. It does not clear this invented route or provide technical training.

A worked example: an experienced walker brings familiar equipment but finds the usual bridge closed. Equipment does not cancel the closure. Choosing a permitted short alternative, telling the agreed contact and returning within the revised plan demonstrates judgment; crossing the barrier to finish the old route does not.

## Alder Park file A4

Sam and Jo know the maintained level path and use their usual mobility supports. Both currently judge this familiar outing suitable. The fictional park desk confirms today's path is open, conditions are mild and dry, and no relevant alert is active. The appropriate fictional weather report agrees. These stipulations are not a live forecast. If any essential confirmation is missing at a real departure, the plan needs another check or deferral.

| Segment | One-way minutes | Current condition |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Entrance E to signed junction J | 6 | Open, familiar level surface |
| J to overlook O | 8 | Open, same surface |
| J to pond P | 5 | Closed for repair; no entry |

The permitted route reverses the same segments to return. No shortcut connects O to E. There is a bench at O; the optional pause lasts five minutes. Start is 09:00, latest return to E is 09:40, and the pair choose a conservative turnaround from wherever they are by 09:20. This is a case decision, not a universal safety margin. A staffed desk remains at E; reception beyond E is unreliable. Pat has agreed to expect a return message by 09:45 and to contact the park desk using the shared route plan if no message arrives. Emergency assistance takes precedence over that ordinary check-in schedule.

Their familiar suitable clothing, personal supplies, water and needed supports have been checked for this short route; no universal quantity is prescribed. A paper map is available. The pair stay together, check J's sign against the map, leave objects and plants alone, carry out their own waste and give other users room. No wildlife approach, collection, fire, water entry or technical terrain belongs in this exercise.

## Decide, then compare with the actual return

1. Draw E–J–O with times in both directions and cross out the closed branch. Calculate the planned return including the bench pause. Write the contact arrangement, support checks and what would trigger earlier return: new closure, unsafe surface, changed weather, pain or unsuitable effort. An unknown essential condition is not a quiet yes.
2. Save that plan before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md). Trace its actual timestamps. Identify the moment when continuing would have depended on ignoring new information. Check both arrival and the message; arriving privately does not notify Pat.
3. Write a five-line return review: planned route/time, actual route/time, navigation decision, effect on place/other users, and one preparation improvement. Keep any untested segment explicit. Do not describe a shortened outing as full overlook completion.

## Extend capability without escalating risk

For support, analyze the map with a reader, use a familiar seated park location, or retain a tabletop-only result. A live route needs actual current land-manager/weather information and individual access checks. Keep aids and assistance. Technical, remote or water activities need their own training and authorization; this small success grants none.

For a harder paper variation, a delay reaches J at 09:18 and staff cannot confirm the overlook segment. Calculate a direct return, then explain why spare clock time cannot resolve an unknown route condition. On a later suitable ordinary outing, practice checking one junction and closing the return communication. Compare real effort and support needs before considering a modest change.

A useful record may show an early return and good stewardship. A complete plan without an outing shows preparation only. Ignoring the closed pond path contradicts the purpose even if nobody is injured. Missing return information stays unresolved; a pleasant report does not fill it in.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Capacity, access and context govern actual attempts. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Human dignity is never scored.
