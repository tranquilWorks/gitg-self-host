# 21.01 — Work worth doing

Compare work options through eight explicit criteria, then test one uncertainty that could change the choice. Use two twenty-five-minute comparisons and one small information step or its complete fictional equivalent. Keep staying and redesigning work in the comparison. This is not an instruction to resign, accept an offer or expose essential income to risk.

## Evaluate the work behind the title

A role title is a claim about a category, not evidence of its daily tasks or fit. Aptitude asks which tasks you can do or realistically learn with support. Livelihood includes necessary income, stability, costs and obligations. Service asks who benefits and whether your contribution is useful to them. Meaning concerns why this work matters to you, which need not be identical to prestige or personal enjoyment.

Environment includes hours, access, management, resources, location and interpersonal conditions. Growth asks what feedback, practice and increasing responsibility are actually available. Dignity concerns how people are treated and whether they can raise concerns, retain appropriate boundaries and do work without unacceptable harm. Real need asks whether the output solves a problem someone actually has; an impressive mission statement does not establish that need.

Give each criterion evidence, a hypothesis or an explicit unknown. A role-holder's account of a typical week may be informative without defining contractual terms or predicting every future week. A compensation headline may omit costs or stability. A promised mentor is different from accepted time with that person. Do not fill an empty cell with the most attractive interpretation of a title.

Some constraints cannot sensibly be averaged away. If a role cannot meet a necessary livelihood or access condition, a high overall score does not remove that conflict. Distinguish a hard requirement from a preference you can trade, and state how uncertain evidence affects the result. A numeric matrix may help organize thinking but should not pretend that unmeasured dignity, meaning and future opportunity have precise exchange rates.

Compare opportunity costs as well as benefits. A visible role may offer influence while reducing time spent on the craft you want to develop. A quieter role may provide relevant practice while narrowing another opportunity. Staying can preserve stability and still carry a cost; redesign may be feasible only with a manager's agreement and resources. None is automatically the courageous or complacent option.

Test the uncertainty most likely to reverse the decision. Ask about actual daily work, unavoidable constraints and whose problem the output solves. A public sample, willing role-holder discussion or authorized short shadow can answer something without making a commitment. Keep exploratory information separate from an offer, a contract, a guaranteed career path or approval of a redesign. Serious employment, ethical or safety concerns need the proper qualified route.

## Worked example

An appealing title suggests substantial design work, but a role-holder describes mostly coordination and regular unplanned hours. The learner revises the role's fit rather than calling the role worthless. They preserve a needed hours limit and investigate a less visible option plus an internal redesign. The decision remains provisional because an anecdotal week and written terms may differ.

## Complete fictional options

Rin needs at least 500 units of weekly income and can commit at most 30 hours. These invented units and constraints are not personal financial advice. Rin enjoys writing usable technical instructions and has a completed harmless instruction sample; no qualification for safety-critical work is implied.

Option A, “Strategy Fellow,” advertises 650 units and 30 hours. It promises influence and growth, but daily task mix, actual hours, mentoring, beneficiary need and complaint routes are unknown. Option B, “Documentation Specialist,” describes 520 units and 30 hours: 14 creating instructions, 14 coordinating facts and 2 mentoring, all within the 30. An internal user has requested clearer routine documentation. Work location is accessible in the case; long-term stability and team treatment remain unverified.

Option C is staying in Rin's current role at 560 units and 30 hours, currently 20 coordination and 10 documentation. The stated redesign proposal would use 16 coordination, 12 documentation and 2 mentoring hours, preserving 30 total and existing pay if accepted. The manager has not approved it and no mentor has accepted time. Existing working conditions are familiar; longer-term growth and the proposal's feasibility remain open.

## Compare, investigate and revise

1. Translate all eight criteria into observable conditions for A, B and C. Include both existing C and its unapproved redesign. Output: facts, hypotheses and unknowns with no title-based substitution.
2. Keep the 500-unit floor and 30-hour limit visible. Compare at least two benefits and two costs per option, including opportunity costs and unverified conditions. Output: a provisional preference that cannot compensate for an unacceptable constraint with prestige.
3. Select A's actual hours/task mix as a decision-reversing question. Prepare three questions: what occupies a normal week, what causes extra hours, and who uses the work? Output: a bounded inquiry plan, not a request for confidential employer data.
4. Save the comparison before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Use the supplied role-holder reply to update the relevant cells while distinguishing an account from binding terms. Output: changed judgment and unresolved discrepancy.
5. Choose a proportionate next step: investigate B, seek written clarification, discuss C's redesign, defer or decline. Output: a true decision/status record with no invented offer acceptance or resignation.

## Support and transfer

Use spoken cards, a public role description or a smaller task redesign when access is limited. A harder case has a meaningful role that fails the income floor; seek a feasible adjustment or preserve the constraint rather than romanticizing sacrifice. Receiving income from work and seeking meaning are both legitimate considerations.

For actual transfer, use public or permitted information and a willing contact. Keep employer-confidential facts and private finances outside app evidence. A task experiment is not a guarantee of employability, future pay or fulfillment. Paid employment is not required for this competency; suitable voluntary, study or household roles can also be evaluated with context-appropriate constraints.
