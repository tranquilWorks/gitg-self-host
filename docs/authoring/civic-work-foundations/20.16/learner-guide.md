# 20.16 — Mutual aid and community resilience

Trace a small reciprocal support system, complete a bounded exchange in fiction or an authorized group, and review who keeps it working. Allow twenty minutes to understand the arrangement, one low-risk exchange and a second contact. Receiving help is legitimate even when you cannot contribute. Mutual aid is not an equal-repayment contract or a public ranking of generosity.

## Look at relationships and capacity, not a heroic helper

One-way assistance can be valuable, but mutual aid also asks how people participate in decisions and share useful capacity over time. A person can provide information today, request an accessible copy tomorrow and contribute nothing during a difficult period. Reciprocity belongs to the network; it need not be an immediate trade between two people or an equal number of hours.

Understand the arrangement before offering. Who receives requests, confirms that an offer is wanted, sets boundaries and handles an unanswered message? A list of willing people is not an accepted service. Availability, competence, authority and actual handoff are separate. Ask how refusal works and how a recipient can report a problem without risking exclusion from future help.

Treat different support areas differently. Food help may require safe handling and an established arrangement. Transport information is not the same as transporting a person. Public information needs a dated source and clear uncertainty. Emergencies belong with appropriate urgent services, not an informal queue. Childcare requires actual competent, authorized care and a confirmed handoff; a group chat does not supply these. Emotional or practical support should be wanted and bounded, with clinical needs outside an ordinary helper's role. Give each area an explicit disposition rather than treating goodwill as universal qualification.

Privacy matters at the network level. A shared directory should not become a map of medical need, home access, vulnerability or who owes whom. Record only what the task requires through an agreed channel. Let people request an access aid without explaining a diagnosis or hardship. A public source can often supply the information needed without collecting private stories.

Verify the useful endpoint. Sending a timetable does not prove that it was current, wanted or usable. A recipient's acknowledgment can establish receipt and usefulness for one task; it does not establish that they made the trip or became safer. Check whether a contribution creates extra work, leaves an essential responsibility unowned or makes the helper the only person who knows the process.

Resilience means the arrangement can handle some change without relying entirely on one person. A clear public-information note and an accepted backup can reduce a particular dependency. They do not establish preparedness for every emergency. Do not recruit backup by assumption or transfer private requests without consent. A future commitment is different from work already performed, and informal support must not silently replace an institution's obligations.

## Worked example

A group shares an old image of opening hours. One member checks the current public notice and sends the requested correction privately. Later that member requests a large-print copy of a different public instruction. The two exchanges need not have equal value. The group notices that only one coordinator knows how to verify dates and agrees a small backup procedure without publishing recipients' needs.

## Complete fictional exchange packet

The Cedar neighbors group shares ordinary public information through an agreed private request channel. Coordinator Jin asks before assigning a task; people may decline or receive help without repayment. Mina offers one hours check. Owen requests it for a fictional library. A stale image says Tuesday 09:00–17:00. The current public notice, dated 1 October and valid through 31 October, says **Tuesday 10:00–16:00; check the public desk notice for exceptional closures**. No exceptional-closure notice is supplied, so unusual changes remain unchecked.

Mina also requests a large-print copy of a fictional public route card. Its complete instruction is **Cedar stop → Oak stop → Library stop; questions: Public Route Desk**. This is a paper direction exercise, not a verified live transport route or promise of accessible vehicles. Owen offers to make the copy. Jin is currently the only person maintaining the date-check note; Tess has been suggested as backup but has not yet accepted. Lee receives information and has no available contribution; that does not limit Lee's standing.

## Complete the exchange and review the arrangement

1. Map request, offer, acceptance, refusal, feedback and escalation routes. Give all six support areas a safe role or outside-scope disposition. Output: a capacity map without private needs, homes or debt scores.
2. Confirm Owen wants the hours check and verify the supplied current notice against the stale image. Keep exceptional closures uncertain. Output: a dated answer with the limited source, not an unqualified travel assurance.
3. Ask for the chosen large-print route copy and confirm whether Owen accepts that task. Do not treat Mina's earlier help as payment. Output: accepted or declined support and the actual copy/handoff status.
4. At the second contact, ask whether each exchange was usable and whether someone carried hidden work. Examine the single-coordinator dependency. Output: a modest network improvement requiring an actual accepting owner.
5. Save your account before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Distinguish completed exchanges, an accepted backup and a future check. Output: reciprocal flow and one unresolved service limit, with no emergency-readiness claim.

## Support and transfer

Use the supplied cards orally, in larger print or with chosen assistance. A harder case leaves the usual coordinator unavailable and the proposed backup silent. Preserve the unaccepted status, use an existing appropriate route or pause the nonurgent task; do not silently assign the backup or improvise essential care.

For actual transfer, use a willing established group and a safe task within your role. Urgent concerns need the proper real service without waiting for a practice review. Keep identifying details in the approved channel, outside app evidence. A fictional exchange can explain reciprocal capacity but cannot establish real community benefit, resilience or another person's safety.
