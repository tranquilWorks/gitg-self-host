# 21.06 — Customer, stakeholder, and user understanding

Let a downstream task change one requirement in a small work product. Allow fifteen minutes to prepare, a twenty-minute observation or supplied fictional equivalent, and a comparable retry. Use a meeting invitation with invented facts. Compliments and senior approval can coexist with a user being unable to complete the task.

## Find the practical need beyond internal preferences

Identify who uses the artifact, who supports them when it fails and who may be affected without being a direct user. A new attendee may need an entrance, while an event helper handles questions caused by an omission. Someone unable to read an image may be excluded before reaching the nominal task. “The requester liked it” does not represent every one of those perspectives.

State the need in the user's terms before specifying a solution. “Know where to enter at the relevant time” is a need. “Add a map” is one possible response that might or might not address it. A requirement should follow evidence about the actual obstacle, with attention to constraints, access and downstream burden. A role title or majority preference does not automatically establish everyone's needs.

Observe a task rather than asking only for an opinion. Give a willing reader the harmless draft and ask them to identify the location, arrival time and access-question route. Let them work without explaining what you meant while the task is underway. Record the action or interpretation and ask a neutral follow-up about difficulty. A warm compliment can be sincere while the location is still wrong.

A failed attempt tests your assumption, not the user's worth or intelligence. Familiarity, language, format, context and missing information may affect the result. Ask what the artifact supplied and what it forced the user to invent. Avoid demanding private history to explain the difficulty. A single reader can reveal a real flaw, but cannot represent all potential users or establish a market segment's prevalence.

Revise a requirement, not just a cosmetic detail. If the user chose the main door because the invitation omitted the evening entrance, the relevant change is explicit time-appropriate entry information. A larger logo would not solve that mismatch. Tie the changed content to the observed need, check it against authorized facts and preserve other correct information.

Test a fresh comparable invitation or ask another willing reader. The same person may remember the correction, while a different person introduces another change in conditions. State those limits. Check whether the solution creates new work for a helper, excludes another format or publishes a contact not authorized for that purpose. A narrowly better task result is valuable without becoming proof that every customer is understood.

## Worked example

A first-time attendee praises an invitation's appearance but selects the main entrance, which is closed at the event time. The author had assumed everyone knew the evening door. They add the named entry route from the owner's information, keep a plain-text version and check a different invitation. The revised requirement is usable arrival information, not more decoration.

## Complete fictional invitations and owner facts

Draft A says: **Design Review —14 October 2026, 18:00–19:00 UTC —Cedar Center, Birch Room —access questions: Event Desk**. It is plain text and has no entrance information. The building's ordinary public sign points to the North main entrance. The owner fact card states that North is closed in the evening; use **East Entrance from 17:45**, with the internal sign to Birch Room. Event Desk is the approved fictional public questions label, not a real address. No real travel or access guarantee is supplied.

The learner's initial assumption is that a brighter title is the main improvement needed. The task asks a willing fictional first-time reader to identify where to enter, the start time and whom to ask about access. A downstream helper can review whether the public contact label is appropriate; no personal number or private condition may be added.

For the comparable retry, Draft B must use the supplied facts **Planning Check —15 October 2026, 19:00–20:00 UTC —Cedar Center, Birch Room —East Entrance from 18:45 —access questions: Event Desk**. This is a new invitation, not permission to reuse A's start or entry-opening time blindly.

## Observe, change the requirement and check its effect

1. Map primary user, downstream helper and a potentially excluded nonuser. State the task and what would contradict the brighter-title assumption. Output: a specific hypothesis and success criterion, not a list of stakeholder names.
2. Present Draft A without hints. Record where the supplied reader would enter, when they would arrive and the questions route. Ask what made the choice difficult. Output: observed interpretation distinguished from praise and from an actual arrival.
3. Compare the obstacle with the owner fact card. Revise the requirement to state the correct entrance and its opening time, keeping UTC, room and the approved contact. Output: evidence-to-requirement reasoning and a corrected A.
4. Save your revision before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Test B using its own date and times with the new supplied reader. Output: a comparable task result without coaching or copied time errors.
5. Ask whether the contact and alternate format shift work or create exposure. Record the helper's limited review and what remains unknown about other users. Output: a bounded downstream result, not universal validation.

## Support and transfer

Use spoken invitations, accessible text or a trusted interpreter as appropriate. A harder case has two equally plausible entrance names in conflicting records; seek owner clarification instead of fabricating certainty. Users may decline without affecting service, employment or a relationship.

Actual research needs the organization's permission, willing participants and minimal relevant data. Do not collect protected client, employee or disability information to justify a design change. Keep private notes in the approved process and broad lessons outside app evidence. Fiction supports reasoning, but the prewritten response is not real user research or actual attendance evidence.

## Source use

[GOV.UK's user-needs guidance, S11](../SOURCES.md#S11), distinguishes evidence-based user problems from assumed solutions and recommends refining needs as understanding improves. Only the relevant sections are used; this guide does not implement the whole government service standard or validate the invitation as universally accessible. All event facts, observations and revisions are original fiction.


## Scope and evidence boundary

The canonical record has no professional_boundary field. The practical limits in this guide are exercise safeguards, not added canonical metadata.

Canonical applicability is context_sensitive. Paid employment, a career change or a particular work role is not compulsory. Normative status remains cross_tradition_core_or_broadly_recurrent. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Keep private or protected records outside app evidence. Formal M6K A/B/C, independent cold-start, actual learner, qualified and owner content acceptance remain pending.
