# Corrective answers

1. No. Establish the rule's actual legal status and examine purpose, method, alternatives, burden and accountability separately. Conscience is relevant but does not create immunity or a complete justification.
2. The evidence for necessity weakens or becomes unresolved; the grievance does not disappear. Examine whether the route is usable, timely and adequate rather than assuming either that it works or that endless delay is required. Preserve the same test for opponents.
3. Neither follows. Responsibility for reasons and consequences can coexist with counsel, challenge and protection of rights. Later claims may be considered, but they do not alter the stipulated earlier motive or excuse harm.
