# 20.14 — Nonviolent action and conflict transformation

Design a proportionate response to a fictional access problem and test it against refusal and partial progress. Use two twenty-five-minute passes and a fifteen-minute tabletop review. You are designing a bounded process, not organizing an actual protest, labor action or confrontation. Nonviolence can demand change and accountability; it does not require passive acceptance of harm.

## Choose a method for its purpose and consequences

Begin with a verifiable change and the people affected. “Make the venue listen” is difficult to assess; “publish a usable access-contact route before the next meeting” identifies an outcome. Separate participants' stated needs from an organizer's assumptions, and determine who can authorize the change. Staff may face real resource limits without being personally responsible for every institutional barrier.

Negotiation involves parties discussing terms directly. Mediation adds an impartial facilitator who assists agreement without simply imposing a decision. Restorative practice attends to harm, responsibility and possible repair; it requires appropriate consent, support and attention to power. None automatically grants a participant authority, replaces a required legal process or makes an unsafe encounter suitable. An affected person can decline without losing standing.

Collective pressure methods differ from those processes. A boycott withholds selected participation or purchases; a strike withholds work; protest publicly expresses a demand; civil resistance can involve organized noncooperation and other nonviolent action. Their legality, obligations, risks and consequences vary by place and circumstance. Do not collapse them into one escalation ladder or assume that a peaceful intention makes every action harmless or lawful. This guide gives no operational plan for a risky method.

Conflict can escalate through changed stakes, public humiliation, threats, fear or loss of trusted communication. A response may also impose costs on uninvolved users, staff with limited power or people who cannot easily refuse. Map those burdens before choosing the loudest available method. Keep refusal and dissent possible, do not expose participants' identities, and avoid coercion or retaliation.

For a small correctable information problem, a factual request and invitation to discuss can be an adequate first step. “Least risky adequate” is not a universal demand to wait indefinitely; urgency and actual harm matter. State when you will review progress, what new facts would change your approach and where qualified guidance is needed. Lack of an immediate reply is a reason to reassess, not automatic permission to intensify disruption.

Partial concessions need scrutiny. A contact route that works only for people who can call during a narrow window may leave others excluded. Recognize what changed while preserving the unmet requirement. Compromise is not automatically betrayal, and agreement is not automatically adequate. A process becomes accountable when it can correct its own errors, state remaining limits and close or continue for reasons participants can examine.

## Worked example

A venue offers a telephone contact after receiving an access request. The requester acknowledges that this adds one route but explains that it does not answer a request for an accessible written channel. They ask the responsible owner whether a monitored written route can be provided by the notice deadline. No staff member is named publicly, and people affected by the gap decide whether further involvement is wanted.

## Complete fictional access packet

Cedar Hall plans a meeting on 18 October at 17:00. The owner controls the notice and contact arrangements; the desk assistant cannot approve new staffing. The current notice is an image containing only the title, time and room. It has no text alternative or access-contact route. Two willing fictional users want a readable text notice and a way to ask access questions without disclosing their private circumstances publicly.

The written request can go to the fictional Hall Owner inbox. The practical request is: **provide matching plain text and a monitored written access-question route by 15 October at 12:00, before the meeting**. No actual address is supplied or needed. The owner has not agreed; staffing capacity and a response timeframe are unknown. Consent extends to this private request, not a public campaign or publication of the two users' stories.

## Design and tabletop the response

1. State the requested change, affected participants, authority and unknown constraints. Distinguish a user report from representation of all users. Output: a goal and stakeholder map that does not invent consent.
2. Compare the dialogue processes and pressure methods by purpose, suitability, authority and possible burden. Select the factual request for this case and explain why it is adequate to try now. Output: a reasoned first step, not a universal rule against collective action.
3. Draft the request with its actual facts and a voluntary discussion invitation. Set a review point, privacy limits and stops for coercion, threats or uncertain authority. Output: a bounded process card with a responsible next owner.
4. Before the [later packet](later-packet.md), rehearse both a refusal and an incomplete offer. Name the clarification and consultation each would need. Output: two contingent responses without automatic escalation.
5. Read the later events, credit actual progress and identify the unsatisfied part. Decide whether to revise, seek advice or close this rehearsal. Output: an honest status with unresolved need and no claim of a successful real campaign.

## Support and transfer

A supported route can use spoken goal and risk cards with a trusted willing reader. For a harder case, a proposed improvement shifts an unreasonable burden to a single staff member. Revisit responsibility and resources; do not demand unpaid labor as the only proof of concern.

Actual conflict processes require appropriate local authority, affected people's agency and competent facilitation where needed. Violence, abuse, labor disputes and legal jeopardy are outside this small task. Do not provoke a refusal, stage disruption or approach a vulnerable person to obtain learning evidence. A tabletop comparison develops judgment but cannot prove reconciliation, lawful action or changed institutional practice.
