# 21.07 — Systems and organizational awareness

Prevent one small handoff failure by looking beyond the task assigned to you. Allow twenty minutes to map the workflow, a short coordination exchange and a paper handoff test. Use the supplied notice packet. This exercise does not authorize bypassing review, accessing restricted systems or redesigning another person's job without agreement.

## A local success can still fail downstream

A writer may finish a draft while the publisher still cannot tell which version is approved. A department may count completed files while users need accurate published information. To understand the system, follow the item across boundaries: who supplies facts, who transforms them, who approves, who receives them and who handles corrections. Each interface needs an input, owner, readiness condition and recovery route.

Dependencies connect tasks that appear separate. A publication deadline may depend on an approver's availability and a confirmed event date. Constraints limit the available response: an approval window, access permission, resource ceiling or required review cannot be solved by pretending it does not exist. Institutional context determines which roles have authority and which records must be preserved.

Incentives affect what a process makes easy or rewarding, but a reward structure does not prove a particular person's motive. Counting drafts could encourage early claims of completion; that remains a hypothesis until behavior is examined. Ask where the system creates ambiguity or rework before blaming people for a failure that a better handoff could prevent.

Confirm the actual failure with the receiver. Your diagram may be wrong or omit an existing control. Ask what arrives, what the receiver cannot determine and how they currently recover. A simple approved/draft field can help, but it must bind to a specific version. A detached “approved” message beside several attachments may reproduce the original ambiguity. “Newest” and “approved” answer different questions.

Compare the fix with alternatives and its burden. A shared handoff card might be adequate for a harmless notice; a regulated system may need formal configuration management and engineering controls. A small local change still needs the relevant owners' agreement. Do not eliminate the approver to make the writer's metric look better or silently make the receiver responsible for reconstructing missing facts.

Test both readiness and recovery. Ask the receiver to select the approved item and identify what to do if a newer draft appears or the marker is missing. A test can pass by withholding an ambiguous item and requesting the correct information, not only by moving it forward. Check whether the fix transfers effort, waiting or risk to someone else, and agree who will maintain it. One improved interface is not evidence that the entire organization is optimized.

## Worked example

An approval label arrives without a version ID. The publisher chooses the most recent file and risks using an unapproved title. The team ties the label to a specific notice and preserves the draft status of other versions. The next paper test selects the correct item and routes proposed changes back through approval. The improvement is a clearer boundary, not a removed review step.

## Complete fictional workflow and packet

On 18 October the writer, Lena, receives request Q1: **Map Club, 20 October, 18:00 UTC, Birch Room; public questions: Event Desk**. Ash owns approval and is available 10:00–10:30 UTC. Ren publishes only approved text; the target publication time is 12:00 UTC. The publication is fictional and no real post is made.

Three files arrive together. **N1**, dated 09:40, says “Map Club, 19 October, 18:00 UTC, Birch Room; Event Desk” and has the wrong date. **N2**, dated 09:55, exactly matches Q1; Ash approves N2 at 10:00. **N3**, dated 10:15, says “Mapping Night, 20 October, 18:00 UTC, Birch Room; Event Desk.” N3 has a newer timestamp but its title change has not been approved. Initially the delivery contains no version-bound approval card. The receiver cannot safely infer approval from recency.

The permitted proposed card fields are **request ID; exact notice ID; status; approving role and time; correction route**. The correction route is back to Lena for a draft and Ash for approval before Ren publishes. Ash, Lena and Ren must agree the change; no one has yet accepted extra work. Treat a “drafts completed” metric as a possible incentive mismatch, not proof that Lena deliberately caused the problem.

## Map, confirm and test the interface

1. Trace Q1 through writing, approval, publication and correction. Put owners, required inputs, outputs and readiness at each boundary. Output: a usable flow plus the approval-window constraint and an explicitly hypothetical incentive effect.
2. Ask Ren what is missing and compare the proposed card with simply selecting the newest file. Check the source facts and Ash's authority. Output: a receiver-confirmed need, not a unilateral process diagnosis.
3. Seek agreement from all affected owners and bind the approval status to the exact notice ID. Preserve N3 as draft and the required correction review. Output: a permitted small change with known added burden and no bypass.
4. Save the initial card before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Test a card missing its version and then the corrected handoff. Output: receiver selection or hold, actual readiness and correction route without author rescue.
5. Check writer, approver and receiver effort and accept maintenance explicitly. Output: a whole-handoff result with limitations, rather than a claim that your own step became faster or the notice was actually published.

## Support and transfer

Use three role cards and paper files, spoken labels or an accessible diagram. A harder case changes Q1 after Ash's window closes. Hold publication or renegotiate through the proper owner; a deadline does not make unapproved text approved. Escalation here means raising a dependency, not seizing authority.

Real transfer requires permission and the actual organization's controls. Keep private records, credentials, internal sensitive structures and client material outside app evidence. High-consequence engineering or operational interfaces require their formal processes. The paper example can expose a handoff concept but cannot establish live reliability, throughput or organization-wide change.

## Scope and evidence boundary

The canonical record has no professional_boundary field. The practical limits in this guide are exercise safeguards, not added canonical metadata.

Canonical applicability is context_sensitive. Paid employment, a career change or a particular work role is not compulsory. Normative status remains cross_tradition_core_or_broadly_recurrent. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Keep private or protected records outside app evidence.
