# 20.13 — Propaganda, polarization, and democratic resilience

Analyze three invented public messages and choose a response that preserves evidence and dignity. Allow twenty minutes for analysis and fifteen for a private response rehearsal. Use the supplied case; you do not need to seek out hate content, disclose a political position or engage a hostile person. The result is a responsible information decision, not a diagnosis of the sender or proof of an organized influence operation.

## Recognize the mechanism before judging the side

A claim, a rhetorical tactic and a sharing decision are different objects. A speaker can raise a real problem through manipulative language. A calm message can be false, while an angry complaint can be supported by evidence. Inspect the proposition and the method separately. Calling a message propaganda without showing its mechanism simply replaces one label with another.

Scapegoating assigns broad responsibility to a group without showing who did what. Dehumanizing rhetoric denies people equal dignity or treats them as disposable rather than addressing conduct. Conspiratorial closure makes a belief immune to correction: contrary evidence becomes further proof of the hidden plot. Actual collusion can exist, but a serious allegation needs checkable evidence and possible disconfirmation. Mere suspicion or a claim that “they would hide the evidence” does not supply it.

Manufactured outrage can exploit missing context, urgency and social reward to make forwarding feel like a moral obligation. A leader-centered message can replace accountable decision procedures with personal loyalty. Epistemic capture occurs when every acceptable source must already belong to the same trusted circle and outside correction is disallowed. Agreement among those repeating one assertion does not open that circle. These mechanisms can occur together, but their presence alone does not establish the sender's intent, a coordinated campaign or an authoritarian regime.

Democratic resilience includes habits that leave room for challenge: equal standing, reasons that others can inspect, plural sources, correction, disagreement and accountable authority. None requires treating every assertion as equally supported. Preserve a person's dignity while declining a weak claim or an unsafe exchange. An ordinary mistake should not become evidence that a whole group is incapable of participating.

Apply the same test when the proposed outcome appeals to you. Privately replace a fictional group's label with another invented label that you favor. Does a demand for unquestioning loyalty suddenly seem acceptable? Identify a fact that could revise your view and a relevant source allowed to disagree. This is a consistency test, not a strategy for persuading an audience. Do not publish the manipulative rewrite.

A useful response narrows attention to a checkable issue and a relevant source. It can acknowledge uncertainty, reject collective blame and offer a calm correction. It can also be a decision not to amplify. If you already shared an unsupported claim, address the same audience proportionately. The aim is not to humiliate a sender, force a confession or remain in a fruitless exchange indefinitely.

## Worked example

A booking change prompts an assertion that a whole club controls the venue. The analyst separates the verifiable question—why the booking changed—from the unsupported attribution. They ask for the dated booking and maintenance record, then remove a mocking sentence from their reply. Even if the schedule was mishandled, that would not establish group guilt or justify denying members a voice.

## Complete fictional message packet

Cedar Hall has changed two of twelve bookings. No cause is initially supplied. Three invented posts circulate privately within the case:

- A: “Everyone from Group Z is the problem; their concerns should not count.”
- B: “Any record contradicting this story proves how deep the cover-up goes.”
- C: “Only our leader can fix the hall. Share now; every outside source is against us.”

Group Z has no real identity or protected characteristic. No actual person or platform is involved. The only initial fact is the two changed bookings; their causes, responsible decisions and fairness remain unknown. Use fields for exact concern, tactic, evidence excluded, demanded action and a more accountable alternative.

## Analyze, swap and choose an information action

1. Mark each mechanism in its actual words and separate it from the booking question. Identify what would count as evidence about the decision. Output: three specific analyses rather than a verdict about a group's character.
2. Test one post privately with an invented favored label. Keep the standard unchanged. Name one revising fact and an independent record you would permit to challenge your belief. Output: a consistency check with no targeted audience or dissemination.
3. Draft an accountable alternative focused on dates, decision authority and reasons. Remove blame that exceeds evidence and avoid restating inflammatory language unnecessarily. Output: a short draft or reasoned non-amplification decision.
4. Save the draft before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Revise in response to its limited record, keeping any unresolved fairness question open. Output: the supported finding and a statement of what it does not show.
5. State your actual sharing and engagement status. End an unproductive exchange without insult; if a real prior error exists, correct it through an appropriate route. Output: a true action record, not assumed persuasion or public resilience.

## Support and transfer

Use one message at a time, read it aloud or replace writing with a three-column card. A harder fictional case gives a correct booking complaint alongside collective blame; retain the complaint while rejecting the tactic. Do not make a learner's distress or tolerance for hateful material the difficulty setting.

A real transfer can use a benign public claim without naming private people. You may stop, mute or seek appropriate support where there is harassment or a threat. Do not infer ideology from sharing behavior, investigate individuals or build a profile of a group. Fiction can exercise scrutiny but does not establish a real person's resistance to manipulation over time.
