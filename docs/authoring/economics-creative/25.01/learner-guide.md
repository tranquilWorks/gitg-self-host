# 25.01 — Regular creative production

Finish a small piece on three ordinary days and learn which conditions make returning possible. The default is a six-sentence scene about a missing ordinary object, with each session capped at fifteen minutes. Plan all three within seven days and allow ten minutes afterward for comparison. Creative cultivation is optional. The exercise concerns making and returning, not earning an identity as an artist.

## Give the work a small finish line

A bounded piece gives a session an endpoint that can be recognized without an audience or a perfect idea. Six sentences can establish an object, a place, a change and a response. They need not contain a dramatic revelation or autobiographical material. Stop when the scene is complete within the agreed time; do not turn an ordinary missed session into a debt that demands a marathon.

The Open University's [selected fiction-writing material, S14](../SOURCES.md#S14) discusses notebooks, concrete details and the possibilities of ordinary actions and settings. It does not validate this three-session schedule as a habit intervention. This companion supplies original examples and a practical recording method. Reading three examples in one sitting is not evidence that you made work on three days.

Choose a freely selected medium and a finishable unit. The default uses writing, but a short original melody, arrangement, design sketch or accessible dictated scene can serve the same making-and-returning purpose when its finish line is explicit. Keep the unit manageable and materials familiar. You do not need to purchase tools, perform publicly or disclose private experiences.

## Supplied plan P8

The fictional maker chooses Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 7:10 p.m., with a fifteen-minute maximum. The materials are one existing notebook and a pen, or an accessible text entry method. The prompt is “an ordinary object is unexpectedly missing”: use a cup for session one, a key for session two and a chair for session three. Each scene must differ in viewpoint, setting or mood; a single piece copied three times would not provide three making attempts.

The initial plan leaves the notebook beside the chosen seat, opens to the next page and writes the prompt before the session. Its purpose is to reduce the next start to one visible action. If the time becomes unavailable, record that fact and offer one feasible replacement. The user of the plan may decline, shorten or stop it without a negative judgment about worth.

The record has planned day, actual day, time used, finished artifact, starting obstacle and one next adjustment. It does not require a mood score, streak display or proof of enthusiasm. A completed piece can be awkward. A thoughtful unfinished attempt can still reveal a constraint, but should remain marked unfinished rather than renamed complete.

## First supplied scene: the cup

These six sentences are an original teaching specimen, not an actual learner artifact:

> I opened the kitchen cupboard and found a pale circle where the blue cup usually stood.\
> Behind me, the kettle clicked itself quiet.\
> I checked the drying rack, then the shelf where we kept the chipped bowls.\
> A folded towel rose and fell on the table as if something underneath it were breathing.\
> I lifted one corner and found the cup warming beside a small covered loaf.\
> Whoever had set breakfast out had left my place ready.

The scene gives the missing object an ordinary use and changes its meaning through a discovery. The kettle provides an action outside the search; the towel suggests movement before the simple explanation. The ending closes this small unit without explaining a whole household history. You may find the breathing comparison too dramatic for the setting; that is a legitimate later revision question, not a reason the first session cannot finish.

The supplied first session takes **15 minutes** and produces these six sentences. The maker records a concrete obstacle: several minutes were lost looking for the previous draft. The next adjustment is to leave the notebook at the correct page. That is more usable than a general pledge to become disciplined. It does not prove that storage was the only barrier or that the next session will occur.

## Make before evaluating the whole practice

During your first actual writing session, use the cup and a place you can invent safely. Write an opening that establishes the absence, then give the viewpoint something to do. Finish with a changed situation or a deliberate unresolved choice. If the time limit arrives first, save exactly what exists and record whether your chosen unit was finished. A shorter accessible unit may be appropriate for a later attempt; do not retroactively alter the agreed finish line to disguise the result.

For sessions two and three, use the key and chair respectively, changing one meaningful condition while keeping the scale small. A new viewpoint might notice different information; a different setting changes what actions are possible; a humorous mood changes timing. This provides a small comparison across artifacts without requiring every piece to be novel in every respect. Keep your original drafts so that revision does not erase evidence of how the session went.

After each session, name one detail worth keeping and one process observation. “The object turns up” describes plot; “the search passes three specific places before the reveal” describes a choice. “I am uncreative” is not a useful process observation. “I spent five minutes selecting a new app and had two minutes left to write” points to a condition that can be changed.

## Complete the three-day record

1. Choose three ordinary session opportunities, a unit and a maximum burden. Name the medium, where work will be saved and the first visible action.
2. Make the first piece, save it unchanged and record the actual day and duration. Compare it with the cup example only after your own attempt.
3. On later ordinary days, make two further pieces with at least one different viewpoint, setting or mood. Do not compress three days into one session to make the record look complete.
4. Keep a missed session visible and choose a feasible response. The [later packet](later-packet.md) supplies two more original scenes and an interrupted schedule for analysis; these do not substitute for your own attempts.
5. Compare all three artifacts for one repeated strength, one recurring obstacle and one manageable next making commitment. An actual incomplete sequence remains incomplete, with its constraints recorded.

For the supplied case, a modest next commitment is a five-minute scene on the following Tuesday at 7:10 p.m., using the same notebook already open and choosing one possible ending before starting. This choice follows the observed difficulty finding work and the later recurring hesitation over endings, not an assumption that more output is always better. The maker can also stop after the three-session trial. Repetition here is an invitation to build a usable practice, not a demand to optimize all free time.

## Read the evidence at its proper scale

Three actual artifacts on three recorded days would show repeated production under those conditions. They would not establish a permanent habit, artistic excellence, public value or a new identity. A self-reported schedule without work attached cannot show that production occurred. Conversely, a polished sample with no session record cannot establish consistency across time.

For support, prepare the place and object before the session and dictate or use a familiar communication method. For a harder pass, hold the six-sentence limit while varying the viewpoint rather than enlarging the project. Ask for audience response only if someone freely agrees and it serves your chosen purpose. Private work, a missed day or no attempt remains a legitimate outcome.

The useful question is concrete: what made this small act of making possible, and what would make the next return manageable? Answer from the artifacts and the record rather than waiting for inspiration to certify that you may begin.
