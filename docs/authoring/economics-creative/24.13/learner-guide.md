# 24.13 — Responsible innovation and technological change

Decide whether a small tool should be used at all, then define the evidence needed before expansion. Allow thirty minutes for impact mapping and the bounded paper trial, followed by twenty for stop-rule review. The supplied case contains useful output and consequential errors. A justified pause is a successful exercise outcome.

## Anticipate effects beyond the attractive feature

Innovation can make a task easier while creating new work, exposing information, excluding users or shifting power. Assessment needs benefits, displacement, safety, privacy, environmental effects, accessibility and distribution. Ask who gains time, who checks mistakes, who loses a useful role, who maintains the tool and who can stop its use. A faster draft is only one part of that account.

UKRI EPSRC's [responsible-innovation framework, S10](../SOURCES.md#S10) organizes reflection around anticipating, reflecting, engaging and acting. Here those ideas support a concrete pre-release decision. The source does not validate this tool or certify compliance. The toy outputs below are authored fiction; no external service, account, model or real participant data is required.

A reversible paper rehearsal is appropriate because the tool's claim can be checked against a small approved fact set. Do not use a real audience as an involuntary test. In an actual authorized trial, affected people need a meaningful way to question the proposal, and the person doing repair work must agree to that burden. Stop authority should be assigned before enthusiasm makes the decision harder.

## Supplied reminder-drafting proposal R8

The proposed tool creates short event reminders from approved information. Its intended benefit is reduced drafting time for a coordinator. The manual alternative produces each reminder from the same approved card and takes **three minutes per card**, or **nine minutes for three**. That timing is a supplied comparison under fixed conditions, not measured workplace productivity.

| Card | Approved facts | Supplied tool output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Reading circle; Tuesday; 6 p.m.; Room A | Reading circle: Tuesday at 6 p.m. in Room A. |
| R2 | Repair discussion; Wednesday; time not yet confirmed; Room B | Repair discussion: Wednesday at 7 p.m. in Room B. |
| R3 | Drawing hour; Thursday; 5 p.m.; latest approved location Room D, replacing Room C | Drawing hour: Thursday at 5 p.m. in Room C. |

R1 matches its card. R2 invents a time, and R3 uses an obsolete room. These are not merely stylistic differences. A recipient might arrive at the wrong time or place. All three outputs already exist as fictional paper specimens; none has been released. Discovering R2's failure pauses release of the entire proposed set. Examining the already-supplied R3 specimen afterward is failure analysis, not continued deployment.

The proposed use is limited to approved nonsensitive facts, a readable text version and a human comparison against the current card. No personal attendee data, private contact information or automatic sending is allowed by the case. A recipient who needs another format must have a usable route; the presence of text alone is not proof of accessibility. No particular accessibility standard has been tested here.

## Set the gate before counting a benefit

Coordinator Sal owns the paper trial and its record. Sal can stop it immediately. The release condition is a correct current time and place, explicit uncertainty where a fact is missing, a confirmed accessible format for the intended audience, and authorized human review. Any exposed private data or uncorrected consequential falsehood at the release check stops use. In this case the R2 error triggers the gate before any delivery.

The gate does not mean errors may be casually sent and repaired later. Reworking a draft is allowed offline, but a repaired sample is not evidence of reliable independent operation. The manual method stays available. Expansion would require a new authorized decision supported by a fresh trial and affected-user evidence, not merely absence of complaint from people who never saw the output.

Count the full supplied effort. Draft generation takes one minute per card, **three total**. Checking takes one, two and three minutes, **six total**. Repair takes zero, two and two minutes, **four total**. Combined effort is **13 minutes**, compared with nine manually: **four more minutes**. The three-minute generation step is not a six-minute net saving. There are two initial substantive errors among three specimens, with too few cases to estimate a dependable long-run error rate.

Corrected R2 reads: “Repair discussion: Wednesday in Room B; time to be confirmed.” Corrected R3 reads: “Drawing hour: Thursday at 5 p.m. in Room D.” These repairs demonstrate what current facts support. They do not establish that the tool will use the right source version next time or that anyone received the reminder.

## Include the people and costs outside the demo

A fictional coordinator response says the review interruptions are harder to fit into the day than ordinary manual drafting. Record that supplied perspective alongside the time total. An intended reader asks for larger text and a version that does not depend on an image; the requested format has not yet been evaluated by that reader. Do not convert the request into an accessibility pass.

Displacement is uncertain: the proposal might shift a coordinator's work from composing to checking, while someone else inherits troubleshooting. Maintenance needs an owner, available time and a way to retire obsolete templates. Energy, equipment and environmental effects are unmeasured; the paper exercise does not establish lower computing impact. Benefits and burdens could be distributed unevenly even if an average time improved.

The provisional decision is **pause**. Revise the tool specification to preserve missing facts visibly and require the latest approved location version. Keep a small error log with card identifier, consequence, repair owner and unresolved cause. Because the cases contain no actual private data, record privacy controls as design requirements rather than a tested privacy result. Avoid collecting new personal information simply to make the evaluation look thorough.

## Produce the stop-or-proceed record

1. State the task, intended benefit, manual alternative and affected roles. Map all seven effect areas, including unknown environmental and distributional consequences.
2. Save the approved cards and the three unedited outputs. Check each claim against its current source and mark R2 and R3 errors separately.
3. Apply the prewritten release gate at R2. Calculate all drafting, checking and repair time; explain why the trial does not demonstrate a net saving.
4. Record the supplied coordinator response and reader request as fiction. Write a revised specification, stop owner, observation plan and conditions for a new authorized rehearsal.
5. Open the [later packet](later-packet.md). Update the decision using its incomplete result; do not treat one repaired output as a deployment approval.

For support, compare time and place in a two-column sheet before mapping the wider effects. For a harder pass, double review time while keeping draft speed constant and explain how the decision changes. A no-tool choice is legitimate. Actual progress would require an artifact plus independent observations of a permitted trial, including whose work and access changed. A hypothetical mitigation list alone does not show responsible deployment.
