# 24.10 — Public finance, taxation, and redistribution

Evaluate a small public-budget proposal with its tax base, behavioral response and distributional burden visible. Allow thirty minutes for the budget and sensitivity calculation, followed by twenty minutes for distribution review. You will make a conditional recommendation rather than approve a tax, borrowing decision or public expenditure.

## A balanced total does not answer every public question

Public finance asks what is funded, who contributes, who benefits and what commitments continue. A tax base is the activity or value to which a tax rate applies. Revenue can change if that base changes, even when the rate stays fixed. Administration also consumes resources, while a spending program can create maintenance obligations beyond its first period.

The IMF's [fiscal discussion, S04](../SOURCES.md#S04) connects taxation, spending and financing. Its [externalities and public-goods discussion, S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) distinguishes benefits that are difficult to exclude people from and can be shared without reducing another person's use. A nonrival benefit can be used by another person without reducing the benefit available to someone else; nonexcludability concerns difficulty preventing access. Not every publicly funded service is a pure public good. A crowded facility can have capacity limits; a broadcast notice may be widely shared at low marginal cost. Identify the actual access and provision problem instead of using “public good” as praise.

A one-period surplus means modeled revenue exceeds modeled spending during that period. A deficit is the opposite flow. Accumulated debt is a stock shaped by previous borrowing and repayment, among other factors. A surplus in one small project does not establish that the whole government has no debt, and a deficit does not by itself show that the spending is wasteful or sustainable. The future obligations and financing conditions matter.

## Supplied local project T8

A fictional local authority proposes a public information service. It would provide a freely readable noticeboard and an optional assisted explanation period. The noticeboard can benefit people who do not pay the levy; the explanation period has limited staff capacity. Existing law, authority to levy, exemptions, privacy rules and actual community support are deliberately unspecified.

The taxable base is **1000 units** and the proposed rate **10%**. Program spending is **80**, administration **10**, and no maintenance beyond this period is included. No debt stock, interest payment or other public revenue is supplied.

| Scenario | Tax base | Rate | Revenue | Program plus administration | Period balance |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Original | 1000 | 10% | 100 | 90 | 10 |
| Behavioral response | 900 | 10% | 90 | 90 | 0 |

The second base is an assumed scenario, not a measured prediction of avoidance or reduced activity. It could reflect several behaviors or accounting changes in a real case, which would need investigation. The fixed rate alone cannot tell us which base is likely. Missing maintenance is a real limit in both rows, not evidence that it will cost nothing.

## Separate the legal collector from the economic burden

In a **separate incidence illustration**, a ten-unit charge is legally collected from a seller. The supplied hypothetical price response leaves **six units borne by buyers and four by the seller**. The split totals ten but does not measure this project's levy or determine another market's pass-through. The St. Louis Fed's [incidence discussion, S05](../SOURCES.md#S05) explains why the party remitting a tax need not bear all its burden. Only that introductory distinction is used; the article's argument about negative rates is not adopted.

A buyer unable to switch products may face a different burden from one with an easy substitute. A seller might absorb lower returns, change staffing or change output; these are possible channels requiring evidence, not instructions to cut wages. Economic incidence should therefore be estimated from actual market responses rather than assumed from the tax form. Equity analysis also asks who receives the service and who cannot access it despite paying.

The 6/4 illustration has no supplied household incomes, demand elasticities or long-term effects. Do not call it progressive or regressive without the relevant distributional information. A levy with equal nominal payments may impose unequal proportional burdens; a benefit may offset those burdens for some people and not others. State the principle being used, such as ability to pay or access to a basic service, and allow disagreement about its weight.

## Compare a concrete phased alternative

Alternative P would provide the noticeboard first for **60 program units** plus the same **10 administration**, postponing the assisted explanation period. At a base of 900 and the unchanged 10% rate, revenue 90 less cost 70 leaves **20** for the period. The change creates more financial room but sacrifices staffed access for people who cannot use the board independently. It also postpones rather than resolves the maintenance question.

Compare the proposals across five dimensions: adequacy of the service, administrative complexity, distribution of benefits and burdens, behavioral incentives, and future financing. The original offers broader access but has no modeled balance at the smaller base. The phased version spends less immediately but may exclude intended users and require later expansion costs. Neither is a free improvement.

A conditional recommendation can favor the original only if its funding and accessible delivery can be confirmed, or propose a revised phase with a costed access route. If those conditions are unresolved, defer the commitment and ask the appropriate institution for the missing facts. A learner's preference does not create the authority to levy, borrow or spend.

## Produce the policy comparison

1. Reconcile revenue, program cost, administration and balance for both tax bases. List maintenance, debt and financing information that the table omits.
2. Explain who can use each service and where nonexclusion, free-riding or limited capacity affects provision. Do not imply that every resident has the same access.
3. Work the separate ten-unit incidence example and identify what evidence would be required to estimate a real split. Keep legal collection and economic burden distinct.
4. Compare the original and phased proposal using all five dimensions. Name the sacrificed benefit and whose perspective is missing before recommending either.
5. Set a monitoring trigger: reopen the proposal if the base falls below 900 or intended users cannot obtain the promised explanation. Save the first decision and open the [later packet](later-packet.md).

For support, use ten counters for each ten-percent revenue block and separate counters for program and administration. For a harder pass, add a targeted offset with its own processing cost and identify who could miss it. Actual policy analysis needs current official fiscal information, lawful authority, qualified review and affected-community input. This model supplies none of those approvals. A well-reasoned comparison should survive a changed base and a legitimate competing equity concern without becoming an ideological loyalty test.
