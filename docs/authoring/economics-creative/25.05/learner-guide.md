# 25.05 — Originality through synthesis

Make an invitation in which influences help solve the same communication problem. Allow twenty minutes to map influences, thirty to make and fifteen to review. The work is eight lines long and fictional. Its aim is an inviting tone with clear time, place and access, not proof of legal originality or a reason to imitate a recognizable artist.

## Translate influences into functions

The three supplied influences are the rhythm of an ordinary knock, the structure of a clear event notice and the image of a light left on for someone arriving late. They can contribute pacing, practical clarity and welcome. Listing them beside one another is not yet synthesis. They begin to interact when a rhythm must accommodate a necessary fact, or an image changes how a late arrival is addressed.

You may tap an ordinary comfortable rhythm, mark it silently or use the supplied pattern of two short beats followed by a pause. Record whether a pattern is actually your observation or a supplied prompt. Do not claim you heard a real knock when you only read its description. No external music, poem, image or artist's wording is needed for this exercise.

The Creative Commons [considerations for licensors and licensees, S17](../SOURCES.md#S17) support checking rights, license conditions and the intended use of external material. Credit alone does not give permission, and one license does not settle every third-party right. This exercise uses original teaching prompts and newly authored text; the source was not used to clear a particular artwork or provide a legal originality determination.

If you choose external influences in an actual attempt, identify what you are taking: a general structural idea, an observed pattern or protected expression. Check permission and conditions for the particular reuse before including it. Cultural or restricted material may also require appropriate community context. A long list of sources does not turn copied wording into an original work.

## Supplied invitation brief I8

The fictional neighborhood reading evening takes place **Friday, 7–8 p.m., in Juniper Room at Cedar Hall**. The **east entrance has a step-free route** to the room. People may bring a page or attend just to listen, arrive during the stated hour, and leave early. These are invented event facts for the exercise, not a real venue's accessibility information or an invitation to a live event. The case assumes the route stated on the card; no actual site has been inspected.

The eight-line limit is part of the making problem. Put essential facts inside the invitation, rather than hiding them in an explanation readers would not receive. A warm metaphor cannot substitute for a time or an entrance. Welcome also needs freedom to decline: participation, sharing a page and staying the full hour are optional in this fictional brief.

| Influence | Function | Decision it should affect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two short beats and a pause | A compact opening followed by room to continue | Vary line length without losing necessary information |
| Clear event notice | Orient the reader to purpose, time, place and access | Reserve enough space for accurate facts and an unambiguous entrance |
| Light for a late arrival | Make welcome concrete | Address late arrival without pressure or an implied obligation to attend |

The supplied author has not observed an actual knock. The pattern, notice function, light image and event card are teaching inputs. The wording and arrangement below are new work made for this case. Keep those categories visible in the influence note; honest debts are compatible with synthesis.

## Make a complete eight-line draft

Fictional draft:

> Two quick knocks.\
> Then room to breathe.\
> Friday, 7–8 p.m., a neighborhood reading evening.\
> Juniper Room, Cedar Hall.\
> Use the east entrance for the step-free route to our room.\
> Bring a page, or come to listen.\
> We leave a light for anyone arriving during the hour.\
> Come if you wish; leaving early is welcome.

The first two lines imitate the supplied rhythm literally, then the longer notice line introduces practical information. The fifth line is longer than the opening pattern would suggest. That is deliberate: the entrance and route are essential to access. Do not remove “east” or “to our room” just to make the rhythm tidy. The seventh line ties the image to arrival within the stated hour instead of implying an unlimited opening time.

This draft has a weakness. “Two quick knocks” could be read as an entry instruction rather than a pacing cue. It also reproduces the prompt's surface image instead of transforming its function. A reader should not need the author's commentary to know whether knocking is required. The weak line is worth revising even though all three influences are present.

## Resolve the conflict through the actual problem

The useful question is what the opening should do for a person deciding whether to attend. It can suggest a small amount of reading and room to pause, while the notice supplies logistics and the light suggests welcome. The rhythm can survive without mentioning a knock. That turns a borrowed-looking surface feature into a functional choice.

The revised eight-line invitation is:

> A page.\
> A pause.\
> Friday, 7–8 p.m., a neighborhood reading evening.\
> Juniper Room, Cedar Hall.\
> Use the east entrance for the step-free route to our room.\
> Bring a page, or come to listen.\
> We leave a light for anyone arriving during the hour.\
> Come if you wish; leaving early is welcome.

The two short opening lines preserve the two-beat pattern, but now point to the event's activity and unhurried tone. The necessary longer access line still interrupts the rhythm. This is an integration decision: the notice's clarity constrains the rhythm, and the welcome image stays within the notice's time boundary. The influences affect one another rather than appearing as three decorations.

A remaining weakness is that “a page” appears again in line six. Repetition may create cohesion or feel redundant. Do not automatically remove it: line six performs the practical task of making contribution optional. A later revision could change the opening while keeping the participation choice explicit. The sample is finished within its brief without claiming every phrase is optimal.

## Write an honest influence note

The supplied note reads: “This fictional invitation uses three original teaching prompts: a two-beat rhythm, an event-notice structure and a welcoming light image. The event facts are supplied fiction. I transformed the rhythm into two short opening lines, kept the access instruction despite its length, and limited the light image to the event's stated hour. No external artwork or wording was copied, and no actual knock or venue visit is claimed.”

In an actual attempt, revise that note to match what you really used. Name an observed rhythm only if you observed or made it, and identify external sources and required permissions when they exist. Do not use the sample note as a blanket declaration of ownership or clearance for different material. The finished work can acknowledge its influences without claiming that influence itself is a defect.

## Produce the synthesis-and-review record

1. Map each influence to its function and state the actual invitation problem. Distinguish supplied prompts, your observations and any borrowed expression.
2. Make an eight-line draft containing purpose, time, place, access and voluntary participation. Keep the event facts visibly fictional in the surrounding record.
3. Identify a conflict between influences and preserve the practical information when resolving it. Save the draft before revising.
4. Ask a willing reader about coherence and confusing or derivative elements, or return after a break. The [later packet](later-packet.md) supplies an incomplete reception case, not actual feedback on your work.
5. Transform one weakly integrated element and write the influence note. Explain what function changed and what remains uncertain about reception or permission.

For support, reserve lines three through six for essential facts before shaping the opening and ending. For a harder pass, change the fictional access instruction and rework the rhythm without dropping information. An accessible spoken or graphic arrangement can serve the same three functions with a clearly defined equivalent constraint. Sharing is optional, and no real invitation is sent by this exercise.

Progress would combine the made artifact, a traceable revision and evidence of reception at its proper scale. Three named influences alone do not establish synthesis. The stronger evidence is a visible decision that changes an influence so it serves the same purpose as the others.
