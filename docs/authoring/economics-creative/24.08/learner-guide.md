# 24.08 — Externalities and full-cost accounting

Find a cost that a transaction leaves with someone outside its price, then test a permitted way to reduce it. Allow twenty minutes to map the case, one small permitted mitigation and fifteen minutes for later review. Your output is a benefit-and-burden account that includes affected people rather than a declaration that an event is sustainable.

## Follow the effect beyond the invoice

The organizer receives a room and attendees receive a useful meeting. The price may cover the venue's agreed services while leaving noise, waste or additional demands elsewhere. An external effect matters because another person's opportunity or well-being changes without that effect being fully accounted for in the decision. Not every cost outside the organizer's ledger is an externality: an agreed, adequately priced service is part of the transaction. A wage paid to a worker does not prove that unexpected extra work has no opportunity cost.

The IMF's [externalities explanation, S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) distinguishes private and wider costs and benefits. Its examples do not measure this meeting. A full-cost account can begin with separate quantities such as minutes, discarded sheets and access consequences. Do not add unlike units or assign a money value to another person's time without a justified basis. Unknown costs should remain unknown rather than conveniently zero.

Positive effects also belong in the map. An open meeting might produce a useful public explanation that helps nonattendees. That benefit needs its own evidence; saying it could happen is not the same as observing it. Considering spillovers in both directions makes the analysis more accurate without excusing an avoidable burden.

## Supplied meeting file X4

Twenty people attend a fictional local-history discussion. The organizer pays **60 units** for a room, with ten minutes of ordinary closing work included in the agreed venue service. The organizer prints **30 one-page handouts** without checking demand. Eight are taken, leaving **22 unused sheets**. Venue coordinator Leni reports **18 additional minutes** sorting the abandoned materials beyond the ten-minute included closing task. The extra work delays preparation for another booking; its price or staffing adjustment was not agreed.

Leni is authorized to approve the meeting setup and speaks to the venue burden within this fictional case. No actual staff member was interviewed. Participants have different access needs: eight want paper, while others can use an existing readable digital copy or listen. No one has agreed to bring a device, wash shared items or volunteer for cleanup.

| Affected party | Benefit or agreed exchange | Additional effect or uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organizer and attendees | Room access and discussion for the 60-unit fee | Printing effort and access to the material need checking |
| Venue workers and next group | Ten minutes of closing work is already included | Extra sorting delays the next group's preparation |
| Nearby residents | No direct role in the transaction | Noise after closing is possible but not observed in this file |
| Public service | Existing waste collection serves the area | Incremental handling and disposal cost are unknown |
| Future people and environment | Resources remain available when used carefully | Paper, energy and waste impacts are unmeasured |
| Nonattendees | A public summary might be useful | No summary use or wider learning benefit has been recorded |

The evidence supports extra sorting as a concrete priority. It does not establish that neighbors were disturbed, that all discarded material was recyclable or that the room fee was unfair. Keep a report, an inference and an unmeasured possibility in different fields.

## Compare remedies before assigning somebody work

One option is to ask which formats people want and print only ten copies: eight requested and two spare. This preserves paper access and may reduce abandoned material. The organizer accepts up to four extra preparation minutes to count requests. Leni agrees to the setup and a later sorting-time check. The digital copy remains optional and uses an existing permitted channel; no new signup or attendee data collection is needed.

A second option is a reusable display and shared materials. It might reduce paper but would require an accessible sight line and an agreed person to arrange and clean the materials. The proposed helper has not consented, and actual cleaning time is unknown. A third option is paying for an additional agreed venue service; its price and staff capacity need confirmation. Paying can account for a burden, but does not necessarily remove the waste itself.

Choose the first option for the supplied trial because it addresses the reported source of sorting without removing the requested paper format. The decision is conditional on availability of the same readable material, no access complaint and the organizer's four-minute capacity. It is not authority to impose a fee, assign a volunteer or change public infrastructure.

## Work the initial resource account

Reducing planned printing from 30 to ten would mean **20 fewer sheets produced for the meeting**, if that plan is carried out. It would not by itself establish 20 fewer sheets sent to landfill, a measured carbon reduction or a net environmental benefit. Printing method, transport, reuse and disposal pathways are not supplied.

The ten-minute included closing task stays in the account on both occasions. It must not be relabeled as a newly discovered external burden just to make the intervention look larger. Compare the same additional sorting task before and after. Keep organizer preparation as an added cost even if it is willingly undertaken; consent changes who agrees to bear work, not whether that work exists.

## Produce the full-cost decision record

1. Record the room fee, included service and private benefit. Map at least the six groups in X4, distinguishing observed burden from possible effects and agreed costs.
2. Name Leni's reported priority and the limits of that perspective. In an actual attempt, ask the appropriate affected contact rather than deciding their needs for them.
3. Compare the three remedies for access, resources, new labor, consent and cost. Explain why a narrower printing change is feasible while the reusable option remains unapproved.
4. Write the owner, next-meeting timing, ten-copy limit and observation method. Save an initial forecast with unknown effects still visible, then open the [later packet](later-packet.md).
5. Reconcile the later count and time record before choosing retain, revise or stop. Name one larger question, such as how venue agreements should price extra services, that this local trial cannot settle.

For support, use just included closing time, additional sorting time and organizer preparation as separate counters. For a harder pass, make two more attendees require paper and revise the print limit without treating access as waste. A real attempt requires an ordinary permitted event and willing participation. An observation-only map is legitimate when there is no authority to change a setup.

Progress would combine an actual decision artifact with a later observation of the originally identified burden. One visible cleanup improvement cannot prove complete social accounting, fair labor conditions or environmental benefit. The strongest result may be a more explicit agreement about remaining work rather than a claim that all costs have disappeared.
