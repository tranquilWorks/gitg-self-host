# 24.11 — Social insurance and welfare-state design

Design support around a risk, the people who can actually reach it, and a credible financing account. Allow thirty minutes for the pool and design comparison, plus twenty for an access stress test. The output is a design comparison and an access improvement, not a judgment about who deserves care.

## Name the risk before choosing the program

Unemployment support addresses an interruption in earnings; health protection addresses access to care and its costs; disability support may address extra costs, barriers or reduced earnings; retirement provision addresses income across later life; family support may address care and child-related costs. These risks overlap without being interchangeable. A program can pay a benefit promptly and still leave its recipient unable to meet the particular need it was intended to cover.

The ILO's [social-protection overview, S06](../SOURCES.md#S06) describes life-course risks and contributory and tax-financed arrangements. This exercise uses a fictional pool rather than any country's benefit rules. It does not establish entitlement, a filing deadline or a recommended contribution rate. Coverage, benefit size and funding are separate design decisions.

A contributory arrangement connects eligibility to specified contributions or insurance conditions. A universal program includes everyone in a defined population under its stated rules, not necessarily everyone everywhere. A means-tested program uses a specified income or resource test. Real systems often combine these approaches. Universal coverage does not make funding unnecessary, and a resource test does not automatically identify every person in need.

## Supplied pool S8

S8 models temporary earnings loss associated with unemployment. The exact covered-event and eligibility tests remain unspecified, so this label does not decide N's entitlement. There are **200 participants**, each contributing **10 units** for one fictional period. Income is **2,000**. In the initial scenario, **20 valid claims** each receive **80**, costing **1,600**. Administration costs **200**, leaving **200**. No investment return, external subsidy, starting reserve or future liability is supplied.

The stress scenario has **35 valid claims** at the same benefit and administration cost: **2,800 + 200 = 3,000**, leaving a **1,000 shortfall**. A positive initial balance does not establish long-term sustainability. Nor does the shortfall prove that recipients should receive less; contribution changes, broader financing, reserves and revised commitments have different distributional and practical consequences.

One applicant's stated necessary loss is **110 units** for the covered period. Even if the full 80 arrives on time, **30 remains uncovered**. Adequacy therefore differs from whether the program can pay its promises. The loss is a supplied case assumption, not a needs assessment of a real person. Do not generalize one applicant's costs to all participants.

## Compare three designs on the same questions

| Design | Access and information | Financing and incentives | Dignity and practical limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contributory pool | Explain required contribution history and covered event; interrupted work may leave gaps | The S8 arithmetic applies only to this pool; pooling does not remove correlated losses | Obtain necessary records once where permitted; contribution history is not a measure of human worth |
| Universal support within a defined population | Membership rules still need clear communication and an accessible application or enrollment route | Identify the tax or other funding base; do not import S8's 200 contributions as its budget | Fewer resource tests may reduce disclosure, but language, identity documentation and payment barriers can remain |
| Means-tested support | State which resources and periods count, and how variable earnings are handled | Benefit withdrawal may change the gain from additional earnings; the actual schedule is needed to assess it | Repeated sensitive documentation can burden applicants; verify relevant facts without treating poverty as suspicion |

None wins every column by definition. Compare adequacy, access, incentives, dignity and sustainability explicitly. For example, a simpler process may improve take-up but require administrative investment; a sharp eligibility cutoff may create different incentives from a gradual withdrawal. Do not assert a benefits cliff without the rules that produce one. A larger nominal benefit may be less useful if it arrives after the relevant expense or cannot be accessed.

## Follow a complete application journey

Applicant N has variable earnings, a disability-related access need and limited capacity for repeated forms. N needs an accessible appointment format and has already supplied one relevant earnings record. N's contribution history and entitlement are not established. The fictional program card assigns the service desk responsibility for information and assisted submission, an assessment team responsibility for verification and decisions, a payment team responsibility for disbursement, and a separate review contact for appeals. The desk cannot promise approval.

The ordinary journey is information, application, verification, decision, payment and review or appeal if needed. At each stage record what N needs to know, which office owns the next action, what evidence is required and whether the stage has actually occurred. A completed form is not a decision, and an approval letter is not money received. No statutory deadline is supplied; asking for an expected date is different from inventing one.

The current form requests the same earnings record again without explaining whether the first copy was retained. N cannot use the offered appointment format. The proposed improvement is a desk-assisted appointment in N's chosen accessible format, with permission to reuse the existing record for this application if program rules allow. Record the consent and purpose, avoid retaining unrelated documents, and let N choose another route. Necessary verification and an appeal route remain available. Reusing an authenticated record with consent can reduce duplication without removing the control against false or mismatched evidence; whether the retained record is current still needs checking. Track the share of eligible applicants who actually receive payment, the interval from submission to receipt, and whether payment meets the covered need. For N, record the actual receipt date and amount if established; do not invent either from an approval notice.

The desk estimates **40 additional administrative units** for the improvement across the modeled period. If approved, administration becomes **240**. The initial balance would fall to **160**; the stress shortfall would grow to **1,040**. Reduced repeat requests might save time later, but no savings are measured yet. Better access should be evaluated alongside its cost, rather than portrayed as automatically free.

## Produce a defensible improvement record

1. Name the covered risk and distinguish it from the other four risks above. Explain what the 80-unit benefit addresses and what it leaves uncertain.
2. Recalculate initial and stress budgets, including the proposed 40-unit administration change. Keep adequacy and solvency in separate fields.
3. Complete the three-design comparison with one benefit, one burden and one unanswered question per design. Identify who would need to supply missing eligibility, funding and withdrawal rules.
4. Map N's journey and choose the repeated-document and appointment barriers as the intervention. Write the responsible desk role, consent condition, budget decision and next observable stage.
5. Save the proposal, then open the [later packet](later-packet.md). Separate the stages achieved from receipt and adequacy. Revise the next action without asking for unnecessary private history.

For support, use six cards for the journey and move a marker only when a supplied event establishes completion. For a harder pass, add a second applicant with irregular contribution history and explain why one accessible appointment cannot resolve a coverage exclusion. If working with a real public information page, record the jurisdiction and current official route; do not submit a claim or collect another person's records as part of this practice.

Progress would pair an actual design artifact with independent evidence of how an authorized process works for its intended users. A useful access proposal does not demonstrate a functioning welfare state, an individual's entitlement or a sustainable funding settlement. The supplied case permits careful practice while keeping those larger claims open.
