# 12.13 — Grooming, dress, and presentation

## Prepare for participation rather than an appearance verdict

Presentation can support hygiene, comfort, identity and the purpose of an occasion. It does not determine human worth. The useful question is what the person and setting actually need: clean hands, a comfortable layer, usable fasteners, room for an aid, or clarification of a real rule. Brand, attractiveness and an imagined audience are poor substitutes for those requirements.

This guide provides a complete fictional preparation routine for a casual library meeting. Allow 10 minutes to plan, 10 for the later movement/occasion record and 10 for checks. Paper, dictation or labeled item cards suffice. No clothing purchase, new product, photograph, body rating, forced posture or private disclosure is required. The default produces a functional preparation plan and a review of supplied outcomes, not evidence that the learner groomed or attended. Health and exercise content must be adapted to the person and does not replace licensed medical care.

## Translate the occasion into requirements

Hygiene is about the relevant care task and setting, not a demand for fragrance, hair removal, cosmetics or a culturally narrow look. Familiar handwashing can matter after handling dirt or before eating: [CDC describes wetting, lathering, scrubbing, rinsing and drying](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/about/index.html), including at least 20 seconds of scrubbing. That is not the entire routine's elapsed time. Use established care advice for skin or medical concerns; do not introduce an irritating product to meet a cosmetic expectation.

Clothing needs to allow the actual activity and the person's temperature, sensory and identity needs. A seated check can reveal a pressing seam; reaching can reveal restriction that standing still misses. Posture should support comfortable participation with usual positioning aids, not imitate a rigid appearance ideal. Do not force a position or hide disability to pass the exercise.

Distinguish verified requirements from preferences and unsupported assumptions. A venue's actual access or safety instruction deserves a practical response; an unverified “people like us must wear this” claim needs clarification. Cultural dress and gender expression are legitimate choices. If a requirement conflicts with access or identity, use an appropriate accommodation/clarification route rather than issue a legal opinion or blame the person.

## Worked example: the admired outfit does not fit the task

A visitor receives a compliment on a jacket but cannot comfortably reach the tabletop activity while wearing it. A familiar alternative permits the movement. The compliment is evidence that someone expressed approval, not proof that the jacket supports participation. Changing the layer can be useful without rejecting the visitor's identity or buying a new one.

## Complete materials: Amari's 14:00 meeting

Amari is attending a casual library reading group at 14:00. Travel from home to the accessible room takes 8 minutes using the usual aid; all route/access arrangements are already confirmed. Amari is comfortable using this route in the case. Preparation starts at 13:40. Room temperature is expected to be 18°C; Amari usually wants a removable layer there. This is a personal preference in the case, not a temperature standard.

The organizer confirms: casual clothing is welcome, avoid added fragrance in the shared room, and keep bags out of the aisle. There is no brand, formal-dress or body-posture rule. Amari wants to retain a familiar identity-related accessory, which fits comfortably and does not obstruct the activity or aid. A shelf spot for the bag is available. Hands are dirty from potting a plant, so a familiar handwashing step is needed. Soap, running water and a clean towel are already available and suitable for Amari.

| Item | Known state | Decision implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A, prestige shirt | clean; restricts the required seated reach | Status does not repair the movement problem |
| B, familiar shirt | clean; ordinary sitting comfortable; required reach not yet checked | Candidate pending the movement check |
| C, favorite stained shirt | cleaning label requires a 30-minute wash; no clean spare of this design | Cannot assume an instant safe cleaning method |
| D, familiar lower garment | clean, comfortable in the required movements; usual aid fits | Suitable existing option |
| L, removable layer | clean; expected warmth useful; reach over B not yet checked | Candidate, not already verified |
| I, identity accessory | familiar, comfortable and compatible with activity/aid | No reason supplied to remove it |

All items are already owned; no purchase is needed. The proposed B/D/L/I combination is provisional until its movement check. Do not label untested reach comfortable merely because the shirt is familiar.

### Action 1 — Build the requirements and timing card

List hygiene, clothing condition, temperature, actual movement, usual posture/support, identity, sensory constraints and the two organizer rules. Separate preference from verified rule. Schedule the supplied tasks without overlap:

| Interval | Task | Minutes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 13:40–13:42 | Familiar handwashing including rinsing and drying | 2 |
| 13:42–13:46 | Put on B/D/I and check fasteners/aid clearance | 4 |
| 13:46–13:48 | Add removable L | 2 |
| 13:48–13:51 | Test required sitting, reach and ordinary route movement with aid | 3 |
| 13:51–13:52 | Unallocated adjustment reserve | 1 |
| 13:52–14:00 | Confirmed journey to meeting | 8 |

This totals 20 minutes. The reserve is one minute of capacity, not another required grooming task. A needed correction must fit it or prompt an honest timing change. The 30-minute wash for C cannot fit this schedule; do not invent an alternative cleaning method or silently shorten travel.

### Action 2 — Preserve the provisional choice, then use the check

Write `chosen items | actual requirements | unchecked movement | hygiene action | intervals | adjustment option | departure`. Select B/D/L/I provisionally and save the plan before [later-packet.md](later-packet.md). The packet supplies the movement result and the allowed correction time. A successful plan includes a way to react to a failed check, not a prewritten claim that everything passed.

### Action 3 — Judge the occasion by function

Review actual comfort, access, temperature, participation and verified rule issues. Record compliments, if present, separately; they cannot cancel pain or restriction. Keep one preparation step and change one avoidable obstacle. If a real condition cannot be met within available time, ask for a practical adjustment or arrive late honestly; do not use painful posture, identity concealment or an unsafe shortcut.

Attempt [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [corrective guidance](check-answers.md). The source artifact is complete with the requirement/timing card, provisional selection, movement correction and occasion review. It adds no application observation, action or completion criterion.

## Access, harder practice and later transfer

Use a seated routine, familiar adaptive fasteners, a consented helper or a reader. Sensory supports and identity choices remain. If laundry, water, housing or clothing access is limited, record the practical support needed rather than tighten appearance demands. No one needs a conventional presentation to be dignified.

Harder variation: the organizer changes the room and cannot yet confirm step-free access. The outfit and timing may still look adequate, but participation is unverified. Ask for access clarification or a suitable alternative; neither a compliment nor a spare clothing layer solves an inaccessible route.

At a later real low-stakes occasion, use familiar safe hygiene care and existing clothes, check actual movements with usual support, and review participation afterward. If no occasion occurs, label a private rehearsal as rehearsal. An actual routine, self-review, another person's unsolicited compliment and independent observation are distinct evidence. The guide is not a clinical skin-care plan or dress-code legal ruling.

**Supportive:** Existing items and a small routine remove a participation obstacle while preserving comfort and identity.

**Mixed:** The initial choice fits one need but a movement or access check requires a change.

**Contradictory:** Painful posture, irritants or unwanted identity concealment are imposed for an imagined appearance standard.

**Inconclusive:** Brands or compliments are counted without observing hygiene, movement, access or the occasion.
