# 12.09 — Body composition and metabolic health

## Choose evidence that can answer the health question

Body weight is a total, not a breakdown of muscle, fat and other components. A stable total does not prove that nothing changed; a changed total does not identify what changed. Waist measurements, clothing fit, daily function and clinical markers answer different questions and have different limitations. A photograph cannot settle them.

This guide helps you interpret a health-change goal and choose a sustainable next action. Its default is a complete fictional evidence review. Allow 15 minutes to build the evidence table, 10 for the later record and 10 for fresh checks. Paper, a text editor, dictation and simple arithmetic suffice. No weigh-in, photograph, tape measure, calorie target, laboratory test, purchase or private disclosure is required. Health and exercise content must be adapted to the person and does not replace licensed medical care.

## A measure is useful within its limits

Weight includes tissues and other bodily contents; weight change alone cannot identify a muscle or fat change. BMI relates weight to height and is a screening measure, not direct tissue measurement. The [CDC's explanation](https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/faq/index.html) distinguishes BMI from fat and lean mass. This guide does not calculate BMI or assign a body category.

Waist can be one contextual indicator; methods and conditions matter, and it is not compulsory here. Clothing can stretch or fit differently for reasons unrelated to health. Function is task-specific: carrying a familiar bag more comfortably is useful evidence about that task, even if appearance is unchanged. But a lighter bag, different route or extra help changes the comparison. Keep those conditions visible instead of calling any faster result improvement.

Blood markers, blood pressure and other clinical results need their own units, methods, context and appropriate interpretation. A portal flag is not a diagnosis; an in-range result does not establish total health. [MedlinePlus explains lab-result context and reference ranges](https://www.medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/how-to-understand-your-lab-results/). Do not combine unlike tests or compare numbers from different reports without checking what they represent.

Sustainable methods preserve nourishment, sleep, access, relevant treatment and a personally meaningful goal. They may include a familiar activity in an established plan, a workable meal pattern or review of existing results. This exercise provides no weight-loss target, diet restriction, supplement regimen or extra testing schedule. If body metrics are distressing or unsuitable, the fictional route remains complete. Existing eating-disorder, pregnancy and condition-specific care governs personal monitoring.

## Worked example: the bag changed

Pat takes 90 seconds to move a six-book bag along a familiar route, then 60 seconds with three books along the same route. The second trip is 30 seconds quicker, but it is not the same task demand. The record supports a faster trip with fewer books, not increased muscle or reduced fat. A usable next question is whether the lighter load makes ordinary participation easier; it need not become a physical-capacity claim.

## Complete materials: Ellis's evidence folder

Ellis wants to keep visiting a nearby book group comfortably. Their existing care plan includes a familiar supported activity and regular meals; neither needs escalation for this exercise. They have existing clinical results awaiting review, with no urgent instruction or current symptom concern supplied in this fictional case. That absence is not a health examination or reassurance for a real concern.

A new symptom or urgent report instruction in real life would override this ordinary follow-up case.

### Action 1 — Build the evidence map

For all seven rows write `what is observed | question it can address | limit or missing fact | appropriate interpretation route`. Separate muscle and fat even though neither was measured. Calculate the task-time difference and state what stayed comparable. Do not infer a clinical improvement from the absence of a scale change, or a tissue change from the easier task.

### Action 2 — Preserve one decision before more results

Choose the question “what does the existing clinical result mean in context, and what follow-up is appropriate?” Draft a short request containing the question, the actual report availability and the written-reply need. Do not invent values or attach private details to a public learning record. Add a sustainable action card: keep the established routine if it remains suitable; retain nourishment and support; seek relevant advice for symptoms, unwanted effects or unclear restrictions.

Before opening [later-packet.md](later-packet.md), write what would justify each claim: request sent, review booked, result interpreted, actual functional comparison, body composition changed. Some claims may remain unsupported throughout.

### Action 3 — Integrate the later record without choosing a favorite indicator

Keep the original folder and add each later event. Distinguish an agreed next step from an action actually performed. If later function looks worse or becomes incomparable, keep it alongside the earlier favorable record. Choose repeat within the existing plan, clarify a condition, or obtain qualified review; do not respond to ambiguity with more body tracking or food restriction.

Attempt [check-prompts.md](check-prompts.md) before [check-answers.md](check-answers.md). Completion of this source exercise means an evidence map, a bounded plan/request and an honest later interpretation. Those reading outputs do not change application actions or completion rules and do not establish actual health behavior.

## Access, challenge and later transfer

A reader can handle the records; use verbal categories rather than body numbers. Keep private clinical information in the appropriate care channel. A lack of access to records or care is a barrier to resolve, not a failed health character trait.

Harder variation: the later portal report is from another laboratory and the earlier unit is missing. List the missing comparison facts—test identity, units, reference range, sampling context and qualified interpretation—without trying a guessed conversion. A same-looking number does not repair missing context.

For later real transfer, choose a meaningful health or participation question and one appropriate action already in the person's plan, or make a qualified contact about existing information. Record actual action, support and agreed follow-up only after they occur. Comparable longitudinal evidence may eventually support a narrower health/function claim; this artifact, two trips and author self-review do not supply it. Independent observation and qualified assessment remain separate.

**Supportive:** Comparable function becomes easier and relevant clinical uncertainty receives a real appropriate review, without inventing tissue change.

**Mixed:** One task improves while clinical information remains unresolved or a later task changes conditions.

**Contradictory:** Appearance or a single body number becomes a health verdict or justification for severe restriction.

**Inconclusive:** Metrics are collected without a question, comparison context or actual next action.
