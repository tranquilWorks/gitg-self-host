# 12.12 — Bodily autonomy and personal boundaries

## Let the answer change what happens

Bodily autonomy involves touch, privacy, medical choice and freedom from coercion or exploitation. In an ordinary voluntary interaction, permission belongs to the specific action and current situation. Familiarity, a previous yes, silence or politeness is not a substitute for a clear current choice. Respect is demonstrated by what happens after the answer, including when it changes.

This guide supplies a complete low-stakes greeting and privacy rehearsal. Allow 10 minutes to prepare, 10 to trace the exchanges and repair, and 10 for checks. No actual touch, photograph, personal history, live participant or confrontation is required. Use text, spoken rehearsal, AAC or cards. Health and exercise content must be adapted to the person and does not replace licensed medical care. The medical-choice companion below is literacy, not legal advice about capacity, emergencies or safeguarding.

## Current, specific and free to change

A greeting question should make a non-contact option easy: “Would you prefer a wave, another greeting, or no greeting?” Asking does not create permission to touch. If the response is uncertain, wait or choose a mutually suitable non-contact interaction; do not keep asking until a yes appears. Either person may decline contact. A legitimate boundary does not require the other person to agree that it is reasonable.

Touch and disclosure are separate permissions. A yes to a handshake is not a yes to a photo, a posted caption or sharing a private explanation. Collect the least information needed for the interaction. An apology after an overstep does not create retroactive permission. Stop, acknowledge what happened, offer a practical repair and respect whether further discussion is welcome; forgiveness is not owed.

For a medical encounter, prepare questions about what is proposed, why, benefits and risks, alternatives, privacy, support, and how a pause or changed choice is handled. The [NHS consent overview](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/consent-to-treatment/) describes voluntary informed consent and the relevant care process in its UK context. Actual legal rules and exceptions vary with jurisdiction and circumstance; this greeting exercise cannot settle capacity, emergency treatment or safeguarding duties. Use the responsible clinician, patient advocate or appropriate qualified adviser for those questions.

If dependence, coercion, exploitation or an unsafe relationship makes refusal risky, do not stage a boundary demonstration. Use the fictional route and a suitable confidential support/safety process. You do not need to prove courage through confrontation or disclose why a boundary exists.

## Worked example: permission does not transfer

Robin agrees to a private audio description of an activity for access. A helper proposes posting that description and Robin's health explanation publicly. Private access support did not authorize publication. The helper can provide the agreed description, leave private reasons out and seek a separate voluntary choice if any sharing is actually needed. No answer means no newly granted publication permission.

## Complete materials: Sasha and Toni at a community welcome

Both fictional adults are willingly participating in an ordinary greeting rehearsal, without a coercive relationship. Sasha prefers a wave and has not offered touch. Toni's preference is initially unknown. There is no photography requirement, no reason for recording private explanations and no penalty for declining the greeting. Either person can end the interaction.

Use these three independent starting cards; do not combine their answers:

| Card | Prompt and answer | Current permission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W | Sasha asks about a non-contact greeting; Toni says “A wave is good” | A wave, not touch or a photo |
| U | Sasha asks; Toni says “I'm not sure” | No touch permission; allow space or a comfortable non-contact option |
| T | Toni asks for a handshake; Sasha says “I prefer a wave” | Sasha's no-touch boundary stands without Toni's approval |

A separate medical-preparation card says: “At a routine non-urgent appointment, the person wants an explanation before a proposed examination and wants to ask about a support person and pause signal.” No examination, clinical recommendation or legal-capacity assessment is supplied. The default output for that card is a question list only.

### Action 1 — State the boundary and reciprocal question

Write Sasha's preference, a no-pressure question Toni can answer in their chosen communication mode, and a pause/end sentence. Identify separately touch and photo/disclosure permissions. For each starting card, say which action is supported and which is not. Do not add a demand for an explanation.

### Action 2 — Rehearse the response before opening the change

Speak, type or arrange the three card responses. For U, practice tolerating uncertainty without escalating the request. For T, respect both people's preferences: no handshake occurs when one person declines. A wave is optional; no interaction remains a valid outcome. Save the responses before [later-packet.md](later-packet.md).

Use `time | actor | specific proposal | response | current permission | action actually taken | repair status`. Record only minimal categories, not personal reasons. The role-play is real rehearsal only if performed; its scripted yes does not establish another real person's consent.

### Action 3 — Trace withdrawal, privacy and repair

Apply the later branches. Preserve an overstep when it occurred rather than rewriting the earlier response. A stop takes effect before repair discussion; do not continue unwanted contact to finish an apology. For the medical card, produce four questions: what will happen and why; what options and consequences should be understood; who will be present and how privacy/support works; how to ask for a pause or discuss a changed decision under the applicable process. You do not need an actual examination to finish this companion exercise.

Attempt [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the key](check-answers.md). Complete source output includes all three rehearsals, the later permission/repair record and medical questions. These are additional reading tasks, not changes to runtime actions, completion or the separate frozen 11.10 boundary protocol.

## Access, harder work and real transfer

Accept AAC, written choices, interpreters and a chosen support person; needing communication support does not itself mean inability to decide. Allow time without converting a delay into assent. Cultural greetings can be adapted, including no touch. Never remove assistance to make a boundary look independent.

Harder variation: a volunteer says a hug is required to join the group, although the verified organizer rule says no contact is required. Identify the coercive condition and seek an appropriate organizer/support route, or leave safely, without touching to prove willingness. Do not generalize this toy rule into a legal ruling about every organization.

At a later safe, ordinary opportunity, ask and respect a willing person's greeting preference or use your own non-contact boundary. Stop if the setting is unsafe. Record actual behavior and minimal outcomes; a changed choice or declined interaction can be successful boundary use. Independent observation and any qualified care discussion must actually occur, not be inferred from a script.

**Supportive:** The action follows current permission, including a no or changed preference, without penalty.

**Mixed:** A question is asked but pressure makes declining awkward; the interaction is stopped and the delivery repaired.

**Contradictory:** Past permission is reused for unwanted touch, private information is shared, or an apology is treated as consent.

**Inconclusive:** Consent words are memorized while no response-dependent action is demonstrated or recorded.
