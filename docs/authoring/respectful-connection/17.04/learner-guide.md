# 17.04 — Offer the kind of support the person can use

Reliable support starts by finding out what would help, stating what you can actually offer and doing the agreed part. It also includes saying promptly when your capacity changes. This practice uses an ordinary difficult week, not a crisis. Allow ten minutes for a conversation, the bounded action you agree and five minutes for a follow-up about usefulness.

## A strong feeling does not identify the needed help

Someone saying “I'm overwhelmed” does not tell you whether to listen, solve, reassure, research, speak on their behalf or leave them room. Ask rather than choosing the mode that makes you feel most helpful. You can offer a small menu without making the person manage your whole response: “Would listening, one practical task or some space help most?”

Six modes have different jobs. **Listening** lets someone describe their experience without immediate advice. **Practical help** completes a specific task they want done. **Encouragement** recognizes effort or offers confidence without promising an outcome. **Information** answers an actual question with relevant, checked material. **Advocacy** communicates a concern through an agreed route with permission about what may be shared. **Space** respects a request for less contact while making only a welcome follow-up commitment.

Feeney and Collins frame support as an interpersonal process rather than just an expectation that support exists [S13](../SOURCES.md#s13). This theoretical point motivates checking what was useful. The six-mode menu and case are original practice design, not a clinical assessment or a guarantee of well-being.

Reliability requires truthful capacity. “I can listen for twenty minutes today” may be more dependable than “anything you need.” State the limit before the person relies on a promise. Competence matters too: a friend's legal, medical or safety crisis is not an opportunity to improvise professional advice. Help them access appropriate qualified or emergency support when needed; this exercise does not replace it.

## Worked example: an errand is not permission to reorganize

An overwhelmed friend asks Sol to return one library book tomorrow. Sol agrees to that task, confirms the branch and return deadline, and checks that the task is feasible. Sol does not also sort the friend's belongings or contact their employer to explain the situation. Those additions may create work, embarrassment or loss of control.

If Sol becomes unavailable, the useful move is a timely message: “I can't do the return tomorrow as promised. I can do it Thursday, but I understand if that is too late.” The friend then has a real chance to choose another route. Disappearing until Thursday hides the information the person needs to plan; insisting they wait makes Sol's help a condition imposed on them.

## Your case: twenty minutes or one errand

This fictional case concerns Rae, a friend having an ordinary busy week. Rae says, “Everything is piling up.” You can listen for twenty minutes today between 18:00 and 18:20, or return one already packed library book tomorrow before 17:00. You cannot do both. You have no authority to contact Rae's workplace or family. There is no emergency, abuse, medical issue or unsafe task in the supplied facts.

Rae has not specified the desired support mode. You know the book-return branch closes at 17:00, the trip takes fifteen minutes each way, and the return itself takes five minutes. Tomorrow you have a free window from 16:15 to 17:00. The alternative is that Rae returns it another day under the library's own terms; you must not invent that there is no cost or deadline consequence.

1. **Ask for the mode and share capacity.** Explain the two feasible options and that you can do one. Leave Rae free to choose neither or name something else you may not be able to provide. Output: a question and an honest offer, without assuming distress requires advice.
2. **Make the chosen action specific.** If Rae chooses the errand, confirm the branch, book, handover and actual return deadline. The journey and return take thirty-five minutes, leaving ten minutes in your window. Do not promise it before checking the necessary handover. Output: who will do what and by when, with any dependency still visible.
3. **Carry out only what was agreed.** Listening is not a disguised advice session. Information should be relevant and checked. Advocacy needs permission for the exact audience and content. Space means no repeated checking disguised as care. Output: action performed or unresolved obstacle, not a list of imagined good intentions.
4. **Read the [later packet](later-packet.md) and renegotiate.** Recalculate capacity when the window changes. Let Rae decide whether a revised offer helps. Output: a timely update, agreed alternative if any, and a clear distinction between completed help and remaining need.

## Check usefulness without asking for praise

After a real action, ask a concrete question such as, “Did returning that book remove the task you wanted help with?” A yes concerns that task; it does not mean you solved the whole difficult week. If the person says the help missed the need, listen and revise what is within your role. Do not make them reassure you because you worked hard.

Support can create burden if it requires extensive supervision, exposes private information or obligates gratitude. Notice these costs without using them as a reason to avoid all help. The person may need something you cannot offer. You can say so clearly and, if welcome, help locate a suitable alternative rather than taking over.

For support with this practice, rehearse the six modes using the case and write the capacity limit before offering. A role-play establishes preparation only. For a harder attempt, the person changes the desired mode halfway through: ask whether you can meet the new request and agree a revised boundary. For real transfer, choose a willing person with an ordinary bounded need, do one agreed action and check its usefulness without requiring a positive answer. If they choose space, a respected pause can be the action; repeated unsolicited contact is not reliable presence. Finish with the [fresh checks](check-prompts.md).
