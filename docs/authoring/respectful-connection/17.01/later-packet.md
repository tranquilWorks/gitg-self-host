# 17.01 — A friendly limit

Supplied fiction, not actual participant evidence.

Sam says, “You're welcome to work on that corner. I'm trying to finish this section quietly before I leave.” Sam then asks where the spare pieces are. You know they are in the tray at the other end of the table.

Answer the practical question and respect the quiet preference. Permission to share the table is not an invitation to continue personal questions. You can participate alongside Sam without making silence awkward or withdrawing as punishment.

Later Sam says, “Thanks for finding those pieces,” and leaves. No future meeting or contact exchange is proposed. Do not add a friendship, private invitation or inferred rejection to the record. Describe a respectful brief approach and an accepted limit.

If this were a real attempt, the actual words and your adjustment would matter more than how charming you felt. Here they are supplied learning material only.
