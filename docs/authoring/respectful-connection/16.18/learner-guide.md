# 16.18 — Make it possible to understand and answer

A message is not accessible merely because you supplied a second format. The person needs a workable way to understand the choices and express a preference, question or disagreement. This practice repairs one communication barrier in an ordinary invitation or small activity, then checks the actual task through the person's preferred route. Allow twenty minutes to prepare, a short willing-participant check and ten minutes to repair.

## Match the support to the barrier and the person

Ask privately what would help; a diagnosis is not required. ASHA's communication-access guidance emphasizes asking about preferences, communicating with the person and checking understanding in both directions [S08](../SOURCES.md#s08). Its healthcare context does not turn this informal exercise into clinical service. We apply the participation principle to a low-stakes choice.

An **interpreter** supports exchange across languages, including signed languages; agree language, role, confidentiality and competence for the setting. **Plain language** makes purpose, choices and actions explicit without removing important facts. **Captions** make spoken content available as text, but need usable timing and accuracy. **Augmentative and alternative communication (AAC)** can include a person's communication board, writing or device; allow their response time and do not speak over them. **Written follow-up** provides a usable record of decisions, questions and changes. **Accessible formats** may involve structured text, large print, essential image descriptions or a compatible file instead of an image-only announcement.

These are options to select with someone, not six treatments to impose on every person. Captions will not by themselves translate an unfamiliar language, and large print will not solve an inaccessible reply form. An assistant may support expression without replacing the person's answer. Avoid requiring quick speech as proof that someone understood.

W3C's event guidance covers advance materials, captioning/interpreting arrangements and participation activities [S09](../SOURCES.md#s09). The task below is our own small usability check, not a claim of full accessibility or legal compliance. For healthcare or other regulated high-stakes decisions, use the responsible service's qualified language-access process. HHS CLAS guidance discourages untrained or minor interpreters in healthcare [S10](../SOURCES.md#s10); a child, relative or unverified machine translation is not a default substitute for qualified support.

## Worked example: the readable invitation still has no answer route

Dev replaces an image-only invitation with readable text. A participant can now identify the event time, but the only response button requires a rapid voice call. Dev has repaired the incoming message while leaving outgoing communication blocked. A useful second version allows the participant's preferred written reply and enough time to use it. Completion of the edit is not proof the person has successfully responded.

A check should resemble the real task: “What are your options, and how would you tell me your choice?” It should not be a public literacy test or require the expected positive answer. A clear no may demonstrate effective access better than an agreeable yes obtained through a helper.

## Your case: choose an activity and ask a question

Fictional invitation: “Saturday 21 November, 14:00–15:00, Room E, free. Choose a drawing table, a quiet reading table or neither. Materials supplied. Reply by Thursday 19 November, 17:00. You may change your choice on arrival. Jo is the private contact for access questions.” The original announcement is an image; it gives choices only through blue and green boxes and asks for a telephone reply. It does not state where the room entrance is.

Participant Ren requests selectable text, written replies and time to respond using an AAC device. Ren does not request an interpreter. Ren's helper is present but has not been authorized to choose. You can confirm the entrance with the host, and you control the invitation text and reply route.

1. **Specify the participation task.** Ren should be able to identify time, place, cost, choices and response date, then state a choice or disagreement and ask an access question. Ask how Ren wants the helper involved; do not ask for medical history. Output: a task and agreed support preference.
2. **Repair the material.** Write a complete selectable-text invitation with named options, not color-only meaning. Ask the host about the entrance instead of inventing access details. Provide the written reply route and adequate response time. Output: the revised invitation with any unresolved entrance fact clearly pending.
3. **Check the actual route.** With a willing participant, use their real device or chosen format. Address them directly, wait for their answer and allow corrections. If interpretation or captioning is needed in a different case, arrange it and share non-sensitive terms beforehand; naming the service is not arranging it. Output: actual task result or “not yet tested.”
4. **Read the [later packet](later-packet.md), repair and retry.** Separate successful comprehension from inability to submit a response. Preserve a declined invitation as a valid choice. Output: barrier found, concrete repair, retest result and remaining uncertainty.

## Keep the person in charge of meaning

Written follow-up should preserve the person's actual choice and unresolved questions, not silently convert uncertainty into agreement. Ask whether the record is usable before sending sensitive content. Do not save diagnoses, private messages or identifiable access histories merely to prove that you completed the exercise.

For support, narrow the task to a free familiar activity and one willing partner's stated preference. You can prepare alone, but a self-audit cannot establish another person's participation. If a partner is unavailable, retain the task as pending. For a harder attempt, the requested format works but the response route fails; repair the route and obtain a fresh response rather than asking the helper to guess.

For transfer, use another ordinary decision where you can control the material or ask an authorized organizer for a change. Review both reception and expression, including the possibility of refusal. Record one practical difference made and one limit still present. Do not claim all future interactions are accessible because a single person completed one task. The [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) distinguish format production, usable choice and actual participation.
