# 17.06 — Reduce harmful access with care for safety

A boundary identifies what you will participate in or permit within your control. It does not require another person to agree with your account or become remorseful. This practice distinguishes ordinary mismatch from harmful patterns, chooses a proportionate controlled response and checks its actual effect. Use the fictional case first. Allow twenty minutes to review, any appropriate support contact and ten minutes to check what happened.

## Different problems call for different routes

**Incompatibility** can mean that preferred contact, activities or expectations do not fit, without either person being abusive. A **mistake** may be isolated and followed by a concrete correction. **Chronic contempt** repeatedly treats someone as beneath regard through ridicule or degradation. **Manipulation** pressures choice through tactics such as guilt or false obligations. **Abuse** can involve intimidation, control or serious harm; you do not need repeated incidents before taking a threat seriously. **Repeated non-repair** means the damaging conduct continues despite apology or claimed understanding.

These descriptions guide attention, not diagnosis. The Hotline's emotional-abuse guidance identifies harmful control and threats [S02](../SOURCES.md#s02). It does not turn this exercise into a clinical or legal classification. You may decide that contact is unwanted without proving abuse, and you may seek help without proving a complete pattern.

An apology is a statement. Repair requires something observable: correcting an effect, respecting a limit or changing future conduct. Warmth immediately after an incident can be welcome yet leave the problem unresolved. Ask what changed, over what period and under which conditions. Avoid making forgiveness, reconciliation or continued access an exercise requirement.

## Safety comes before a boundary performance

Before acting, consider retaliation, stalking, monitoring, financial or housing dependence, care responsibilities and formal obligations. These can make a seemingly simple instruction to “just leave” inappropriate. A specialist can help plan around actual constraints. The Hotline's safety-planning guidance treats planning as individualized [S15](../SOURCES.md#s15); its resources are US-based, so verify a suitable route where you are.

NNEDV's Safety Net guidance highlights safer-device considerations and local advocacy [S04](../SOURCES.md#s04). If technology may be monitored, do not assume private browsing hides help-seeking. Abruptly changing accounts or devices can have consequences; get individualized advice when possible. Do not collect allegations in this application's practice notes, threaten exposure or confront someone to test whether the threat is real. For immediate danger, use locally appropriate emergency help when safe.

## Worked example: limited contact without a character trial

An ordinary acquaintance repeatedly turns a person's stated preference into a joke at hobby meetings. There is no supplied threat or dependence. The person can decide to leave a conversation when that happens, attend with a different group or stop one-to-one contact. They do not need to win a debate about whether they are “too sensitive” before using a controlled option.

A response such as “I won't stay in conversations where my request is mocked” describes participation. “You must finally understand how cruel you are” demands an internal change the speaker cannot ensure. The second sentence can keep someone stuck waiting for validation before acting. The first still needs a feasible way to leave; a boundary is not effective merely because it sounds decisive.

## Your case: apology followed by repetition

All facts are fictional. At a free weekly game group, Jules makes jokes about your request not to be called a nickname. You clearly ask for your chosen name. Jules apologizes that evening. At the next gathering, Jules uses the nickname again and invites others to laugh. You are an independent adult visitor, can leave safely by your usual route and have no shared housing, employment or financial dependence with Jules. The host controls group participation and welcomes private concerns. There is no supplied threat or monitoring.

1. **Separate six parts of the account.** Write behavior, impact, attempted repair, recurrence, safety and controlled response. Record the repeated nickname and apology as observations; do not invent Jules's motive or everyone's feelings. Output: a concise account grounded in the facts.
2. **Choose a proportionate action.** You might end the exchange, tell the host what happened with only necessary detail, use group-only contact or stop attending this group. Choose what fits your actual goal and access needs. Output: the action, how to carry it out and what authority you do or do not have.
3. **Rehearse only the low-risk branch.** A brief statement or no further explanation can accompany leaving. Avoid humiliation, threats, retaliation or an endless argument about intent. You cannot ban Jules yourself if that is the host's role. Output: a self-controlled response and a feasible exit or alternative.
4. **Read the [later packet](later-packet.md).** Distinguish your performed action from the host's promise and Jules's future behavior. Then consider the separate threat branch, which stops the ordinary rehearsal. Output: actual status, remaining exposure and a support route if the safety conditions change.

## Review consequences, not whether you won

After a real safe action, ask whether access was reduced as intended, whether an unexpected consequence appeared and whether further support is needed. Another person's remorse is not your completion criterion. A support contact may be a useful action without establishing that you have exited or that the risk is resolved. A plan remains a plan until something happens.

For support, keep the entire exercise fictional, or speak with a trusted person about a low-risk pattern without unnecessary identifying detail. If a real harmful situation is present, verify the support service's locality, contact method, accessibility and availability through a safe route. This source cannot provide a personalized safety plan or promise confidentiality across every service.

For a harder case, the apology is sincere but the conduct still repeats. You can recognize sincerity as possible and still reduce access based on the observed pattern. For transfer, use only a safe real option or an appropriate support contact; never provoke retaliation to obtain stronger evidence. Staying temporarily because of constrained options is not consent to harm or a moral failure. Answer the [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) while keeping ordinary mismatch and the safety branch distinct.
