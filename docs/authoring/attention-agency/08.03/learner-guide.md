# 08.03 — Impulse control

## Give a choice enough room to become a choice

An impulse is an urge toward an action; it is not the action itself or a command you must obey. A short interval can make an endorsed reason available. Choosing yes, no, later or a needed break can all be deliberate. The target is a reason you can stand behind, not suppression of every spontaneous response.

Over seven days, use two naturally occurring, low-stakes opportunities. Allow up to thirty seconds only when a delay is safe. The NHS guidance on checking interpretations [S05](../SOURCES.md#S05) supports distinguishing a thought from a fact; this choice interval is original and carries no treatment claim. Do not manufacture urges or delay food, sleep, medication, care or an urgent response. Sexual, financial, addictive or dangerous impulses need appropriate safeguards and support beyond this harmless rehearsal.

## A novelty urge with a defined stopping point

Fictional Ellis is reading a three-line event notice when a novelty tab attracts attention. The complete notice reads: “The drawing circle meets Saturday at 14:00. Bring ordinary paper if available; spare paper is provided. Register by Friday using the usual group channel.” Ellis wants to tell a willing friend the time and materials before taking a planned break. No actual registration or personal details are needed.

The first cue is reaching toward a tab titled “Five surprising desk objects.” The urged action is opening it immediately. The endorsed reason for a short delay is to finish the two requested facts so the friend has a usable answer. Ellis can say privately, “I want to open it; first I will send the time and paper information.” A worked answer is “Saturday at 14:00; bring paper if you have it, and spare paper is available.” Choosing the tab after the answer would not automatically be a failure.

1. Write a compact cue–urge–reason–choice record. Rehearse the complete notice first. In a natural attempt, notice the urge, take a safe interval of up to thirty seconds, identify the reason and choose. Record what actually happened rather than how strong your character seemed.
2. At the second suitable opportunity, use the same distinction. If the automatic action happened before you noticed it, record late noticing. You can still redirect next, but cannot claim the earlier action was prevented. Save your initial plan before the [two-opportunity record](later-packet.md).
3. Review both sequences: did a choice interval occur before action, what reason mattered, and what response followed? Compare the actual response with the purpose. An absent urge means no opportunity, not a successful resistance count.

For support, use fiction or a silent cue word; no timer or bodily monitoring is required. For a harder case, an urgent message arrives during the interval: answer the urgent need without finishing the pause. Other domains such as speech, spending, eating, sex, anger and quitting are part of the competency's breadth, but this lesson does not license provoking or self-treating those risks. A later natural transfer should preserve consent and essential needs. Two small cases cannot establish general impulse control.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
