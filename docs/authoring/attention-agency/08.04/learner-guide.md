# 08.04 — Desire, craving, and lust

## Want something without making it inevitable

Desire can highlight a real good while hiding competing goods, another person's freedom or uncertainty about the promised reward. Feeling desire does not itself make someone blameworthy. The useful question is what action fits the whole situation, including consent and obligations. People are not instruments for satisfying a feeling.

Use a low-stakes, non-intimate example for a ten-minute mapping exercise and one natural choice. NHS evidence-checking guidance [S05](../SOURCES.md#S05) supports examining an attractive prediction. The three headings here—what I want, the reward I expect, and what else matters—are original teaching material. They are not addiction treatment, sexual disclosure or a withdrawal plan.

## One more video and a real agreement

Fictional Noor has agreed to call a friend at 19:00. At 18:58 a service offers another eight-minute hobby video. Noor wants it and predicts, “One more will leave me satisfied.” The prediction is unknown, not a demonstrated effect. The friend has not agreed to a later call; there is no emergency and the video can be saved.

The worked map is: want—watch the suggested video now; expected reward—enjoyment and a feeling of enough; other goods—the friend's time, the freely made call agreement and freedom to choose later. The video is neither evil nor mandatory. Starting it at 18:58 would extend to 19:06 if watched fully, overlapping the agreed call by six minutes. Desire does not silently amend the agreement.

1. Fill the three headings and mark the uncertain promise. For a real example, choose a harmless optional attraction, not restriction of food, care or prescribed treatment. If a person is involved, add their independent wishes and freedom; your desire supplies no evidence of their consent.
2. If useful, allow up to ninety seconds to notice the urge without acting on it. This is optional, can stop earlier and does not require the feeling to fade. Choose a response: save the video and call as agreed, or ask about a later time before changing the plan and accept refusal. Save the choice before the [later outcome](later-packet.md).
3. Compare wanted reward, chosen action and actual consequences. A retained desire can coexist with a respected agreement. If you chose to watch, report the timing honestly; do not recast a late call as consensual because the video was enjoyable.

For support, use only the arithmetic and supplied case. For a harder case, the friend says no to postponing: keep the agreement or communicate an honest breach and appropriate repair; do not pressure them to validate the desire. Ethical reflection about lust can distinguish attraction from consent without intimate history, explicit detail or an acted sexual scenario.

In later transfer, notice one attractive promise that narrows attention and restore one overlooked fact before choosing. Addiction, compulsive behavior, eating concerns and unsafe relationship dynamics warrant suitable qualified help rather than escalating this exercise. The lesson neither requires abstinence from ordinary pleasure nor proves freedom from craving.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
