# 08.06 — Action independent of motivation

## Start a chosen task without waiting for enthusiasm

Low motivation and lack of capacity are different. You may not feel inspired by a necessary task while still being able to take a small step. Illness, exhaustion, access barriers or urgent care can instead make deferral appropriate. This practice tests a chosen start, not obedience to an employer, helper or internal demand to ignore your limits.

The existing seven-day practice compares two naturally low-motivation windows on the same chosen task: an ordinary start by day three, a start with one selected cue by day six, and a comparison by day seven. Keep each start between two and five minutes. The NHS discussion of manageable starts and reminders [S06](../SOURCES.md#S06) supports those broad ideas; it does not validate these particular evidence rules or imply that low mood is cured by working.

## A task with a visible first action

Fictional Avery needs a usable personal packing note for a familiar daytime visit. There is no emergency or travel safety decision. The full required content is “water bottle, ordinary notebook, key.” Avery already knows any personal medical or access requirements and keeps those in the existing plan, outside this exercise. The bounded first action is opening a note titled “Day visit” and entering a rough heading or one of the three supplied items. Imagining the note or deciding to start does not count as beginning it.

Avery chooses a three-minute limit for each window. The usual condition has no added cue. The single cue for the second is a visible card saying “Open Day visit and enter one item.” Do not also add an alarm, reward and accountability partner. The cue category is a visual reminder; its wording is ordinary and contains no private content.

1. Before the usual window, check that the task is still needed, chosen and feasible. If naturally unmotivated but able, make the actual first edit and work within two to five minutes. Stop at the limit. Record whether a start occurred, its duration and any capacity cost, without treating more output as better evidence.
2. In the separate second window, use the one chosen cue under suitable conditions. A visible card is not a performed action. If care or capacity requires deferral, preserve that status instead of marking it a failed attempt or inventing work. Save the plan before the [comparison record](later-packet.md).
3. By day seven distinguish both windows, count only known actual starts from zero to two, and choose whether to keep the cue, adjust it later or defer the practice. Missing observations remain unknown; a known deferral is not proof of an attempted failure. Do not turn a comparison into a demand for two starts regardless of health.

 Keep fictional notes separate from a record of your actual attempt. A summary of one actual start plus a deferred second opportunity is not a claim that the two-start target or practice completion was met. No reward for endurance or quantity is added.

For support, use dictation, an accessible editor or the supplied fiction. The existing accessibility adaptations also permit reduced duration and support; record the actual adapted step and its limitation rather than silently calling it the standard two-to-five-minute start. For a harder case, the cue is noticed while an urgent duty needs attention: honor that duty and record the opportunity accurately. Persistent loss of motivation with impaired functioning merits appropriate qualified support. Later repeated starts can inform usefulness, but this small comparison cannot establish a durable habit, recovery or unlimited capacity.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
