# 08.11 — Discipline without domination or purity

## Keep the purpose of a rule available for inspection

Structure is useful when it supports health, duties or meaningful activity. A rule can become costly when obeying it displaces its purpose, punishes ordinary limits or becomes a basis for superiority. Revising such a rule can require judgment rather than more force. This is an audit of an optional personal rule, not permission to disregard consent, safety, law or a duty to someone else.

Allow ten minutes for a purpose-and-cost review, one reversible test and a later check within seven days. Edinburgh's reflection questions [S07](../SOURCES.md#S07) support inspecting an experience before deciding what follows. This exercise does not diagnose compulsions or provide exposure treatment. If changing a rule brings severe distress or concerns about compulsive behavior, stop and seek suitable qualified support.

## A note that cannot be saved until it is perfect

Fictional Morgan has a self-imposed rule: every personal reminder must be perfectly worded before saving. The intended purpose is a useful reminder. The observed cost is that two recent drafts were abandoned unsaved while Morgan tried to polish them. These are optional private notes; no safety procedure, professional record or dependent person's agreement is involved.

The complete current reminder is “Ask about the library's Saturday opening time.” The wording need not answer the question or promise the library is open. The reversible alternative is to save a rough, adequate note immediately and check whether it can later be found and understood. Morgan can restore a useful element of structure if retrieval fails; the choices are not unlimited perfection or no structure at all.

Use four headings: rule and intended good; actual costs; adjustable part and protected duties; test and review point. A worked entry is: intended good—remember one question; cost—two lost drafts; adjustable part—polish before saving; protected boundary—accurate content without pretending the opening time is known; test—save rough now and retrieve tomorrow.

1. Identify one optional rule you actually control, or use Morgan's case. Ask whether the rule serves health, duty or meaning under current conditions. Distinguish evidence of cost from a global claim that all discipline is harmful. Keep important constraints and other people's freedom visible.
2. Make one reversible relaxation: save the supplied reminder before polishing. Do not combine it with abandoning all names, folders or checks by design. Save the initial purpose map before the [retrieval record](later-packet.md). If distress rises substantially, stop; proving freedom does not require forcing the test.
3. At the later check, inspect both benefit and cost. A note saved but not retrievable is a mixed result. Retain what helped and restore the smallest useful structure, then mark the revision untested until another real opportunity. The goal is fit, not proving that every limit is domination.

For support, use the complete fictional note without changing a real rule. For a harder case, a rule protects someone else's access or a shared duty: do not relax it unilaterally; seek an appropriate agreed revision. No public confession, self-punishment or comparison with less disciplined people is required.

Later transfer should ask whether the revised structure supports meaningful action while leaving rest and ordinary imperfection possible. Keeping a useful rule, retiring an unnecessary one or pausing the experiment are all possible outcomes. One small note test cannot establish recovery from perfectionism or a wholly undominated life.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
