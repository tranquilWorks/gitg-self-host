# 08.05 — Tolerance of discomfort

## Distinguish tolerable frustration from a reason to stop

Some worthwhile actions are briefly boring, uncertain or awkward. Continuing a small chosen task in that discomfort can be useful when the task remains safe and meaningful. Pain, illness, dangerous fatigue and violated boundaries are different signals. They are not training targets here. Stopping for a genuine need is compatible with judgment.

Try two two-minute passes at an ordinary paper-sorting task, with an ordinary break between them. Edinburgh's reflection sequence [S07](../SOURCES.md#S07) supports describing the event, examining its meaning and choosing a next step. The sort is original; it is not a pain-tolerance, exposure or endurance protocol.

## Five papers and a complete rule

Fictional Mika has five paper descriptions, no private documents: a current appointment reminder, an obsolete duplicate of that reminder, an undated note, a receipt needed for a pending return and an old blank advertising insert. Sort into KEEP, DISCARD or UNSURE using these exact rules: keep current commitments and records needed for an unresolved matter; discard known obsolete duplicates and unwanted blank advertising; put missing-date or unclear-purpose items in UNSURE. Sorting a description does not authorize destroying someone else's property.

The worked destinations are reminder—KEEP; duplicate—DISCARD; undated note—UNSURE; return receipt—KEEP; advertising—DISCARD. Uncertainty is allowed to remain in its own category. You do not need to discover the undated note's history to finish the bounded task.

Mika anticipates mild boredom and the urge to switch tasks, not pain or severe distress. The purpose is a usable three-pile arrangement. Success is not maximum suffering or speed. A two-minute window is a ceiling, and completing the simple sort early does not require inventing extra work just to tolerate more.

1. State the purpose, expected ordinary discomfort and stop conditions before beginning. Use the five descriptions rather than sensitive real records. Start the first two-minute pass, notice a mild urge to switch if one naturally occurs, and choose whether to continue within the safe boundary. Do not create discomfort on purpose.
2. After an ordinary break, try the second pass only if suitable. It can check the same assignments and uncertainty rule. Keep essential needs, posture changes and access aids available. Save the first plan before the [two-pass record](later-packet.md).
3. Review actual duration, decisions, discomfort if known and any stop. Two completed windows differ from one completed and one stopped. Correct classification does not prove increased tolerance, and a shorter attempt with pain does not mean insufficient discipline.

For support, read the categories aloud or use large labels and a helper who does not make the decisions. For a harder case, the undated note's ownership is unknown: retain it safely without inventing certainty. The broader competency includes uncertainty, embarrassment, craving and fatigue, but this source deliberately practices only benign boredom/frustration. Temporary pain is not prescribed and clinical concerns require appropriate care. A later natural repetition can inspect usefulness, not authorize escalating duration or ignoring the body.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
