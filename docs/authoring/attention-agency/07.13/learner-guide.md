# 07.13 — Mental-health literacy and help-seeking

## Know which kind of help the situation needs

Self-help has limits. Recognizing one is a practical skill, not a diagnosis or a failure of character. A support plan connects observable changes to an appropriate route and a workable first contact. Friendship or spiritual care can matter without replacing medical, psychological, legal or emergency expertise.

Spend fifteen minutes preparing a four-level card and five rehearsing an opening. For a real plan, begin with your country and region; verify each service's current contact method, eligibility, hours and accessibility on its official page. Record the date checked. Do not call a crisis service merely to test the exercise. A fictional card is a complete learning option; actual thresholds require action rather than finishing this worksheet.

NIMH [S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) describes professional support when symptoms are severe, distressing or interfere with ordinary functioning. Its duration examples are not a waiting requirement. In the United States, 988 accepts calls, texts and online chat for suicide or emotional crisis; 911 is for life-threatening emergencies. The official Lifeline description [S02](../SOURCES.md#S02) explains what contact can involve. These are US routes, not worldwide numbers. Check the relevant local emergency and crisis service elsewhere; do not assume access, instant response or absolute confidentiality.

## Build a card from the supplied fictional situation

Fictional Alex lives in the United States. For three weeks, distress and poor sleep have made ordinary meals and work tasks difficult. No immediate danger is described. Alex has an established primary-care clinic and permission to contact its appointment team during its stated weekday hours. A trusted friend, Sam, has offered a ten-minute private conversation tonight. An optional spiritual adviser offers listening by appointment; neither person is a substitute for clinical assessment.

| Level | Observable reason to use it | Route and practical next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary support | Manageable strain with everyday functioning intact | Ask a willing friend for bounded listening or practical assistance. |
| Routine professional | Persistent distress, disrupted sleep or difficulty with usual tasks | Contact a suitable clinician; describe duration and functional effects and ask for an assessment route. |
| Urgent or specialized | Rapid worsening, inability to meet basic needs, concerning changes in perception, abuse, or a time-sensitive medical or legal concern | Seek timely appropriate qualified advice; use an established urgent-care or specialist route, with emergency help if danger is immediate. |
| Emergency | Immediate danger to self or others or a life-threatening medical situation | Use the local emergency route now; do not wait for an appointment or a completed card. |

The examples guide routing, not self-diagnosis. Overlap is possible; uncertainty about urgent safety merits prompt appropriate help. A legal problem needs a qualified legal route for the actual jurisdiction, while a medical emergency needs medical help. This exercise does not decide treatment, legal rights or another person's diagnosis.

1. Mark Alex's current reason for professional contact: three weeks plus impaired meals and work. Keep Sam's separate role. Add one access barrier: Alex cannot make a private call at work, but can use the clinic's existing appointment channel privately after work. No invented clinic number is needed for fiction.
2. Rehearse: “For three weeks distress and sleep problems have interfered with meals and work. I need help assessing this. What is the next appointment or more urgent route if it worsens?” Identify who can help with transport, interpretation or access if needed, with their consent. Save the card and opening before the [later record](later-packet.md).
3. Distinguish a sent request, a booked appointment, an attended assessment and treatment. Review the actual next step and any unmet need. A rejected or unanswered request calls for checking another appropriate route, not declaring support obtained.

For support, use only the fictional routing table or dictate the card. For a harder case, the first clinic is unavailable: preserve the original concern and ask about an appropriate alternative; do not lower the threshold because access is difficult. Recheck real contact details when circumstances or location change. Keep sensitive history off shared worksheets. This rehearsal cannot establish clinical improvement or that any real person has received help.

## Scope and evidence boundary

Canonical professional boundary (unchanged): Not therapy or diagnosis; severe or persistent symptoms require qualified mental-health or medical care.

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.

Formal M6K A/B/C, independent cold-start, actual learner, qualified and owner content acceptance remain pending. No runtime action, evidence field, scoring or completion rule changes.
