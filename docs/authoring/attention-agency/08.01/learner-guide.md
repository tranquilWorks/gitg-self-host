# 08.01 — Attention audit

## Look for a mismatch, not a bad category of attention

An attention audit compares what you intended with what actually occupied a small observed window. Devices can support relationships or deliberate study; work can include avoidant busywork; entertainment can be chosen rest. Rumination is repetitive thought without a useful next step, but an outside observer cannot infer it from silence. Categories overlap and do not measure moral worth.

Use three brief checkpoints on each of two ordinary days, then two comparable ten-minute redesign windows. The NHS discussion of task patterns and environment [S06](../SOURCES.md#S06) supports observing a recurring obstacle and making a manageable adjustment. The six-category audit and timing below are original teaching choices, not validated measurements of total attention.

## Six observations with a recurring cue

Fictional Casey records the ten minutes immediately before 09:00, 13:00 and 18:00. These are sampled windows, not a whole-day history. Use device, entertainment, work, relationships, rumination and deliberate practice as overlapping tags. Mark an unobserved activity unknown rather than zero.

| Day/checkpoint | Intention | Actual window | Cue and interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday 09:00 | Read a short learning card | Four minutes reading, six browsing optional headlines | A home-screen news tile; mismatch with study intention. |
| Monday 13:00 | Talk to a friend by phone | Ten minutes talking | Device plus relationship; intended use. |
| Monday 18:00 | Watch a chosen comedy | Ten minutes watching | Entertainment plus device; intended rest. |
| Tuesday 09:00 | Read the same card | Three minutes reading, seven optional headlines | Same news tile; recurring mismatch. |
| Tuesday 13:00 | Finish a routine draft | Ten minutes working on it | Work; no drift reported. |
| Tuesday 18:00 | Rest | Thoughts replaying an old minor exchange; duration uncertain | Possible rumination by Casey's report; no minute estimate. |

The full learning card says: “For this fictional index, blue means a book, green means a map and orange means a game. An uncolored entry needs clarification.” Study its four rules without making speed or recall the outcome. The repeated cue is the news tile, not every phone use. Summing overlapping tags would double-count some minutes; the uncertain evening cannot be filled with a guessed number.

1. Label each observed window with intention, actual use and cue. Identify the two recurring mismatches and leave the uncertain duration unresolved. State one important intended use that should remain possible: the friend's call.
2. Change one condition: remove the optional news tile from the first screen, keeping the device, card, essential alerts and access supports available. Try two later ten-minute morning windows with the same card. Save the redesign before the [later comparison](later-packet.md). Do not add blocking software, notification removal and a reward simultaneously.
3. Compare intended and actual use in those windows, including changed circumstances. Keep, adjust or undo the arrangement based on usefulness and costs. No screen surveillance, application telemetry or employer productivity ranking is needed.

For support, use one accurately remembered window and treat the two-day audit as incomplete, or work entirely from this table. For a harder case, the news shortcut is needed for a genuine duty: choose another optional cue or retain it. Never remove emergency, accessibility or caregiving access. A later natural review can test recurrence, but four morning samples cannot establish a stable attention trait or prove causation.
