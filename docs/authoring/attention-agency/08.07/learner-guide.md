# 08.07 — Habit and environment design

## Let an arrangement support a chosen behavior

Repeatedly missing a small action may involve an invisible cue, inconvenient layout, excess friction or an unsuitable default. Changing the environment can help the action become easier to begin. It does not remove the need to choose a worthwhile behavior or authorize arranging other people's space without permission.

Allow ten minutes to design one reversible change, two natural opportunities to test it and a short review. NHS guidance on reminders and suitable places [S06](../SOURCES.md#S06) supports the general approach. The case tests one arrangement, not a validated habit-formation timetable or a claim that willpower is irrelevant.

## A notebook that is never where the task starts

Fictional Rowan wants to write one next-day reminder after finishing a personal evening note. The last two opportunities ended without a reminder because the reminder notebook was inside a closed drawer across the room. Rowan uses a private desk; moving items onto a shared dining table is not authorized. The supplied reminder content is “Return the borrowed blue folder on Thursday.” The folder's actual borrowing agreement stays unchanged.

Consider four possible mechanisms before choosing: a cue makes the action noticeable; a default makes a suitable option ready; reduced friction removes an unnecessary step; a checklist supports an easily missed sequence. Accountability adds another person's involvement and requires willing, specific consent. Combining all of them would make the small comparison harder to interpret and might add needless burden.

The worked choice is a single layout arrangement: place the reminder notebook beside the ordinary evening notebook before the next opportunity. Keep the same pen and routine. No alarm, reward, helper message or extra checklist is added. The first observed behavior is whether Rowan independently writes the reminder at the relevant opportunity, not whether the desk looks organized.

1. Describe the specific failed cue/action link and select one mechanism. Confirm ownership, shared-space limits and any accessibility needs. Install only the reversible notebook placement. A written intention to move it is not an installed design.
2. Observe two natural opportunities. Record whether the arrangement was present, whether the cue was noticed, whether the reminder was actually written and any cost. Save the initial design before the [two-opportunity outcome](later-packet.md). If no opportunity occurs, the behavior is untested rather than reliably repeated.
3. Keep, adjust or undo the arrangement according to its usefulness and burden. If you revise it, name two future opportunities for checking the revision; do not report those as already completed. A helper doing the whole reminder task would not establish Rowan's new habit.

For support, use a large visible card or a digital location already accessible, choosing only one change. For a harder case, another person needs the desk: negotiate a reversible placement or use private space; do not make them enforce the routine. Accountability should specify what contact is welcome, make declining easy and avoid private task contents. A later review can test reliability under ordinary variation. Two observations cannot establish automaticity or a universal environmental cause.
