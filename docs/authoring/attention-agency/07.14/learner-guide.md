# 07.14 — Chronic dysregulation and skill generalization

## Carry a strategy into a second ordinary setting

A skill that works only in a quiet room may be unavailable during a conversation or under time pressure. Generalization means trying a suitable strategy across naturally occurring contexts and noticing its limits. It does not mean making yourself more distressed to prove resilience. Keep existing treatment, medication and safety plans intact.

Use two contexts, two strategies and up to four natural opportunities across one or two weeks. NHS planning guidance [S03](../SOURCES.md#S03) supports noticing triggers, what helps and when support is needed. The menu below is an original small practice design, not treatment for chronic dysregulation. If distress is severe, persistent, worsening or impairing everyday life, seek qualified care rather than increasing practice intensity.

## A conditional menu with observable markers

Fictional Robin wants to answer ordinary scheduling requests without sending an incomplete answer. The two contexts are a private desk message and a friendly in-person planning conversation. No conflict, trauma trigger or urgent duty is involved. The behavior to observe is whether Robin checks both requested details before answering, not whether Robin looks calm.

The complete desk request reads: “Would you prefer Tuesday or Thursday, and morning or afternoon?” Robin is available Thursday afternoon. The conversation question is: “Should we use the round or square table, and do you need a chair with a back?” Either table is acceptable; Robin needs a chair with a back. No room booking or physical setup depends on the rehearsal.

Strategy A, available at the desk: put the unsent answer aside briefly, reread the request, identify its two questions and answer both. Stop if rereading increases distress or prevents an urgent necessary response. Strategy B, available with a willing conversation partner: say, “Give me a moment to check both parts,” identify them aloud or silently, then answer. A partner can decline a practice interaction; no one owes emotional regulation labor.

The worked desk answer is “Thursday afternoon.” The worked spoken answer is “Either table works; I need a chair with a back.” Correct content alone cannot establish which strategy was used. A useful record contains context, early cue, chosen strategy, whether it was available, actual behavior and next adjustment. “Not available” differs from “tried without benefit.”

1. Prepare an if-then menu: at the private desk, use A when safe; in a suitable conversation, ask for B's brief moment; if neither fits, postpone the nonurgent answer with an honest return plan. Select two natural opportunities per context, without creating agitation.
2. Save the menu and initial answers before the [four-use record](later-packet.md). In real use, record only enough to distinguish an actual attempt from a plan. No body scan, breath exercise or personal trigger history is required. Missing opportunities remain missing rather than being fabricated.
3. Compare where each strategy was available, helpful, ineffective or unwelcome. Retain a behavioral improvement even if tension remains, and retain worsening even if the answer was correct. Revise the menu once; an untested revision is not generalized skill.

For support, rehearse both requests alone using dictation or large print. For a harder case, the partner cannot pause: respect that, seek a later suitable moment and keep required access needs clear. Switching strategies is allowed; pushing through worsening is not a success condition. A real later review should include more than one strategy and context before claiming broader usefulness. Four fictional uses do not establish durable regulation across relationships or high stress.

## Scope and evidence boundary

Canonical professional boundary (unchanged): Not therapy or diagnosis; severe or persistent symptoms require qualified mental-health or medical care.

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
