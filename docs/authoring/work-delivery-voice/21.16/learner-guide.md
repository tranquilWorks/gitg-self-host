# 21.16 — Power asymmetry, exploitation, and precarity

Compare demanding work with leverage that undermines meaningful choice, then select a bounded support question. Allow two twenty-minute comparisons and fifteen minutes to examine routes. Use the invented cases. This is not a legal finding or an instruction to resign, confront someone, collect restricted evidence or make a public allegation. A person's inability to refuse immediately is a constraint to understand, not a personal deficiency.

## Look beyond how hard the work feels

Work can be difficult and fairly agreed, or easy in its physical demands and coercive in its terms. Examine agreement, compensation, safety, dignity and the ability to raise a concern or decline. High effort alone does not identify exploitation. Nor does the existence of a signed agreement establish that consent remains meaningful when its terms are changed or threats remove realistic choice.

Power asymmetry concerns who controls important resources and what happens when the other person says no. Income, housing, immigration status, references, future shifts or dependent care may constrain exit. Two people can receive the same instruction but face very different consequences for refusal. “Just leave” assumes resources and alternatives that may not exist. A plan should preserve practical options rather than test courage through a dramatic gesture.

Distinguish signs, allegations and determinations. Withheld pay, threats tied to housing and dismissed hazards are serious facts to investigate and seek support about. Specific legal terms such as wage theft, retaliation, forced labor or employee status require the relevant facts and law. Keeping that distinction does not require minimizing the concern. It makes the next inquiry more accurate and avoids pretending that a short scenario is a tribunal.

Consider precarity and disposable labor without treating all short contracts alike. Unpredictable future work can make speaking up costly. A clearly described temporary role with fairly observed terms differs from using replaceability to threaten anyone who questions changed conditions. Ask what was agreed, what changed, how objections are handled and whether one party can shift risks without accountability. Do not blame the worker for needing income or accepting a limited option.

Map support by function and independence. An official labor or safety body may explain coverage or take a report under its rules. A qualified adviser may help assess legal options and deadlines. A worker organization may offer representation or support within its remit. A trusted personal contact may help with practical needs but cannot replace a legal or safety authority. Internal human resources may have useful responsibilities but is part of the institution, not automatically an independent confidential adviser.

Possible outcomes differ by problem and authority. A pay inquiry may lead to correction of a record or, where applicable, a route to recover unpaid compensation. A safety concern may call for a safer arrangement or an appropriate inspection. A retaliation concern may require qualified advice about protections and remedies; housing dependence may require separate practical support or a supported transition. These are possibilities to investigate, not results supplied by the case. An apology, payment or new promise would not automatically resolve every other dependency.

For each route, ask what it can do, whether it covers the issue, what information it needs and what confidentiality or notification rules apply. “External” does not mean guaranteed remedy or safety. A promised contact may be unavailable, and information sharing can have consequences. Avoid collecting a perfect dossier before addressing immediate danger. A live emergency belongs with the appropriate urgent response, not a learning worksheet.

Choose a small next step that fits the worker's actual situation. It might be a private information inquiry through a verified channel or qualified advice about safe records and deadlines. Do not assume that a “confidential” label makes a shared device private. A fictional rehearsal is appropriate when personal reflection or device access would be unsafe. The goal is informed agency under constraints, not compliance with a preferred confrontation script.

## Worked example

A first plan says “leave tonight and stay with a friend.” The later facts reveal that the friend has offered a conversation but no place to stay. Treating sympathy as housing would create a dangerous false dependency. Revise the plan toward a verified advice route and realistic support questions. The need for wages and shelter explains constraints; it does not make the concerning conditions acceptable.

## Complete comparative cases

**Case A — Bounded busy period.** A paper-inventory team asks Dev to add two hours on one named day. The extra hours and compensation are explained before agreement; Dev accepts. The supplied record says the agreed compensation is paid, a safe work arrangement is maintained, and Dev declines a further optional extension without threat. Housing, documents and family support are independent of this role. These stipulated facts support a demanding but bounded arrangement; they do not certify an entire employer or every legal duty.

**Case B — Dependent arrangement.** Sol performs routine inventory work. The supplied agreement promises payment on the named payday, but **200 fictional units remain unpaid three weeks later**. A supervisor dismisses a reported unsafe condition and says Sol's employer-provided room depends on remaining silent. Sol has little available cash and no confirmed alternative accommodation. A reference and future shifts also depend on the supervisor. The jurisdiction, work classification, full record and immediate hazard details are not supplied. Do not invent an emergency timetable or a legal verdict.

Possible paper routes are: a jurisdiction-matched official labor/safety authority; a qualified independent adviser; a worker organization whose eligibility is to be checked; and trusted friend Imani for practical support. HR reports to the employer. **No route's confidentiality, capacity, eligibility, costs or response time has been verified** in the fictional case. Imani has said only “I can talk.” The next task is to ask what support is actually available, not treat every listed option as accepted protection.

## Compare leverage and preserve options

1. Compare demand, agreement, pay, safety, refusal, threats and dependencies in A and B. Output: concrete differences, with legal allegations marked for qualified assessment.
2. Map the four external/personal routes and HR by function and independence. Identify the information and confidentiality questions each needs. Output: a support map with availability unknown.
3. Draft one narrow inquiry asking about safe records, applicable deadlines and options given housing dependence. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Output: a private fictional draft without a public allegation or instruction to quit.
4. Revise any plan that relied on unconfirmed housing or guaranteed confidentiality. Output: a realistic next information step and a clear urgent-safety boundary if an actual danger emerges.
5. State what remains unresolved and what no one should be blamed for. Output: a bounded agency plan, not a score for willingness to endure or refuse.

## Support and transfer

Use third-person fiction and avoid personal details if that is safer. A harder branch adds an immediate hazard: obtaining appropriate urgent help takes precedence over completing the comparison. Real coercion, threats, trafficking concerns, unsafe work or retaliation warrant appropriate labor, legal, safeguarding or emergency support. Do not take restricted documents or make unlawful recordings for this exercise.

The canonical record has no professional_boundary field. These practical safeguards do not add canonical metadata. Applicability remains context_sensitive; normative status remains cross_tradition_core_or_broadly_recurrent. Paid employment, a role change or public disclosure is not compulsory. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Keep private or protected records outside app evidence. Human dignity is never scored.
