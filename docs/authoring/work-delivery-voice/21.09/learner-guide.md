# 21.09 — Collaboration and team contribution

Own a useful part of a shared task, challenge a concern constructively and leave a handoff another person can use. Allow fifteen minutes to coordinate, one short task and fifteen minutes for a retrospective. Use the fictional team below or an authorized willing collaboration. Team contribution is not measured by how often you speak, whether your preferred method wins or whether colleagues praise you.

## Improve the shared work rather than your visibility

Start with the next dependency. A polished artifact that arrives after its receiver needed it can add work. An unglamorous fact check may prevent several people from repeating the same question. Ask what the team needs, agree the input and output, and state how much you can own. “I will help” is generous but leaves coordination unresolved. “I will turn the approved facts into a readable invitation and flag any gap by the checkpoint” gives others something to plan around.

Coordinate ownership at the boundaries. The writer may own wording without owning schedule facts, approval or sending. Document the current version, decision, unresolved item and next person. A copied name is not a confirmed handoff. Ask the receiver to identify what they can now do and what is still missing. The goal is shared understanding, not a ceremonial acknowledgment that conceals a blocked dependency.

Constructive challenge separates observation, possible consequence and proposed correction. “The brief says 17:50 and the invitation says 18:00; are these arrival and start times?” leaves room for context. “You always get times wrong” adds an unsupported personal claim and invites a status contest. Respect does not require suppressing a real problem, but confidence in your interpretation does not remove the need to check it.

When your concern is mistaken, correct the record where it could affect the work. Do not quietly abandon the accusation while leaving others to believe there was an error. A justified correction to your own view is a useful team outcome. When a concern is valid, record the agreed change and who may approve it. Constructive challenge does not confer authority to rewrite another person's requirement or bypass an approval.

Credit should follow actual contributions, including invisible ones. Fact checking, accessibility work, scheduling and review may matter without appearing as authored text. State your own contribution honestly as well: erasing it is not required for fairness. Do not claim a whole team result from your own draft, and do not assign work to someone merely because their name would make the credit line balanced.

Evaluate the collective effect narrowly. Receiver readiness can show that a dependency is clearer. One smooth handoff cannot establish that the team is now broadly more reliable. Rework minutes, missing questions and confusion require actual records, not optimistic estimates after a pleasant meeting. Keep a personal learning point and a system learning point separate: you may need to ask better questions while the team needs clearer source labels.

Power changes which challenge is safe. A practice exercise is not a requirement to confront a supervisor, pressure a subordinate into feedback or make a public accusation. Use a private fictional rehearsal when the real setting is unsafe. The ordinary low-stakes case below teaches coordination; it does not grant permission over colleagues or establish protection from retaliation.

## Worked example

Kai sees 17:50 in Sora's note and 18:00 in the invitation and prepares to “fix” the invitation. Sora explains that the former is arrival and the latter is the session start. The invitation's time was correct. Kai withdraws the accusation and proposes labeling both times explicitly. The team can improve clarity without pretending Kai discovered a schedule error or that Sora's method was disloyal.

## Complete fictional team materials

Kai and Sora prepare an invitation and agenda for a paper-poster review. Sora owns the facts, agenda, approval and any eventual sending. Kai owns readable invitation wording after asking what is needed. The session is **27 October 2026, 18:00–18:30 UTC**, **Cedar Center, Elm Room**; arrival is **17:50**. Bring **one paper poster**. Questions go to **Poster Desk**, a fictional public role label. No actual message should be sent.

The agenda is: purpose and orientation, **5 minutes**; compare the two supplied poster layouts, **15 minutes**; choose one revision owner and date, **10 minutes**. The agenda total is thirty minutes. Sora's brief reads “arrival 17:50; session starts 18:00.” Draft invitation K1 reads “Poster review, 27 October, 18:00–18:30 UTC, Elm Room at Cedar Center. Bring one paper poster.” K1 omits arrival and the questions contact. Its session start is not wrong.

Sora has requested plain text because the next step is to place it in an accessible shared notice. Kai has twenty active minutes and accepts a check-in at **26 October, 12:00 UTC** if facts or approval are missing. Sora will receive the invitation by **14:00 UTC** that day. The permitted shared note is a fictional paper sheet headed **POSTER-HANDOFF**. Neither person can use a draft to represent another person's consent to public distribution.

## Contribute, check and hand over

1. Agree Kai's specific contribution, inputs, capacity, receiver and help trigger. Identify Sora's fact, coordination and approval work. Output: a brief ownership agreement including invisible work.
2. Write a challenge about the apparent time mismatch using the actual two texts. Include consequence, possible correction and a question inviting context. Output: a revisable observation, not a character judgment.
3. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Use the clarification to correct the concern and invitation. Output: a new version and a shared decision note preserving what was and was not wrong.
4. Supply version, verified facts, approval status, open question and next owner. Ask for readiness confirmation. Output: a completed or explicitly blocked handoff with accurate credit.
5. Review the effect using only the supplied response. Output: one justified team benefit and one missing measurement, plus a next-time improvement.

## Support and transfer

Use asynchronous text, spoken notes or a two-role private rehearsal. A harder branch has Sora unavailable for approval: prepare the draft and hold distribution; silence does not delegate authority. In actual work, keep confidential content in approved systems, ask for willing feedback and respect formal review. A disagreement over method can coexist with a useful shared outcome.
