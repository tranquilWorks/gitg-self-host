# 21.18 — Inclusive and accessible work

Remove an irrelevant barrier from a low-stakes information task while retaining the actual standard. Allow twenty minutes to analyze the requirement, twenty to design an alternative and a short trial. Use the fictional volunteer selection exercise. This is neither a disability assessment nor a legal accommodation or hiring decision. No diagnosis or private medical information is required to compare these task designs.

## Separate the outcome from the customary method

An expectation should describe what useful work must be accomplished. “Explain five facts accurately and answer a clarification” specifies an outcome. “Speak quickly on an unannounced ninety-second video call” specifies a method and condition. Those conditions might be relevant in some jobs, but they are not essential merely because they are customary. Ask the responsible owner which constraints follow from the actual task and which can change.

Preserving a legitimate requirement does not mean preserving every obstacle attached to its old measurement. Accuracy can remain constant across writing, speech, signing or supported communication. If speed truly matters, its relevance needs evidence and an appropriate standard; if it does not, a timer may measure an unrelated skill. Do not infer someone's competence from their accent, processing style, disability label or familiarity with one platform.

Examine several layers of access. Expectations may be unclear or announced too late. Tools may require an unavailable device or difficult interface. Communication may depend on a single modality. Hiring may select for familiarity with an irrelevant test format. Advancement may reward visibility in one type of meeting rather than the work itself. A small trial cannot solve all of these, but its principle should not end at initial entry if everyday work and progression recreate the same barrier.

An alternative must measure the same meaningful outcome. Keep the source facts, accuracy criteria and clarification demand explicit. Necessary supports can change how a person engages without substituting an answer or deleting an essential function. Distinguish access support from task coaching: providing readable text is different from supplying the missing correct response. Record what was offered so the result is interpretable.

Ask whether a barrier moved. An asynchronous form can remove pressure to speak live while adding an unfamiliar account or inaccessible interface. If the person cannot submit through that tool, you have observed a process blockage, not necessarily incorrect understanding. Do not score an absent response as a factual failure without considering why it is absent. Repair the channel and then test the actual requirement.

Evaluate equivalence and remaining limits. A fresh prompt can check that the alternative supports understanding beyond memorizing a prior answer. Use the same categories and comparable difficulty, while noting that a repeated participant may have learned the process. One successful fictional response is not universal accessibility, psychometric validation or proof that every hiring criterion is lawful. Actual employment decisions belong to the authorized organizational and legal process.

Broadly accessible design and individual accommodation are related but different. A process can offer plain text to everyone without collecting diagnoses. A real accommodation request may require an individualized formal process under applicable law. Do not turn the exercise into a medical questionnaire or declare an employer's legal duties from a generic example. Preserve privacy and the responsible professional role.

## Worked example

A participant understands a schedule but cannot submit an answer through a new platform. The designer initially treats this as failure to follow instructions. Reviewing the actual task reveals that platform fluency was not an agreed requirement. The owner permits the same response through an existing paper channel. The learner can now check accuracy and clarification separately from the unnecessary tool barrier.

## Complete task and equivalent retry

The fictional owner needs a volunteer to explain five categories accurately: **purpose; date/start/end time with zone; location; required materials; questions contact**. The volunteer must also answer one clarification from the supplied facts. The owner stipulates that this role does not require rapid live speech, a particular accent, a video platform or an unannounced prompt. This is a case-specific requirement, not a finding about all work.

**Prompt A:** Map Table; purpose is comparing public maps; **9 November 2026, 17:00–17:30 UTC**; **Cedar Center, Rowan Room**; bring **paper and a pencil**; a printed map is optional; questions go to **Map Desk**. Clarification: “May I attend without a printed map?” Correct meaning: yes, the map is optional; paper and pencil are the stated required materials. The initial selection method is an unannounced ninety-second live call.

**Fresh Prompt B:** Route Table; purpose is discussing public routes; **10 November 2026, 18:00–18:30 UTC**; **Cedar Center, Ash Room**; bring **paper and a pencil**; a printed route sheet is optional; questions go to **Route Desk**. Clarification: “Is a printed route sheet required?” Correct meaning: no; the stated requirement is paper and pencil. The same five accuracy categories and one clarification criterion apply.

The first proposed alternative is asynchronous text through a new account-based platform. The owner can authorize a **paper rehearsal** through an existing permitted channel, but no actual recruitment or advancement policy change is approved. All people and roles are fictional. The test requires no diagnostic explanation, medical record, real applicant data or publication.

## Preserve the standard and test access

1. Separate the six required outcomes from the live-call, timer and platform conditions. Explain why the stipulated owner can vary those methods here. Output: an essential-outcome statement with method assumptions exposed.
2. Propose an alternative with the same factual and clarification checks, and identify possible tool, language, sensory or opportunity barriers without diagnosing anyone. Output: a comparable task design and support description.
3. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Classify the first tool blockage accurately, revise the channel and use fresh B. Output: distinct process-access and task-accuracy results.
4. Ask which barrier remains and how the principle would affect training, daily communication and advancement. Output: a limited design conclusion with actual policy authority left intact.

## Support and transfer

Offer accessible text, signed/spoken response, large print or other appropriate supports while retaining the legitimate output. A harder case adds a genuinely time-critical duty: reassess the standard through the responsible process instead of assuming all timing is irrelevant. Real selection and accommodation work requires authorized review; a successful paper demonstration changes no person's employment status.
