# 21.13 — Documentation, maintainability, and professional legacy

Leave a small notice-updating process usable when its original author is absent. Allow thirty minutes to document, fifteen for a successor attempt and fifteen to revise. Use the paper process below. There are no real credentials, accounts or confidential records to share. A useful handover transfers understandable work and upkeep responsibility; it does not merely preserve files or give someone a pleasant tour.

## Document the decisions hidden behind the artifact

A successor needs purpose, authority and limits before steps. “Copy the new details into the notice” does not explain which details are authoritative, what may change or what to do when the source is incomplete. Record the permitted source, necessary inputs and who can resolve uncertainty. If the process depends on the original worker's personal account or memory, the dependency remains even when the instructions look complete.

Explain why the process has its current shape. A short decision record names the choice, reason, rejected alternative and condition for reconsideration. “Plain text was chosen because the notice must be usable without a design tool” helps a successor preserve the purpose while adapting implementation. “Always use this format” without a reason can become ritual after its context changes. A decision's rationale is not permission to ignore a governing standard.

Write steps that expose checks and recovery. Identify required fields, the authority for each and the condition that stops release. A missing room is not an invitation to reuse last week's room. A successor should know how to mark a draft, ask the right owner and resume once a confirmed input arrives. Recovery guidance can be an escalation or hold; it need not be an improvised solution.

Test with a new task, not only a walkthrough. Someone following your explanation while you point at every step may understand the tour but still be unable to act alone. Let a willing authorized successor attempt the synthetic update, and record any hint or undocumented fact supplied. Stop a step that exceeds authority. Assistance reveals an instructional gap; it should not be silently counted as independence.

Revise from the actual blockage, then retry. Adding more pages is not inherently helpful. A precise missing-field rule can remove a critical dependency while preserving a short guide. Retest the failed part with a fresh comparable input so remembered answers do not masquerade as transferable understanding. A correct hold is sometimes the best evidence that the successor understands the process.

Maintenance needs an accepted owner, access, review trigger and correction location. A handover that nobody owns will become stale. An accepted role is still a future commitment, not proof of continued upkeep. State which specialist judgment remains outside the document: perhaps a room owner must still approve locations or an accessibility expert must review a consequential change. Good documentation makes that dependency visible rather than claiming it has eliminated expertise.

Professional legacy can be quiet continuity. Others can recover a decision, avoid repeating a mistake or improve a process because the knowledge is understandable. It does not require preserving your preferred method forever or making your authorship central. Retain provenance where useful, give successors room to question the design, and keep private information in its authorized place.

## Worked example

A successor receives an instruction to update a notice from the change card. The new card lacks a room. The successor guesses the old room because the instruction offers no recovery rule. The author intervenes. A revised instruction says to mark the notice DRAFT, leave the missing field visibly unresolved and ask the room owner. On a fresh missing-room case, the successor holds the notice without a hint. That is successful exception handling, not a failure to finish quickly.

## Complete paper process and two input cards

The fictional shared folder is **NOTICE-KIT**, represented by paper sheets available to both author and successor without any personal login. It contains a blank template, an approved input card, a revision log and an archive. The template fields are **title, date, start/end time with zone, room and questions contact**. Only the approved input card supplies facts. **Room Owner** approves missing or changed rooms; **Notice Owner** approves completed notices. Those are fictional roles, not accounts to contact.

The process purpose is an accurate readable notice. Plain text was chosen so permitted users need no special design software. A decorative image-only alternative was rejected because it hides editable text; reconsider another format only if the authorized owner confirms equal access and maintainability. Successor Sam may draft and check, but cannot invent facts, grant access or approve a release. Approval remains necessary after a successful draft check.

Existing approved notice N0 is **Sketch Circle, 2 November 2026, 17:00–17:30 UTC, Oak Room, Notice Desk**. First new card N1 is **Map Circle, 3 November 2026, 18:00–18:30 UTC, room blank, Notice Desk**. Initial instruction H1 says: copy the new card into a fresh template, check the five fields, send to Notice Owner. It does not explain what a blank field means. Retain N0 as history, never as automatic authority for N1.

Fresh retry card N2 is **Paper Circle, 4 November 2026, 16:00–16:30 UTC, room blank, Notice Desk**. The missing room is intentional. All other inputs are supplied. Required handover sections are purpose/source, permissions, steps/checks, decision rationale, missing-input recovery, revision log, maintenance owner and trigger. No actual publication is part of the task.

## Transfer knowledge through an attempt

1. Write a concise handover using NOTICE-KIT and distinguish established H1 steps from your proposed recovery rule. Include the rejected format and revisiting condition. Output: a runnable instruction with explicit authority.
2. Apply it to N1 or inspect the supplied successor attempt after saving your version. Output: completed fields, unresolved input and any assistance required.
3. Use the [later packet](later-packet.md) to revise the precise gap and test N2. Output: a fresh exception result with hints recorded, then a fact-correct draft if authorized information arrives.
4. Confirm source access and accepted maintenance responsibility. State the review trigger and correction location. Output: an owned handover with remaining specialist/approval dependencies.
5. Write a bounded continuity claim. Output: what the successor demonstrated while the author was absent, not a promise that every future exception is covered.

## Support and transfer

Use audio instructions, large print or a private two-role rehearsal. A harder case supplies conflicting approved cards: hold and ask the owner rather than choose the latest timestamp without a rule. In actual work, preserve training, access and change-control requirements. Never place passwords, protected records or a bypass procedure in a handover to make it appear self-sufficient.

## Scope and evidence boundary

The canonical record has no professional_boundary field. These practical safeguards do not add canonical metadata. Applicability remains context_sensitive; normative status remains cross_tradition_core_or_broadly_recurrent. Paid employment, a role change or public disclosure is not compulsory. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Keep private or protected records outside app evidence. Human dignity is never scored.
