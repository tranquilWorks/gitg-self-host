# 21.08 — Project delivery

Deliver a small newcomer guide from an agreed need through a checked handover. Allow thirty minutes to plan, one short work session and twenty minutes to review. The supplied project is fictional; there is no event to publish, purchase to make or organization to contact. A usable project needs more than a finished file: someone must agree what it is for, decide whether it is acceptable and know who will maintain it.

## Turn an intention into an acceptance agreement

Scope describes the result and its boundaries. “Help newcomers” is a purpose; “one readable guide answering five questions” is an inspectable deliverable. A requirement says what must be true. A task says what someone will do to make it true. Writing a location paragraph does not prove that the location is confirmed or that a newcomer can enter. Keep the requirement, work and evidence connected without treating them as interchangeable.

Name the intended user and accepting authority before planning dates. A peer can find an error; a recipient can judge fitness for the agreed purpose; a venue owner can confirm access facts. None automatically has the others' authority. Internal verification asks whether the draft contains the right facts. A use check asks whether a reader can obtain the needed answers. Acceptance is the authorized recipient's explicit decision about the identified version. A favorable comment about an earlier draft is not acceptance of a later one.

Milestones expose dependencies. Fact confirmation precedes a trustworthy draft, which precedes a useful test and final handover. Give each milestone an owner, expected date and condition for moving forward. An owner is someone who has accepted responsibility within their authority, not a name inserted to make a plan look complete. An estimate is a forecast of effort; a due date is a commitment. Waiting for a decision may delay delivery without consuming the same amount of writing time.

A risk is an uncertain event that could affect the result. Once an essential input is missing at the checkpoint, it is also a present issue to manage. State the trigger, consequence, response and decision owner. “We might be late” does not show who should decide whether to move the date or what can safely be omitted. Preserve essential requirements; do not hide a risk by relabeling a draft as complete.

Tradeoffs need visibility. Reducing illustration work can be reasonable if the illustration is optional. Removing the only entrance instruction changes whether the guide serves its user. Negotiate a scope or date change with the accepting person and record what was agreed, why and what remains. A tight schedule cannot create a missing fact. Stop at a truthful draft status when the necessary decision has not happened.

Closeout includes the editable source, remaining issues, upkeep ownership and one lesson tied to evidence. Acceptance of today's guide does not prove it will remain accurate next month. A maintenance owner needs access and a review trigger; future upkeep is not completed simply because someone accepts the role. Nor does the project's success establish that the event itself occurred or benefited participants.

## Worked example

A one-page guide contains all five headings. Its author marks it complete. A newcomer answers four questions but cannot tell which entrance opens in the evening. The heading “location” passed a superficial check while the functional access requirement failed. The recipient keeps acceptance open. Adding the confirmed route and retesting repairs a specific defect; the version history retains the failed attempt rather than presenting an uninterrupted success.

## Complete project brief

Asha is the accepting coordinator for a fictional Map Reading Circle. Leo authors the guide; Neri supplies venue facts. Em is a proposed maintenance owner whose acceptance must still be obtained. The guide must let a newcomer answer: purpose, meeting date/time, location/access, what to bring and questions contact. The agreed format is readable plain text, one page. A website, participant list and decorative map are excluded from required scope.

The confirmed facts are: share and compare public maps; **24 October 2026, 18:00–18:40 UTC**; **Cedar Center, Willow Room**; bring **paper and a pencil**; ask **Circle Desk**. Circle Desk is a fictional role label, not a real address. The entrance and opening time are initially unconfirmed. Asha requires a confirmed usable evening route as part of the location answer. Do not guess from a daytime entrance.

The agreed deadline is **22 October at 16:00 UTC**. Neri accepts a venue-fact checkpoint on **21 October at 12:00 UTC**; Leo owns the draft and reader check; Asha owns scope/date decisions. Leo estimates thirty active minutes and has forty-five available. An optional illustration would add an estimated eight minutes. The editable paper source is labeled **GUIDE-SHARED**, available to the fictional team without a personal login. These are all the facts needed to plan; uncertainty about access is intentional.

## Deliver with an inspectable record

1. Write five requirements and their tests, identify Asha's acceptance role, and state exclusions. Lay out fact, draft, reader-check and handover milestones with owners and dates. Output: a short agreement distinguishing a test from acceptance.
2. Record the missing-route risk, the checkpoint trigger and Asha's decision. Choose a proposed response that preserves all five essentials. Output: a draft status and explicit tradeoff, not an invented entrance.
3. Draft from confirmed facts. Save the first attempt before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Output: a version with each requirement marked verified, blocked or not yet tested.
4. Use the later evidence to revise, check all five answers and record the accepting person's version-specific decision. Output: two test results and the actual acceptance status.
5. Hand over the editable source and accepted upkeep role; state one lesson from the defect and effort estimate. Output: remaining work, maintenance trigger and bounded completion claim.

## Support and transfer

Use spoken or large-print text and a five-question checklist if that makes the task accessible. A harder branch leaves the venue route unconfirmed through the deadline: keep the guide unready or agree a genuine scope/date change rather than infer success. For actual transfer, choose a harmless authorized project and obtain real facts and acceptance. Formal procurement, safety and professional approvals retain their own requirements.

## Scope and evidence boundary

The canonical record has no professional_boundary field. These practical safeguards do not add canonical metadata. Applicability remains context_sensitive; normative status remains cross_tradition_core_or_broadly_recurrent. Paid employment, a role change or public disclosure is not compulsory. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Keep private or protected records outside app evidence. Human dignity is never scored.
