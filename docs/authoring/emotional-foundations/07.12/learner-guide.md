# 07.12 — Resilience and post-setback adaptation

## Change something specific and keep the result honest

After a manageable setback, facts, responsibility, outside conditions, support and recovery deserve separate attention. A lesson is provisional and may not yet be available. Resilience here means an honest adjustment and a renewed attempt, not speed, stoicism or uninterrupted output. Trauma, injustice and uncontrollable loss do not owe you a useful lesson.

Allow twenty minutes for the supplied debrief, one ordinary recovery action and a twenty-minute restart. NHS problem-solving guidance [S07](../SOURCES.md#S07) supports specifying an action and reviewing what happens. This case is an original household experiment, not evidence of a general resilience trait or a controlled causal study. Wait until any acute danger or shock has passed before optional reflection.

## Complete setback record and restart materials

Fictional Alex planned to clear both an entry table and a shelf from 16:00 to 16:20 before guests arrived. At 16:05 an unexpected necessary delivery required ten minutes. Alex returned at 16:15 and spent five minutes moving items between the two surfaces. Neither surface was ready at 16:20. Alex chose an oversized two-surface goal; the delivery was an external condition, not a personal failure. The guest arrival was inconvenient but no safety harm is supplied.

The entry table holds six ordinary items: Alex's book, Alex's empty cup, a shared pen, a shared notebook, household member Bo's unopened envelope and an unidentified key. Agreed destinations are Alex's bookcase, the kitchen cup tray, the shared drawer for pen/notebook, and Bo's own tray for the envelope. Bo has explicitly approved moving the sealed envelope to that tray without opening it. The key has no identified owner and must stay in an agreed visible holding dish; do not discard it or invent permission.

The six debrief headings are facts, effect, my responsibility, outside conditions, recovery need and available support. Alex needs the already planned comfortable break. Bo agrees to clarify destinations, not to perform all the work. A worked provisional lesson is: “Reducing the target from two surfaces to one may make the next twenty-minute window usable.” It does not claim the delivery caused every difficulty or that good people should work through necessary interruptions.

The chosen planned variable is task scope: table only instead of table plus shelf. The next attempt uses the same twenty-minute allowance and existing supplies on a later suitable day. Natural conditions may differ, so a comparison must retain them. The visible target is a usable table with known items at authorized destinations and unresolved ownership visible. It is not “everything gone at any cost.” The shelf remains outside the new target, not secretly completed.

## Debrief, restart and inspect the cost

1. Write the six headings and calculate actual task time: the first five and last five minutes were available, totaling ten. Preserve the ten-minute interruption inside the twenty-minute window. Name one narrow choice Alex owns and one fact outside that control. A clean universal lesson is unnecessary.
2. Define the single intended change, target and permissions. Take the ordinary recovery action and confirm Bo's destination clarification. Save the plan before the [later packet](later-packet.md). If a real situation needs more recovery or appropriate support, defer rather than testing toughness.
3. Compare effort, actual conditions, result and any new cost with the first attempt. A clearer table can still leave an unidentified key or create clutter elsewhere. Keep an adverse result visible and decide to repeat, revise, seek help or stop for a reason. Do not infer causation when natural conditions also changed.

For support, use a six-box visual form, dictation or help with permission questions. A harder case gives no authority to move Bo's envelope: leave it appropriately and ask, rather than opening it to speed the task. In later transfer, choose a manageable setback and a visible renewed action. Recent trauma, bereavement or medical crisis calls for stabilization and suitable care, not a rushed experiment. A supplied household restart cannot establish recovery from those experiences.

## Scope and evidence boundary

Canonical professional boundary (unchanged): Not therapy or diagnosis; severe or persistent symptoms require qualified mental-health or medical care.

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.

Formal M6K A/B/C, independent cold-start, actual learner, qualified and owner content acceptance remain pending. No runtime action, evidence field, scoring or completion rule changes.
