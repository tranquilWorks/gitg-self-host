# 07.05 — Anger and assertive repair

## Keep the issue while removing intimidation

Anger may point toward an important boundary or value, but its intensity does not settle who is right. Separate the observable event, practical impact, threatened concern and escalation story. A direct request makes the desired change understandable; a boundary describes what you can legitimately choose. Neither requires the other person to agree with your emotional interpretation.

Use two fifteen-minute preparation passes, one brief willing exchange or rehearsal and ten minutes for follow-up. NHS anger guidance [S02](../SOURCES.md#S02) supports recognizing escalation and giving yourself time before reacting. The borrowing agreement below is fictional; it is not advice to confront abuse, discrimination or retaliation. Rehearsal is sufficient when direct contact is unwelcome or unsafe.

## A borrowed item and an amended fact

Fictional Quinn owns a small measuring ruler used for paper crafts. Borrower Sol agreed to return it to the marked indoor shelf by 17:00 Tuesday. At 17:10 Quinn cannot find it there and spends six minutes looking. Quinn feels anger at 6/10 and thinks, “Sol wants everyone to see that my rules do not matter.” No one else is present, and no motive evidence is supplied.

Quinn also remembers one earlier wrong-place return. Two specified occasions support a recurring inconvenience; they do not support “you always ruin everything.” The value may be reliable access to personal property. The status story adds an unverified public humiliation claim. A fact that could alter the story is a different agreed location or a message Quinn has missed.

An unhelpful draft says: “Return it now or I will embarrass you in the group.” It threatens retaliation beyond the task. A worked replacement is: “The ruler was not on the agreed shelf at 17:10, and I spent six minutes looking. Please use that shelf for future returns. I will lend it again only if we agree on a return plan.” This boundary concerns Quinn's own item and future lending; it does not remove shared access, seize Sol's belongings or demand a particular emotion.

Before contact, Quinn can take a short comfortable break, write privately or consult someone unrelated to the conflict without exposing private details. The safe exit is to end the exchange and arrange another appropriate channel if either person becomes threatening or unwilling. No recording, crowding or surveillance is needed.

## Request, listen and retain the remaining decision

1. Make four headings: event, impact, value/boundary and requested action. Put the public humiliation story outside the event column. Write one fact that would change your interpretation. Keep the real inconvenience visible even if that story later proves weak.
2. Ask whether Sol is willing to hear the concern, then use one factual request and at most one proportionate boundary. Invite correction of facts. Save the draft before the [later packet](later-packet.md). An accepted conversation is not advance agreement with the request.
3. Record the reply literally enough to distinguish return, explanation, acceptance and refusal. Repair any overstatement in Quinn's delivery while retaining a legitimate boundary. If the shelf request is declined, choose a safe action within Quinn's authority rather than threatening compliance. Anger can remain after a sound choice.

## A boundary is not punishment disguised as care

For support, use writing, AAC or a consented neutral helper. A private fictional rehearsal demonstrates preparation, not live assertiveness. For a harder case, the ruler is community-owned and Quinn has no authority to restrict lending: revise the boundary to a legitimate personal action or the responsible shared process. Do not carry the private-property rule into that different case.

Later transfer can review whether an agreed return plan was followed, not whether another person became deferential. Qualified or safety support takes precedence in threatening situations. One manageable exchange cannot repair abuse, establish character or demonstrate anger regulation across contexts.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Not therapy or diagnosis; severe or persistent symptoms require qualified mental-health or medical care.

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
