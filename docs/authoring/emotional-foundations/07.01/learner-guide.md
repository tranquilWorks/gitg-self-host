# 07.01 — Emotional vocabulary and differentiation

## Name the experience without treating the name as a verdict

An emotion label is a working description, not proof of what another person intended. Precision comes from separating event, bodily observation, intensity, possible emotions, meaning and action urge. More than one feeling may fit. “Not noticed” and “uncertain” are useful entries when a sensation or distinction is inaccessible.

Use two ten-minute passes over the supplied fictional episode and five minutes for a response review. NHS guidance on checking thoughts [S01](../SOURCES.md#S01) supports examining evidence and alternatives. The word bank and six-part record here are original teaching aids, not a diagnostic scale. The 0–10 ratings are supplied subjective descriptions and do not determine truth, safety or score advancement.

## A message, an interpretation and several plausible feelings

At 17:20 fictional Lee receives a friend’s message: “I cannot make the 18:00 walk today. Sorry for the late change.” No reason or replacement time is supplied. Lee notices a hot face, tight jaw and low energy, describes intensity as 6/10 and wants to send, “You never care about my time.” Lee also skipped an ordinary rest break earlier. Nothing here establishes the friend's motive.

Use this compact word bank as possibilities, not universal definitions: anger can concern an apparent violation; hurt can concern a perceived relational wound; shame can include a global negative view of oneself; fear anticipates a threat; envy concerns a desired good another seems to have; grief concerns loss; disappointment concerns an unmet hope; exhaustion describes depleted energy and can coexist with emotion. Do not infer a feeling solely from a hot face or an urge.

A worked comparison separates three candidates. Disappointment fits the lost walk, but says little about the friend's intention. Anger fits the urge to protest late notice, but no repeated disregard has been established. Exhaustion fits low energy and the missed break, but cannot alone explain the relational meaning. Hurt may also be present; uncertainty is allowed. “Never” exceeds the supplied record of one cancellation.

The six-part record can begin: event—one cancellation forty minutes before the walk; body—hot face, jaw tension, low energy; intensity—6/10 as Lee's report; labels—disappointment with possible anger and exhaustion; meaning—“my time may not matter,” still a hypothesis; urge—send an accusation. This does not require ranking every emotion or recounting private history.

## Let a finer distinction change one response

1. Copy only what is known into the six headings. Compare at least three candidate labels using one fitting feature and one limitation for each. Distinguish the event from Lee's interpretation and the impulse from an action already taken. Add “uncertain” rather than choosing a label merely to complete the form.
2. Choose a response that addresses the identified concern. A worked draft is: “I’m disappointed about losing the walk. Is another time possible? Earlier notice would help when you can give it.” Lee may also take the ordinary rest break. A request is different from requiring the friend to remove the feeling. Save the initial labels and response before the [later packet](later-packet.md).
3. Review the supplied response and new information. Revise only what the evidence changes. Improved wording does not prove a calmer mood, repaired relationship or the true cause of the emotion. A reply can clarify availability while leaving disappointment real.

## Use the amount of detail that is helpful

For support, use icons, dictation, a word bank or just event–urge–response. Breath or internal-body attention is not required. For a harder case, two plausible labels remain equally supported: keep both and choose a low-risk clarification instead of forcing certainty. If attending to the episode becomes overwhelming, stop and use suitable support.

Later transfer requires a manageable natural episode and a safe actual response. An observer may describe behavior but cannot certify your private feeling from appearance. Severe or persistent symptoms belong with qualified care. This classification rehearsal establishes no personal emotional change.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Not therapy or diagnosis; severe or persistent symptoms require qualified mental-health or medical care.

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
