# 07.02 — Body-based emotional awareness

## Find an early cue you can actually notice

A body cue can prompt a choice before automatic behavior; it is not a diagnosis of an emotion. Breathing, tension, temperature, energy, posture, pain and general activation can change for many reasons. A useful observation says when something was noticed and what followed. It does not require a sensation to have a special meaning.

Allow five minutes for a neutral comparison, ten to inspect the two natural-cue records and ten for later comparison. NHS anger guidance [S02](../SOURCES.md#S02) supports noticing early changes and making time before reacting. Its symptom examples are not a rule that identifies every feeling or excludes medical causes. Do not provoke stress, pain or physical exertion to create evidence.

## Complete inbox observation file

Fictional Noor works on ordinary nonurgent messages while safely seated. No urgent operational responsibility or medical concern is supplied. On Tuesday typing an accusatory draft begins at 09:05:25. On Thursday rapid typing begins at 09:05:20. Neither draft is sent. These observations show two possible early cues, not a universal jaw signal. No one monitored Noor; all times are supplied fiction.

A worked cue choice is: “When I notice gripping the mouse before an ordinary reply, take my hand off it if comfortable, look at one neutral object in the room and leave the draft unsent while checking its purpose.” No deep breathing, breath holding or muscle tensing is required. The chosen response must fit actual access and safety; a cue that appears only after sending would not be early enough for that target.

## Compare timing, then try the pairing

1. Preserve the neutral record without trying to manufacture relaxation. Mark unavailable sensations honestly. List the first observable change at each inbox event and the later behavior. Tuesday gives fifteen seconds between jaw tension and typing; Thursday gives twelve between hand grip and typing. These are descriptive case intervals, not required reaction speeds.
2. Choose an observable cue and one harmless response. Because jaw tension was absent on Thursday, do not claim it reliably predicts every episode. The worked plan allows hand grip as a second cue. Save the planned pairing before the [later packet](later-packet.md), which provides a third opportunity.
3. Review whether the response began before, during or after the target behavior. Name what the eventual message did differently and what remains uncertain. A changed message can coexist with unchanged activation. Keep, revise or discard the cue based on usefulness, without diagnosing a state from pain or posture.

## External information is a legitimate route

For support, notice hand pressure, speech speed, a visible draft or a trusted cue rather than internal sensations. Use accessible orienting, a comfortable posture change or consultation if breath attention is unpleasant. For a harder case, no cue is noticed until after sending: record late detection and redesign rather than rewriting the timeline.

In a later actual trial, use natural low-stakes opportunities and stop if observation becomes distressing. New, severe or medically concerning symptoms need appropriate care rather than an emotional explanation from this worksheet. Missing a cue is not a worth judgment, and three fictional observations do not establish long-term regulation.
