# 07.04 — Anxiety and courageous exposure

## Test an ordinary prediction without proving toughness

A worthwhile action can feel uncomfortable even when the situation is suitable and safe. A small approach can test a specific prediction, provided the step, actual conditions, access supports and stopping choice are clear. It cannot guarantee approval or certify that a larger situation is safe. Fear is not a moral failure, and stopping is legitimate.

Allow twenty minutes to prepare the supplied ladder, two separate five-minute attempts and ten minutes to compare. NHS guidance on facing fears [S03](../SOURCES.md#S03) describes gradual approach. This exercise uses a narrower ordinary participation case. It does not adopt the page's stay-until-fear-halves instruction, remove supports or provide treatment for anxiety disorders, trauma, panic, OCD or phobias. Clinical exposure needs an appropriate qualified plan.

## A small question in a familiar setting

Fictional adult Asha attends a voluntary craft-planning group whose facilitator explicitly welcomes routine questions. Members can speak, submit text or pass. No grades, employment decisions, hostile participant or serious consequence is involved. Asha's aim is to know what material is already provided, not to become a confident public speaker.

The complete notice says: “At the next session, paper and pencils are provided. Bring scissors only if you already have suitable ones; shared pairs will be available.” Asha's question is: “May I use the shared scissors rather than bring my own?” This is low-stakes clarification even though the notice partly answers it. The actual feared prediction is: “Someone will say I should already know the answer.” Asha assigns it a rough 60-percent expectation, which is a subjective estimate, not calibrated probability.

The supplied ladder is: write the question privately, expected distress 1/10; say it to a willing trusted member outside the meeting, 2/10; ask during the familiar group's question period using a written card if wanted, 3/10. The source exercise's local ceiling is 4/10. That number is not a general safety threshold: real threat, coercion, medical risk or a trauma cue excludes a live attempt regardless of rating. Asha chooses the third step freely in this fiction and will repeat that same step on two appropriate occasions without escalating.

The second occasion's complete notice concerns cardboard: “Cardboard is supplied; extra pieces are optional.” Its question is, “Is bringing extra cardboard optional?” Before each attempt Asha records the prediction and expected distress. During an attempt Asha may stop whenever desired, must stop this exercise if risk changes or the chosen ceiling is exceeded, and keeps all ordinary access supports. No one is instructed to endure until distress falls.

## Compare a prediction with two actual records

1. Separate the participation aim, observable feared outcome and unsupported global story. “A member says this was already covered” could support part of the prediction; “everyone thinks I am incapable” cannot be established from silence. Check suitability before considering a rating.
2. Save the chosen step, support, stop condition and predicted outcome before the [later packet](later-packet.md). In a real version use only naturally suitable occasions and repeat the same manageable step. Writing alone remains preparation for the meeting step, not equivalent live evidence.
3. Compare both observations, including partial feared outcomes and unchanged discomfort. Decide to repeat, use another accessible route, defer or seek qualified support. There is no automatic ladder promotion. One comment does not establish universal rejection; one kind answer does not disprove every concern.

For support, use text, AAC, an interpreter or a trusted companion if appropriate. These are not aids to strip away as proof of courage. For a harder case, the group becomes hostile or the topic gains serious consequences: reassess and stop the experiment rather than applying the old rating. Persistent, worsening or function-limiting anxiety belongs with qualified care. The supplied exercise rehearses evidence comparison, not clinical recovery.

## Scope and evidence boundary

Canonical professional boundary (unchanged): Not therapy or diagnosis; severe or persistent symptoms require qualified mental-health or medical care.

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
