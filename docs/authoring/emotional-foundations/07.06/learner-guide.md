# 07.06 — Sadness, grief, and loss

## Carry a loss without requiring it to become a lesson

Sorrow can concern a person, place, role, routine or hoped-for future. Naming what remains does not cancel what is gone. Remembrance, support, changed expectations and continued living may coexist with sadness, relief, numbness or uncertainty. There is no required order, public ritual or date by which the feeling must resolve.

Use twenty minutes with the supplied manageable loss, one short remembrance or support action and ten minutes for later review. NHS information on grief [S04](../SOURCES.md#S04) recognizes varied responses and available support. This exercise does not implement a stage sequence or diagnose bereavement. Use fiction when personal loss is too close, recent, traumatic or destabilizing; qualified or trusted support may be more appropriate than self-guided reflection.

## The end of a familiar group

The fictional Saturday Sketch Circle announces on October 3 that its last meeting will be October 10. The venue is no longer available; no replacement group or future gathering is promised. Participant Mika has attended for six months. The group has permission to share its own ordinary meeting notice, but members' names, photographs and artwork are private unless separately authorized.

Mika will lose a reliable Saturday routine, a familiar room and the expectation of casual company. Mika retains a sketchbook, a practiced drawing habit and the ordinary household commitment to prepare a shared meal at 18:00. One friend, Inez, welcomes a private message about the closing and is willing to consider a specific request; this is not an unlimited offer of care. A future class advertised elsewhere has no confirmed accessible space or availability in this packet.

A worked three-column entry reads: lost—weekly company in this room; remaining—drawing skill and past memories; now changed—there is no automatic Saturday meeting after October 10. “I should feel grateful instead of sad” does not belong in the record. Appreciation, if present, need not erase sorrow; the closing notice alone does not make a replacement possible.

For remembrance Mika can write the final date and one private memory of learning to shade a cup. No copyrighted image, song lyric or personal story from another member is needed. For support, the bounded request is: “Would you keep me company by phone for ten minutes while I sort my own pencils on Sunday?” Inez can accept, decline or modify it. Continued living could include removing obsolete reminders and preparing the already agreed meal, without labeling either action closure.

## Remember, ask and adjust the expectation

1. Complete lost, remaining and now-changed headings from the packet. Record Mika's supplied present sadness as 5/10 without treating the number as a standard. Name one expectation that must change and one responsibility that remains. If a personal route becomes overwhelming, stop; detailed recall is not required.
2. Choose one private remembrance and one bounded support request. Save the intended actions before the [later packet](later-packet.md). An unanswered request means support is unconfirmed, not that no one cares. Do not publish group material or contact members who have not welcomed it.
3. Inspect what actually happened. Distinguish remembering, receiving company, maintaining a duty and joining a new group. Name what was carried forward and what still hurts. If an action must wait, record the reason rather than forcing a positive account.

## Let support fit the person and the loss

For support, use a chosen object, a short audio note, an accessible quiet ritual or a trusted person; silence can also be a fitting response. For a harder case, the hoped-for replacement group declines a place. Preserve that second disappointment and seek a different suitable option only if wanted. Avoid turning adaptation into a demand to replace relationships quickly.

Later review may notice changed routines or continuing waves of feeling without a timetable. Function-impairing, severe or persistent symptoms, traumatic loss or safety concerns merit appropriate qualified support. This fictional closure of a hobby group is not evidence that a learner has processed personal grief or can manage every kind of loss.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Not therapy or diagnosis; severe or persistent symptoms require qualified mental-health or medical care.

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
