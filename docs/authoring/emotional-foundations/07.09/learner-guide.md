# 07.09 — Healthy emotional expression

## Say the emotional truth without making it a demand

Clear emotional expression separates what happened, how you felt, what mattered and what you ask. It avoids turning feelings into motive claims, public performance or a requirement that another person regulate them for you. A listener can hear a real feeling and still decline a request. Proportion concerns timing, detail, relationship and impact; it does not require minimizing every difficult feeling.

Use fifteen minutes to prepare, one five-to-ten-minute willing exchange or rehearsal and ten minutes to review. CCI's assertiveness overview [S06](../SOURCES.md#S06) supports clear, direct communication with respect for others. Its linked workbook modules were not read for this exercise. The four-line form is original supplied teaching, not a communication guarantee or a therapy intervention.

## A changed plan and a willing channel

Fictional Erin and friend Jo usually meet for a Tuesday lunch. On Monday the group moved this week's lunch from 12:30 to 12:00 in a chat Erin had muted. Erin arrived at 12:30 and missed the first thirty minutes. Jo did not personally send Erin a separate message. It is unknown whether Jo knew the chat was muted. There is no pattern of exclusion supplied.

Erin feels left out and disappointed, described as 5/10, and values ordinary notice of changed arrangements. Erin's first impulse is to ignore Jo until Jo notices. That would leave both the feeling and the request hidden. A dramatic draft, “You deliberately excluded me, so prove I matter,” adds intent and makes reassurance a loyalty test.

The worked four lines are: “I arrived at 12:30 after the plan changed to 12:00. I felt left out and disappointed. Knowing when an agreed plan changes matters to me. Would you send me a direct note for our next plan change, or suggest another workable way?” The first sentence leaves room for correction, the second reports Erin's experience, the third gives meaning and the fourth permits alternatives.

Jo has said a private written exchange on Wednesday after 18:00 is welcome, for up to ten minutes. This consent is limited to the time and channel; it does not commit Jo to the request. Erin can also use the group notification setting, but should not change somebody else's settings or expose the conversation. No real account access is required in the fictional route.

## Deliver, listen and decide the next action

1. Rewrite the dramatic draft into observation, feeling, value and request. Keep the thirty-minute missed portion accurate. Remove unverified intent, threats and indirect punishment. Decide how much detail fits this ordinary event rather than recounting every past grievance.
2. Confirm the agreed time and channel, deliver the message, then pause for Jo's account. Invite factual correction and ask what Jo understood. Save the prepared message before the [later packet](later-packet.md). If a real listener declines, accept that and use private processing, another time or suitable support without public hints or retaliation.
3. Record the actual response and whether the request was accepted, modified or declined. Repair an overstatement if welcome without declaring the feeling unreal. Choose a next action that both respects Jo's freedom and addresses Erin's legitimate need for timely information. Drafting, rehearsing and delivering are distinct statuses.

For support, use AAC, interpretation or a consented supporter under the speaker's control. Without a trustworthy listener, rehearse the supplied exchange and leave live disclosure untested. Do not disclose to a coercive, abusive or retaliatory person to complete an exercise; use safety planning and appropriate support instead.

A harder case includes a corrected fact: the direct message was actually sent and unread. Update the event description rather than using the strength of the feeling to override the evidence. Later transfer can inspect whether the new notification arrangement worked. One candid exchange cannot establish permanent intimacy, repair every exclusion or prove that either person must provide reassurance on demand.

## Use the result at its proper scale

Not therapy or diagnosis; severe or persistent symptoms require qualified mental-health or medical care.

Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Subjective ratings, source exercises and completion do not diagnose, establish mastery or score human dignity. Actual situations require appropriate access, consent, authority and support.
