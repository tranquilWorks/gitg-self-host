# 22.03 — Authority and ethical use of power

Design a fair opportunity decision when the chooser also controls resources that make refusal difficult. Allow twenty minutes for the power map and fifteen for the later review. Use the supplied fictional group. Do not test a real person's freedom by asking them to refuse someone who controls their livelihood, references or essential access.

## Locate power before asking for agreement

Formal authority is the recognized right to decide within a role. Informal power includes reputation, access to influential people, control of information and the ability to make participation easier or harder. Both shape a choice. A leader may sincerely say “voluntary” while participants reasonably worry that declining will affect future opportunities. The word does not remove the dependency.

Map what the decision maker controls and what participants might lose. Separate supplied facts from hypotheses: a leader who schedules shifts has a source of power; this does not prove that they have threatened anyone. Conversely, the absence of an explicit threat does not establish a cost-free refusal. Consider private response routes, independent selection and relevant review arrangements before relying on an apparently enthusiastic public answer.

A conflict of interest is a situation that can interfere with impartial judgment; it is not automatically a finding of corruption. Personal relationships, material benefits, loyalty and the desire for admiration can matter. Disclosure makes the issue visible but does not by itself decide whether the person may participate. Follow the applicable authority's process for disclosure, recusal or independent review. Privately promising to be objective is not a substitute for the required safeguard.

Criteria should serve the opportunity's purpose. Readiness and consent matter, but so can fair access to development. If experience is always required and never offered, the same people may receive every chance. Define the minimum needed for this task and what support can close a safe learning gap. Do not invent a scoring system that rewards friendship or public praise while presenting it as merit.

Dignity requires a meaningful refusal route and a truthful explanation of its limits. A private channel can reduce public pressure, but the channel owner still learns something. Do not promise anonymity when identifiers or circumstances reveal the person. Collect only what the decision requires; declining need not involve personal justification. A reviewer must have actual authority and availability, not merely a reassuring title in a form.

Use authority to expand capability through a supported opportunity, not to obtain obedience. A selected person needs preparation, boundaries and a way to ask for help. Those not selected should retain a fair future route. A leader can be disappointed that someone declines without changing their standing. Monitor what is within your control, such as whether scheduling or invitations changed; do not infer that every dependency has vanished.

Accountability includes a concise reasons record and a usable correction route. Keep private circumstances out of a public decision note. If someone raises favoritism or pressure, preserve the concern and refer it appropriately. Gratitude, silence and the leader's own confidence do not establish fairness. An independent process can improve the decision without proving that nobody felt pressure.

## Worked example

A volunteer coordinator selects their close friend to represent a group, saying the friend is enthusiastic. The friendship does not automatically make the friend unqualified. The defect is an unmanaged conflict and criteria that nobody could inspect. An authorized independent selector can consider the same person's readiness under published criteria. Fairness does not require punishing a candidate for knowing the coordinator; it requires an appropriate process.

## Complete fictional selection packet

The Paper Atlas group has one **four-minute demonstration slot on 8 November 2026**. Its purpose is to let a member explain an existing public map comparison, with preparation support. Dana normally assigns the slot, controls next month's volunteer rota and supplies optional references. Dana is close friends with candidate Ivo. These are supplied facts, not findings of misconduct.

The group's fictional rule **OP-3** requires a connected selector to disclose the relationship to review steward Jules, who appoints an unconnected selector. Jules has authorized Lea to select this slot. Dana must not lobby Lea or collect participants' private reasons. A review request can go to Jules before **6 November at 12:00 UTC**; Jules can correct the selection process, not promise everyone a slot. This is invented governance, not law.

Published criteria are: willingness; ability, with the available ten-minute rehearsal, to explain the map's two labels accurately; and fair access, using fewer previous slots as the tie-break between equally ready willing candidates. **Ivo** is ready and has had **two** slots. **Bea** is ready after rehearsal and has had **zero**. **Chen** is ready, has had **zero**, and has not yet expressed interest. No candidate's interest should be inferred from availability. All have the same offered preparation time and may send a private yes/no to Lea without reasons. Declining cannot itself change the rota, references or later eligibility under OP-3.

Dana's proposed announcement is: “Show your commitment by volunteering publicly; I hope everyone supports my choice.” It creates a loyalty cue and reveals refusals. Replace it. The paper selection record may contain criteria, decision, conflict disposition, support and review route; it must not contain personal explanations for declining.

## Restructure and review the decision

1. Map Dana's formal decision role, rota/reference influence, friendship and participants' refusal costs. Distinguish a conflict from proven misuse. Output: power map with facts, concerns and unknowns.
2. Apply OP-3, identify Lea and Jules's separate roles and rewrite the invitation. Output: private choice route, actual conflict disposition, criteria and limits on data collection.
3. Compare candidates conditionally: do not count Chen as willing. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Output: a selection rationale that can change with actual interest.
4. Read the response and rehearsal results. Record support, the selected person's demonstrated capacity, others' future access and any review concern. Output: a privacy-minimized decision note with an explicit limit on what fairness evidence exists.

## Support and transfer

Use a spoken private response through a willing neutral helper when writing is a barrier. In a harder case Jules is also connected and no authorized substitute is available: pause the decision or seek the legitimate next authority rather than appoint yourself impartial. Actual hiring, promotion, procurement and essential services require their governing procedures and qualified review. A harmless fiction does not authorize experiments with another person's dependence.

## Scope and evidence boundary

The canonical record has no professional_boundary field. These practical safeguards do not add canonical metadata. Applicability and normative status both remain role_conditional. Taking a leadership role is not compulsory. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Use actual authority and willing participation; keep protected records outside app evidence. Human dignity is never scored.
