# 22.11 — Crisis leadership

Run an announced tabletop disruption in which a workshop venue becomes unavailable. Allow fifteen minutes to prepare, twenty for the scenario and fifteen for the review. Everything is fictional. Do not create a real alarm, interrupt services, simulate distress to bystanders, call emergency services or enter an unavailable site. This is practice in bounded coordination, not incident-command qualification.

## Stabilize the situation within your actual role

The immediate objective in a disruption is often narrower than the original mission. A workshop leader may need to prevent travel to an unavailable venue before considering how to continue the event. People’s safety and the responsible site's instructions take priority. Leadership does not mean personally diagnosing a building problem or directing responders without authority.

Build a shared picture that separates confirmed facts, unknowns and rumors. Add source and time where they matter. “The venue owner says the room is unavailable today” is different from “there is a dangerous fault,” even if someone repeats the latter. Do not invent a cause to make the update feel complete. A known operational restriction can be enough to stop the event while the reason remains unconfirmed.

Assign responsibilities that fit both competence and authority. Someone can ask the venue owner for status without inspecting equipment. A communications owner can draft the attendee update without making independent safety claims. An alternative checker can establish availability without committing the group to an unapproved room. Get acceptance from each owner and name the person who decides whether to defer or cancel. A status board with unaccepted names conceals gaps.

Communicate a useful action now and a next update time. State what is known, what people should do, what is still being checked and when you will speak again. Acknowledge inconvenience without offering reassurance you cannot support. An accessible text or agreed alternative channel should carry the same material instructions. Multiple people improvising different versions can create confusion even if each wants to help.

A message being prepared, sent, received and understood are different states. Track what the case actually provides. Missing acknowledgments may need follow-up through permitted channels, but do not invent contact details or claim everyone is safe from a delivery indicator. The leader remains responsible for identifying unresolved reach, not merely announcing that communication is complete.

Set a decision time that leaves room for action. If the alternative remains unconfirmed at that point, use the authorized fallback instead of waiting indefinitely. A review time is still useful when there is no new information: explain that uncertainty and the current instruction. When conditions change, retain the original sequence and revise explicitly so an old hopeful possibility is not mistaken for a current plan.

Preserve learning after the pressure eases. Ask which task lacked an owner, which information arrived late and which assumption was unsupported. Separate individual action from system preparation, such as a missing update channel. One smooth tabletop cannot show how the same people will perform under real stress or that a venue plan is adequate. Preparedness improvements remain proposed until actually approved and performed.

## Worked example

A coordinator says, “We will probably use the spare room,” before its owner responds. Participants hear a relocation instruction rather than a possibility. A better update says the current room is unavailable, asks people not to travel there for the workshop and names the next update time while the alternative is checked. Honest uncertainty can give clearer guidance than an optimistic guess.

## Complete fictional tabletop brief

It is **25 November 2026, 16:40 UTC**. A paper-map workshop is scheduled for **18:00–18:40** in Cedar Center, Oak Room. No attendee is onsite in the supplied starting facts. Authorized venue steward Venn reports: “Oak Room is unavailable for today's workshop. Do not bring the group here. I will confirm venue status at 16:50.” The cause is unconfirmed. A rumor says an electrical fault occurred; no competent authority has confirmed that claim and nobody in this exercise should investigate it.

Coordinator Sal may defer or cancel this workshop. Sal cannot approve venue safety or command any emergency response. Venn controls site status and instructions. The group's ordinary emergency procedure remains with responsible site personnel and authorized services; if the tabletop introduces an actual emergency, stop the exercise and use the actual applicable route. The supplied case introduces no live emergency.

There are **12 registered attendees** on a fictional roster held only by the communications role; no private names or addresses are supplied or needed. **Kim** accepts responsibility for the approved attendee text and acknowledgment tally. **Oli** accepts asking Venn for the status update. **Pia** accepts checking whether Birch Room is available and approved for this use. Sal owns the decision at **17:00** and the next attendee update by **17:05**. All owners confirm acceptance in the starting packet.

The permitted channel is the group's existing accessible text notice; a previously agreed phone relay can be used by Kim for attendees who need it. This describes fictional authority, not an instruction to contact anyone now. The alternative is **unconfirmed**, so no relocation instruction may be issued. The limited immediate objective is to prevent workshop travel to Oak and give a definite next update. The initial message must be labeled “TABLETOP DRAFT — DO NOT SEND” in your exercise output.

## Stabilize, communicate and reassess

1. Make a status board with safety/site authority, known facts, unknown cause, rumor, objective and accepted owners. Output: a factual picture that keeps venue investigation outside the team’s role.
2. Draft the first update with action now, empathy, uncertainty and the 17:05 update commitment. Output: one accessible unsent message and acknowledgment plan, with no unconfirmed relocation.
3. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Use its changed facts to decide by 17:00 and update by 17:05. Output: a versioned decision and communication record with unresolved reach retained.
4. Review timing, ownership and what people could understand. Output: one specific preparedness improvement, its proposed owner and the distinction between rehearsal observations and live readiness.

## Support and harder conditions

A paper timeline, text-to-speech, quiet pace and visible roles keep the task about coordination rather than theatrical stress. A harder case removes Kim's acknowledgment tally: retain reach as unknown and identify a permitted follow-up owner. Do not make the drill realistic by surprising anyone. Actual crises require the local plan, responsible authorities and qualified help; this scenario supplies no emergency procedure or safety certification.
