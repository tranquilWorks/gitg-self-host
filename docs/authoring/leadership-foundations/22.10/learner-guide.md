# 22.10 — Organizational politics and stakeholder navigation

Pursue an accessible meeting-time proposal by understanding interests, influence and the legitimate decision route. Spend twenty minutes on the stakeholder map and fifteen on the revised proposal. Politics here means how people and groups affect decisions in practice. Understanding that process does not authorize hidden favors, personal profiling, coercion or incompatible promises.

## Understand influence without inventing motives

Begin with a defensible objective and a principle you will not trade. A proposal can seek broader participation while preserving truthful communication and a fair hearing for those burdened by the change. “Get my preferred time approved” is narrower and can tempt a proposer to hide costs. State what success would mean for affected people and which legitimate interests may still conflict.

Formal roles identify who can approve, allocate a room or interpret a rule. Informal influence identifies whose judgment people seek, who remembers past failures and who can bring a credible concern into the discussion. Ignoring informal influence may make a formally correct proposal impractical. Treating influential people as obstacles to manipulate can destroy the trust needed to implement it.

Separate a position from the underlying interest. “Keep the old time” might reflect setup burden, transport availability, continuity or simple preference. Ask instead of assigning a motive. Do not record private vulnerabilities as persuasion targets. Use relevant, volunteered information and distinguish confirmed constraints from your hypotheses. A coalition is a shared position or coordinated effort supported by evidence, not a secret plot inferred from two people knowing each other.

Listen across the decision boundary. The person approving the schedule may not own the room, and the person supporting access may not be able to provide setup. A useful agreement combines actual accepted contributions. A promise to “find someone” leaves ownership unresolved. Test whether your proposal still works when a key supporter cannot supply what you hoped.

Negotiation can reveal compatible interests without satisfying every preference. A limited trial, alternate arrangement or transparent review can make a reversible proposal more workable. State who benefits, who bears inconvenience, who mitigates it and what the trial will evaluate. Do not call a burden small merely because it falls on someone else. Consent to a trial is also not proof that the longer-term change will succeed.

Preserve one consistent account. You may emphasize different relevant details for different audiences, but the material terms must match. Do not promise the scheduler permanence while promising opponents an automatic reversion unless both are part of the same agreed rule. Reject secret favors, misleading claims of majority support and public attribution without permission. A proposal you could explain openly to affected people is easier to defend.

Use the authorized route and retain refusal as a possible result. If the decision is declined, inspect the reason: a real constraint may suggest another option or a later review. Do not retaliate against a dissenter or declare corruption merely because influence affected the discussion. Likewise, official approval does not erase unfair means used to obtain it. Assess both the objective achieved and whether trust and principles were preserved.

## Worked example

A proposer assumes a longstanding member opposes a later meeting to protect status. A conversation reveals that the member supplies setup and cannot stay later. The member is not a scheduling authority, but their work is a real dependency. Finding an accepted alternate setup owner could change the proposal; promising an unconfirmed helper would merely hide the same constraint.

## Complete fictional stakeholder packet

The Cedar Map group currently meets **17:00–17:40 UTC**. Proposer Nila seeks a **17:30–18:10** trial for two meetings so members whose stated work schedules end at 17:00 can participate. This is a participation objective, not a claim that every member prefers the change. Scheduling chair Farah has approval authority. Room steward Ellis controls room availability and confirms the room must be clear by **18:20**. Setup and clearance each require **10 minutes** of accepted volunteer work.

Morgan is a longstanding member whose practical judgment others often seek. Morgan currently sets up and clears but says, “I must leave by 18:00; I cannot clear at 18:10.” Morgan has no veto under the supplied rules. Members Paz and Rui confirm that 17:30 would help them attend. Their views do not establish the views of the whole group. Nila initially writes “Morgan and Ellis are blocking us together”; no evidence of a shared position is supplied.

Two consultation records are provided for your map: Ellis says the proposed finish leaves exactly ten minutes to clear, so a named clearance owner is essential; Morgan says the concern is workload and departure time, and that another accepted owner would address it. Farah asks for one consistent written proposal with dates **24 November and 1 December 2026**, trial limits, burden, unresolved concerns and review on **2 December**. Attendance, setup reliability and reported access difficulties would be reviewed; the supplied case includes no actual observations of them.

Nila can offer to own setup at 17:20 but has not yet found a clearance owner. The initial trial is therefore a proposal with an unresolved dependency. No private contact data, political preference or personal circumstance beyond the relevant supplied constraints is needed.

## Navigate toward a defensible decision

1. Map formal powers, informal influence, evidenced interests and unknowns. Replace the unsupported coalition claim. Output: an accurate map and a non-negotiable principle of truthful terms and fair access.
2. Use Ellis and Morgan's accounts to revise the proposal. Calculate timing and state who benefits and who bears work. Output: two-date trial with an explicitly unresolved clearance dependency.
3. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Incorporate the accepted or declined contributions and Farah's decision. Output: one final account all affected people could see, with no incompatible promises.
4. Review objective and process separately. Output: approval status, work commitments, remaining disagreement and a truthful limit on participation or trust claims.

## Support and harder routes

A written consultation, interpreter or neutral facilitator can support participation without making public speaking a requirement. A harder case has no willing clearance owner: keep the original proposal unready, alter scope through authority or accept refusal. Do not claim nonexistent resources to secure approval. Actual procurement, employment, public-policy or political decisions have their own rules; this harmless scheduling fiction does not substitute for them.
