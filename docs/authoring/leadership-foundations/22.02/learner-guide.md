# 22.02 — Direction, vision, and priorities

Turn a worthwhile destination into a limited focus that another person can explain. Spend twenty minutes on the fictional direction brief and fifteen on its revision. The work is to connect purpose, capacity and immediate choices. A memorable slogan can help communication, but it cannot settle whose needs matter, authorize a project or make competing commitments disappear.

## Describe a future people can recognize

A destination states who should be able to do what under which conditions. “Become the best community group” is difficult to use when deciding between a new logo and clearer arrival information. “A newcomer can find the right session and know how to join it without guessing” makes the next obstacle visible. It still needs a legitimate purpose and the participation of people affected by the change. The ability to articulate a future does not entitle a leader to impose it.

Ask why that experience matters and what another worthwhile goal might lose. Newcomer access may deserve attention while existing members also need continuity and volunteers need bounded work. A direction is stronger when it acknowledges those goods and proposes a defensible balance. Do not portray every objection as resistance to progress. A person can understand the destination and reasonably disagree about its cost or timing.

A priority is a choice under constraint. For each proposed priority, show its next action, accepting owner and progress signal. Count effort as well as the number of headings. A list with three broad headings can conceal twenty projects. Distinguish preparatory evidence, such as verified information, from an eventual outcome, such as someone successfully participating. Neither a polished brief nor a completed task proves the destination has been reached.

Non-priorities make opportunity cost inspectable. Naming something that was never seriously considered proves little. Identify attractive work that would use the same scarce time, explain why it waits and state when it might be reconsidered. Deferral is not a permanent judgment of worth. It is a boundary that stops today's attractive extra from silently displacing today's agreed essential.

Ownership is also a constraint. A named person has not accepted responsibility merely because a leader wrote their name in a table. Check capacity, needed authority and dependencies. When an owner declines, reduce, resequence or renegotiate the work; do not make the capacity problem invisible. The person who sets priorities remains responsible for resolving the conflict rather than instructing the team to be more committed.

Check understanding by asking for a fresh explanation: what future are we trying to create, what happens first and what waits? Repetition of your exact words can conceal confusion. If a person explains the destination but opposes a tradeoff, record disagreement rather than reteaching until they agree. If the wording creates the wrong task, revise the brief. A comprehension check is not a vote, consent receipt or evidence of enthusiasm.

Review triggers keep direction responsive without making it arbitrary. A date gives a planned checkpoint; changed capacity or a blocked essential gives an earlier reason to reconsider. Preserve the destination unless the evidence challenges it, and be willing to change the immediate route. Explain what changed and why so people can stop obsolete work.

## Worked example

A team says “welcome newcomers” but lists a new brand, video, directory overhaul, social campaign and entrance check as this week's essentials. Only the entrance check addresses an immediate inability to join. After comparing capacity and affected people's needs, the team can choose a smaller set and explicitly defer publicity. More visitors to a confusing process would not itself demonstrate better access.

## Supplied direction materials

The fictional Atlas Circle exists to let people compare public maps together. Its members authorize Noor to propose this week's preparation priorities; the group must confirm the final scope. A newcomer can currently find the date but not the entrance or a questions contact. Existing members want the already agreed **40-minute** session preserved. No event expansion or change in venue is authorized.

Available preparation capacity is **60 person-minutes**. Candidate tasks are: **A**, correct the date/time/location notice, **20 minutes**, Lee tentatively available; **B**, confirm the venue's approved entrance wording, **15**, Pat tentatively available; **C**, name a welcome contact and add participation instructions, **25**, Ari tentatively available; **D**, draw a new logo, **30**; **E**, make a promotional video, **45**. Estimates are supplied planning assumptions, not measured durations. A, B and C each serve the proposed newcomer experience. D and E may help later publicity but do not answer the missing joining questions.

Proposed signals are: A has one current checked notice; B has venue-owner-confirmed access wording; C has an accepted questions role and a reader can say what to bring and whom to ask. The initial brief says “Make Atlas Circle bigger through a welcoming brand.” A reader may interpret that as publicity or expansion, neither of which is authorized. The review is **4 November 2026 at 16:00 UTC**, sooner if an essential owner or fact is unavailable.

## Produce and revise a direction brief

1. Replace the slogan with a concrete future experience and why it serves the Circle's purpose. Name existing-member continuity and volunteer capacity as competing goods. Output: destination, value, constraint and who must shape or approve it.
2. Select at most three priorities from A–E. Calculate effort, list next actions and provisional owners, and make D and E explicit candidates for deferral if you choose A–C. Output: an achievable proposed focus and at least two non-priorities with reasons.
3. Write a plain-language request for ownership and a destination/first-step/non-priority understanding check. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Output: proposed rather than presumed commitments.
4. Incorporate the changed capacity and two supplied interpretations. Distinguish misunderstanding from disagreement. Output: a revised effort total, accepted owners, unresolved tradeoff and review trigger.

## Support, challenge and actual use

A spoken brief, simple picture sequence or large-print task cards can replace a dense table. A harder case keeps capacity at forty minutes with no adequate substitute for C: state that the destination is only partly supported this week and seek a genuine scope/date decision. Do not claim that a missing welcome route exists. Real use requires the group's legitimate decision process and affected people's input; the fiction checks your reasoning, not collective commitment.
