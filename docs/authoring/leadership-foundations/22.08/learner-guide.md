# 22.08 — Conflict, dissent, and psychological safety

Invite a specific challenge before finalizing a plan, respond without status defense and close the loop. Set aside fifteen minutes to design the invitation and fifteen to review the fictional responses. A single discussion can show how a concern was handled; it cannot certify that a team is psychologically safe or require a less powerful person to demonstrate courage.

## Make disagreement usable before requesting it

Begin with what can still change. Asking for input after an irreversible decision while implying that the plan remains open misrepresents people's influence. A specific question about a missing dependency or unclear responsibility makes participation easier than “any objections?” Give people time and an accessible alternative to public speech. A private or asynchronous route can reduce exposure but has limits that should be stated accurately.

Power affects the interpretation of silence. A participant may have no concern, may need more time, may not understand, or may fear consequences. None of these can be diagnosed from silence alone. Do not call on the least powerful person to prove openness. Let people pass and retain unknowns in the plan. A unanimous-looking meeting can coexist with an unspoken problem.

Receive the concern as information to examine. Summarize it fairly, ask what supports it and state what will be checked. Thank the contributor without making them responsible for solving every related problem. You can disagree with a proposed solution while taking its underlying observation seriously. Conversely, good faith does not make every factual claim correct; inspect the evidence and explain the result.

Protect challenge while addressing harmful conduct. An insult or discriminatory comment may need a separate response, but it does not logically erase a valid concern raised in the same conversation. Separate the factual question, behavior and appropriate channel. Avoid treating discomfort with criticism as proof of harm, or using “safety” to prohibit any challenge to a leader's preference. Do not stage hostility or solicit traumatic disclosures for practice.

Follow-through gives the invitation substance. Record an owner, next check and response time. At that point say what changed, what stayed and why, and what remains unknown. A logged concern with no response can teach people that speaking wastes effort. If the decision stays unchanged, provide the relevant evidence rather than requiring agreement with your authority.

Privacy and protection claims must fit your power. Explain who receives a private note and when an issue would need referral. Do not promise absolute confidentiality or that retaliation cannot happen. Take responsibility for access and assignments within your control, and preserve an appropriate route for concerns beyond it. A person should not have to publicly identify themselves to receive a factual plan update.

Psychological safety concerns expectations about interpersonal risk over time. One person speaking once or one leader responding well is incomplete evidence. Ask whether the route was usable without demanding disclosure about personal fears. Look for patterns and missing perspectives before drawing broader conclusions. The task can produce a bounded record of responsiveness while the group's wider climate remains unknown.

## Worked example

A participant says an entrance plan assumes access before the venue opens. Another calls the participant negative. The facilitator can stop the personal label, check opening authority and avoid announcing that the whole plan is unsafe before the facts are known. If access is confirmed later, the concern still served a useful verification function; it need not be punished for failing to reveal an actual fault.

## Complete fictional dissent packet

The Paper Routes team plans a session on **18 November 2026, 18:00–18:40 UTC**, Cedar Center, Willow Room. Draft **P1** asks setup volunteers to arrive at **17:30**, assumes an entrance is open then and has no named person to confirm access. The date, duration and approved room are fixed; setup timing and assignments remain changeable until **17 November at 12:00 UTC**. Facilitator Hana can revise the plan but cannot certify venue access.

Hana's proposed question is: “What input or responsibility would make this plan hard to carry out?” Participants may reply during the paper discussion or send a private paper note to Hana before **11:00 on 17 November**. Only Hana initially reads private notes; facts needing venue verification go to steward Mo without attribution where possible. Serious issues outside Hana's role require the proper channel, so no absolute confidentiality promise is made. Participants may decline to comment.

The supplied comments are: **A**, a private note, “I have only seen confirmation for 17:50 access; who approved 17:30?”; **B**, an in-meeting remark, “People who ask these questions are always negative”; **C**, silence from two attendees, with no stated reason. The note author has not consented to public credit. Mo is available to check access at **11:15**. Hana promises a factual update by **11:30**, before finalization. These are all fictional interactions, not invitations to send messages.

## Invite, respond and close the loop

1. Write an invitation explaining the changeable scope, routes, time and privacy limits. Output: an invitation that allows passing and does not claim silence means alignment.
2. Separate A's evidence question, B's conduct and C's unknown meaning. Name Mo's authority and Hana's follow-through. Output: a factual acknowledgment, a bounded conduct response and a checking plan.
3. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Revise P1 using the venue evidence, keeping the author's attribution choice. Output: plan version, update content and on-time or late response status.
4. Inspect whether access or assignments within Hana's control changed as punishment. Record what is known and not known. Output: a narrow response-quality finding and an unanswered question about the route's usability.

## Support and higher difficulty

Offer written, signed, spoken or assisted input without requiring a public explanation of the access need. A harder branch has the concern implicate Hana: route it to an actually authorized independent person and disclose limits instead of privately adjudicating it yourself. Actual harassment, discrimination, danger or whistleblowing concerns require appropriate support and current procedures beyond this harmless rehearsal. Do not pressure someone to report them for an exercise.
