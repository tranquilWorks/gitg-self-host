# 22.04 — Decision-making under responsibility

Choose a room by a deadline, record the reasons against your preference and update the choice when the relevant facts change. Allow twenty minutes for the decision and fifteen for the later review. Both fictional rooms already have responsible-owner approval for the stated uses and access arrangements. The exercise cannot establish real building safety or grant authority to override an actual restriction.

## Decide with uncertainty without concealing it

Frame the decision before comparing attractive features. Who has authority, by when is a choice needed, which options are permitted and what happens if you wait? Some missing information blocks a responsible choice: required approval cannot be guessed. Other uncertainty can be managed with a reversible plan, such as a range of attendance estimates when every plausible number fits an approved option.

Seek the expertise that matches the question. A venue steward can confirm permitted capacity; a participant can describe an access need or communication difficulty; a booking coordinator can explain a hold deadline. Confidence in one role does not create expertise in the others. Consultation improves the information available to the decision maker but does not transfer all responsibility to advisers.

Invite the strongest reason against the leading option. This is more useful than asking supporters to list benefits after you have effectively decided. State dissent fairly, including a condition under which it should change the choice. A quieter room may aid conversation while a larger room better accommodates uncertain attendance. Do not disguise a tradeoff by claiming that the preferred option wins every criterion.

Separate constraints, estimates and preferences. A maximum approved occupancy is a boundary, not a target to exceed if the group is enthusiastic. An estimated headcount is a forecast, not a confirmed count. A pleasant atmosphere is a preference that may matter after essentials are satisfied. Arithmetic can rule out an option without supplying the full judgment. A responsible choice explains both why it fits and what remains uncertain.

Reversibility has a time window and a cost. Being able to edit a booking note does not guarantee that another room remains available or that attendees will receive the change. Record the last useful switch time and the communication owner. If the window closes, a previously reversible plan may need a different response. Avoid implying that a decision is low risk merely because the document is editable.

A review trigger is evidence that calls for reassessment, not an automatic excuse to ignore everything else. Define it before the new fact arrives. Preserve the original rationale and add an update rather than rewriting the first record to make yourself look prescient. You can own the consequences of a decision while distinguishing a preventable error from a reasonable choice followed by new information.

Timeliness matters too. Waiting for perfect knowledge may forfeit both acceptable options. Make the best supported choice within the deadline, identify residual risk and communicate the next step. A later revision need not mean the first choice was reckless, and a lucky outcome cannot rescue a process that ignored a known boundary. Evaluate the decision using information actually available at the time.

## Worked example

A coordinator chooses a room approved for ten because eight people are expected. A recorded trigger requires reassessment above ten while a larger-room hold remains open. A later count of twelve activates the review. Moving to the larger room can be responsible follow-through, not proof that the coordinator should have predicted twelve. Keeping the small room to appear consistent would misuse the original decision's authority.

## Complete fictional options and evidence

On **5 November 2026 at 12:00 UTC**, Rem must book a room for the next day's paper-map workshop. Rem has delegated booking authority within **30 units** of fictional budget. Elm Room costs **20**, has approved capacity **10**, a quiet layout and a venue-confirmed step-free route. Oak Room costs **30**, has approved capacity **16**, the same confirmed access suitability and more background sound. Neither cost is actual money to spend.

Venue steward Tal confirms both rooms' stated use and capacities. Facilitator Uma says the quiet setting helps spoken comparisons but can use a printed prompt sheet in Oak. Participant Sol confirms that the supplied access arrangement meets the needs disclosed for this fictional session; this is not a universal access assessment. Booking officer Fen says the initial choice is due at **12:00** and a no-fee switch between these two held rooms is possible until **15:00**, after which an unchosen room may be released.

At **11:30**, there are **8 confirmed attendees** and **2 possible**, with no other expected arrivals. Rem prefers Elm. Uma's strongest caution is that additional confirmations beyond ten would make Elm unsuitable, and a late switch could confuse attendees. Fen owns recording a booking change; Uma accepts responsibility for sending the single approved attendee update in the fiction. The initial decision must state what happens if the approved capacity would be exceeded or an essential approval is withdrawn.

## Make an accountable choice

1. Build a compact card separating authority, deadline, constraints, expertise, affected-person input and attendance uncertainty. Output: viable options and missing facts that would block either.
2. Compare cost, capacity margin, communication usefulness and reversibility. Record Uma's dissent fairly. Output: choice by noon, residual risk and a specific attendance/switch-window review trigger.
3. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Reassess using the new count and remaining time. Output: appended decision, difference from the original assumptions and assigned communication action.
4. Review whether any known constraint was ignored initially, whether the update was timely and what the record cannot prove. Output: process judgment distinct from the eventual workshop outcome.

## Support and harder conditions

Use two option cards and a calculator; arithmetic assistance does not remove decision ownership. For a harder branch, let the attendance update arrive after 15:00 with Oak unavailable. Keep Elm's approved limit intact and seek an authorized alternative, rescheduling or capacity arrangement; do not improvise building approval. Real transfer should concern an ordinary reversible choice within actual authority. Safety, clinical and emergency decisions retain qualified processes beyond this task.

## Scope and evidence boundary

The canonical record has no professional_boundary field. These practical safeguards do not add canonical metadata. Applicability and normative status both remain role_conditional. Taking a leadership role is not compulsory. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Use actual authority and willing participation; keep protected records outside app evidence.
