# 22.05 — Delegation and empowerment

Delegate a five-topic agenda with usable authority, a deliberate stretch and one checkpoint. Spend fifteen minutes on the agreement and fifteen reviewing the handoff. Use the supplied fictional roles or a willing peer on harmless work. Delegation should let another person exercise judgment while real work advances; it is not a way to transfer blame or obtain unpaid availability.

## Match the task, support and authority

Readiness is task-specific. Someone may write clear summaries but be new to allocating discussion time. Ask what they have done, what is unfamiliar and which support helps. Do not infer general competence from status, confidence or one error. Choose a stretch that can be checked without exposing people to consequential risk. Keep tasks requiring qualifications or authority outside the delegation unless the proper process supplies them.

Explain the intended result, its users and why it matters. An agenda is not merely a list of headings: participants need to know which topics require discussion, which choices are already made and when the meeting ends. Define acceptance criteria before the person works so they can judge their own draft. Provide approved inputs and access, not just a deadline. Lack of access cannot be repaired by exhorting someone to take ownership.

Separate decisions the delegate owns from those that require approval. Wording and topic order may be theirs while meeting length, budget and official commitments remain with another person. Responsibility without the relevant discretion produces avoidable waiting. Unlimited discretion without context can create preventable errors. An agreement should make both boundaries intelligible to the person doing the work.

Confirm responsibility through willing acceptance or the applicable legitimate role process. A task appears on your list of assignments before it is actually delegated. If a person cannot accept, resolve capacity or scope rather than recording imaginary ownership. In a peer setting you cannot manufacture supervisory authority. A person can ask for support or decline a voluntary task without proving disloyalty.

A checkpoint is a purposeful occasion to surface a risk or inspect a draft. It differs from continuous status demands and silent surveillance. Agree what should trigger an earlier question: conflicting approved facts, an impossible time total or a request outside authority. Then allow valid choices within the boundary. Secretly rewriting someone's work makes the person accountable for an artifact they did not control and prevents a useful review of their judgment.

Intervene transparently when an essential criterion or real boundary is at risk. Say what the issue is, why it matters and which decision remains theirs. A correction need not reclaim the entire task. For example, if the draft runs five minutes long, ask the delegate to redistribute discussion time within agreed minima. Supplying the complete revised schedule would change the amount of independence demonstrated.

Review work outcome and development separately. The agenda can be accepted while the new time-allocation skill still needs a checklist. A person can learn from a failed draft while the work remains incomplete. Ask them to explain one choice and one help trigger. Continued accessibility aids do not automatically negate independence; supplied answers do. Decide next scope using observed readiness and requested support, not a desire to declare empowerment complete.

## Worked example

A lead delegates an agenda but dictates every word, then praises the delegate's initiative. The file may be usable, but the process gave little discretion. A better agreement reserves the meeting's purpose and hard limits while allowing the delegate to choose order and timing within them. If the lead dislikes a valid order, that preference alone does not justify taking control back.

## Complete fictional agenda assignment

Kira asks Jo to prepare a plain-text agenda for a **30-minute** planning meeting on **9 November 2026, 17:00–17:30 UTC**. Jo accepts after confirming twenty active preparation minutes. Jo has experience writing summaries and wants practice allocating time. Kira approves five required topics and minima: purpose/check-in **3 minutes**; review last task **4**; compare poster layouts **8**; choose next owner/date **5**; recap/open questions **3**. The remaining **7 minutes** may be distributed among topics. The comparison must precede the owner/date decision, and the recap must be last.

The supplied prior task was to compare two paper layouts: Layout A puts the date first; Layout B puts the location first. Neither has been selected. The meeting will decide which layout to revise, who owns the revision and its due date; Jo must not insert a decision already made. Jo may choose wording, permissible ordering and time distribution. Kira retains authority over the total meeting duration, changing required topics and any external commitment. No budget or public release is part of this work.

The deadline is **8 November at 15:00 UTC**. They agree one draft checkpoint at **14:00**, with an earlier question for conflicting source facts or a requirement that cannot fit. The paper source **AGENDA-INPUTS** is available to Jo. Kira accepts responsibility to answer a boundary question by **14:20**. Acceptance requires all five topics, at least the stated minima, a thirty-minute total, correct sequence, plain text and no invented decision.

## Delegate without taking back the work

1. Write the result, criteria, Jo's relevant experience and the time-allocation stretch. Separate Jo's decisions from Kira's. Output: accepted delegation agreement with available inputs and capacity.
2. Draft a valid agenda or use it to judge the later fictional attempt. Specify the checkpoint and early help triggers. Output: an inspectable thirty-minute plan with the seven discretionary minutes visible.
3. Save before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Respond to its actual defect without rewriting the full agenda for Jo. Output: a bounded corrective question and clear approval boundary.
4. Evaluate the returned agenda, Jo's explanation and support request. Output: acceptance status plus a specific next-scope decision, retaining any support still needed.

## Adaptation and further challenge

A calculator, large-print topic cards or a spoken agenda can support the same reasoning. A harder variant gives only twenty minutes of meeting time while the five minima total twenty-three: Jo should ask Kira to change an authorized constraint; hiding the conflict is not initiative. In real work obtain actual access, clear role authority and adequate time. Do not treat this agenda task as readiness for regulated, safety-critical or disciplinary responsibility.
