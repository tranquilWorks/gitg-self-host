# 17.10 — Networks and social capital

Offer one useful, permission-based connection or resource. Allow ten minutes to clarify the need, separate time for each person's consent, and a brief later check if welcome. Useful networking preserves people's freedom and trust. It does not turn a contact list into a stockpile of favors.

## Contribution before access

A network consists of relationships through which knowledge, opportunity and help may move. Social capital names possibilities associated with those relationships; it does not make the people your property. A person who once helped can be unavailable now. A highly placed contact may be less relevant than a public guide or someone with the specific practical experience needed.

Ethical contribution can be small: share a checked public resource, answer a question within competence, acknowledge another person's work, or make an introduction both people want. State what you actually know about someone. “Has led two beginner sessions” is more defensible than “can solve anything.” Do not imply endorsement, employment opportunity or unlimited free advice that was never offered.

An introduction joins two people's attention and information. Ask each separately before bringing them into a shared message. Permission needs scope: who will receive what details, why, through which channel, and what size request is contemplated. One person's agreement does not provide the other's. A published professional address is not consent to share a private history or enroll its owner in a group chat.

Trust is maintained after the connection too. Let the people decide whether and how to continue. You can ask whether the resource was useful without requesting their private conversation. Thank a helper without keeping a debt ledger, and continue ordinary contribution even when no immediate favor is available. Selectivity about time is compatible with care; relentless follow-up can erode the trust the introduction was supposed to support.

## Worked example

An acquaintance asks for help finding beginner sewing instruction. You know Lee has taught classes, but you do not know Lee's present availability. The library's public course list may answer the need without using a personal introduction. If a specific question remains, first ask the acquaintance whether you may describe that question and name them to Lee. Then ask Lee privately whether a brief exchange is welcome. Only when the permitted details and channel overlap should you connect them. “I know someone” is a possibility, not a completed act of help.

## Complete fictional case

Mina is new to a public hobby group and asks you where to learn basic paper folding. Mina wants a beginner resource, not a job, paid coaching or a private lesson. You know Arun casually through the group. Arun has publicly led two beginner sessions; no current personal tutoring offer is posted. The group's public beginner sheet is available at the desk and covers three starter shapes. You have checked that it exists and is free.

Mina says you may tell Arun that “Mina from the hobby group is looking for a beginner paper-folding resource” and may share Mina's group username, but not a phone number. Arun has not yet been asked. You have no authority to reveal either person's private contact information or add them to a message thread. A ten-minute conversation is one possible request; it has not been promised. No benefit or reply is guaranteed.

## Make the smallest useful connection

1. Restate the actual need and the least intrusive response. Compare the public sheet with an introduction: what would personal contact add? Output: a one-sentence need, one checked resource and a reason to ask for more only if necessary. Do not make an introduction solely to demonstrate how well connected you are.
2. Record Mina's exact permission and the missing permission from Arun. Draft a private question to Arun that includes only the approved description, asks whether any brief exchange is welcome, and allows an easy refusal. Output: two distinct permission statuses and a bounded request. A saved draft is not a sent request or a consent receipt.
3. Save your proposed action before reading the [later packet](later-packet.md). Use the response to decide whether to introduce, offer the public resource, or take no further action. If both eventually agree, specify the permitted channel and scope in a short factual introduction. Output: an actual disposition, including “no introduction” when permission is absent.
4. If welcome, check usefulness once without asking for private details or a return favor. Note whether you performed the agreed action and whether the person says it helped. Output: contribution made, usefulness known or unknown, and any commitment you still owe. Do not record a dossier of people's influence, vulnerability or indebtedness.

Keep factual permission notes minimal and outside a public application record. For real workplace or community contexts, follow applicable confidentiality and conflict-of-interest processes. This exercise cannot authorize disclosure of client information, a protected mailing list or internal hiring material.

## Support and transfer

For a supported route, use only a public resource and an explicitly fictional permission sequence. Practice distinguishing “may ask” from “may introduce.” Someone can help compose a clear message without sending it or deciding for the recipient. A declined introduction can be the correct completed decision even though the original need remains.

For a harder route, handle a changed request: the beginner now wants ongoing private tutoring. That expansion needs new willingness and scope; earlier permission for one question cannot carry it. Do not create artificial scarcity or name-drop to pressure acceptance.

For real transfer, contribute something relevant in an existing professional or community relationship, honor any accepted limits, and maintain the relationship without making every contact a request. Later evidence could include a completed introduction and freely offered usefulness feedback. A successful introduction alone cannot establish durable trust or prove that a network is ethically maintained over time.
