# 18.03 — Partner selection and compatibility

Make a provisional relationship decision from relevant evidence and honest uncertainty. Allow thirty minutes to review, one willing conversation if suitable, and twenty minutes to reconsider. This role-conditional adult practice is optional, with no requirement to find a partner or remain partnered. A single life is legitimate. Use the complete fiction if a live decision would be unwanted, unsafe or inappropriate; never create a relationship to complete the task.

## Eight areas of fit

Values concern what each person treats as important when choices have costs. Character is inferred cautiously from conduct across situations, such as responsibility after a mistake; charm is not a complete assessment. Conflict style concerns what happens during disagreement, including whether people listen, repair, threaten or avoid necessary decisions. Do not manufacture a conflict to test it.

Direction concerns the life each person wants to build. Attraction can matter, but intensity alone cannot resolve incompatible aims. Trust concerns supported confidence and truthfulness; it is not achieved by passwords, monitoring or secret tests. Responsibility includes keeping or renegotiating undertakings and attending to shared consequences. Life constraints include location, work, care, finances and other realities that affect whether a desired arrangement is feasible.

For each area, separate an observation, an interpretation and an unknown. “They told me before changing the plan” is an observation; “they will always be reliable” is a much broader conclusion. One harmful act may be sufficient for a boundary without a large dataset. Conversely, a pleasant month cannot certify lifelong compatibility. Avoid demographic shortcuts about religion, class, gender, age or family background.

Intensity, loneliness, idealization and fear can make certainty feel urgent. Notice their influence without dismissing genuine affection. A useful decision can be “continue learning without increasing commitment,” “deepen by mutual agreement,” “keep the present form,” or “end or seek support.” The other person's choice matters too; your private conclusion cannot create a joint commitment.

Distinguish negotiable differences from fundamental or role-dependent mismatches. Different hobbies can coexist. Opposed wishes about children, safety or where to build a life may have consequences that love alone does not remove. Do not promise that someone will eventually change. Honest discussion of a consequential unknown is more respectful than a hidden compatibility examination.

## Worked example

Two adults feel strong attraction and enjoy time together. One wants to move permanently abroad; the other intends to remain near an existing care responsibility. They can appreciate each other and still face an unresolved direction and constraint mismatch. A decision to avoid a shared lease while discussing realistic options uses evidence beyond intensity. It is neither a diagnosis of the partner nor a verdict that the relationship was worthless.

## Complete fictional review

Adult partners Dana and Remy have been dating voluntarily for four months. They enjoy shared walks and each reports attraction. Dana has observed Remy keep three modest plans and notify Dana promptly about one necessary cancellation. During a disagreement about weekend scheduling, Remy listened and helped find an alternative. No threats or coercive dependency are supplied in this ordinary case; absence from a short case is not a real-world safety certification.

Dana wants to remain in the current town for at least two years. Remy has applied for a year-long placement in another city starting in January; the application result is unknown. They have not discussed whether either wants a long-distance relationship. A shared lease decision is due on 20 October, with no commitment yet made. Dana's fear of being alone is increasing pressure to sign. Neither person's wish about children or longer-term finances is supplied; these remain unknown, not assumed matches.

## Review and decide without a score

1. Make eight headings matching the areas above. Place the known observations under them and mark unknowns without filling them with guesses. Output: a bounded evidence review, not a numerical partner ranking or a dossier. Identify which unknown matters to the current decision rather than demanding answers to every intimate question.
2. Name Dana's attraction and loneliness pressure separately from the lease question. Draft a transparent question about the placement, location and whether a long-distance arrangement is desired. Share Dana's own intentions too. Output: a willing conversation proposal that allows a truthful mismatch, not an interrogation designed to secure reassurance.
3. Save the initial decision before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md). Reconsider it using only the new supplied information. Output: continue learning, deepen, maintain, end or seek support, with reasons and uncertainties. Do not call a decision mutual until both have agreed.
4. State what commitment is authorized now and a real review trigger before the lease deadline. If the answer is still unknown, choose an option that does not pretend it is settled. Output: next action, deadline and what could change the decision. Do not create tests, inspect private accounts or enlist friends to investigate the partner.

## Support and harder transfer

For support, work entirely from the fictional table and use a trusted appropriate adviser to separate observed conduct from fear-driven inference. Preserve the partner's privacy. Regulated financial or legal commitments need qualified advice beyond this educational decision. No one should be compelled to disclose private history to satisfy a worksheet.

For a harder fictional version, let positive evidence coexist with a fundamental incompatibility. Practice a respectful decision that does not erase either. For real transfer, discuss one relevant unknown willingly, revisit conduct over time, and accept that uncertainty may remain. Abuse, threats or unsafe dependency require suitable safety support rather than treating harm as a negotiable mutual-fit problem. Ending safely may require individualized help; immediate confrontation is not prescribed.
