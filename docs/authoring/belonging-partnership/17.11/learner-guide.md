# 17.11 — Intergenerational friendship and mentorship

Arrange a reciprocal exchange across adult life stages, not an interview in which one age group supplies all the wisdom. Allow ten minutes to agree a topic, twenty minutes together and a modest follow-up within two weeks. Use the supplied fiction when no willing, appropriate contact exists. Friendship and ongoing mentorship require mutual willingness over time; one conversation cannot establish either.

## Perspective without age stereotypes

Age and life stage can change the circumstances someone has encountered, but they do not establish expertise, virtue or technological ability. Ask what the person has actually tried. A younger adult may have relevant experience of a transition an older adult is facing; an older adult may be new to a particular role. Neither should have to represent a generation.

Guidance becomes useful when its conditions are visible. “Visit in person” might have worked when a small organization welcomed drop-ins; an online appointment system may now make that impossible. Keep the underlying lesson, such as asking clearly for a role, separate from the old mechanism. Examine technology, institutions, opportunity, care responsibilities and resources before transferring advice.

Reciprocity means that both can receive perspective and contribute something welcome. It need not mean equal minutes, equal expertise or a repayment of every kindness. One person might share an experience and the other notice a changed process or ask a question that helps the speaker reconsider it. Ask whether that contribution is useful instead of assuming that younger people should provide technology help or older people should provide life counsel.

Mentorship also has a boundary. Agree the topic, duration, confidentiality limits and whether another exchange is desired. A welcoming first conversation is not permission for ongoing demands. Where employment, assessment or care creates power differences, use the appropriate organizational process and make declining genuinely possible. Default to adults in an appropriate existing relationship. Work involving minors or dependent adults requires authorized programs and applicable safeguarding, not private unsupervised recruitment.

## Worked example

An experienced volunteer advises a new coordinator to ask everyone personally to join a rota. The new coordinator explains that the current group uses a booking portal and that some members cannot take calls. Together they retain the principle of a clear invitation while replacing phone rounds with an accessible opt-in message. The volunteer gains an updated understanding of participation barriers. The coordinator gains a useful question about making responsibilities explicit. Neither person needs to declare the other generation superior.

## Complete fictional exchange

Tess, aged 29 and starting a new volunteer role, and Nabil, aged 67 and recently retired from paid work, know each other through an open hobby group. They agree to a twenty-minute conversation on 4 October about joining an unfamiliar organization. Either may decline a question or use an ordinary fictional example. They agree not to discuss private family histories or share the conversation publicly.

Nabil offers one past experience: when joining a small association in 2008, arriving at the office and asking the coordinator for a specific task worked well. Tess's current organization states that roles are assigned through an online portal; unannounced visits are not accepted. Tess can explain the portal's publicly posted process if Nabil wants, but has no privileged access or authority to place anyone. The current first orientation is on 9 October. Neither has committed to a second meeting or an ongoing mentoring role.

## Exchange and adapt

1. Confirm the ordinary topic, twenty-minute limit, right to pass and preferred communication format. Write one question each person could ask about an actual transition. Output: a mutual agenda with no obligation to disclose painful events or defend an age group.
2. Ask what was tried, which conditions made it work and what the person would now reconsider. Record Nabil's specific experience separately from the general lesson you infer. Invite correction of your summary. Output: one experience, one possible lesson and at least one transfer limit. A quote remembered imperfectly should be described as a paraphrase.
3. Offer a relevant contribution back: Tess can explain the portal if welcome and can ask how to state a bounded task preference. Decide what to adapt, reject or defer. Output: a context-sensitive next action, not a promise to obey all advice. Save the plan before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md).
4. Follow up within two weeks if agreed. Report what was actually attempted and what happened, including a rejected adaptation or changed plan. Ask whether another exchange would be welcome instead of announcing a standing mentorship. Output: a dated follow-up, a concrete influence on thinking or conduct and the actual status of future contact.

A useful exchange may change your question rather than solve your transition. Do not exaggerate a small insight into a transformed life. Similarly, respectful disagreement with guidance can demonstrate careful reception if you explain the relevant changed conditions without dismissing the person's experience.

## Adjust the practice

For support, exchange short letters, use translation or an agreed communication aid, or prepare questions in advance. Make processing time part of the arrangement. An authorized community program can help find a willing adult contact. A simulated dialogue is suitable preparation but should not be recorded as a relationship with a real mentor.

For a harder version, compare two conflicting pieces of advice. Ask which conditions each assumes and choose a safe, reversible adaptation. Avoid a contest about which generation had life harder. For real transfer, repeat an exchange only when both wish, notice whether perspective and care move in both directions, and check burden as well as benefit. Maintaining one reciprocal relationship is a longer observation than completing today's conversation.
