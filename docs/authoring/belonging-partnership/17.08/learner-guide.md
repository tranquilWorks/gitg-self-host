# 17.08 — Collaboration and complementary strengths

Make one small shared product whose usefulness can be checked. Allow fifteen minutes to agree roles, thirty to work and ten to review. The real route needs willing collaborators with authority over their own contributions; use the complete fiction below when that is unavailable. A solo plan prepares for collaboration but cannot demonstrate a real handoff or joint benefit.

## Learn the distinction

Complementarity concerns the work: one person can verify venue facts, another can organize a clear explanation, and another can notice what a newcomer cannot infer. These capabilities need not reside in different people, and a confident speaker is not automatically the leader. Ask about current skill, interest in learning, access needs and available time. A permanent label such as “the creative one” can conceal expertise and trap someone in unwanted work.

Coordination connects contributions. A role needs a bounded output, a recipient and a time; “everyone helps” leaves the integration work invisible. A handoff also needs a return path for questions. If the writer discovers a contradictory address, the fact owner should help resolve it. Silently repairing everything can produce a polished product while hiding an unsustainable collaboration.

Difference becomes useful when it changes the product or catches a problem. Disagreement alone is not added value. Agree a definition of done before dividing tasks, and examine both benefit and coordination cost afterward. A successful joint guide does not prove the team outperformed isolated work without a credible comparison. It can support the narrower claim that identifiable contributions improved particular parts.

Credit describes what people actually did, including checking, coordination and revision. Ask how contributors wish to be named; public recognition is not everyone's preference. Sharing credit does not mean inventing equal workloads or treating a factual correction as an attack on dignity. Acknowledging a missed handoff can be respectful and accurate at once.

## Worked example

Two people prepare a meeting reminder. Bea knows the new room; Sol writes the invitation. Sol's attractive draft retains the old room from a previous message. Bea checks it before circulation, Sol revises it, and they ask a willing reader to locate the meeting from the revised version. The reader succeeds. They can identify a prevented wrong-room instruction and a usable revision. They cannot claim that two people are always faster: checking and revision also consumed time. If Bea had never received the draft, naming Bea as verifier would be false credit.

## Supplied fictional task

The Elm hobby group needs a one-page welcome guide. Its confirmed facts are: free paper-craft session, Saturday 3 October, 14:00–15:00, Cedar Hall Room B, entrance on Pine Street, materials provided, and questions through the group's public desk. Do not invent a private phone number or accessibility guarantee. A newcomer should be able to name where, when and how to ask a question.

Mira volunteers to check facts and has ten minutes. Dev volunteers to draft and wants practice making instructions clearer, with twenty minutes available. Jo volunteers to review from a newcomer perspective for ten minutes. Jo cannot see the tiny entrance label in the initial mock-up. All agree to these roles, to private acknowledgment, and to a draft handoff by 13:20. Mira's verified fact note is due at 13:10. At the starting point, Dev has an older note saying Room A; no corrected draft or reader test exists yet. You coordinate, but cannot spend other people's time beyond their offers.

## Make and inspect the work

1. Write the three newcomer questions as the definition of done. Record each willing role, available minutes, output and recipient. Include your own coordination work. Ask which task Dev wants to learn rather than assigning solely by past speed. Output: a small role agreement, not a personality inventory.
2. Prepare Mira's verified fact note from the supplied facts. Hand it to the drafter in the fiction. Draft the guide yourself as a simulation of Dev's part, preserving the date, duration, room, entrance and public contact route. Output: fact note and a complete usable draft, with fictional authorship explicitly marked.
3. Review the Room A/Room B conflict with the fact owner and enlarge or rewrite the entrance information so meaning does not depend on tiny print. In real work, return questions to the relevant contributor rather than quietly taking over. Output: the specific mismatch, its resolution and the revised artifact.
4. Save that version before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md). Use its reader response to judge the three questions separately. Then write a contribution account that participants could correct, plus the time or rework that coordination cost. Output: demonstrated benefit, unresolved usability, accurate credit and one process change.

The review is incomplete if it only says “great teamwork.” Identify the exact improvement and its source. If the reader still cannot find the entrance, a correct date does not cancel that defect. Keep proposed repair separate from a successful retest.

## Adjust and transfer

For support, use two willing people, a three-sentence guide and asynchronous written handoffs. Permit dictated text, large print or another workable format. Reduce the task before borrowing unoffered labor. A facilitator may clarify the agreement but should not substitute an absent person's contribution and call it teamwork.

For a harder attempt, change one verified fact after drafting. Require a version check so the obsolete fact does not survive in one copy. Do not manufacture a crisis or surprise a collaborator to test loyalty. For real transfer, choose a modest shared artifact people actually want, get role agreement, complete the handoffs, and ask a willing intended user to try the result. Record any actual feedback without private contact details. One successful trial supports this product, not a permanent team ranking.
