# Corrective answers

1. Compare the observation with the actual agreement: unavailability was expected, and no reply promise was broken on these facts. The feeling can still be real. A later direct request may be appropriate without inventing a breach.
2. Calling a demand a boundary does not change its effect. Continuous access can become surveillance and coerced proof. Seek a voluntary, proportionate communication arrangement and use appropriate support if control or fear is present.
3. No. Repeated breaches are relevant behavior. Regulate enough to choose a response, then discuss reliability, needs and fit or seek suitable support. The exercise must not convert all legitimate concerns into a defect in the learner.
