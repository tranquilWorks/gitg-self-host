# 15.06 — Outdoor survival fundamentals

## Plan a short outdoor session that can safely end early

Begin indoors with the fictional park card below. In about thirty minutes you will allocate seven survival priorities, trace a return route and respond to an announced change. Use paper, ordinary clothing and closed drinking-water containers if available; no purchase is required. This default is planning practice. The real transfer route includes qualified supervision and an actual benign outdoor task before any claim of demonstrated outdoor skill.

## Priorities follow conditions

Survival priorities are not a rigid countdown or a competition to tolerate deprivation. Exposure, injury, weather, terrain, water access and the group's needs change what matters first. For this short session, warmth and a reliable return route can matter more immediately than finding food in the wild. Do not use “rules of three” as permission to wait for a medical problem or continue without water.

Shelter reduces environmental exposure; it is only useful if suitable, accessible and available. A distant cabin with unknown opening hours is a question, not a shelter plan. Warmth includes suitable layers and staying dry, not simply carrying a fire starter. Water must be safe and adequate for the actual trip and personal needs. Clear-looking stream water is not established as drinkable; treatment methods require instruction appropriate to the hazard. This exercise uses carried drinking water and does not teach purification.

Navigation joins your position, a route and a destination. Naming a landmark without knowing how it relates to the map is incomplete. A phone map needs battery and availability; a paper map also needs interpretation. Signaling is a plan for reaching help with location and circumstances. Merely carrying a whistle or phone does not establish that a message will be heard. Make no false distress calls or signals during rehearsal.

Fire may support warmth or signaling in some settings, but brings burn and wildfire risks and may be prohibited. It is not required here. Food means carrying ordinary suitable supplies, not foraging or trapping. The NPS Ten Essentials supports trip-specific equipment preparation; its trip guide emphasizes conditions, skills and shared plans. Neither resource makes this page a wilderness qualification. See S04–S05 in [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md).

## Worked example

Sam plans a short supervised walk near a staffed visitor building. Their initial note says “warmth: fire.” The site's rule prohibits fires, and Sam has no fire training. Sam replaces that dependency with appropriate carried layers and a return-to-building option, then asks the instructor to confirm suitability before departure. Fire stays “not authorized for this session.” This is a resolved disposition, not a claim to know firecraft.

## Fictional park and equipment card

The session starts at Visitor Building V at 10:00. A marked level path reaches Junction J in 10 minutes at this fictional group's assessed pace. The same path returns from J to V in 10 minutes. A farther viewpoint P adds 20 minutes each way from J. No other routes are established. The instructor's session rule is: do not continue beyond J after 10:20; cancel or return earlier for changed conditions, unsuitable equipment, discomfort or an instructor decision. Times are case facts, not universal outdoor limits.

V is staffed until noon and has drinking water. The map is available on paper. J has no building or confirmed mobile signal. The forecast on the card says mild and dry, but the instructor must recheck actual conditions before any real departure. A small stream near J has no established treatment assessment. Fires are prohibited. The group has ordinary food, carried drinking water, suitable dry layers, a charged phone, a paper route card, and an instructor-approved emergency shelter item. A trusted contact knows the route, expected return and agreed response to an overdue group. No actual contact is made in this fictional exercise.

## Rehearse with all seven priorities

1. Draw V–J–P with the stated times. Trace the return from J. Explain why reaching J does not commit anyone to continuing to P.
2. Give shelter, water, warmth, navigation, signaling, fire and food a specific disposition. Use “carried and checked,” “qualified supervised practice needed,” “not needed for this session,” or “not authorized,” with the relevant item or reason. Do not mark an item checked if you only read its name.
3. Rehearse one harmless retrieval indoors: locate your paper route card or an ordinary suitable layer you already own. If none is available, record missing rather than imagining successful retrieval. Do not construct a shelter around your body, use heat, or test exposure indoors.
4. At fictional J, read this announced cue: it is 10:22 and the instructor reports worsening weather. Decide whether to continue, return with the instructor using the established route, or seek a different supported action if the route is no longer safe. Under the supplied facts the return route remains suitable. Trace it and update the expected arrival and contact message.
5. Say a practice-only help description without dialing: location reference, group situation, relevant conditions and callback route if available. Distinguish information known from missing. If a real emergency occurs, use local emergency instructions; the rehearsal is over.
6. Identify one weakness: inaccessible layer, unlocated map, unverified contact arrangement, or a plan depending on the prohibited fire. Correct only a harmless authorized item and repeat that check. Save what was actually retrieved and what still needs instruction.

Open the [later packet](later-packet.md) after committing your route decision. Your output is a seven-priority disposition card, return calculation, help-message rehearsal and one honest retrieval result. It can show preparedness reasoning, not survival performance.

## Supervised and adapted routes

For a smaller first step, work V–J only with a large-print or tactile route representation; a support person may read the card. Preserve their contribution. A harder paper version removes mobile coverage and asks what the trusted contact knows. The response is to resolve the communication arrangement before the trip, not to wander toward imagined reception.

For real transfer, arrange a qualified instructor-led benign session near established support, with consent, accessibility, local rules, weather and individual needs checked beforehand. Agree on a stop/return rule and what the instructor will observe: map orientation, safe use of the approved warmth/shelter equipment, carried safe water/food arrangements, and a practice-only location message. The instructor selects and supervises any physical task; no fire, untreated water, fasting, cold exposure, remote overnight stay or navigation challenge is required by this guide. Record the actual task and correction, supervision and conditions. An indoor alternative remains indoor evidence. Complete the [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before the [key](check-answers.md).
