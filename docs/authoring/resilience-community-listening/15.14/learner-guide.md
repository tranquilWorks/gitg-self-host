# 15.14 — Workplace and activity-specific safety

## Remove a small hazard and stop at the edge of your authority

Use the fictional work cards, then perform only the harmless paper-handling task described below if your setting is suitable. Allow twenty-five minutes. You need a few sheets, a stable reachable surface and a clear ordinary route. Do not use tools, chemicals, machinery, electricity, ladders or heavy objects. Never create a hazard to make the practice more impressive. The skill is recognizing hazards, selecting an effective authorized control and stopping when safe conditions are not established.

## Think through the task, not just the equipment

Hazard analysis asks what could cause harm during setup, the activity and cleanup; who could be affected; and which conditions make the harm possible. A clear workspace can become obstructed when materials are put away. Repetition without injury does not prove a shortcut safe. Workers should have a way to report uncertainty and stop unsafe activity under the applicable site process without being treated as the problem.

NIOSH's hierarchy orders controls from elimination and substitution through engineering and administrative measures to personal protective equipment. Removing a needless obstruction can address its source; warning someone to step around it relies on repeated attention. A substitute can introduce a different risk, so check the result. PPE remains important when appropriate, but it must fit the hazard and user and does not make an unsafe process acceptable. See S17 in [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md). This guide does not select respirators, electrical PPE or other specialist equipment.

Ergonomics concerns the fit between the task, person and environment. For this small paper task, a comfortable reachable position, sufficient light and avoiding awkward repeated reaching matter. There is no single posture everyone must imitate. Adjust only what you can do safely and are authorized to change; assistance or a different setup may be appropriate. Do not stretch, climb or move heavy furniture to fulfill the exercise.

An off switch is not proof of energy isolation. Powered equipment can retain electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic or thermal hazards, or restart unexpectedly. HSE's maintenance guidance addresses isolation, stored energy and competent maintenance. See S18. Actual lockout/isolation is an equipment- and site-specific process for trained authorized people. This paper exercise never asks you to unplug, open, test, lock, remove a lock, clear a jam or service equipment. Asking a qualified person to assess the issue is an action; pretending you performed isolation is not.

## Worked example

A folder cart is parked across a walkway. The first proposal is “tell everyone to be careful.” A responsible worker with permission relocates the cart to a designated clear bay without handling a load beyond their ability. They recheck that the bay does not block another route. If moving it would be unsafe or unauthorized, they stop the affected task and report through the site's process. The preferred control depends on what can actually be done safely, not on always making the learner fix it personally.

## Original fictional work cards

| Card | Observed situation | Authority supplied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W1 | Three light paper folders on a stable table; a small empty cardboard sleeve lies in the walking route | You may move this harmless sleeve to the waste container if you can do so safely |
| W2 | Folder placement requires an awkward reach across a wide table | You may place the folders within comfortable reach on your own clear work area; no furniture moving |
| W3 | A heavy unstable cabinet leans near the retrieval route | You may stop and report to the site lead; you are not authorized or equipped to stabilize it |
| W4 | A powered cutting machine is jammed; a coworker says the off button makes it safe to reach inside | You have no maintenance or isolation authorization; designated qualified staff handle the machine |
| W5 | Cleanup puts discarded packaging in front of the exit | You may safely dispose of your own harmless packaging in the designated container; exit suitability remains the site's responsibility |

For the actual benign step, use your own clear table and a few ordinary sheets. Do not recreate W1's obstruction, W3's instability or W4's machine. If no suitable workspace is available, complete the paper analysis and record the physical task unattempted.

## Analyze, act and recheck

1. For W1–W5, name the hazard, possible effect, person affected and stage of work. Use concrete descriptions rather than “unsafe.” Include people passing through, not only the person holding folders.
2. Choose a control and who may perform it. Compare removing W1's harmless obstruction with a warning sign alone. For W2, explain how a reachable placement changes the demand. For W3 and W4, stop/report is the appropriate learner action; do not invent authority or a safe repair.
3. In your actual ordinary space, inspect setup, reach, light, route and cleanup destination before touching the sheets. Choose one harmless authorized improvement if needed, or record that no change was needed. Do not manufacture a defect.
4. Perform the small task: retrieve the sheets from a comfortable location, place them on the stable surface, then put them away without blocking access. Stop if pain, instability, a blocked route or uncertainty appears. A seated version or a helper following your directions is acceptable; record what assistance occurred.
5. Recheck the area after cleanup. Did your change create another obstruction or uncomfortable reach? Correct only a safe, authorized small issue, or report what prevents completion. Record before/after facts, not “all risks eliminated.”
6. Rehearse a stop-work report for W4 on paper: machine identifier if known, observed jam, no intervention performed, area/process needing authorized attention, and request for the site's qualified isolation/maintenance procedure. Do not operate any real controls. Then open the [later packet](later-packet.md).

Your evidence is a five-card analysis, an actual harmless-task result if attempted, and a scoped report. It does not certify workplace safety, legal rights in every jurisdiction, machine isolation competence or correct PPE for an unrelated activity.

## Make the stopping point credible

Before resuming a stopped real task, the responsible authorized process must establish that controls are adequate and the task is permitted. “Someone said it is fine” without the relevant role or verification is not enough. Report near misses and recurring shortcuts through the applicable process so the design can improve; do not normalize them because nobody was hurt this time.

For a smaller version, analyze W1 and W3 first to distinguish a safe small correction from an issue requiring escalation. Use accessible text, good lighting and your ordinary aids. A harder paper version has a proposed substitute that creates a blocked exit; reject or revise it based on the new risk, rather than treating all substitutions as improvements.

For real transfer, review a low-risk activity's authorized procedure with the responsible person: hazards, control choices, required equipment/training, report route, stop authority and restart conditions. Qualified instruction is needed for activity-specific risks, including actual lockout and physical techniques. This guide's paper jam scenario remains paper-only.
