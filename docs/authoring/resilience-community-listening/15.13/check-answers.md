# 15.13 — Corrective key — compare reasoning, not exact wording

1. Accept the refusal, allow space and avoid following or demanding explanation. A voluntary offer includes the option to decline. Different emergency or safeguarding facts could change obligations, but do not invent them to override this answer.
2. No. Assess a safe way to obtain or offer support, including delegation; documentation is not a substitute for help. Recording/use also has safety, consent and legal constraints. A safe filming position alone does not make publication or recording appropriate.
3. The report was received. Whether staff acted, whether the person wanted assistance and whether harm ended remain unknown unless actually established. Do not convert acknowledged delegation into a verified prevention outcome.

## Changed-case answer

Respect the refusal to represent them. You may ask the facilitator to apply the general turn-taking rule without naming private wishes, or offer a private practical option the person can decline. Avoid a public rescue narrative, forced disclosure or claiming they appreciated an intervention. Urgent danger would require a different assessment; it is not supplied here.
