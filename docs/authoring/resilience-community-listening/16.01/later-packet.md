# 16.01 — Later observations — original fictional case

The fictional speaker corrects one interpretation: “It isn’t that I hate planning. A timed exhibition ticket is fine. I don't want every hour filled. I need to be home by five, and I want us to choose where to sit afterward on the day.”

A revised reflection should retain: wants the exhibition together; accepts a timed ticket; wants an uncommitted choice afterward; needs to be home by five. “Out until five” was ambiguous if travel home also takes time. Ask about the return journey before proposing a time that uses the whole window away from home.

The fictional speaker then says, “Yes, that's what I mean. I'd like one suggestion, not a whole schedule.” A response with five competing itineraries would ignore the newly expressed request even if the summary was accurate. Offer one scoped suggestion only after the confirmation step in this simulated exchange.

Alternative outcomes matter: if the speaker declines feedback, confirmation is unknown; if the listener says “you just dislike planning,” the correction was ignored; if the speaker confirms but disagrees with a suggestion, understanding and agreement differ. If no real partner took part, no real speaker has confirmed anything.

Your actual artifact is the reflection and revision you wrote or rehearsed. The supplied “yes” is a learning example, never observer evidence about your real listening.
