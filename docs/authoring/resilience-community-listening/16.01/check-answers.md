# 16.01 — Corrective key — compare reasoning, not exact wording

1. Preserve both interest and the limit: “You want to contribute within daytime availability.” Ask, for example, “Which daytime hours could work?” Do not turn a constraint into disinterest or use “Why can't you just do evenings?” as disguised persuasion.
2. No. Confirmed understanding and agreement are different outcomes. Record the confirmation for this exchange, then engage respectfully with the disagreement without claiming the speaker accepted your plan.
3. Feedback is ambiguous; understanding is not clearly confirmed. You may gently allow correction without pressure if the conversation remains open, or leave confirmation pending. A smile or lack of challenge, especially across a power difference, is not reliable proof of agreement or understanding.
