# 15.04 — Personal safety and de-escalation

## Leave a tense exchange with a usable route

Start with the fictional queue below and a sheet of paper. In about twenty minutes you will make and rehearse a short exit decision, including what changes when help is unavailable. No one needs to act angry. You may speak seated, use a communication device, or move a paper marker instead of your body. The goal is a proportionate safety response, not winning an argument or proving composure.

## What de-escalation can and cannot do

A boundary states your own next action: “I’m stepping away now.” A demand tries to control someone else: “You will apologize before I leave.” The first can support an exit; the second can keep you engaged. Neither sentence guarantees safety. You are not responsible for another person's aggression, and leaving without the perfect phrase is a valid response.

Space and access matter alongside words. Distance can give more opportunity to disengage, but a fixed number of steps is not a universal safe distance. A move into an isolated corner may increase separation while removing options. Look for a route that suits your mobility, leads to help or a safer place, and does not require pushing past the person. Do not create distance by cornering them either. A crowded doorway can cease to be usable even when its sign still says exit.

A calm, short statement can reduce conversational friction. Explaining every detail, arguing about motives, making threats, or trying to extract agreement can prolong the encounter. You can acknowledge a practical concern without admitting a false accusation: “The queue is confusing; staff can sort it out.” HSE's workplace guidance supports calm communication, personal space and escalation to established help procedures; it does not prove a script will prevent violence. See sources S01–S02 in [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md).

Observe behavior and conditions, not a person's appearance or presumed diagnosis. A louder complaint may warrant ending a low-stakes discussion; a threat, pursuit, blocked route or weapon changes the situation more substantially. Do not stay to complete this exercise during danger. Seek a safer position and appropriate emergency help as circumstances allow. This practice is unsuitable for managing abuse, coercive control or stalking: an announced boundary can increase risk in those settings. Use specialist safety planning instead of testing this script on an unsafe person.

## Worked example

At a library counter, Lee is accused of taking someone else's place. Lee's first draft is, “Actually, I arrived first, and you need to calm down.” It adds a dispute and a command. Lee revises it to, “I’ll let the desk handle the order,” and identifies the staffed desk along a clear level route. The useful change is not simply fewer words: Lee now has an endpoint and a way to stop discussing the accusation. If that desk closes, the sentence alone no longer supplies the plan.

## Your complete fictional scene

You are collecting a reserved book. Another visitor says, “You jumped the queue.” They remain beside the queue rope; no one is threatening or blocking you. You do not need to collect the book today.

| Place | Current observation |
| --- | --- |
| North counter | Staff member present; level path around the queue, without passing the other visitor |
| East lobby | Open public entrance; level route, occupied reception visible beyond it |
| West alcove | Quiet, but a dead end with no staff |
| Queue opening | Narrow gap beside the complaining visitor; unnecessary to use |

A willing rehearsal partner may read that single accusation in an ordinary voice, then stop speaking. Agree on “pause” before beginning. Do not shout, touch, follow, obstruct, film, surprise, or use real grievances. Alone, read the line once or imagine it without performing the other role.

## Make and try the decision

1. Mark your starting point and two possible endpoints on paper. Choose the first route using the observations, not the shortest distance alone. Name one route you reject and why.
2. Draft one optional sentence that states your next action or redirects the practical issue. Remove any demand for apology, diagnosis, insult or promise of punishment. You may choose silence if speaking is unhelpful.
3. Rehearse once at normal intensity: read the prompt, state your sentence if using one, then point or move the marker along the chosen route. Say what specific help you would request at the endpoint: for example, help keeping the encounter from continuing while you leave.
4. Introduce this announced change: the north staff member is unavailable. Choose again using the same map. Do not invent replacement staff or require your partner to become more aggressive. Rehearse the changed endpoint.
5. Write the conditions that end this low-stakes rehearsal's applicability: pursuit, threats, loss of an exit, weapon, distress, or any other immediate danger. For the fictional case, identify how you would seek appropriate help without returning to argue. Do not make any actual alarm or emergency call.
6. Compare both attempts. Was the destination reachable under the stated facts? Did your words delay leaving? Revise one weak point and repeat that portion once. Save the original alongside the correction.

Your output is a two-route sketch, the optional sentence, one concrete help request, and the before/after correction. Record paper rehearsal or consenting low-intensity rehearsal, whichever actually occurred. A marker reaching reception is not evidence that a real confrontation would end safely.

## Read the outcome without inflating it

Open the [later packet](later-packet.md) after your first attempt. A clear endpoint supports this bounded planning skill. A calm phrase followed by an unavailable endpoint is mixed performance. Staying to force acknowledgment contradicts the exit goal. No rehearsal or no information about the route is inconclusive, not failure of courage. Your internal feeling of confidence is useful context but cannot certify safety.

## Adapt and transfer

For a lighter version, select between the north counter and west alcove and give one reason; then add the staff-unavailable change. Keep mobility aids and communication supports. A partner can move the marker under your direction without pretending you can physically use an inaccessible route. A harder paper version removes the east route too; a valid answer may be that the supplied plan has no established safe exit and needs venue-specific support, rather than inventing one.

For real transfer, during an ordinary calm visit identify the actual assistance point and a suitable way to leave. Do not create a disagreement to test yourself. If work exposes you to aggression, review the employer's procedures and relevant training with the responsible person. Keep incident notes factual and private under the applicable process. Then try the [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) and compare with the [corrective key](check-answers.md).
