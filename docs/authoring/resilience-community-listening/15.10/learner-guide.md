# 15.10 — Risk ownership and contingency thinking

## Give a fallback an owner, trigger and finite reserve

Use the fictional reading session below. In about thirty minutes you will make a small contingency plan, run an announced tabletop failure and decide whether to continue in reduced form or cancel. Paper or an accessible local note is enough. Nobody needs to lose access to a real device or miss an actual commitment. The skill is owning a risk through prevention, detection, response and recovery.

## A dependency is something the outcome needs

The outcome here is a hosted reading discussion, not preserving the organizer's preferred process. An agenda, a host, access to the room and a way to notify participants all support that outcome. A single point of failure exists when losing one component disables an essential function without an available alternative. Two copies on the same unavailable phone may still share the same failure.

Name the failure mode precisely. “Technology might fail” is too broad to guide action. “The only person with the room instructions cannot be contacted before opening” has a recognizable effect and detection point. An owner takes responsibility for a decision or action; that does not make them morally responsible for causing the failure. A backup owner must agree, have access and have the necessary authority. A name in a document is not consent.

Prevention reduces likelihood or impact beforehand. Detection tells someone when the plan needs attention. Response is what the authorized person does while the problem exists. Recovery restores an agreed normal state and reconciles changes made during the fallback. These stages can have different owners. NIST's contingency-planning publication addresses requirements and priorities for federal information systems; the small event exercise here is an author-created analogy, not NIST certification or a mandated standard for clubs. See S12 in [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md).

Reserves are usable capacity, not hopeful labels. Money already promised elsewhere is not free reserve. A volunteer who must leave before the minimum session ends cannot cover that minimum alone. Redundancy can also share a dependency: two messaging apps on the same locked device do not create an independent communication route. Define a cancellation threshold so the fallback does not consume essential resources or quietly transfer risk to someone else.

## Worked example

An organizer writes “backup: Noor.” Noor has never agreed and cannot access the agenda. The repair is not adding Noor's phone number to more copies. The organizer asks whether Noor can accept a bounded role, provides only the material needed, confirms a rehearsal and records the stop condition. If Noor declines, the plan retains an uncovered dependency and uses a cancellation route. Naming an unmet need is better than assigning imaginary capacity.

## Complete fictional session card

The reading group meets at 18:00. A minimum useful session needs a host for 30 minutes, the one-page agenda and authorized room access. A reduced session is permitted; a full session would last 60 minutes. No unsafe physical task or essential service depends on the event.

| Dependency | Current fact |
| --- | --- |
| Main organizer, Inez | Owns agenda and participant-message route; expected check-in by 17:40 |
| Agreed backup, Noor | Consented to host from 18:00 until 18:40; may use the supplied paper agenda and cancel; cannot spend money |
| Room | Staff confirms access for the group from 17:50; only authorized staff handle keys |
| Agenda | Opening question, 10-minute reading, discussion, close; harmless paper copy available to Noor |
| Notice route | Staffed group desk can post the agreed cancellation/reduced-session notice; no private participant list needed |
| Reserve | 40 minutes of Noor's availability; no extra budget or unconfirmed volunteer |
| Trigger | No organizer check-in at 17:40: Noor verifies absence through the agreed route, then decides by 17:50 |
| Recovery | Returning organizer and Noor agree who is responsible before handback; retain any notice and actual session change |

The paper agenda is: welcome and purpose; read the supplied sentence “A plan is useful when another person can carry out its next step”; discuss one everyday example; close by naming an unresolved assumption. The case supplies the agenda so you need not invent a reading program. It contains no confidential material.

## Run an announced tabletop

1. Map the four essential dependencies: person, information/device, place and communication. For each, state a failure, its effect, how it would be detected and who may act. Keep the room-key authority with staff.
2. Identify what the current card already prevents and what it cannot prevent. The paper agenda reduces dependence on Inez's phone; it does not guarantee a host or room.
3. Use the [supplied fallback card](materials/fallback-card.txt) to make a separate note containing the agenda, notice route, trigger, authority and time limits. Close the main case. Retrieve the note and answer the opening question, minimum duration, backup's end time and cancellation authority. Record any missing field and repair it before repeating.
4. Announce the simulated failure: at 17:40 Inez and Inez's phone are unavailable. Do not contact a real person or disable a phone. Using only the fallback card, choose the reduced session or cancellation by the stated decision point. Calculate the spare host time after a 30-minute session.
5. Write the notice you would authorize in this fictional case: what is happening, the time limit, where to find the current decision and no unnecessary explanation of Inez's absence. Do not publish it.
6. Open the [later packet](later-packet.md), respond to the changed reserve and then write a handback with owner, effective time, changes and remaining gaps. “Organizer returned” alone is not a completed handover.

Keep the dependency map, retrieved card, original decision, revised decision and handback. This is observable tabletop work, not proof the group has an operational continuity program.

## Review and extend

The first fallback works only if the stated access and availability hold. A useful review can find both a strength and a failure: the agenda was retrievable, yet the backup's shortened availability made the minimum session infeasible. Do not hide that defect by lowering the minimum after seeing the result unless the group explicitly authorizes a new kind of event. No drama or blame is needed.

For a smaller start, examine only agenda access and host time, then add the notice and room. A helper may read the note or assist with time arithmetic; retain the support used. A harder tabletop removes the staffed notice route while leaving the paper agenda available. Identify the communication gap; two good dependencies do not fix a missing third.

For real transfer, choose a low-stakes commitment you own, ask a willing person about a bounded backup role, and test retrieval during an announced practice. Share minimal material, agree authority and reserves, and record how responsibility returns. If no one can accept the role, prepare a clear cancellation route rather than treating yourself as infinitely available.
