# 15.11 — Community disaster readiness and mutual aid

## Coordinate one bounded mutual-aid task

Use the fictional neighborhood outage exercise below. In about thirty minutes you will assign only accepted tasks, relay a notice accurately and expose a transport gap without inventing capacity. This tabletop can prepare you to join a real community plan; it cannot establish that you participated with actual neighbors. No real alerts, household visits, welfare calls or requests for personal information are part of the default.

## Community readiness is a relationship with limits

A household can store supplies and still depend on neighbors, transport, public information and professional services. Mutual aid connects needs and willing resources, but it is not authority to enter homes, collect health histories or promise care. Ask what an existing group already does and where a bounded contribution would fit. People can decline help, decline volunteering or withdraw a previously offered role.

A resource has a quantity, availability, scope and owner. “We have a driver” is incomplete if the driver is unavailable when needed or cannot provide accessible transport. “We will check everyone” hides consent and capacity questions. Agree a welfare-check method, expected response and escalation route with participants in advance where possible. Lack of response is not proof of injury and does not authorize entry. Immediate danger uses appropriate emergency services; nonurgent unanswered check-ins follow the locally agreed organizer process.

Communication is more than forwarding. Preserve the source, time, place, action and uncertainty in an official notice. Ask the recipient to repeat the practical meaning, especially if a limitation could be lost. Do not turn “supply collection point” into “overnight shelter” or “planned” into “available.” Accessible language, interpretation and alternatives to an app help people participate without being required to disclose a diagnosis.

FEMA's volunteer-management material emphasizes defined roles, training, supervision and matching tasks to capability. It does not make an unaffiliated learner an emergency responder. See S13 in [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md). Recovery also needs coordination: close tasks, hand over unresolved needs, replenish agreed supplies and end access to temporary contact records according to the group's policy. Volunteering is not a promise of permanent availability.

## Worked example

A neighborhood group asks Pat to relay a verified notice to two consenting households. Pat offers twenty minutes and translation of the notice, but no transport or medical advice. The organizer accepts that bounded role. One household asks for a ride. Pat records the request through the agreed private route and asks the organizer to find a suitable provider. Pat's useful contribution is not erased by the transport gap, but the gap must not be marked solved merely because it was forwarded.

## Complete fictional exercise packet

Everything here is invented and labeled rehearsal. The fictional organizer, Mara, holds the official-source contact and coordinates assignments. Volunteers may stop, and Mara must reassign or leave a need explicitly unmet.

**Practice notice, Day 1 at 14:00:** The municipal desk says the power outage is being assessed. A staffed supply collection point at Hall H is open from 15:00 to 17:00 for sealed drinking-water containers. Hall H is not an overnight shelter. Transport is not included. Check the municipal desk for changes; restoration time is unknown.

| Participant or resource | Agreed scope |
| --- | --- |
| You | Twenty-minute notice relay and recipient teach-back; no transport, entry or care role |
| Jo | Can distribute four containers at Hall H under organizer instructions; no home delivery |
| Ren | Initially offers two passenger places at 15:30, ordinary vehicle; suitability for any particular passenger not assessed |
| Household A | Consents to a text notice and asks for two containers; can collect independently |
| Household B | Consents to a voice notice; asks for one container and step-free transport; no diagnosis requested |
| Household C | Declines participation; do not contact through this exercise |
| Supplies | Four containers allocated to this small fictional plan; professional services remain separate |

Mara's agreed process: report unmet needs privately using household labels, not medical histories; confirm whether a task was accepted, attempted and completed; unresolved nonurgent requests return to Mara. Nobody has permission to enter a home. The welfare-check component is a pre-agreed check-in with A and B by 14:30 on Day 1; at that deadline no response returns to Mara for the agreed next step, not to an improvised visit. Immediate danger would use emergency services rather than this tabletop queue.

## Relay, check and revise

1. Make a six-part outline covering communication, supplies, transport, welfare checks, skills/roles and recovery. For each, name what the packet actually establishes and what remains unconfirmed. Leave C outside the contact plan.
2. Draft a short notice in plain language retaining opening times, no overnight shelter, no included transport and unknown restoration time. Label it fictional rehearsal. Do not send it.
3. Role-play the relay with a willing person, using only invented household labels. Ask them to tell you when and where collection occurs and whether a ride or overnight stay is included. Alone, close your draft, then reconstruct those four details later and label it self-recall rather than recipient understanding.
4. Allocate the requested containers without exceeding the stock. Separate having a container available from B being able to reach it. Record transport suitability unresolved even before any driver withdraws.
5. Introduce the announced change: Ren is unavailable. Route B's unmet transport request to Mara, naming the limitation. Do not assign yourself, a stranger or an unsuitable vehicle to make the table look complete.
6. Open the [later packet](later-packet.md). Correct any meaning lost in the relay and close your own twenty-minute assignment with completed actions, unconfirmed delivery, remaining need, responsible organizer and the agreed end of temporary contact use.

Your output is a bounded task record, accurate notice, actual rehearsal method and unresolved-needs handover. A forwarded request is not a fulfilled request. Fictional agreement is not consent from real households.

## Participate without overclaiming

For a smaller task, relay just the opening time and overnight limitation, then add transport and restoration uncertainty. Use accessible text, interpretation or communication aids; verify meaning without testing someone's literacy or asking why they need an accommodation. A harder tabletop adds a competing request for the remaining stock; ask the organizer for the agreed allocation policy rather than inventing a moral ranking of households.

For real transfer, contact an existing willing neighborhood group or recognized local organization through its normal route. Offer a specific skill and availability, ask about training, consent, privacy and boundaries, and participate in an announced task or exercise if accepted. Record the actual role, feedback and correction, not names or vulnerability lists. Outreach without acceptance remains outreach; solo practice remains preparation. Complete the [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) and [key](check-answers.md) separately.
