# 15.05 — Self-protection and lawful force

## Evaluate self-protection without borrowing certainty

Use the three fictional training offers and decision points below. In about twenty-five minutes, choose whether to investigate, defer or reject each offer and draft a local-law question that a qualified adviser could answer. No physical contact, weapons, restraint, pain, surprise attacks or confrontation are part of this exercise. A good outcome is a credible training route and an honest limit, including deciding not to train yet.

## Protection has a purpose and a stopping point

Self-protection begins with avoiding danger where possible, leaving, getting help and reducing exposure. Physical defense can involve severe consequences; a written guide cannot make it safe or qualify you to perform it. Instruction should match your body, needs and setting, with consent, progressive practice, an effective stop procedure and appropriate injury response. A class's confidence claims and its practical evidence are different things.

Distinguish a protective purpose from retaliation. A choice directed at escaping an immediate threat differs from returning after reaching safety to restore pride or punish someone. Your emotions after danger are real, but anger does not itself establish necessity. Reassess as circumstances change; a response justified at one moment is not automatically justified later. Do not translate this ethical distinction into a universal legal formula.

Local law determines such questions as what force is lawful, how necessity and reasonableness are assessed, whether retreat duties apply, and how defense of another or property differs. Jurisdiction, setting, facts and current law matter. “Reasonable force” is not a fixed quantity you can memorize. A trainer's slogan cannot resolve an individual case. GOV.UK's page concerns intruders in the home and warns about continuing an attack after danger ends; it cannot be generalized to every street encounter or country. This is a limited legal-literacy example, not case advice. See S03 in [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md).

Aftermath belongs in preparation. First seek safety and appropriate urgent medical help. Follow relevant reporting requirements and qualified legal advice; distinguish your observations from uncertain recollections. Do not coordinate stories, post accusatory footage, pursue someone, or assume that absence of visible injury settles the matter. A support person or specialist service can help with the effects of an incident. Preserve privacy without concealing information that a lawful process requires.

## Worked example: an attractive claim with missing evidence

A flyer says “certified instructor; never lose again.” Rowan asks who issued the credential, whether it is current and relevant, and how a participant stops an exercise. The provider names a sports award but refuses to explain consent or injury arrangements. Rowan records “sport credential stated; suitability and safety process unresolved,” not “qualified for personal safety.” The promise of guaranteed victory is a reason to reject the claim, not a reason to demand a live demonstration.

## Supplied materials

All providers and statements are invented. They are not recommendations or claims about actual organizations.

| Offer | What is actually supplied |
| --- | --- |
| A: North Hall introduction | Noncontact discussion first; participant may stop or observe; named qualification and issuer available for checking; accessibility conversation offered; curriculum includes avoidance, exit decisions and aftermath; no guarantee; local legal referral provided |
| B: Invincible Weekend | “Works on everyone”; surprise attack assessment; cannot stop midway; “our certificate makes force legal everywhere”; no written injury plan |
| C: Riverside sport class | Relevant sport instructor credential stated; clear sport rules and consent; accessible trial possible; provider explicitly does not teach local self-defense law or street safety; no evidence yet about your personal learning needs |

A qualification listed on a flyer is still unverified. A relevant sport can be valuable without being a complete self-protection course. Rejection of B's unsafe offer says nothing about the dignity of its participants.

Your fictional incident has three checkpoints: P1, an argument ends and a clear exit is available; P2, from a safer staffed area you see the other person walking away; P3, help arrives and asks what happened. There is no continuing pursuit or attack in these facts. Do not add one to justify a preferred action.

## Build the training and decision record

1. For P1–P3, write the current protective objective and one action inconsistent with it. For example, at P2 preserve distance and support rather than returning to punish. Do not prescribe a physical technique.
2. For each offer, mark what is stated, what needs verification and what is disqualifying for this exercise. Include qualifications, consent/stop, adaptation, injury care, avoidance/escape teaching, legal limits and aftermath. A high total on other features cannot compensate for a refused stop procedure.
3. Choose a provisional next step. For A, it might be an information request before any attendance; for C, a sport-only trial if that meets a separate goal; for B, decline. Explain what evidence could change a deferred decision and what promise you would still reject.
4. Draft this local inquiry in your own words: “For [actual jurisdiction and setting], where can I read current official guidance on necessity, proportionality/reasonableness, retreat where applicable, and obligations after an incident? Which questions require individual legal advice?” Leave the placeholders clearly unresolved if you are using the fictional route. Do not fill them with internet folklore.
5. Create an aftermath sequence with safety, urgent health needs, appropriate reporting/legal help, factual private notes and personal support. Explain which step cannot wait for completion of a worksheet in a real emergency.
6. Audit your final wording. Replace “I can defend myself now” with what you actually did: “I evaluated training offers and identified legal questions.” Save the comparison and open the [later packet](later-packet.md).

## Keep the evidence specific

A provisionally suitable provider is not verified enrollment; enrollment is not observed skill; one observed skill is not guaranteed effectiveness under assault. Record setting, support, instruction and any assessed task when actual training occurs. You do not owe anyone a demonstration of violence to prove progress.

For a lighter version, compare A and B and explain the stop rule before considering C. Discuss or dictate rather than write if helpful. Avoid autobiographical violence accounts; the fictional checkpoints are sufficient. A harder version asks whether A's verified sport credential covers the proposed defensive curriculum. The answer may remain unresolved until a relevant accreditor or qualified reviewer explains its scope.

For real transfer, use an independently located local provider or qualified service to verify one relevant credential and discuss accommodations before committing. If risk involves coercive control, stalking, discriminatory targeting or a specific threat, generic classes may not address it; seek appropriate specialist planning. Complete the [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before the [corrective key](check-answers.md). Neither the key nor a course advertisement supplies legal advice or hands-on qualification.
