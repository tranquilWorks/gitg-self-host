# 16.04 — Make room for a conversation to move

Conversation involves more than asking good questions. People need opportunities to begin, contribute, follow a topic, move between topics, participate without being cornered and finish. This practice separates a one-to-one exchange from a small group so that success in one does not imply success in both. Set aside ten minutes to prepare, two ordinary willing opportunities over a week and a short review. Fictional rehearsal is available when people or suitable settings are unavailable.

## Reciprocity is a pattern, not a quota

A follow-up connects to what a person just offered. “What made that part interesting?” follows their experience; “What's your job?” may switch the topic. Either can be appropriate, but repeated switches can leave an exchange feeling like an interview. Sharing a related detail of your own gives the other person something to respond to. A long autobiography does not become reciprocal merely because you end with a question.

Research on question asking includes both positive findings and a methodological dispute about speed-dating data. The 2025 correction reports unchanged conclusions; a separate reanalysis challenges broader interpretations [S03–S04](../SOURCES.md#s03). This practice uses no question quota, promised increase in liking or equation between question count and listening. The observable aim is responsiveness to contributions and a workable opening for others.

In a group, inclusion is not compulsory speech. An open invitation, time to respond, a text channel or an accessible way to join may help. Calling a quiet person out by name and demanding their view can increase pressure. Silence can mean many things, and some groups use overlapping speech while others prefer explicit turns. Ask about a workable process when needed. Balance concerns the opportunity to participate, not identical seconds or a universal norm of quick replies.

## Worked example: two topics collide

At a puzzle club, Lee says, “The large pieces helped me finish this one.” Mo begins a story about a difficult puzzle, then notices it is taking over. Mo stops at the relevant point: “I had trouble telling the edge pieces apart. Did the larger pieces help with that too?” Lee explains that the issue was handling, not seeing. Mo follows the correction and offers to leave larger pieces on the shared table.

When Pat joins, Mo says, “We're comparing which pieces are comfortable to use; there's room here if you'd like.” Pat can join, watch or decline without explaining. Later Mo closes with, “I need to leave in five minutes; thanks for showing me the different pieces.” No promise of a future meeting is invented to soften the ending. The exchange is fictional and does not establish anyone's actual ease or satisfaction.

## Your two settings

Use these supplied role facts for rehearsal. In the pair, Jules has just said: “I tried making a small planter; the painting was fun, but finding time to finish was harder.” You enjoy choosing colors and have only five minutes before a scheduled task. Jules has not asked for productivity advice. In the group, three willing hobby members are arranging a display. Noor discusses colors, Tavi discusses table space, and Em joins after the conversation has begun. No one has asked Em to speak. The group has ten minutes before setup must start and can accept spoken or written suggestions.

1. **Open and sustain the pair exchange.** Use a context-linked opener that makes it easy to decline. Follow one part of Jules's contribution, then offer a brief related experience of your own. Avoid diagnosing poor time management or supplying a solution to an unrequested problem. Output: a short rehearsal sequence or minimal notes from an actual welcome exchange, identifying how your next contribution connected.
2. **Transition and close the pair.** Decide whether there is time for another topic. State the real ending constraint and close without an empty promise. If you interrupt accidentally, return the floor: “I cut across your point; did you want to finish?” Output: the transition/ending used and whether Jules had a chance to complete the thought. An ending is allowed even if every topic is unfinished.
3. **Join the group without replacing its purpose.** Briefly connect Noor's and Tavi's contributions, then offer Em an optional way to join. Suggest how spoken and written ideas can be considered before setup. Leave time for actual responses. Output: one connection between existing contributions, an invitation with an opt-out and a clear move toward the group's next activity.
4. **Use the [later packet](later-packet.md).** Respond to a correction in the pair and an opted-out invitation in the group. Output: what changes in your next turn, not a judgment about anyone's friendliness.

## Observe the exchange fairly

After a real attempt, note one place where you followed rather than switched, one contribution of your own, an inclusion opportunity and the ending. If a willing person offers feedback, preserve their actual description, including “that felt like too many questions” or “I wanted more time.” Do not secretly count another person's words or record their private story. Your review can contain only the interaction pattern needed to improve your part.

For support, practice with two prepared topic links, a quiet familiar setting, a written exchange or a mutually agreed turn cue. AAC and longer response times are compatible with reciprocity. A group that cannot provide enough time may need a format change. Do not interpret a slower response as disinterest or a quick interruption as a stable character flaw.

For a harder attempt, two members speak at once while another posts a text suggestion. Acknowledge the competing contributions, suggest a temporary order with agreement and bring the text idea into consideration without appropriating it. Let the task determine what needs a decision now; do not require everybody to share personal material to count as included.

For real transfer, attempt the pair and group separately over the week. A missing group opportunity stays unattempted. Compare what you actually did in each setting and choose one change for the next conversation. A partner's politeness is not proof that the exchange felt reciprocal; an honest correction is useful evidence. The [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) focus on situations where doing more talking or more asking would not solve the problem.
