# 16.03 — Corrective answers

1. Record folded arms and the person's cold-room explanation. Their decision on the proposal remains unstated. An inner rejection claim goes beyond these observations; treating their explanation as a denial that confirms your guess makes the inference impossible to correct. Ask about the practical proposal only if welcome and necessary.
2. Slow down, present one step at a time or offer text according to their preference. Ask whether they can follow the task or which step needs clarification. Audibility/comprehension evidence is more relevant than judging engagement from eye contact.
3. Preserve the initial inference as unconfirmed and record that clarification was declined and no later information was obtained. Do not relabel it correct, penalize refusal or invent follow-up. Existing legacy timing and fields remain unchanged; this companion supplies no new credit rule.
