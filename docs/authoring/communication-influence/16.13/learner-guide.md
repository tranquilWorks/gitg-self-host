# 16.13 — Offer warmth without making someone pay for the joke

Humor can add lightness to a welcome moment, but it is optional. Social ease also comes from making participation comfortable, remembering a preference and accepting a quiet response without demanding reassurance. This practice asks you to consider the target, timing and social cost of a playful comment, offer it at most once when appropriate, and respond to its actual effect. Allow ten minutes to prepare, one natural low-stakes opportunity and five minutes to review.

## The speaker's intention is not the whole interaction

A line may seem harmless to its author while making someone else feel exposed or excluded. Laughter can reflect amusement, politeness, nervousness or pressure; silence can have several meanings too. Keep interpretations tentative and take a direct objection seriously. You do not need to solve a person's inner state before stopping a line they say is unwelcome.

Kant and Norman's conceptual analysis discusses how social distance, power and culture can shape whether humor seems benign [S15](../SOURCES.md#s15). It is a theoretical discussion, not a validated formula for classifying a joke as safe or predicting how every person will react. This companion's target/timing/cost check is an original practical safeguard, not a diagnostic humor scale.

A shared harmless situation can offer playfulness without making a person the cost: an unnecessarily elaborate game setup might be “our opening puzzle.” Even that line can land poorly if someone has worked hard on the setup or is struggling to access it. Kindness means remaining willing to change course. Self-deprecation also needs care: a small non-sensitive mishap can be light, but “I'm useless; someone tell me I'm not” recruits others to repair your self-worth.

Humor is not an obligation to be quick, extroverted or culturally fluent. Quiet assistance, clear welcome and allowing a pass can create warmth. You need not laugh at ridicule to demonstrate resilience, prove belonging or preserve someone else's performance. Grief, danger, distress or a serious disclosure may call for straightforward attention instead of an attempt to lighten the mood.

## Worked example: too many instruction cards

At a familiar hobby session, a table contains several harmless instruction cards. Uma considers saying, “The instructions have brought their own instructions.” Before speaking, Uma notices someone is seriously asking for help finding the first step. Uma answers that question instead of interrupting it for the joke. Later, during a relaxed pause, Uma may offer the line once or simply say, “I'm glad we can work through this together.”

If no one laughs, Uma returns to the task. Explaining the joke, repeating it louder or asking “Was that not funny?” makes others responsible for rescuing the performance. If a participant says the line felt like criticism of their preparation, Uma can acknowledge that effect and stop without arguing about intent. Neither the imagined good timing nor the supplied response is real social evidence.

## Your case: a board-game setup

The fictional group is setting up a game with a large instruction pile. Pat is hosting, Casey is new and using a large-print rule sheet, and Rem is quietly sorting pieces. Pat says, “I spent a while getting these versions ready.” Casey asks which page starts the game. Rem has not spoken. You have prepared, “Looks like the instruction pile is tonight's first puzzle.” No one has invited jokes about reading, slowness, accent, memory or skill.

You also have three non-humorous options: point out the first-step heading without taking over, ask whether a clear space for the large-print sheet would help, or offer a warm greeting and let people settle. No diagnosis or explanation is needed for the format preference. A quiet person can participate without being made a target or called upon to perform sociability.

1. **Check target, timing and cost.** Write what your line is about, who might feel implicated, whether a serious question is pending and whether anyone would find it costly to object. Ask whether the line would still be acceptable if no one laughed. Remove any dependence on private vulnerabilities, identity, humiliation or insider exclusion. Output: a decision to use, revise, defer or omit the joke, with a concrete reason.
2. **Choose a moment or choose warmth.** Rehearse responding to Casey's practical question first. If play later becomes welcome, offer a brief line once and leave space. A simple warm acknowledgment is an equally valid choice. Do not require laughter or a reciprocal joke. Output: your chosen contribution and why it fits the current activity rather than merely your prepared material.
3. **Attend and repair without mind-reading.** Note actual words and context, keeping nonverbal interpretations tentative. If someone reports discomfort, acknowledge it briefly, stop the line and restore the practical activity. Do not insist that the person recognize your good intention before you repair. Output: a response plan that works for amusement, neutrality or an objection.
4. **Open the [later packet](later-packet.md) and add a warmth action.** Compare intended lightness with the supplied effect, then identify one practical or relational contribution independent of humor. In a real welcome opportunity, attempt it and review whether participation became easier. Output: impact/uncertainty notes and attempted or pending warmth status.

## Review connection without turning people into ratings

Ask whether your contribution helped people join, distracted from a task or excluded someone. These are distinct questions. A group can laugh while one member is hurt, or remain quiet while a helpful gesture makes participation easier. Do not tally smiles, rank people by humor tolerance or use an objection as evidence that they lack social skill. The useful learning target is how you respond and adjust.

For support, use a written greeting, remembering a stated preference, offering practical help with permission or making room without a speech. A person using AAC need not produce rapid banter. Avoid copied cultural references that require someone to pretend understanding to belong. If unsure about timing, omit the joke; the exercise remains substantive through the context decision, response and warmth action.

For a harder attempt, others laugh but the person implicated says the line hurt. Acknowledge that report and stop, even when the majority liked it. Do not poll the group to overrule the objection or turn your apology into a request for public absolution. A proportionate repair can be brief, followed by changed behavior.

For real transfer, use an ordinary familiar setting where your presence is welcome. Never manufacture distress or test a sensitive joke to gather a response. Preserve only minimal non-sensitive notes about your own action, any volunteered feedback and what you did next. If the attempt stays fictional, social connection remains unobserved. The [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) emphasize the ability to stay present and kind when humor receives no reward.
