# 16.10 — Corrective answers

1. Reduce distracting detail and strengthen the causal transition linking the person's goal, obstacle and result. Retest that mechanism in a fresh retelling. More entertaining detail may improve recall of the wrong thing.
2. Preserve the within-case causal claim and state its boundary: this delay arose from the missing note, while other delays may have other causes. An analogy or one example cannot establish prevalence or a universal explanation. Ask what evidence would be needed for the wider claim.
3. No. Recognizable details can still expose the person; a changed name is not permission. Use an original clearly labeled fictional case or an event whose sharing is authorized, retaining the explanatory mechanism without private material. Fiction must not be presented as evidence that a real event occurred.
