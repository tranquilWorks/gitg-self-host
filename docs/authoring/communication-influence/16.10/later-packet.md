# 16.10 — Later packet: a memorable but wrong lesson

Fictional retelling: “Ivo forgot to read the new sign because volunteers don't pay attention. Then the organizer explained the room.”

Compare with the supplied facts. Ivo followed the sign at the entrance; the new sign was beside Room C, not at the place Ivo was deciding where to go. The repair removed the obsolete entrance direction and installed the correct one there. The retelling changes a conflicting-information mechanism into blame and omits the physical correction.

Revise the transition that makes the two sign locations clear. Preserve Ivo's reasonable goal and say what the organizer changed. A better retelling need not repeat the story word for word, but should retain old direction at decision point, conflicting new direction elsewhere and corrected entrance instruction.

A second fictional retelling gets that mechanism right but concludes that all mistakes come from outdated signs. State the limit: this case explains one preventable source of error; other causes require their own evidence. These supplied retellings are practice material, not an actual listener's response or proof of long-term retention.
