# 16.02 — Say what happened and what you are asking

A useful difficult message lets the recipient identify the event, why it matters, the requested action and your own next step. It can be understood and still declined. The practice is to remove avoidable decoding while preserving the other person's freedom to disagree. Allow twenty minutes for drafting, a short voluntary rehearsal and ten minutes for revision. Choose an ordinary shared-item problem; do not use an unsafe relationship as a practice opportunity.

## Separate the parts before choosing the wording

An observation names an event someone could check: the projector was not on its agreed shelf at 18:00. “You never think of others” is an interpretation of character. Reasoning explains the connection between an event and your concern: without the projector you could not run the planned screening. A feeling belongs to the speaker; “I felt dismissed” may contain a claim about someone else's intention, whereas “I was frustrated and uncertain about the plan” makes less of that assumption.

A need names what matters, such as dependable access to shared equipment. A request proposes a specific action someone can accept, decline or counter. A boundary describes what you will do within your authority: lend your own projector only when a return arrangement is confirmed. It cannot turn a shared asset into your private property or make another person's compliance inevitable. These distinctions are planning tools, not a required six-sentence speech or a demand to disclose every feeling.

The NVC teaching aid separates observation, feeling, need and request [S01](../SOURCES.md#s01). This companion adds explicit reasoning and a controlled boundary to meet this competency's wider scope. It does not treat one communication tradition as culturally superior or promise that careful words prevent conflict. A formal note, indirect respectful opening, signed exchange or brief text can all carry a clear request.

## Worked example: a late file

Sam agreed to share the rehearsal file by Tuesday evening; on Wednesday morning it is absent. A first draft says, “Apparently the group doesn't matter.” That mixes a real delay with an unsupported motive and leaves the next action obscure. A repaired message is: “The file wasn't in our folder this morning, after the Tuesday plan. I need it to prepare Thursday's rehearsal, and I'm concerned about time. Can you upload the current version by 15:00, or tell me by noon if that won't work? If I don't have a usable version, I'll prepare the shorter exercise I already have.”

The alternative is Sam's to choose and the fallback is the speaker's to implement. If Sam accurately repeats the request but says 15:00 is impossible, clarity may have improved without agreement. If Sam thinks a finished polished file is required, the writer should clarify “current version” rather than repeat the same message more forcefully. The example is fictional; no delivery or understanding has been observed.

## Your case: the missing projector

These are the complete fictional facts. You own a small projector and lent it to Ari. The agreed return was Wednesday at 18:00 to the hobby-room shelf. You arrived at 18:15 and it was absent. You used a paper activity instead. You have no information about why the return was missed. You need the projector for Friday at 17:00; you can collect it on Thursday between 18:00 and 19:00 if Ari agrees, but cannot travel on Friday. You want dependable returns and are willing to pause further lending until a workable return plan is agreed. Ari has not yet replied. Your draft reads: “It would be nice if people were more considerate.”

1. **Make a fact-and-purpose note.** List the observed absence and agreed time separately from assumptions about Ari. Write the practical effect, your reasoning about Friday, one honest feeling if useful, and the underlying need. Mark the reason for the delay unknown. Output: six short planning lines, with no accusation disguised as a fact.
2. **Draft a request and a boundary.** Ask for a return arrangement that could actually meet Friday's need. Include a response time appropriate to the Thursday collection window. State what you control about future lending. Do not imply you may enter Ari's space or collect without agreement. Output: a message of roughly five sentences, with an answerable request and feasible fallback.
3. **Check decoding before using it.** Read or render the message in your intended medium. Remove one vague pronoun, hidden deadline or unnecessary explanation. Ask a willing rehearsal reader what happened, what Ari is being asked to do, and what you intend to do. Let them answer before showing your planning note. Record their actual wording or a minimal accurate summary with permission. If alone, do a delayed reread and label it self-review. Output: first version, comprehension mismatch and revision.
4. **Open the [later packet](later-packet.md).** Respond to the supplied counteroffer without converting understanding into agreement. Keep drafted, rehearsed, delivered, understood and agreed statuses separate. Output: your final practical disposition and remaining uncertainty.

## Adjust difficulty without changing the task

For support, use a two-column event/request card and add the boundary only after both are concrete. Have a trusted willing reader check language, use translation or AAC, or allow asynchronous processing. Keep private emotional details out if they do not help the practical decision. Clarity does not require a confrontational tone or a public conversation.

For a harder attempt, Ari disputes the original return time. Preserve the disagreement, refer only to an existing mutually available record if there is one, and ask what can be arranged now. Do not invent a screenshot, demand an admission or settle the history by repeating your version. The practical request may remain clear while the historical claim is unresolved.

For real transfer, choose one welcome low-stakes request within the next week. Get permission for a short clarity check, use a suitable time and channel, and record whether the recipient understood the request, accepted it, declined it or suggested another option. Do not send this fictional message to a real Ari. An actual artifact plus an actual recipient response can inform review; your satisfaction alone cannot show understanding, and neither kind of evidence automatically changes a score.

Finish with the [fresh checks](check-prompts.md). Keep an example of an understood refusal in your review: it prevents you from learning that communication only worked when someone complied.
