# 16.08 — Persuade with reasons an audience can inspect

Rhetoric concerns how a message gains attention, credibility and assent. Ethical skill involves recognizing those mechanisms and making a case without hiding material truth. This practice analyzes an appeal and builds a short proposal that an audience can question or decline. Allow twenty minutes for analysis, a one-minute draft and a short willing reader check. The case is a fictional community activity, not a targeted political campaign or a plan to exploit someone's vulnerability.

## Distinguish persuasive force from grounds for belief

In the classical account, ethos concerns the speaker's presented character or credibility, pathos the audience's emotions and logos the argument's reasoning [S09](../SOURCES.md#s09). These are analytic categories, not three mandatory tricks. A speaker can be sincere and mistaken; a moving example can be true but unrepresentative; a numerical claim can be precise and irrelevant. Identify what each device is doing before deciding whether the conclusion follows.

Framing makes some features more prominent: “a modest trial” emphasizes reversibility, while “our only chance” emphasizes loss and urgency. A narrative can make a consequence concrete. Repetition can help an audience remember a decision, but repeating a claim adds no independent support. A visual, spoken talk and short message permit different levels of detail and follow-up. Audience psychology includes attention, prior commitments and social pressure; understanding these factors should help you make reasons usable, not conceal weaknesses.

Dahlstrom's science-communication discussion links narrative's appeal with ethical questions about its persuasive use [S10](../SOURCES.md#s10). Here the teaching application is an original claim-and-burden audit. Neither a story nor an audience's favorable reaction establishes that the proposed activity will deliver its promised benefits. Material uncertainty needs to remain visible in the message itself, not hidden in a source list no recipient will read.

## Worked example: the tool shelf

A speaker says, “Everyone in a caring neighborhood wants a borrowing shelf. Imagine children learning to build; twelve people liked our post, so the shelf will pay for itself.” The first sentence makes disagreement a test of belonging. The image supplies an appealing possible benefit. Twelve reactions indicate interest in a post, not funding, demand or repayment. The financial conclusion has no supplied evidence.

A more defensible pitch would request a small, separately approved trial, identify what is known about space and costs, and ask who will maintain it. A listener can support the goal yet prefer another option. Removing a false guarantee may make the pitch less forceful while making the decision better informed. Do not replace one fabricated number with a more plausible fabricated number.

## Your proposal: one planting morning

Original fictional appeal: “Everyone who cares about this neighborhood must support the garden. Picture children learning outside instead of wasting another season. Three volunteers are ready, so lasting success is assured. Say yes now.”

Approved facts: the site owner permits one supervised planting morning on a small marked plot. A one-time supplies budget of 60 tokens is available and cannot be increased. Materials cost 48 tokens and cleanup supplies cost 12. Three adults have offered to attend that morning; none has agreed to ongoing maintenance. Future water access is unconfirmed. No evidence of learning outcomes or neighborhood-wide support has been collected. Alternatives are to postpone, hold a discussion without planting or decline. The actual decision requested is whether to proceed with this bounded morning, subject to a workable maintenance decision, not whether to approve a permanent garden.

1. **Annotate the appeal.** Identify ethos, pathos, logos, framing, narrative imagery, repetition if you add any, medium and likely audience assumptions. Mark where the language substitutes moral pressure or imagined benefits for evidence. If a mechanism is absent, say so; do not force every label onto every sentence. Output: a device/claim/support table.
2. **Audit material truth.** Separate confirmed permission, available money, attendance offers and unknown maintenance. Calculate the supplies total and remaining reserve. State which conclusions are unsupported: permanence, guaranteed education, complete local support and future volunteer commitment. Output: a claim ledger with a decision-relevant limit beside each important fact.
3. **Write a one-minute proposal.** Use a reason to care and a concrete image if helpful, then state the bounded decision, costs, unresolved condition and alternatives. Represent the three offers as attendance offers only. If you repeat a key phrase, repeat the trial's limit as well as its attraction. Choose a medium that leaves the facts and response route available. Output: a complete proposal with a real decline path.
4. **Check what the audience thinks is justified.** Ask a willing reader to identify the decision, strongest reason, emotional appeal, main uncertainty and option to refuse before seeing your ledger. Then open the [later packet](later-packet.md). Output: mistaken inference, revised claim and unresolved decision status. Being persuaded is not the same as understanding the grounds.

## Increase clarity without manufacturing assent

For support, begin with a four-line proposal: purpose, known facts, unresolved condition and choice. Use plain language, accessible text and enough response time. Do not rely on an attractive image to carry a material condition. A reader should not need to share the speaker's cultural references to identify what is being requested.

For a harder attempt, a supporter asks you to remove the maintenance uncertainty because “it will scare people off.” Retain it. You can explain how it could be resolved or narrow the proposal to planning only, but cannot imply a solution exists. Distinguish a legitimate deadline from invented urgency. Publicly attributing support to silent people or a respected organization without authorization remains misleading even if it would help the proposal.

For real transfer, choose a low-stakes proposal you are authorized to make. Verify claims with their actual sources, get permission for any personal story, name burdens and invite objections. Record whether readers understood the proposition and its limits; do not claim ethical quality solely because they agreed. The [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) ask whether you can improve an argument while retaining facts that make it less convenient to sell.
