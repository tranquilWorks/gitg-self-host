# 16.11 — Negotiate a workable package or choose a principled refusal

Negotiation is a joint search for terms that can serve interests within real constraints. Agreement is one possible result; a reasoned refusal can be better than an arrangement that either person cannot carry out. This practice uses two friends sharing a hobby room, with no money or binding commitment. Allow fifteen minutes to prepare, a fifteen-minute voluntary role-play and ten minutes for a recap. Do not use housing, livelihood, legal settlement, essential care or coercive dependency as the practice case.

## Know what you need and what you can do without a deal

A position states preferred terms: “I want the room quiet for the whole session.” An interest explains the purpose: “I need uninterrupted preparation before I can work alongside someone.” A constraint rules out some options: the room closes at 16:00. An alternative is what you can actually do if no agreement is reached. BATNA means the best available alternative to a negotiated agreement; it is not a threat, an imaginary option or a desired deal you have not secured.

A walk-away condition identifies terms you cannot accept. Fairness criteria help compare options without making power the only rule: equal useful access, alternating preferred periods or a mutually accepted allocation can each be relevant. Equal minutes are not always equal benefit. A package trades across issues, such as quiet time, shared time and interruption rules, instead of splitting every demand mechanically.

Harvard's Ombuds teaching material separates interests, options, alternatives, legitimacy and implementation commitments [S12](../SOURCES.md#s12). This companion does not adopt its suggestion to worsen the other party's alternative. The original case instead preserves refusal without punishment. PON's discussion of the agreement trap reinforces comparing a deal with a feasible alternative [S13](../SOURCES.md#s13); no result from a business simulation guarantees a good outcome between friends.

## Worked example: the display table

One friend wants a display table all morning to lay out work; the other wants it for a twenty-minute photograph. Their positions appear incompatible until they identify the time-sensitive part. A package allows setup first, a twenty-minute photo interval and then continued display, with a clear rule about who moves items. Both compare this with using separate tables.

The package is useful only if space, time and handling permissions are real. “You can photograph it later” does not work if daylight is unavailable then. A person may decline because moving fragile work is unacceptable. That refusal is information about the options, not evidence that the friendship lacks goodwill.

## Your role cards: two hours in the hobby room

Shared facts: the room is available from 14:00 to 16:00, with no extension. Both people can work separately at home at no cost. No one may commit the other person without agreement. This is a fictional rehearsal; no booking occurs.

Role A: you need at least 30 uninterrupted minutes of quiet preparation at the start. Afterward you would enjoy shared work. During preparation, headphones alone do not solve interruptions; you need no conversation directed at you. Your alternative is to work at home, quietly, for the full two hours. Your walk-away condition is a proposal that does not protect the initial 30 minutes or requires staying beyond 16:00.

Role B: you want at least 60 minutes of shared working time, enjoy company and are willing to begin with a quiet task. You must leave at 15:45. Your alternative is to work at home and arrange company another day; today's alternative does not provide company. You do not accept being responsible for remembering A's preparation time unless a visible agreed timer or written time boundary is used. These preferences belong to the role, not a real person's inferred mind.

1. **Prepare before making an offer.** Separate your position, interest, constraint, alternative and walk-away condition. List two possible interests for the other person as hypotheses, then consult their role card in rehearsal or ask them in a real willing exchange. Name a fairness reference and one leverage tactic you will not use, such as making friendship conditional on assent. Output: a preparation sheet that distinguishes feasible alternatives from wishful ones.
2. **Ask and build two packages.** Reflect the stated interests and propose exact times, an interruption rule and responsibilities. Consider a second package or a separate-plan alternative rather than demanding a midpoint. Explain gains and concessions for both people. Output: two compared options with arithmetic and constraints visible.
3. **Confirm or refuse clearly.** Ask each person to restate the selected terms, who does what and how a change can be requested. If a condition is not met, state the reason briefly and use the prepared alternative without retaliation. Output: a plain-language agreement or refusal, with implementation still unobserved.
4. **Open the [later packet](later-packet.md).** Recompute after a time constraint changes. Do not keep a once-feasible package by silently relaxing the other person's minimum or adding nonexistent room time. Output: revised feasibility and chosen disposition.

## What a better outcome means here

For the original facts, compare a package with reactive bargaining such as both insisting on the full period. The comparison should name which interests are served, what remains sacrificed and whether both can actually carry out the terms. Agreement quality and later follow-through are separate: a confirmed plan does not prove the timer was used or the quiet interval respected.

For support, exchange written proposals, give processing time or use a mutually acceptable neutral facilitator in a safe case. A person may prefer to state a constraint without its private reason. For a harder attempt, a feasible alternative becomes unavailable. Reassess honestly rather than pretending your leverage is unchanged. You may still decline an unsuitable agreement; an attractive BATNA is not a condition of deserving respect.

For real transfer, choose a voluntary low-stakes shared arrangement and verify actual alternatives and authority. Record the terms or principled refusal, then separately review follow-through at the agreed time. Where rights, legal obligations or coercion arise, use the appropriate qualified or formal process rather than expanding this rehearsal into advice. The [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) test whether you can recognize a respectful no-deal outcome as competent negotiation.
