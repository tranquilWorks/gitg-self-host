# 14.09 — Digital information verification

## Find the record that bears on the exact claim

Verification starts by making a claim precise enough to check. “The library is closing” could mean a doorway, a service, a day or an institution. Ten reposts of the same vague sentence do not clarify it. Identify the place, time, extent and asserted permanence before searching for evidence.

Allow 35 minutes with the fictional record set below. It provides complete text and search-result descriptions, so no live browsing is required for the default. Use paper or an accessible notes tool. You will produce a source-dependency map, a chronology and a short correction that can survive a later conflicting update.

## Read outward, then return to the proposition

Lateral reading means leaving a source's own presentation to investigate it elsewhere, locate original evidence and compare relevant independent accounts. [Brodsky and colleagues' study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-021-00291-4) examines instruction in these strategies in a particular college setting. It supports the method's research context, not a promise that this exercise makes every reader accurate.

Source independence concerns how information was obtained, not whether different websites display it. A reporter who copies a notice and an aggregator that copies the reporter form one evidence chain for the notice's contents. A direct inspection can be another chain, but its observer may have seen only one entrance at one time. Primary records also have scope, dates and possible errors; “official” is a reason to inspect closely, not suspend judgment.

Worked example: three posts say a train line is canceled. Their only source is one alert about the 09:10 departure on Tuesday. That alert supports the cancellation of that departure. It does not establish all-day suspension or permanent line closure. The correction should identify exactly what changed, with the alert's date, rather than calling every post wholly invented.

## Complete library records

It is May 5, 2026. All times are local. The question is: **Will the whole Alder Library close permanently from May 6?** Treat the following as invented training records, not events at a real library.

| Record | Full relevant content and provenance |
| --- | --- |
| A, May 4 at 18:00 | A neighborhood post: “Alder Library closes permanently tomorrow. Look at this CLOSED sign.” No named witness or document link; image P attached. |
| B, May 5 at 08:00 | Community roundup: “Alder is closing permanently,” linking only to A. |
| C, May 5 at 08:15 | Another feed item: “Confirmed by local roundup,” linking only to B. |
| N, published May 3 at 12:00 | Library notice: “West entrance closed May 6–8 for repairs. East entrance remains open during ordinary opening hours. Library services continue.” The established library notice page is its original location. |
| P | Cropped photo of a CLOSED sign. No visible date, library name or entrance. No original upload is supplied. |
| W, May 5 at 09:00 | Independent visitor's observation: “I entered Alder through the east door this morning and used the library.” It addresses that visit only. |
| R, supplied reverse-search result | A visually matching wider photo appeared in a public repair article dated May 2024 about Birch Library's west door. Whether P is exactly that file is not established. |

A's word “tomorrow,” anchored to its publication on May 4, means May 5. B's May 5 repost can make a reader mistakenly shift it to May 6. Preserve the original timestamp; the question's date must not silently move as the content travels.

### Action 1 — Trace the dependency chain

Draw `C → B → A` as citation dependencies. Mark N and W separately, and P as context-limited. Write what each record can directly support. Do not count B and C as independent confirmations of A. R is a possible image-context lead, not proof that a particular current claim is false.

List missing information that would matter: A's original source, P's full context and whether a current notice supersedes N. Avoid general credibility scores for the people involved. The task concerns one proposition and its evidence.

### Action 2 — Build the chronology and compare scope

Make a short table with publication time, event time and proposition. N has a May 3 publication date but a May 6–8 closure interval. W supports actual access on May 5, not future access. For the question as posed, N contradicts whole-library permanent closure by specifying one entrance and continued services.

Explain what a reverse-image search can contribute: earlier contexts or candidate originals. A match requires inspection of the actual result, including differences and dates; a no-match result cannot prove an image new, authentic or false. In a live case, use only appropriate public images and respect privacy and access controls. Do not upload private images to perform this exercise.

### Action 3 — Write a verdict that can be updated

Before opening [the later packet](later-packet.md), write up to 70 words that answer the exact question using N, explain the copied-source chain and preserve uncertainty about P. Include the relevant notice date and closure interval. Do not promise that no future closure will occur or attack the original poster's motives.

Then use the later record to update only what it changes. Current records can supersede older ones for a particular interval without vindicating every element of a rumor. Keep both versions in your notes so readers can see why your conclusion changed.

Complete [fresh checks](check-prompts.md), then [the key](check-answers.md). The final artifact includes the initial conclusion and dated revision. Revising in response to better evidence is part of verification, not a reason to hide the first record.

## Access and transfer

An offline route compares these supplied documents; it does not demonstrate actual lateral browsing or reverse-search skill. For a supported route, read the timestamps aloud and underline the words that limit place or duration. For a harder route, remove N and W. The permanent-closure claim then remains unverified; copying volume cannot fill the gap. Name the next original record or responsible source needed.

For real transfer, choose a harmless current service announcement. Open the source behind a headline, inspect its dated original record, compare one genuinely independent relevant source where available, and save a minimal link-and-claim note. If access fails, say what you could not inspect. End when the decision is adequately supported or a clear unresolved question has been identified; endless searching is not itself better evidence.

**Supportive:** Scope and chronology support a calibrated conclusion. **Mixed:** The initial rumor is overstated but a new limited closure requires revision. **Contradictory:** Reposts become independent confirmations or an old image becomes a current event. **Inconclusive:** The key record is missing and judgment is appropriately suspended with a specific next check.
