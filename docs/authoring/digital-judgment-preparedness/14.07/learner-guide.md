# 14.07 — AI literacy and appropriate use

## The useful draft still needs an accountable reader

Generative models learn patterns from data and generate content conditioned on inputs. That can help with wording, summarizing and exploring alternatives, but plausible output is not a guarantee that a claim was retrieved or checked. A model may confidently add a fact, misread a constraint or invent a citation. Tools that retrieve sources still need checks that those sources support the specific statements made.

Allow 35 minutes with the fictional source and sample draft below. You need no AI account, subscription or upload. The sample is deliberately authored for this exercise; it is not an observed output from a named model. Your job is to define an acceptable task, audit claims and framing, and decide whether assistance was worth using.

[NIST's generative-AI risk profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) discusses confabulation, privacy, harmful bias and human oversight. Those are possible risk categories, not a finding that any particular error here was caused by a particular training source. This exercise cannot establish a model's general accuracy or fairness.

## Task boundaries make verification possible

“Make this sound inviting” leaves room for invented offers. A better boundary identifies the audience, authorized facts, permitted transformation and accountable reviewer. For a short notice, facts should remain fixed while wording can change. When the source is silent, ask the owner or leave the claim out.

Worked example: a source says “registration opens Monday.” A draft says “places are guaranteed for everyone who registers Monday.” The day is supported; the guarantee is an addition. Asking the model “are you sure?” creates another generated statement, not independent evidence. Inspect the actual source or ask the responsible organizer.

Automation increases the consequence of an error by passing it to another step. An unverified notice sent automatically to hundreds of people is harder to correct than a private draft. Set a human release point before distribution. For medical, legal, financial or safety decisions, a small drafting success provides no authority to automate the decision or replace qualified judgment.

## Complete source packet

The fictional Cedar Club organizer supplies these authoritative facts for this exercise:

| Fact | Approved content |
| --- | --- |
| Day and time | Tuesday at 18:00 local time |
| Place | Room B, Cedar Hall |
| Cost | Attendance is free |
| Preparation | New members should bring a notebook |
| Accessibility | Arrangements have not yet been confirmed; questions may be sent to the organizer through the club's existing contact route |
| Audience | New and returning members; no age or occupation restriction is stated |

The authorized task is a notice of no more than 70 words. No external facts are needed. The club's invented publication rule requires a human to approve the final wording and requires disclosure if a real AI tool assisted drafting. The offline sample is a training exercise, so you must not claim actual tool use.

**Sample unverified draft:** “Young professionals: join Cedar Club on Thursday at 18:00 in Room B, Cedar Hall. A ten-dollar fee includes a notebook. Our fully accessible venue guarantees suitable equipment for everyone. The club welcomes new members. These details were verified in the Cedar Access Report 2026.” No such report is supplied.

### Action 1 — Specify the permitted work

Write a four-line brief: audience, fixed facts, permitted wording changes and human release point. Add an input rule: only this fictional packet. If using a real service later, inspect current data-use settings and organizational rules before providing material; “I have access to it” does not mean “I may upload it.” Do not use private membership lists, accommodation details or workplace records here.

State what the system may do when it cannot answer: identify the missing fact and return it to the human. This is a workflow rule you will enforce, not a guarantee that a prompt prevents errors.

### Action 2 — Audit claims and framing separately

Mark each factual unit **supported**, **contradicted**, or **unsupported**. A contradiction conflicts with an explicit fact; unsupported means the packet does not settle it. The day and fee conflict with the packet. Included notebooks, universal accessibility and the supposed report lack support. The time, room, hall and welcome to new members have source support. Split sentences that mix categories.

Then inspect the audience framing. “Young professionals” unnecessarily narrows an audience for which neither youth nor occupation is a requirement. This matters even if the logistical facts are repaired. Conversely, you cannot cure exclusion by promising unconfirmed accommodations. Preserve the request to bring a notebook and direct access questions to the organizer without claiming equipment will be supplied.

Record why the citation cannot verify itself: its existence and contents have not been established. For a real citation, open the actual record and inspect the relevant passage. A real-looking title or link is not enough.

### Action 3 — Correct, compare and decide

Write the notice within 70 words, preserving Tuesday, 18:00, Room B/Cedar Hall, free attendance and the notebook request. Remove the unsupported guarantees and invented report. Include the existing contact route for access questions if space permits. Review the output as the accountable publisher, then save it before opening [the later packet](later-packet.md).

Use the packet's effort records to compare manual drafting with assisted drafting **including verification and correction**. The faster initial draft is not necessarily the faster usable result. Either accept a reviewed draft, revise the workflow or choose manual drafting for this small task. The decision should follow this use case, not a blanket opinion about AI.

Complete [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the key](check-answers.md). Retain the brief, claim audit, corrected notice and use/reject decision. Do not publish anything as part of the default exercise.

## Adaptations and transfer

Use speech-to-text, a screen reader or a trusted reader with the same fictional facts. A supported route audits the six source rows one at a time. A harder route adds a conflicting second source: preserve both versions and ask which controls publication rather than silently blending them. Neither majority vote among model outputs nor repeated confident assurances resolves authority.

For real transfer, choose an authorized, low-stakes paragraph with a short inspectable source. Record actual tool involvement, check every factual addition and follow the setting's disclosure rule. Keep sensitive input out. If checking takes more effort than writing, use the manual route. A future tool or model update requires renewed evaluation of the actual task and policies.

**Supportive:** Facts and framing are reviewed before human release. **Mixed:** Facts are corrected but an access promise remains unsupported. **Contradictory:** The model's assurance is treated as evidence or publication is automated without review. **Inconclusive:** Risks are listed without auditing the draft or making a use decision.
