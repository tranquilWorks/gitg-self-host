# 14.05 — Online fraud, scams, and social engineering

## Verify the request, not the performance

A convincing message can still ask you to do something harmful. Social engineering uses authority, affection, fear, opportunity or time pressure to move a decision outside your normal checks. Phishing asks you to disclose information or follow a harmful route while impersonating a trusted source. Neither requires spelling mistakes. A real name, an accurate account detail or a familiar voice is evidence about what the sender knows, not permission to transfer money or reveal a code.

Allow 30 minutes with paper or an accessible editor. The supplied messages, institution and replies are fictional. There are no live links, real accounts or actual reports to submit. Your output will be six request assessments, an independent verification script and an exposure-specific response. This is a defensive practice; do not engage suspected scammers to gather more material.

## Independence is a property of the route

Calling a number inside the suspicious message asks the same channel to vouch for itself. Instead, start from a previously trusted app, statement or contact record. [FTC guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-scam) supports resisting pressure and contacting the purported organization through a separately trusted route. Do not treat the first sponsored search result as independently verified support.

Separate three questions: **Who is contacting me? What exact action is requested? What evidence authorizes that action?** A genuine service can have an urgent issue, but it does not follow that an unsolicited caller's proposed remedy is genuine. Conversely, an odd-looking notice can describe a real task that you can complete safely through the normal interface. Verification should resolve the particular request, not label all future messages from an organization safe.

Worked example: a message says a club subscription expires tomorrow. The existing club portal independently shows the same renewal date and normal payment options. You can use that portal without deciding whether the message itself was authentic. You have verified the task and its legitimate route, not the sender's identity from its logo.

## Complete message packet

For each row, extract the requested action, claimed relationship, pressure and known facts. The absence of independent confirmation is deliberate.

| ID | Entire relevant message |
| --- | --- |
| S1 | “Harbor Bank security: move your savings to our safe account within ten minutes. Use the transfer instructions in this message. Tell nobody while we investigate.” |
| S2 | “We've been talking for months. My flight is held up. Send 300 by gift card tonight so we can finally meet. If you trusted me, you would help.” |
| S3 | “I'm Postbox support. Read me the one-time sign-in code you just received so I can stop your account being closed.” |
| S4 | “Our investment group guarantees a 20% weekly return. Your friend appears in our testimonial video. Deposit cryptocurrency before this invitation expires.” |
| S5 | “I have private material about you. Pay now or I'll distribute it. Don't ask anyone for help.” No material or evidence is supplied. |
| S6 | “Your club membership may need renewal. Open the attached renewal program and disable its security warning if necessary.” |

Available independent routes: Harbor Bank's app was installed and used before these messages; a paper bank statement is already held privately; Postbox's help page is reachable from its existing saved home page; the club's saved member portal shows status and normal renewal controls. A testimonial, a second account recommended by the sender and a caller-ID display are not additional trusted routes. S2's contact has never been met in person. None of the six requests has yet been acted on.

### Action 1 — Build the request assessments

Use one short row per message: `request | pressure | independent fact missing | immediate boundary | next route`. Include romance, investment and extortion separately; “phishing” alone loses the relationship-specific pressure. For S5, distinguish the threat from proof that the claimed material exists. You can take a threat seriously and seek support without accepting every claim in it.

Do not open an attachment, supply a code, send a small “test payment” or repeat intimate material to friends. Record each initial disposition as **suspected scam / harmful request**, with any underlying account or membership question still unresolved. You do not need forensic certainty to decline a dangerous requested action.

### Action 2 — Rehearse two independent checks

Write a bank question: “I received a request to transfer funds to a new account. Did your service issue that request, and what action is actually required?” Specify how you reached the bank without using the message. Then write a club check that examines membership status inside the existing portal without opening S6's attachment. Save both before opening [the later packet](later-packet.md).

For romance or investment pressure, a neutral trusted person can help you pause and identify official consumer or financial-regulator information. They cannot authenticate a stranger merely by liking the story. Do not use the suspicious sender's recommended “verification agent.” An apparently convincing live call or video does not replace checking the consequential request.

### Action 3 — Respond to the exposure that actually occurred

Use the later facts to distinguish **requested**, **sent**, **reported**, **recovery requested** and **recovered**. [The FTC's recovery guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed) recommends promptly contacting the payment provider when money was sent and using the relevant account or device recovery route when those were exposed. A request for reversal is not a refund guarantee.

For a real incident, use the provider's current official reporting controls and your location's consumer/cybercrime services. The FTC is a US route, not a universal authority. Immediate physical danger calls for local emergency help. Extortion involving intimate material warrants appropriate specialist support; avoid further payment, additional material and redistribution. Keep only the lawful evidence the report needs. Do not hire an unsolicited “recovery expert” who demands an advance fee.

Complete [three fresh checks](check-prompts.md), then read [the corrective key](check-answers.md). Your final artifact must say which action is supported now and what remains unknown.

## Adapt and transfer

A trusted reader or interpreter can help parse pressure without taking control of your account or learning secrets. Use only two messages initially if fatigue is high, then return to the remaining patterns. Being targeted is not evidence of stupidity; shame can make independent help harder to obtain.

Harder route: suppose the bank app itself cannot be trusted because the device may be compromised. Identify a separately trusted device or previously held official contact route; do not keep using the suspect route simply because it was once legitimate.

For transfer, prepare a private verification route for one service you actually use and rehearse locating it. Record a real safe lookup separately from a fictional scam response. You need not wait for a scam or contact an organization with a fake incident.

**Supportive:** The request pauses and an independent source determines the next action. **Mixed:** The dangerous action stops, but account recovery remains pending. **Contradictory:** A code or test payment is shared to establish trust. **Inconclusive:** “Scam” is written without a usable verification or exposure-response route.
