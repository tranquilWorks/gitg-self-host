# 14.10 — Online communication and community conduct

## Make the disagreement easier to resolve

A useful online reply makes the disputed claim clearer while respecting the people and information involved. Politeness alone is insufficient if the message exposes a private conversation, recruits a crowd or demands public agreement. Conversely, disagreement with a public claim is not automatically harassment. Attend to content, audience, consent, repetition and the community's actual rules.

Allow 30 minutes for a fictional hobby-forum exchange. Use paper or an accessible editor. No post, message to a real person or moderator report is required. Your output is an audience/privacy decision, a revised public reply, a response to new evidence and a proportionate moderator note if one is justified.

## Context and permission travel differently

A private message may make sense within a relationship while becoming misleading or exposing when copied into a public thread. Permission to read it is not permission to publish it. Cropping a screenshot or removing a name may not prevent recognition through context. A post can also outlive deletion through copies. Think about likely audience expansion before sharing, rather than treating removal as guaranteed erasure.

Pseudonymous participation can protect privacy. Creating a deceptive second identity to manufacture agreement or bypass moderation undermines the conversation. Do not confuse those behaviors. The question is what the account is doing, not whether its display name is a legal name.

Worked example: “You never know what you are talking about; everyone should report this person” turns a checkable claim into a personal campaign. “The manual I have gives a different setting; which revision are you using?” identifies the evidence and leaves room for you to be wrong. The second message is useful only if it also omits private material and stops short of repeated pressure.

## Complete forum packet

The fictional PaperCraft forum discusses a harmless desktop paper-layout application. It is not advice about wiring, machinery or other hazardous equipment.

**Public thread:** Morgan says, “Set PaperCraft's margin to 8 units; that's the default in the manual.” You have manual version 3: “Default margin: 6 units.” Neither participant has yet specified a version in the public thread.

**Private chat:** Morgan previously wrote, “I'm using version 2, and I can only answer next week because of a family appointment.” Morgan has not consented to publication of any of this chat. It contains an appointment detail irrelevant to the technical question.

**Your unposted draft:** “You always give terrible advice. Here is our private chat proving it. Everyone tag Morgan until they admit they are wrong.” No screenshot or message has actually been posted.

**Complete relevant forum rules:** Discuss a specific claim and source; do not publish private messages or personal details without permission; do not coordinate repeated targeting; pseudonyms are allowed; deceptive accounts and impersonation are prohibited. Report a rule concern through the private moderator form with the public post link and a concise factual description. Do not attach private material unless the moderation process appropriately requests necessary evidence. Ordinary technical disagreement is not grounds for punishment.

### Action 1 — Decide what belongs in the audience

Create four rows: public claim, public manual, private version information, private appointment. For each, state whether you can use it in a public reply, what consent/context is missing and a less exposing alternative. Even a relevant private version detail can be elicited through a neutral public question instead of publishing the chat.

Name the actual rule at issue in your unposted draft. The problem is your proposed personal attack, exposure and pile-on, not Morgan's unverified intent. Because the draft is unposted, do not invent a completed privacy breach or pretend deletion is needed. If an actual breach had occurred, correction/removal and appropriate acknowledgment would matter, while copies could remain.

### Action 2 — Draft one narrow response

Write no more than 50 words: cite the version 3 default you can support, ask which version the public advice refers to and remove the screenshot, appointment, attack and recruitment. You may say: “My version 3 manual lists a default margin of 6 units. Which version is your 8-unit advice for? Adding the version would help readers choose the right setting.”

Save the response without posting. State a stopping rule: one question, then allow time; do not send repeated reminders to demand agreement. If you discover your own error, the correction should be as visible to affected readers as the original claim where reasonably possible.

### Action 3 — Choose the next action from the reply

Open [the later packet](later-packet.md). Handle the ordinary clarification and the separate targeting branch separately. A helpful reply may warrant acknowledgment and a revised shared answer. Refusal to debate does not itself justify moderator punishment. Repeated targeted messages, private disclosure or threats require a different response from a technical disagreement.

For a rule violation, draft a short factual moderator note identifying the relevant behavior and rule. Do not organize mass reports or use another account to continue contact. For real serious abuse, use the platform's current safety guidance and appropriate local support. [Australia's eSafety guidance](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-topics/adult-cyber-abuse/report-adult-cyber-abuse) explains evidence/reporting steps and its specific jurisdictional threshold; that threshold is not a universal definition of all bad conduct. Where safe and lawful, preserve necessary report evidence before controls that may hide it. Immediate safety takes priority over completing this exercise.

Attempt [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the key](check-answers.md). Record whether each action is a private rehearsal, a plan, or an actual authorized interaction. A fictional moderator reply is not a real resolution.

## Adapt and transfer

Use asynchronous text, a trusted reader or careful translation. No legal name, personal vulnerability or private life story is required. A supported route uses the public claim and public manual only. A harder route has your manual turn out to be the wrong revision: write an explicit correction without blaming the other person for your inference.

For real transfer, choose an existing low-stakes exchange you are already authorized to participate in. Read its rules, ask one focused question or make one supported correction, then observe the response without creating conflict for practice. If there is no suitable exchange, the offline rehearsal is sufficient for this bounded writing task and remains labeled as such.

**Supportive:** The version issue is clarified with privacy and consent preserved. **Mixed:** Wording improves, but repeated follow-ups still exert pressure. **Contradictory:** Screenshots or deceptive accounts recruit a pile-on. **Inconclusive:** A polite tone masks an unresolved privacy violation or no actual draft is examined.
