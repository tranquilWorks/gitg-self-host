# 14.06 — Algorithmic feeds and persuasive design

## A ranked feed is an invitation, not your agenda

A recommender selects and orders candidates using signals and objectives. Personalization can help you discover relevant material, but a prediction that you will engage does not necessarily match what you intended to accomplish. Ranking, autoplay and a bottomless list also make continuing easy. The practical question is which route serves your purpose and leaves room for a deliberate stop.

Allow 35 minutes for a ten-card paper analysis and two supplied session audits. Use paper or an accessible editor; no account or exposure to upsetting material is needed. The cards and system description below are invented. The offline exercise practices reasoning about a feed, not observation of a live algorithm. An optional real route comes later.

## Separate mechanism, evidence and hypothesis

An engagement objective might use clicks or viewing duration as signals. A system can also consider satisfaction, quality, subscriptions or explicit feedback. [YouTube's own explanation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387?hl=en) describes several signals, including surveys; it does not justify saying that every feed simply maximizes outrage or watch time. A provider's description is evidence of what it says, not an independent audit of outcomes.

A filter bubble is a concern about narrowed exposure through selection and feedback. Ten similar items can suggest a cluster worth examining, but cannot establish your whole information environment or its cause. Your subscriptions, available content and recent topic choice may contribute. Variable reward means that a rewarding result is not obtained predictably on every attempt. Intermittently appealing items can invite another check, but that is a hypothesis to examine here, not a measured mechanism. [Lindström and colleagues](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-19607-x) studied social rewards and posting behavior; their work does not by itself establish the cause of someone’s feed scrolling. A short session cannot diagnose addiction. [Bakshy and colleagues’ study](https://solomonmg.github.io/publications/bakshy-2015-exposure/) also distinguishes network composition, ranked exposure and user choice in a particular news setting; it does not settle filter-bubble effects for every service.

Worked example: you arrive for a bus timetable, find it in the second item, and continue through eight entertaining clips. The clips can be enjoyable while the route misses your chosen endpoint. You could retain a separate entertainment session and use a saved timetable link for travel. “Spent less time” would still be a poor success measure if the timetable was outdated.

## Complete fictional feed

Purpose: find the **training garden's rule for checking whether a pot needs water**, obtain a second relevant explanation, and stop after writing a two-sentence answer. These are fictional garden rules, not horticultural advice.

The made-up StreamGarden service says it ranks candidates using previous views, follows, completion and “useful” feedback. It does not publish weights or user-level explanations. An autoplay switch and a search box are available. A route using saved sources does not change these ranking rules.

| Card | Supplied content | Invitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Garden G rule: check the marked indicator; water only when its card reads D | “Read the full rule” |
| F2 | Tour of Garden G's painted pots; no care rule | “Next tour starts automatically” |
| F3 | “Everyone else is ruining their garden”; no care instruction | “Join the argument” |
| F4 | Garden G explanation: D means the case's agreed watering condition; B means wait | “Save this reference” |
| F5 | Short comedy unrelated to gardening | “Another surprise clip” |
| F6 | Garden G merchandise; no care rule | “Offer ends tonight” |
| F7 | Neighborhood nursery's independent training note: read the supplied indicator before choosing D/water or B/wait | “Compare the diagram” |
| F8 | Celebrity kitchen tour | “Keep watching” |
| F9 | Garden G's republished F1 text | “Share the tip” |
| F10 | Compost collection timetable; no pot-watering rule | “Follow the collection series” |

Treat F1, F4, F7 and F9 as directly useful; F2, F6 and F10 as adjacent; F3 as provocative without useful rule content; F5 and F8 as unrelated. Those labels are relative to this exact purpose, not quality ratings. F9 is another occurrence of F1, not another independent explanation. You may disagree about a secondary label if you preserve what each card actually supplies.

### Action 1 — Audit the invitations

Make a ten-row table: `card | relevance | source cluster | continuation invitation | observation or inference`. Count useful cards and distinct relevant source groups separately. Write one observed pattern and one hypothesis that requires more evidence. “Four directly useful cards appear” is observable; “the company knows I am vulnerable” is not established by these materials.

Then write the point at which your purpose could be met. F1/F4 supply Garden G's rule, and F7 supplies another relevant explanation. A novel source with no usable evidence would not satisfy the second-source requirement.

### Action 2 — Design a purposeful route

Use saved Garden G instructions plus the nursery note, or a direct search for those supplied sources. Write an endpoint before beginning: the two-sentence answer and source note, then a choice to stop or consciously start another activity. You may turn off autoplay as an additional control, but identify which change you are evaluating. Changing five settings at once makes the comparison less interpretable.

Record a possible cost: direct retrieval may reduce pleasant discovery. Preserve a bounded discovery route if it serves a chosen purpose. The target is usable control, not a rule that entertainment must earn its place through productivity.

### Action 3 — Examine two sessions

Save your plan, then open [the later packet](later-packet.md). Compare answer completeness, source independence, shifts away from purpose, stopping and discovery benefits. Calculate duration only for sessions that occurred. A canceled opportunity is not a zero-minute success; a missing stop time gives an unknown duration.

Make a keep/change decision for this case. Two sessions with different questions and order cannot isolate a causal effect of the route. State the smaller conclusion the observations support and what another comparable attempt could clarify. Do not claim to have retrained StreamGarden's model by using saved links.

Attempt [fresh checks](check-prompts.md), then read [the answers](check-answers.md). Your completed artifact includes the card audit, purpose/endpoint, session comparison and a bounded route decision.

## Access, challenge and real use

Read cards aloud or group them on paper. If live feeds cause distress or interfere with functioning, keep the fictional route; no harmful exposure is needed. Avoid recording private posts or other people's handles. A companion can help describe controls without taking over your account.

Harder route: the direct source is stale, while the feed surfaces a genuine correction. Decide how your source-checking rule should change without abandoning purposeful access. This tests whether the endpoint includes answer quality rather than mere speed.

For real transfer, choose one harmless question, inspect the platform's current official controls and try a small change during two ordinary opportunities. Note question difficulty, interruptions and the actual outcome. Keep the practice small enough to resume the activity you wanted to do.

**Supportive:** The route reaches a checked answer and chosen stop. **Mixed:** Drift decreases but useful discovery or answer quality suffers. **Contradictory:** A tiny sample becomes proof of hidden motives or justifies distressing exposure. **Inconclusive:** A setting changes, but the purpose and session outcome are not observed.
