# 14.11 — Digital creation, ownership, and attribution

## A credit line answers only one of the questions

Before reusing digital work, distinguish who created it, who controls relevant rights, what permission covers your use, what credit is required and what compensation was agreed. Naming a creator does not itself grant permission. Purchasing a copy does not automatically transfer copyright. Permission also does not make presenting another person's expression as your own good attribution practice.

Allow 40 minutes to prepare a private fictional event notice and its rights record. No purchase, external image search or public distribution is required. Use paper or a plain-text editor. The asset offers below are invented training facts, not licenses to actual third-party works. Your output demonstrates permission reasoning; actual publication would require verification of actual ingredients and authority.

## Read the permission for the intended use

Copyright protects qualifying expression under the applicable law; ownership can differ from authorship, including some employment arrangements. The [US Copyright Office's overview](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/) provides a US introduction, not a ruling about every jurisdiction or this user's work. Data can involve privacy, contracts and database rights as well as copyright. Possessing a spreadsheet does not settle authority to publish its personal records.

[CC BY 4.0's legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode) permits sharing and adaptation subject to its conditions, including retaining supplied attribution/notices, linking the license and indicating changes. It does not license every possible personality or trademark right or imply endorsement. Other CC variants add conditions such as NonCommercial, NoDerivatives or ShareAlike; inspect the exact version. A deed is a summary; consult the legal terms when the use is consequential or uncertain.

Software has its own licenses and project contribution rules. For example, [MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit) requires retention of its copyright and permission notice in copies or substantial portions. A CC image credit does not satisfy a code license. A repository without a clear license is not permission to reuse its code just because it is public.

Worked example: a commissioned photo costs 40 units and the agreement allows one print flyer. Uploading it to a public merchandise store is a different use. Payment fulfilled one obligation; it did not silently expand the permission. Ask for additional permission or replace the image.

## Complete fictional publishing brief

Create a private draft for a commercial company's paid craft workshop: “Paper Shapes Workshop — June 20, 14:00 — East Studio — ticket 12 units.” The intended real-world analogue would promote the company's paid service. For this exercise the use is explicitly **primarily commercial**, and the company wants a blue recoloring of any reused motif. All offers below come from their purported creators, whose authority is stipulated only inside this fictional case.

| Ingredient | Supplied offer / information |
| --- | --- |
| A: “Two Squares,” creator Mira | Original motif described completely: two equal outlined squares side by side. Creator page record A, dated June 1, supplies CC BY 4.0, copyright “Mira, 2026,” source record A and no other notices. Recoloring and commercial use fit the stated license if its conditions are met. No people, logos or third-party content are included. |
| B: “Leaf Window,” creator Sol | CC BY-NC 4.0; no separate commercial permission. Sol requests voluntary support but requires no payment under this offer. |
| C: “Folded Fan,” creator Lee | CC BY-ND 4.0. The proposed redesign changes the creative drawing, not just file format. No separate permission to share that adaptation. |
| D: “Bright Star” | Found in a repost with “credit to owner”; actual creator and permission unknown. |
| E: collaborator's attendee spreadsheet | Includes names and contact details; collaborator authorized event administration only. No publication or upload permission is supplied. |
| F: your new text and a simple shape you draw yourself | No copied expression, employment claim or collaborator contribution is stipulated in this case. Keep it separate from actual works whose ownership you have not checked. |

A complete fictional source record is enough for the exercise, but “record A” would be replaced by the actual original-source link and preserved terms for real reuse. Do not fabricate an internet URL or imply that Mira is a real rights holder you contacted.

### Action 1 — Make an ingredient ledger

For A–F record `creator/owner | permission basis | planned use/change | conditions | include/replace/hold | payment status`. A is suitable within the stipulated case; B lacks permission for the stipulated commercial use; C cannot be publicly shared as the planned creative adaptation under the supplied grant; D remains unresolved; E stays private; F is the self-created alternative.

Do not broaden those decisions into “all charging is commercial” or “all file changes are adaptations.” Those questions can require legal interpretation. The case deliberately supplies the relevant facts. Likewise, ShareAlike conditions concern relevant licensed adaptations; do not assume every separate work placed beside an image inherits its license.

### Action 2 — Assemble a traceable private draft

Write the event facts and draw the two-square motif as a representation of A, or use your own new shape under F. If using the A scenario, add: “Two Squares — Mira, 2026, source record A; CC BY 4.0; recolored blue.” Include the actual [license link](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) in the draft record, retain supplied notices and avoid claiming endorsement. Label the whole artifact **fictional rights exercise — private draft**.

For F, record exactly which elements you created; do not claim ownership of E's data or copied material. Plain-text layout avoids needing an external template, decorative font asset or photograph. If you add an ingredient, add its permission basis before using it. Keep an accessible text description of the visual.

### Action 3 — Review distribution and compensation

Inspect the final credit in the format a recipient would see. A credit hidden in an inaccessible internal note would not reliably accompany the work. Check the intended audience, permitted changes, required notices and any actual payment agreement. Voluntary support, required license fee and assignment of rights are different arrangements.

Save your draft and ledger before reading [the later packet](later-packet.md). Revise for the proposed distribution change and missing credit. A file prepared privately is not proof of public publication or full legal clearance. For real uncertainty, use a self-created replacement or appropriate legal/institutional advice rather than inventing an exception.

Attempt [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the key](check-answers.md). The output should let someone trace every included element without relying on your memory.

## Adapt and transfer

Use a text-only notice or your own geometric shapes when rights are unclear. A trusted reader can inspect the permission ledger. Harder route: adapt a CC BY-SA work and distribute the adaptation; identify the applicable version and compatible terms before assuming your preferred exclusive license fits. Do not infer a rule for code from this image example.

For real transfer, inspect one actual ingredient in a small work you are authorized to prepare. Retain the original source, date, exact terms, changes, credit and payment agreement. Check third-party/personality rights and the platform's distribution requirements. Publish only through an authorized channel; this practice does not require publication.

**Supportive:** Every included ingredient has suitable permission and inspectable credit. **Mixed:** A credit is correct but the planned use requires a replacement or further grant. **Contradictory:** Attribution is substituted for permission or private data is published as “owned.” **Inconclusive:** Concepts are listed without reviewing the actual draft ingredients.
