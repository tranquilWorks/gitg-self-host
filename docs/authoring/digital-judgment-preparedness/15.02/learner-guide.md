# 15.02 — Household emergency planning

## A plan must be findable when ordinary conveniences disappear

An emergency plan joins people, information, supplies and assistance into a usable response. A shopping list alone cannot decide whether to leave or shelter, contact an absent person or keep essential care available. Plan for the household that actually exists, including changing capacity, pets, communication needs and helpers who may be unavailable.

Allow 40 minutes for this fictional household tabletop, including a short retrieval retest. Use paper or an accessible local document. A tabletop means talking through decisions under stated assumptions. Everyone participating must know it is practice. Do not turn off power, block an exit, trigger an alarm, discharge equipment or create smoke. Local emergency instructions and individual care/building plans govern actual incidents.

[CDC's planning guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-emergency-preparedness/people-with-disabilities/make-a-plan.html) emphasizes individual needs, communication, support and practice. [Red Cross planning guidance](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/make-a-plan.html) distinguishes shelter and evacuation scenarios and includes household roles and separated members. Neither makes an unconfirmed helper or untested device arrangement reliable simply because it appears on a worksheet.

## Different hazards require different decisions

Fire, power loss, severe weather, evacuation, urgent illness and service loss may overlap, but they are not interchangeable. A severe-weather instruction to shelter does not override a fire requiring safe escape. An outage may be inconvenient for one household and threaten essential care for another. The plan should identify the warning source, trigger, first response, assistance and contact/meeting route for each hazard.

Worked example: all contact numbers are stored in a phone that cannot be charged. Saying “call the contact list” assumes the failed dependency away. A private accessible paper list may solve information retrieval, but still does not establish a working phone network. Test both what you can know and what you can actually do.

## Complete fictional household packet

Rae and Imani live in a single-level home. Imani uses a mobility aid and prescribed powered equipment; its outage continuity must follow a care-provider plan. The equipment's safe battery duration is **not supplied**. Rae is away until evening. Their cat has a carrier in the hall cupboard. Neighbor Jo has not agreed to provide assistance. An out-of-area contact, Dev, has agreed to receive check-ins, but Dev's contact details exist only in the usual phone. Do not invent real contact details; use a paper “Dev — practice contact record” card.

The following **invented local/building instructions** are supplied for case reasoning only:

| Disruption | Case instruction / information |
| --- | --- |
| Fire | Use a safe clear exit under the building fire plan; meet at the designated front-tree point if safe; summon emergency help and do not re-enter. Both ordinary doors are level, but current availability must be checked. |
| Power loss | Use the battery lantern; preserve communication; follow the equipment provider's outage plan. That care plan is currently missing from the household packet. |
| Evacuation order | The verified local alert identifies the community center as destination; use confirmed accessible transport. No transport booking or helper acceptance is supplied. |
| Severe-weather shelter alert | For this case, the official alert directs shelter in the interior room away from windows; do not substitute the evacuation destination unless instructions change. |
| Medical emergency | Use the local emergency route and provide the household's actual location/access and observed condition; follow dispatcher guidance. The exercise uses only placeholders. |
| Water/shops unavailable | Consult the relevant official service notice; inspect stored water/food and household-specific needs, record gaps and a verified assistance route. No universal quantity is stipulated. |

Existing inventory: lantern on an accessible shelf, battery condition unknown; food for some ordinary meals, household suitability/duration not checked; water containers present, volume/current usability not checked; documents in a private folder known to Rae, retrieval by Imani not tested; cat carrier found but not practiced; medication list private and care continuity not yet agreed. “Present” is not “adequate,” and unknown quantities are not zero or sufficient by default.

### Action 1 — Build six response rows

For every disruption record `trigger/source | first response | person/animal needs | contact/meeting route | gap owner`. Use the supplied instructions and mark the unresolved care plan, transport and helper consent explicitly. Keep sensitive documents and medical detail out of the learning record; generic locations and statuses suffice.

Assign a responsible household decision-maker for each gap without assuming that assigning Jo a role creates Jo's consent. Prioritize the powered-equipment plan as an external dependency requiring the care provider, not something solved by guessing battery hours or borrowing an unspecified generator. A real person whose essential care is threatened should seek appropriate help promptly rather than continue the drill.

### Action 2 — Run the announced outage tabletop

Say “this is practice.” On paper, make the usual charging route unavailable while Rae is away. Retrieve your actual practice plan and Dev placeholder card without opening the normal online record. Explain Imani's first response, how Rae and Dev would be contacted, and what happens if communications also fail. A card can establish retrievable information; successful contact remains untested.

If alone, speak both decision roles and label them as self-rehearsal, not participation by Rae or Imani. Do not move or test a real person's care equipment. Do not walk an exit if conditions are unsafe; an ordinary safe access check is optional and separate from an emergency evacuation.

Save what you could actually retrieve and what remained unknown, then open [the later packet](later-packet.md) for the fictional household's outcomes. Those supplied outcomes are not your own drill evidence.

### Action 3 — Correct and repeat one failed part

Choose a safe, proportionate repair you can actually make in the practice materials: put the contact card with the plan, use readable print, or add a second accessible location. Put the normal source away again and repeat the same retrieval. Record first failure, correction and retest result. Merely planning the repair is not installing it.

Separately list unresolved external actions: obtaining the care-provider continuity plan, confirming accessible transport and obtaining consent from a backup helper. Keep the gap open until a relevant confirmation or test exists. A lantern repair does not close medical continuity.

Complete [fresh checks](check-prompts.md) before [the key](check-answers.md). Your finished artifact is a six-hazard plan, an actual harmless retrieval result and an honest list of external dependencies. Set a review trigger such as a move, changed care need, changed contact or new household member.

## Adapt and transfer

Use large print, symbols, audio available offline or a consented helper. A person living alone can use agreed contacts and verified building assistance; living alone does not imply someone nearby has agreed to carry or transport them. Plan pets and service animals with the appropriate destination rules rather than assuming every site accommodates them.

Harder route: Rae, Jo and mobile service are all unavailable. Identify which routes remain known and which require official/building/care-provider arrangements. Do not turn missing capacity into a dramatic improvisation.

For real transfer, obtain your locality's current hazard guidance and household-specific plans, then announce a small safe tabletop. Test one ordinary retrieval or explanation, fix a discovered gap and repeat it. Do not interpret a successful card retrieval as a full evacuation, adequate supply reserve or demonstrated clinical continuity.

**Supportive:** A real safe repair passes the same retrieval constraint while care gaps remain visible. **Mixed:** Contacts and lighting work but transport or essential-care arrangements remain unresolved. **Contradictory:** A drill disables equipment or assumes unwilling physical assistance. **Inconclusive:** A plan is saved but never retrieved or used.
