# Later packet — capacity and access

Baseline capacity is 2 × 30 = 60 moderator-minutes. Four ordinary reviews at ten minutes plus one urgent review at twenty consume all 60. The plan has zero reserve; it cannot honestly promise spare same-day capacity.

The next day has six ordinary reviews plus one urgent review: 80 minutes. With unchanged staffing there is a 20-minute shortfall. Prioritizing the urgent case leaves 40 minutes for four ordinary reviews and two ordinary reviews pending. This is a model calculation, not a required legal triage policy or proof which cases are truly urgent.

One appellant cannot use the slider. Another is banned and cannot reach the form behind login. Adding “appeals welcome” did not fix either barrier. Revise access and notice so the alternative is actually reachable, verify ownership proportionately and ensure an available reviewer receives the case. An unstaffed inbox is not an immediate response service.

Illustrative case dispositions: Q needs context review and correction of an erroneous removal if the supplied context is confirmed. H warrants protective action against repeated targeted contact with a reason and review route. T needs prompt safety escalation appropriate to the facts and location, restricted necessary evidence, and review of protective action; a volunteer forum cannot promise emergency response.

A defensible revision publishes a daily review window, sends pending-status notices, prioritizes urgent safety handling, limits additional queue promises and identifies the need for backup capacity. It still leaves off-hours coverage unresolved until a real arrangement exists. No actual appeal, policy adoption or fair-enforcement result is established here.
