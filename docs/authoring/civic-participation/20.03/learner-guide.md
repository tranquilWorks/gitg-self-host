# 20.03 — Rights, duties, law, and civil liberty

Apply one rights-and-process rule to two groups you evaluate differently, then test a relevant safety difference. Use two twenty-minute comparisons and ten minutes to revise. This is fictional analysis. It does not invite contact with an unsafe group, require debate over anyone's dignity or advise action in an actual legal dispute.

## Separate principle, rule and decision

A right identifies an important protected interest; its legal scope and remedies depend on the relevant system. A duty identifies something a person or institution owes under an applicable rule or relationship. A preferred outcome is neither automatically a right nor a duty. Stating which interest is at stake makes reasoning clearer than using “freedom” or “safety” as a complete answer.

The [Universal Declaration of Human Rights, S05](../SOURCES.md#S05), provides a general reference for equality, fair process, expression, peaceful association and duties to others. Its principles are not a substitute for identifying the law that governs a particular forum. A public hall, private club and workplace may have different applicable arrangements. Do not claim that one room-use exercise establishes constitutional rights everywhere.

Liberty, safety and order can pull in different directions. A room needs capacity limits and workable booking rules; users retain interests in fair treatment and expression. A limit should have a relevant reason, appropriate authority and an explanation of why it fits the actual problem. “I dislike their view” and “there is specific evidence of a threat” are different propositions. Neither automatically settles every legal question.

Due process concerns how a decision is made and can be challenged, not simply whether you like its result. For this small fictional forum, consider clear notice, relevant reasons, a chance to correct factual error and a review route. These are educational design questions, not a legally sufficient procedure for every dispute. Real urgent danger can require prompt protective action while preserving the appropriate later review.

Consistency does not require ignoring relevant differences. Swapping group labels is useful because it exposes favoritism when conduct and circumstances stay the same. A changed attendance number, actual threat or access need may justify a different response. State the fact that matters and test whether it would matter just as much for a favored group.

## Worked example

A hall's capacity is thirty. Two lawful discussion groups each request space for twenty-four people under identical conditions. Denying only the disliked group because it is “controversial” leaves the relevant difference unexplained. A third request for forty people raises a capacity issue whatever the viewpoint. That example still does not authorize a learner to adjudicate a real forum's law; it tests the coherence of a proposed rule.

## Supplied fictional cases

Cedar Hall offers ordinary discussion bookings. Group A is familiar and agreeable to the learner; Group B holds a lawful view the learner dislikes. Both request separate available times for twenty-four attendees, accept the same ordinary conduct conditions and present no supplied safety concern. The hall's fictional capacity is thirty. No particular political, religious or demographic identity is assigned.

A separate Case C concerns a specific threatening message whose authenticity has been confirmed in the fiction by the designated hall safety lead. The message concerns the proposed event. No operational details are supplied. The authorized manager must consider an appropriate protective response and qualified guidance; the learner is not assigned to confront, investigate or contact anyone. Case C's legal context and final risk assessment remain unknown.

## Write the rule before selecting the beneficiary

1. Name the interests in expression, association, equal treatment and safety, together with users' ordinary obligations. Write a capacity-and-conduct rule before applying labels. Output: a rule whose terms do not depend on liking a group.
2. Apply it to A and B, then exchange their labels without changing any supplied fact. Explain whether the result changes. Output: two consistent ordinary-case decisions, or an identified inconsistency to repair. Do not invent a hidden threat to rationalize preference.
3. Examine C separately. Identify the specific relevant evidence, proper decision authority, less restrictive possibilities that require professional assessment and the limit of your knowledge. Output: an analytical response that takes the concern seriously without declaring a permanent ban or an event safe.
4. Draft a short fictional decision with reasons, notice, a route for correcting factual error and review by the appropriate authorized role. Name a fact and a legal question that could change it. Output: a bounded process proposal, not a legal opinion or operational safety plan.
5. Save the first decisions before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Review whether a preferred group received an unexplained exemption. Output: the correction and the unresolved case, with neither hidden by a general slogan.

## Support and transfer

For support, use two invented hobby associations and read the capacities with counters or a simple table. No one must rehearse hateful speech or personally threatening material. For a harder fictional comparison, change one relevant condition after the identity swap and explain why consistent principles can now yield a different outcome.

Real transfer can begin by reading an organization's actual published participation and review rules without initiating a dispute. Questions about discrimination, legal jeopardy or a credible threat require appropriate qualified channels. Do not treat this lesson's review template as a filing, waiver of rights or guarantee of safety. A well-reasoned fictional decision shows consistency under supplied facts, not readiness to judge all civil-liberty conflicts.

## Source limits

S05 is the original declaration reproduced by the Council of Europe, with the relevant articles inspected. Direct UN and OHCHR routes returned access errors; the successful official reproduction is identified in the register. The room cases, capacity numbers and review process are original teaching design. General principles neither determine local enforceability nor remove duties to respond appropriately to real safety concerns.
