# Later packet — improved attendance with more than one change

Supplied fiction, not actual participant evidence.

The organizer obtains permission for a step-free room and also sends an additional reminder before the next workshop. Forty people express interest again and twenty-eight attend. The packet does not establish whether these are the same forty people, whether other circumstances changed or why anyone attended. No demographic or individual reason data are supplied.

Attendance is now 70 percent, compared with 50 percent before: eight more attendees and a twenty-percentage-point difference. Both venue and reminder changed, and the participant mix is unknown. The result therefore does not isolate the room's effect or prove that the earlier gap resulted from one structural cause. It also does not establish that the other twelve lacked motivation.

A willing fictional user reports that the step-free route was usable for them. That is a specific access observation, not certification of all access needs or proof of the attendance mechanism. Transit complexity and online-only signup remain. Revise the conclusion to distinguish a reported access improvement, an attendance change and unanswered causal and distributional questions.
