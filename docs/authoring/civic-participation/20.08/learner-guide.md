# 20.08 — Collective action and organizing

Coordinate a small shared task from a verified problem through a usable result and accepted maintenance. Allow thirty minutes to plan, a short agreed work session and twenty minutes to review within two weeks. Use a community noticeboard with permission or the complete fiction. A good meeting or attractive plan is preparation; the learning task includes doing the permitted work and checking its result.

## Build an effort people can actually carry

A shared problem needs more than one person's annoyance. Identify who uses the resource, who bears the inconvenience, who maintains it and who may authorize changes. People who attend a planning conversation do not automatically represent absent users. State what you observed and what you still need to ask. Aesthetic preferences should not displace access, accuracy or ownership.

A compact charter connects the problem to one result. Include the affected users, authorized owner, desired change, boundaries, roles, resources, risks, deadline and acceptance test. For a board, “make it better” is vague; “remove notices the owner confirms expired, preserve uncertain material, and let a willing user locate a current event without coaching” supplies decisions and evidence. Permission can limit the task. Do not remove disputed speech, private material or safety instructions under the banner of tidying.

Recruit around a specific contribution, not an assumed obligation. Explain the work, time and decision room, then ask. An offered role becomes assigned only when accepted. Give each unit an owner and a check-back point. Date checking, preparing a label, approving removals and testing use are different responsibilities. Avoid leaving one enthusiastic organizer to chase every loose end while describing the effort as shared ownership.

Coordinate dependencies before work starts. An approval may be needed before removal; the new arrangement may need testing before the owner accepts it. State how uncertainty or an absent volunteer changes the plan. A smaller completed task can be preferable to unapproved work. No one needs to reveal political views, personal circumstances or a reason for declining. Disagreement about the solution can be recorded without making someone an enemy of the community.

Measure the result at the level you can observe. A board with fewer notices can be less useful if the removed ones were still relevant or labels are unreadable. Ask a willing user to perform an ordinary finding task without hints. A difficulty is design information, not a failure of the user. Repair within the permission granted, retry and record the limits of that trial. The same user's second attempt may benefit from familiarity.

Maintenance needs its own accepted owner and feasible rhythm. Announcing that “someone will check weekly” does not establish a commitment. Capture a practical lesson about coordination as well as the artifact. One board trial does not show community consensus, universal accessibility or long-term impact; it can show that a bounded effort produced a particular usable result and an agreed next responsibility.

## Worked example

A team proposes removing every notice without a date. The owner points out that one undated instruction is still in use. The team narrows the task to confirmed expired event notices, keeps uncertain material and tests a clearer heading. The changed criterion avoids confusing neatness with accuracy. The owner accepts a maintenance role separately from approving today's work.

## Supplied fictional case

Cedar Hall's board has twelve notices: three owner-confirmed expired event notices, eight current notices and one undated notice whose status is unknown. The owner, Mel, authorizes removal of the three expired notices and a reversible heading. The undated item must remain pending Mel's separate review; private or disputed material is outside scope.

For the paper rehearsal, use this complete inventory. The three owner-confirmed expired notices are **September Welcome, 20 September; Repair Chat, 21 September; and Seed Talk, 22 September**. The eight confirmed current notices are **Reading Circle, 16 October at 18:00; Book Swap, 17 October at 10:00; Story Hour, 18 October at 11:00; Craft Table, 19 October at 16:00; Local History, 20 October at 18:00; Music Listening, 21 October at 17:00; Map Club, 22 October at 18:00; and Repair Stories, 23 October at 15:00**. All are fictional Cedar Hall events. The undated item is **Room-use reminder**, with no further status supplied. Preserve its text placeholder and refer it to Mel. The proposed heading is **Current events — check the notice date**; Noor's task is to find the time for **Reading Circle**. Leave the undated item in place. The inventory marks its status as awaiting owner review; do not add a label to that notice or change its content without permission.

Ari offers to check dates and make an inventory; Dev offers to prepare the heading. Both accept those roles and a joint check before any change. Mel accepts approval and final review. A willing fictional user, Noor, agrees to try finding the time of one current event. No one has yet done the work. Supplies already available suffice; no purchase or budget estimate is required.

## Organize through a checked endpoint

1. Describe the actual problem and whose permission and experience matter. Separate known notice status from assumptions about absent users. Output: a short problem statement and stakeholder/authority map.
2. Write the compact charter, with the removal limit, finding task and stop conditions. Ask for roles and record acceptances separately from invitations. Output: an accepted coordination plan, not a claim of impact.
3. Complete the inventory and owner check before changing the board. Carry out only authorized removals and the reversible heading. Output: an accurate before/after account that preserves the undated item.
4. Ask the willing user to find the event time without coaching. Record success or difficulty, correct a permitted design problem and retry if welcome. Output: an observed result with the trial's small scope and any familiarity effect stated.
5. Ask who can maintain the result and on what terms. Use the [later packet](later-packet.md) to revise your success claim and capture a coordination lesson. Output: accepted future ownership distinguished from maintenance already performed.

## Support and transfer

For support, use three role cards and a paper copy of the fictional board. An accessible verbal plan can replace a chart. For a harder case, the owner is unavailable before the deadline. Complete the inventory, preserve disputed items and renegotiate the deadline; do not treat silence as approval.

A real effort needs willing participants, appropriate authority and a low-risk task. Do not collect names or political affiliations for proof, remove public content without permission or use this exercise to manage hazardous work. A solo fictional plan develops coordination reasoning but is not collective action in the world. Transfer the accepted-role and user-test sequence to another modest common task, then judge that task on its own evidence.
