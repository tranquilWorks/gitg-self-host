# 20.06 — Service and volunteering

Complete one requested service unit, use task-specific feedback and repeat once within your capacity. Allow thirty minutes to check fit, two agreed short service periods over two to four weeks, and fifteen minutes to review. Use the supplied fictional organization if there is no actual request and appropriate role. Enthusiasm is a starting condition, not evidence of community benefit.

## Begin with a need the community recognizes

A cause you care about is not yet a useful task. Ask the organization what work is needed, what is already covered and what output it can use. Extra publicity, surprise donations or a new project may create more work than they remove. The people receiving or coordinating service should not have to perform gratitude to make the contribution meaningful.

A suitable role fits competence, time, access, resources and likely interruptions. Identify the coordinator and the actual limits before agreeing. Some work requires training, safeguarding checks or legal authorization; a volunteer label supplies none of these. Choose an ordinary task such as preparing non-sensitive notices when that is actually requested. No vulnerable-person contact, travel, money or regulated care is required by this guide.

Clear expectations describe the output, method, available materials, support route and handoff. They also leave room to raise an obstacle or stop a role responsibly. Volunteering remains voluntary; this exercise does not create an employment contract or a legal service obligation. Existing organizational and local requirements must be followed through the appropriate process.

Benefit and burden should be examined together. Ten files may look productive but still require the coordinator to correct every date. A helper's hours are not the same as usable outputs. Record errors, revisions, support time and what the organization actually accepts. Avoid hiding learning costs, while recognizing that reasonable training can be a legitimate investment rather than a failure.

Sustainability appears across repeated commitments and responsible changes, not simply the first enthusiastic visit. If capacity changes, communicate early and seek an accepted handoff or a smaller scope. Do not quietly assign a replacement or disappear from an essential responsibility. Continuing, reducing and ending can each be appropriate when the final task and owner are clear.

## Worked example

A volunteer wants to design a campaign, but a coordinator needs ten large-print event notices using verified public information. The volunteer accepts the smaller task. Two notices initially have a wrong contact line and cannot be counted as usable. Correcting those files and improving the next attempt is a more honest contribution than reporting “ten delivered” while leaving the repair with staff.

## Supplied fictional role

Cedar Community Hub asks Priya to prepare ten notices on 5 October and ten on 19 October using its supplied template and public event facts. Coordinator Sam will check usability. Priya offers at most forty minutes per unit and has permission to edit these notices only. No recipient stories, private records, publication rights or broader campaign role are included.

The supplied four-line template is: **title / date and time / place / public questions contact**, using 18-point type for this fictional role. Actual users may need a different format; the chosen size is not universal access certification. For the 5 October unit, make ten identical copies of: **Repair Stories / 12 October, 14:00–15:00 / Cedar Hub, Room A / Hub Event Desk**. For the 19 October unit, make ten identical copies of: **Local History Hour / 26 October, 14:00–15:00 / Cedar Hub, Room A / Hub Event Desk**. The desk name is a fictional public label, not a real address. Ten copies are ten requested notices, not ten different events. Keep them for coordinator handoff; no real publication is requested. Other event details are unknown.

The accepted standard is correct date, place and public contact information in the specified large-print format. Sam supplies the template and instructions through the ordinary channel. Priya should raise uncertainty before inventing details and should identify unfinished items at handoff. The fictional dates and quantities are teaching inputs, not an actual volunteering opportunity.

## Deliver twice and account for the support needed

1. Check the requested need, useful endpoint, role boundaries and support route. Compare both dates with actual capacity and foreseeable interruptions. Output: an explicitly accepted bounded role or an honest decision to decline, not a heroic promise of unlimited availability.
2. Prepare the first unit using the supplied materials. Ask before changing the method. At handoff identify the number submitted and any known incomplete part. Output: submitted work and actual time, with usability awaiting the coordinator's response where not yet known.
3. Receive the specific correction without making the coordinator reassure you. Repair only the authorized items and distinguish initial usable output from final accepted output. Output: an error-and-correction record that does not double-count corrected files as new notices.
4. Carry the lesson into the second agreed unit, or communicate an inability early and seek a properly accepted handoff. Output: what actually happened at the second opportunity, including support burden and remaining work.
5. Save your expectation before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Review benefit, reliability, time and coordinator effort. Agree to continue, reduce or close the role with the relevant owner. Output: a sustainable next status, not an assumed recurring commitment.

## Support and transfer

For support, start with a smaller requested unit and an accessible written or spoken role description. For a harder fictional case, the second date conflicts with another obligation: a timely accepted replacement may preserve service, while an unanswered request for help does not. Do not let completion pressure turn a voluntary task into an unsafe or unqualified role.

For real transfer, follow the organization's current onboarding and task instructions. Keep recipient identities, photographs and confidential information out of app evidence. Record broad output and handoff status instead. Two units can demonstrate an initial repeat and response to feedback; longer-term service requires later evidence. A role inquiry or rehearsal alone cannot establish a tangible community benefit.
