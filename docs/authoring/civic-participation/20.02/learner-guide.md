# 20.02 — Civic and governmental literacy

Find who can answer one public-service question and distinguish that role from who decides policy or approves funding. Use two twenty-five-minute source checks and a concise genuine inquiry or labeled rehearsal. The default asks who sets a library's opening hours. This is civic navigation, not a campaign, legal representation or a promise that contacting an official will produce your preferred result.

## Authority has more than one dimension

Government levels divide responsibilities across jurisdictions. Local, regional, national, tribal and other arrangements differ; do not project a familiar hierarchy onto every place. Branches are another distinction. In the United States federal example, legislatures make laws and exercise budget functions, executive agencies administer responsibilities, and courts decide legal questions in cases. These broad categories do not identify who controls a particular library schedule.

Elections select some officeholders and create one route of public accountability. Other roles are appointed or employed. A prominent elected official may lack authority over the issue you care about. An advisory board may recommend changes without controlling a budget or directing staff. The important question is what a specific body is empowered to do, supported by an official source.

Public finance connects policy to resources. An operator may schedule staff within an approved amount while a separate body decides whether to fund longer opening hours. A budget proposal, appropriation, operational decision and actual expenditure are different stages. A line in a proposed budget is not proof that the money has been approved or the service changed.

Participation routes also have different purposes. A service desk may explain current practice; a clerk may receive a policy submission; an election office provides the applicable voting process; a complaint or review route addresses certain errors. Courts are not the default customer-service channel. Check the actual jurisdiction, scope, deadlines, access options and what information becomes public before using a route.

A responsible inquiry asks a question the recipient can answer. It need not demand a political position or accuse someone. Record whether the message was drafted, sent, acknowledged, answered or redirected. A nonresponse does not prove corruption, and acknowledgment does not establish that the proposed change is adopted.

## Worked example from an official directory

[Pima County's Library Advisory Board page, S04](../SOURCES.md#S04), describes its mission as advisory on library policies, services, programs and operations. That supports identifying an advisory role. It does not by itself establish who can change a branch's hours, approve every expense or resolve a legal dispute. A careful map retains those questions instead of converting “board” into unlimited authority. No current officeholder, meeting deadline or contact number needs to be memorized here.

## Supplied fictional service directory

Cedar's municipal library service operates the library. Its manager sets ordinary staffing schedules within the council's adopted opening policy and budget. Cedar Council approves that policy and annual budget. The Library Advisory Panel makes recommendations and cannot itself appropriate funds. The municipal clerk receives written policy submissions. The service office answers routine operational questions. An official election office provides election information; no eligibility rule, deadline or ballot is supplied. A complaint/review procedure exists but its scope and timing have not yet been obtained.

A cropped fictional post says “the board decides hours” without identifying which body. Cedar is invented; its directory is not a description of Pima County or any other real government. Use it to practice a map, then verify real authority afresh if transferring the task.

## Build and test the authority map

1. Create rows for operator, policy authority, budget authority, advisory role, election information and complaint/review. Record level of government, source and uncertainty for each. Output: a map that keeps Cedar's manager, council and panel distinct.
2. Consult [USAGov's federal-branch explanation, S02](../SOURCES.md#S02), and [state/local directory, S03](../SOURCES.md#S03), as United States examples of different functions and routing. For another location use its official sources. Output: one distinction the general directory helps with and one local authority question it cannot settle.
3. Match “Where is the current hours decision recorded?” to an operational information route, and “What process considers funding more hours?” to the appropriate policy/budget route. Identify missing complaint scope, submission conditions and election details. Output: two narrowly routed questions without inventing powers or deadlines.
4. Draft a concise factual inquiry identifying the service and the clarification sought. In fiction, keep it unsubmitted until the later packet supplies an event. In a real suitable inquiry, use the verified channel and protect unnecessary personal information. Output: the true contact status, not an assumed decision.
5. Save the map before reading the [later packet](later-packet.md). Use a redirect to update the relevant role, not erase an authority distinction. Output: the corrected route and remaining unanswered questions.

## Support and transfer

For support, begin with “operates, decides, funds” and add the remaining rows once those are clear. A reference librarian or appropriate civic-information service can help locate official records. For a harder fictional case, two pages disagree because one is an old proposal. Check date, decision status and issuing authority before choosing the convenient version.

Real transfer should concern a genuine ordinary question. Verify current meeting or submission details from the responsible body, including accessibility and public-record implications. This guide does not submit messages, register anyone to vote or direct political choices. An accurate map and a relevant reply establish navigation, not effective advocacy, legal entitlement or a completed policy change.
