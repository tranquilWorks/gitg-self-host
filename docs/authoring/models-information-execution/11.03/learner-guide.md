# 11.03 — Goal definition and success criteria

## Purpose and a useful finish

Turn an aspiration into a changed condition whose value and achievement can be inspected. Then challenge the goal itself. A measurable target can be the wrong target; completing activities or buying equipment does not establish the intended benefit.

**Canonical scope:** Translate aspirations into specific outcomes, constraints, measures, and reasons.

Your deliverable is a versioned goal card, predetermined checks, a smallest useful artifact, and a decision to continue, revise, or stop. Allow 30–40 minutes for the paper route, using one sheet and three movable paper labels. A text-only simulation is fully supplied when moving paper is inaccessible or inconvenient. No purchase, household rearrangement, actual partner, or private disclosure is required.

This guide develops one goal-definition skill. It does not decide your life purpose, require optimization, or equate a completed task with human worth. The paper prototype and fictional results cannot establish usability for a real household without appropriate participation and permission.

## Reference: five parts that do different jobs

**Reason:** Who benefits, and why does the changed condition matter now? A reason such as “reduce confusion when returning shared items” is different from “produce an impressive photograph.” Reasons should remain open to revision when circumstances or the beneficiary's wishes change.

**Outcome:** What will be different? Separate it from the method. Purchasing containers, spending an hour, or attending a class can be methods or activities; none guarantees the intended result.

**Constraints:** What must remain protected while pursuing the outcome? Include authority, consent, cost, effort, access, disruption, and effects on others. A target does not authorize violating a constraint simply because that makes a metric improve.

**Measures and acceptance:** What observations distinguish met, partly met, not met, and unknown? Choose criteria before the attempt. A missing measurement is not a pass. Observing “found” does not necessarily establish “returned,” and a helper choosing the answer is different from a helper presenting accessible materials.

**Review and change control:** When will you inspect both performance and continued value? Keep the original card if you revise it. A reasonable scope reduction is a new version, not evidence that the earlier larger goal was already achieved. You may stop a goal whose benefit no longer justifies its cost.

NASA's requirements guidance provides background for distinguishing outcomes, rationale, constraints, and verifiable criteria. Its engineering context does not validate this personal-development task or turn everyday goals into a bureaucratic process. See [source notes](../SOURCES.md).

## Different worked example: make a useful meeting summary

“Spend 30 minutes writing notes” measures effort. A more inspectable outcome is, “By the end of the next planning session, produce a private summary that distinguishes three agreed decisions from unresolved questions, gives each agreed action an owner who accepted it, and contains no private remarks that participants did not consent to share.”

The method could be dictation or typing. A summary with every action assigned but no accepted owners fails an important criterion. If the meeting is cancelled, the useful response may be to stop or revise the goal rather than invent decisions to hit a count. The numbers are illustrative, not a general rule for meetings.

## Complete practice packet: find and return three items

The starting aspiration is **“be more organized.”** In the fictional context, the chosen reason is easier finding and returning of three ordinary items, not an attractive display. Use paper cards labeled **PENCIL**, **ERASER**, and **BLANK CARD**. They stand for items; no actual shared possessions need be moved.

Draw or write four zones named **A**, **B**, **C**, and **D**. D is a reserved access strip in the toy layout and must remain empty. You control your paper only. On a real shared surface, permissions and established access arrangements would need agreement before changes; this fictional authority is not permission to alter someone else's setup.

The trial goal has these supplied boundaries:

- Produce and inspect one paper layout by the end of this session, with at most **20 minutes of construction effort**, no purchases, and no change to real shared space.
- Give each of the three item cards one distinct labeled home among A, B, and C. Zone D stays clear.
- Use a find-and-return sequence **ERASER, BLANK CARD, PENCIL**. For each request, identify the right item and return it to its labeled home without another person selecting the answer. Reading, pointing, dictation, tactile marking, or a helper moving a card at your direction are allowed and should be recorded.
- A local prototype meets its operational target only when all three finds and all three returns are correct, the reserved zone stays clear, permission and cost constraints hold, and the effort limit is met. This does not establish independent-user usability or long-term household benefit.

There is no retrieval-speed requirement. The purpose is unambiguous access and return, not speed or dexterity. The numerical and procedural criteria are original exercise choices, not a universal definition of organization.

### Action 1 — Write the goal before choosing a method

Fill this goal card in your own words while preserving the supplied boundaries:

`Version/date | beneficiary and chosen reason | changed condition | context and review point | constraints | observable checks | partial/unknown result | stop or revision condition`

Distinguish the outcome from making labels. Explain why both finding and returning matter. Record a reason that would justify abandoning or changing the goal—for example, the intended user no longer needs that arrangement, or required access would be made worse. Do not fabricate another person's endorsement.

Create an acceptance record before acting:

`Request | correct item found? | correct labeled return? | answer-selection help? | access strip clear? | observed limitation`

Add separate fields for permission, purchases, construction effort, route (self-test/live willing user/supplied simulation), and user-reported burden where actually obtained. A blank burden field does not authorize saying the layout is easy for someone else.

### Action 2 — Produce the smallest useful artifact and test it

Make three item cards and three home labels, or write a text layout with the same distinctions. One possible method is A=PENCIL, B=ERASER, C=BLANK CARD, with D reserved; another clear one-to-one assignment is acceptable. Save the chosen mapping before testing.

Carry out the three requests in order. After finding each card, put it outside the layout briefly, then return it to the home indicated by the label. Record the actual choice. Do not silently correct a misplaced return before recording the miss. A helper may operate the paper at your direction without choosing for you.

A self-test is useful for inspecting a prototype, but you know its design and cannot claim that a new user found it intuitive. For a live willing-user route, obtain consent and explain that the layout—not the person's worth—is being tested; do not introduce speed pressure or remove needed supports. Stop if permission or access is in doubt.

For a fully text-based route, use the supplied prototype results in **later-packet.md** after saving your goal card and criteria. That route exercises acceptance decisions without pretending you physically built or tested the paper layout.

### Action 3 — Judge the result and challenge the continued value

Compare every result with the original criteria. Report met for the tested scope, partial, not met because a constraint failed, or inconclusive because necessary evidence is missing. Explain why a visually appealing prototype with a return error is not fully complete.

Keep version 1 and make a version 2 only when you can state the specific defect or changed need. A repeated self-test may show that the repair works for you but remains influenced by practice. Define a fresh comparable check or a willing-user test before claiming a broader benefit. If reducing the item count or changing an acceptance threshold is justified, record that change explicitly; do not rewrite version 1's miss as a pass.

End with a continue/revise/stop decision and its reason. Completing a target is not a command to keep pursuing it after its benefit disappears. Equally, one imperfection need not invalidate the useful part of an otherwise bounded result.

## Fresh checks and an honest completion boundary

Attempt **check-prompts.md**, then open **check-answers.md**. One check tests activity-versus-outcome reasoning; the other tests constraint and missing-evidence handling. The task is not solved by adding precise numbers to a vague aspiration while leaving its reason unexplained.

A complete source exercise produces a goal card, operational checks, an artifact or explicitly labeled simulation, an acceptance record, and a versioned next decision. It does not establish achievement of an unrelated real-life aspiration, independent usability, or sustained behavioral change.

## Adaptations and progression

Use large print, distinct shapes, spoken home labels, tactile zones, or text-only mappings. Preserve the distinction between selecting an answer and receiving access assistance. An adaptation should remove incidental barriers without quietly changing three-item find-and-return into a different target. A changed target can be legitimate when versioned and described honestly.

For a harder route, add another user-stated requirement such as a shared item with two acceptable storage conditions. Check whether the criteria remain consistent and whether the added complexity is worth its benefit. Avoid inventing more requirements just to make the exercise look sophisticated.

For later transfer, define a small actual goal in a different domain and inspect it before committing money or other people's time. Choose a review condition that allows abandoning a no-longer-useful goal. Private reasons and free-text reflections remain private; they are not automated scoring inputs.

**Supportive:** The learner fixes the reason and criteria before testing, records a missed return without concealment, and revises the layout while preserving the original result.

**Mixed:** Items are easier to find, but returns or another user's burden remain unresolved and need another check.

**Contradictory:** Purchases or photographs are counted as the benefit, a protected access constraint is violated, or success criteria are rewritten afterward to manufacture completion.

**Inconclusive:** A precise-looking goal has no inspection method, no preserved result, or no explanation of why it remains worth pursuing.

**Final review:** Did your tests measure the benefit you intended, and what would justify revising or stopping the goal even before every target was reached?
