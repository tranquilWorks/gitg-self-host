# 11.03 — Fresh checks

Save your answers before opening the key. More than one well-justified goal formulation can be acceptable.

## Check A — Activity is not the intended benefit

A fictional person wants a reading corner that supports a comfortable, chosen 15-minute reading period. They set the target “buy three storage boxes this weekend.” No evidence shows boxes are needed. They already own a chair and lamp and have authority over their own corner; they do not have permission to move another person's belongings. No new spending is available in this case.

Rewrite the goal as an inspectable changed condition with a reason, a review point, two observable checks, protected constraints, and a stop/revise condition. Distinguish a method from an outcome. Would choosing to stop after learning that the person prefers reading elsewhere necessarily mean the goal-definition exercise failed?

## Check B — Missing evidence is not success

A paper layout finds and returns all three cards correctly, costs nothing, and takes 12 minutes to construct. Its maker filled reserved zone D with decoration despite the fixed clear-strip constraint. A second layout keeps D clear and records three correct finds, but records no returns. Both makers claim full success; the second says “no errors were recorded.”

Evaluate the two claims against the supplied goal, keeping failed and missing evidence distinct. What is the smallest useful next check or repair for each? May the first maker simply delete the D constraint after inspecting the result and report that version 1 passed?
