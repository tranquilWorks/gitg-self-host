# 11.02 — Priority hierarchy

## Purpose and a useful finish

Choose what receives attention first when several worthwhile demands cannot all fit. A priority order is useful only if it changes an actual disposition: do now, do next, reduce by agreement, defer, or decline. Calling everything important does not allocate capacity.

**Canonical scope:** Rank obligations, relationships, projects, maintenance, growth, and leisure using explicit criteria.

Your deliverable is a feasible priority board, one explained close tradeoff, and a revised board after a capacity change. Allow 25–30 minutes for the fictional packet. Paper cards, a text editor, dictation, or a willing planning aid are enough. No real calendar, message, payment, or commitment is changed by this case.

The six categories are dimensions to consider, not a universal moral ranking. Paid work need not outrank care, growth need not outrank leisure, and a forceful request is not necessarily an obligation. A lower position in one planning window does not mean that a person or activity has lower worth.

## Reference: a priority rule that can survive a conflict

First establish **the real capacity and protected floor**: necessary care, access needs, safety, and commitments that cannot be silently abandoned. Do not invent spare time by exporting work to someone else.

Then inspect **the reason and authority** behind each demand. Who agreed to what? Is the deadline real, negotiable, or merely requested? A promise may need renegotiation when circumstances change; ignoring it is not the same as obtaining consent to reduce it.

For feasible options, compare **consequence of delay, timing, affected people, present value, effort, and reversibility**. Make the decisive criterion visible. A weighted score can be a discussion aid, but it cannot cancel another person's rights or turn an unverified deadline into a fact.

Give each demand **one current disposition**, including a specific smaller version where appropriate. A reduced activity should have its own attainable result. “Do a bit of everything” is not sufficient when none of the promised outcomes can then occur.

Finally, record **the reconsideration point and communication needed**. Deferral is intentional waiting with a reason; disappearance from the list is not a decision. Unexpected obligations can legitimately change the order. Preserve the earlier board so the revision is accountable.

## Different worked example: two requests, one promise

A person has 45 available minutes. A previously promised 30-minute proofread is needed today; an optional design request asks for 30 minutes “ASAP” but has no confirmed deadline. Both might be valuable. A reasonable first move is to protect or explicitly renegotiate the promise, clarify the second request's real timing, and refrain from promising both within 45 minutes.

If the first task actually takes 40 minutes, the remaining five minutes do not become a completed 30-minute design. The result should be an updated commitment, not a prettier list. This example does not create a rule that every prior promise outranks a new urgent safety need.

## Complete practice packet: six demands, 210 minutes

The fictional person's protected care, sleep, meals, and recovery are already outside this discretionary planning budget and must remain protected. **The total discretionary budget is 210 minutes, including a 20-minute unallocated buffer.** All task durations below include their stated travel, preparation, and cleanup. No extra helper or money is available. Durations are case assumptions, not measured facts about real tasks.

| ID/category | Demand and reason | Full version | Permitted smaller version | Delay, agreement, or boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| O / obligation | Review an application as promised | 60 minutes; complete agreed review | None under the current agreement | Due Day 2; affected person depends on it |
| M / maintenance | Return borrowed materials as agreed | 30 minutes door-to-door | None | Due Day 3; not a live financial or legal task |
| R / relationship | Visit a willing friend | 60 minutes including transitions | 30-minute visit | Friend explicitly agrees either version; select and communicate one |
| P / project | Develop a personal hobby index | 75 minutes; all five sections | 30 minutes; one usable section | Optional; no outside promise or deadline |
| G / growth | Work through an elective lesson | 45 minutes; complete lesson | 15 minutes; first supplied practice only | Optional; partial route is not the full lesson |
| L / leisure | Play a chosen solo game | 60 minutes | 30 minutes | Freely chosen leisure, not a disposable moral failure |

O and M are current promised obligations; changing them requires explicit renegotiation, which has not occurred. R is a genuine relationship good, and the friend has already agreed that either listed version is welcome. You may defer P, G, or L, but must name the benefit you are forgoing. These facts fix feasibility; they do not dictate one universal best preference order.

### Action 1 — Make the conflict and criteria explicit

Add the full durations and the buffer. Compare that total with 210. Do the same with every nonzero smaller version. Do not subtract the buffer merely because the first plan is inconvenient. Identify the demands whose duration or obligation cannot be changed unilaterally under this packet.

Choose a present-season criterion among the remaining goods, such as preserving connection and some restorative leisure, or advancing a personally important project after obligations. Explain it before choosing the winning combination. Record relevant unknowns for a live version; do not fill a missing deadline or consent with a favorable guess.

Use this board:

`Demand | duty/promise or chosen reason | deadline evidence | full/minimum outcome and total duration | affected people | current disposition | criterion that decided it | communication | reconsideration point`

### Action 2 — Build a feasible first/next/reduced/deferred plan

Allocate O and M or record that the plan is blocked pending explicit renegotiation. Select a version of R, and decide among the remaining optional versions. Give a first action, a next action, at least one reduced version, and at least one intentional deferral. Check that chosen task time plus the protected buffer does not exceed 210.

Two legitimate plans may differ because the person's current priorities differ. Your explanation must still account for all six categories, even those deferred. A plan cannot win by inventing a five-minute version when only 15 or 30 minutes is sufficient for the defined reduced result.

Explain one close tradeoff without a total score. For example, keeping a 30-minute leisure block can mean deferring the hobby project. Choosing a small project section instead can mean postponing the lesson. Neither tradeoff erases the value of what waits. State when the deferred option will be reconsidered, without automatically scheduling anything.

Draft the necessary communication to the friend about the chosen visit length. The fictional packet establishes willingness; in a live case, a unilateral reduction is not the same as agreement. Write drafts only for this exercise.

### Action 3 — Replan when capacity changes

After saving the first board, apply this update: an unavoidable responsibility reduces the total discretionary budget from **210 to 160 minutes**. The **20-minute buffer remains protected**, and O/M have not been renegotiated. The friend still welcomes the 30-minute visit. No other task duration changes.

Compute the capacity left after O, M, a 30-minute R, and the buffer. Choose a revised disposition for the optional activities. State which original intentions now wait and what communication is needed. A new responsibility is not evidence that the earlier person lacked discipline; the feasible set changed.

For a live route, use the order at the next genuine collision, carry out one authorized next action or communicate a deferral, and compare the result with the board. A tabletop revision alone is not evidence of live follow-through. Do not send a refusal that risks essential support or safety merely to complete a priority exercise.

## Fresh checks and completion

Save responses to **check-prompts.md** before opening **check-answers.md**. The checks distinguish urgent wording from a verified obligation, and a coherent order from one made feasible by omitting transitions or agreement.

The useful artifact contains a capacity calculation, criteria, a complete disposition for every category, a close tradeoff, communication drafts, and a preserved revision. No single preferred ordering is automatically marked morally superior. A feasible board is necessary but does not establish that the intended outcomes actually occurred.

## Adaptations and progression

Use two cards at a time, spoken comparisons, pictures, or a trusted planning aid. Keep the final full list visible so a simplified interface does not silently erase care, maintenance, or leisure. Shared responsibilities should be ranked jointly when appropriate. Record help that makes choices for you separately from help presenting options.

For a harder route, add an uncertain duration range and test the plan at its high end, retaining the basis of the estimate. Inspect an expiring opportunity and whether delay is reversible. Do not replace every uncertainty with a worst-case assumption without considering whether that bound is appropriate to the decision.

For later transfer, review whether deferrals were reconsidered or quietly abandoned. Revise unrealistic capacity estimates and criteria rather than pretending the original board was followed. Background planning principles support visible constraints and commitments; this authored ranking is not an empirically validated life-priority algorithm. See [source notes](../SOURCES.md).

**Supportive:** The chosen versions fit, actual agreements are preserved, the sacrificed goods remain visible, and reduced capacity produces an honest revision.

**Mixed:** The order is defensible but a smaller version lacks agreement, or a forgotten transition makes the plan infeasible.

**Contradictory:** Every item is called top priority, care or another person's unpaid labor becomes imaginary capacity, or inconvenient promises disappear without renegotiation.

**Inconclusive:** Attractive categories or scores are written down, but nothing is assigned a usable first, reduced, or deferred disposition.

**Final review:** Which criterion actually decided the close conflict, which worthwhile thing will wait, and what would justify changing the order?
