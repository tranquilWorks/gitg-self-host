## Check A — A genuine change inside an exaggerated story

Post K says, “All community classes are cancelled indefinitely. Nobody can attend anything.” It quotes only “cancelled” from a notice. The complete notice, dated Day 8, says, “The pottery class scheduled for 14:00 on Day 9 is cancelled because its instructor is unavailable. The reading group at 16:00 on Day 9 remains scheduled.” Two local blogs quote Post K without adding records. A reading-group organizer independently shows the group's dated Day 9 attendance record: a session occurred at 16:00.

Separate the true bounded cancellation from the broader claims. Trace which records add independent information. Write a correction that does not falsely restore the cancelled pottery class, guarantee all future classes, or claim that K's operator deliberately deceived people. Which manipulation mechanisms are apparent from the text, and which actor claims remain unknown?

## Check B — Repetition is not attribution

Six public accounts post identical wording within ten minutes and all link to one organizer's announcement. You have no account-control data. The announcement states that a room changed from B to D on Day 10; the venue's independently issued Day 10 room schedule confirms D. One commentator says the identical posts prove that all six accounts are automated and controlled by a hostile group.

Distinguish the room-change claim, source dependence, and the commentator's attribution. Give at least two explanations compatible with the repeated text. State what can responsibly be concluded without investigating private identities. Does the fact that reposts depend on one source make the confirmed room change false? What information-use decision follows?
