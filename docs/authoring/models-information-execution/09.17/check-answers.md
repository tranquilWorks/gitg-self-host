# 09.17 — Corrective guidance

## Main practice

H1's permanent whole-hall closure claim is contradicted as stated by N1, which expressly specifies a temporary North Studio closure and denies permanent closure. W1 independently supports South Room being in use during its recorded interval on Day 6. It does not verify every room or every future date.

The required rescue-payment claim and its claimed official deadline are unsupported by H1's authority and contradicted by N1's explicit payment statement. This finding does not establish whether anyone paid, where money would have gone, or who operated the post. The packet contains no payment-account investigation.

A1, A2, and A3 add copies, not independent confirmation. P1 uses a separate contact route but repeats N1; it helps establish that the notice is current within this fictional case, not a second independently observed event. W1 has its own observation basis. Its independence does not expand its scope.

The text uses selective cropping, identity pressure, urgency, an ambiguous scope of closure, a demand to act, and framing that preemptively dismisses a likely correcting source. Those are reasons to inspect the message; they do not establish a specific operator, motive, or coordinated campaign. The factual contradiction can be reported without pretending those additional facts are known.

A bounded draft correction is: “The full Day 4 notice specifies North Studio cleaning on Day 6 from 10:00 to 12:00, not permanent hall closure. It says no rescue payment is required. South Room was independently recorded in use at 12:10 on Day 6. I do not know who created the cropped post.” Shorter corrections may omit the witness while retaining the essential scope and payment distinction.

Repair a weak answer by replacing a global verdict with one row per claim and by separating an independent route from an independent observation. No actual message, payment, or contact occurred in this exercise.

## Check A — A genuine change inside an exaggerated story

The pottery class at the specified Day 9 time is cancelled according to the complete notice. “All classes indefinitely” goes beyond that notice and conflicts with the independently recorded reading-group session. The two blogs repeat K and add no new observation. The group's attendance record independently supports one session having occurred; it does not establish every class's status.

A suitable correction preserves the pottery cancellation while limiting the broader story: “The notice cancels the Day 9 pottery class at 14:00; the reading group did meet at 16:00. It does not announce indefinite cancellation of all classes.” The selective quote and broad narrative are inspectable features. Deliberate intent and operator identity remain unestablished.

Do not react to misleading framing by dismissing its true fragment. That would create a different inaccurate message and could send someone to a genuinely cancelled class.

## Check B — Repetition is not attribution

The venue schedule independently corroborates Room D for Day 10 within the supplied packet. The six account posts share one announcement, so they are not six independent observations. Dependence reduces the number of independent evidence chains; it does not make an otherwise corroborated fact false.

Ordinary supporters copying an announcement, coordinated volunteers, one scheduling service, or automation could all produce similar timing and wording. The packet cannot distinguish them. Automation would not itself establish a hostile controller, and identical text is not a reliable identity proof.

Use the verified room schedule for the bounded planning decision. Record the attribution as unresolved and do not identify, contact, or target private people. A credible factual update can coexist with an unsupported allegation about the people who shared it.
