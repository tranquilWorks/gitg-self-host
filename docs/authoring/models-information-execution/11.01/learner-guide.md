# 11.01 — Time and energy audit

## Purpose and a useful finish

Compare an endorsed priority with where time actually goes and when that activity is realistically accessible. A blank calendar slot is not necessarily usable capacity. An obligation, a needed recovery period, or an inaccessible task is not a character failure.

**Canonical scope:** Measure how time and usable energy are actually spent rather than relying on impression.

Your deliverable is a bounded observation record, one evidence-based mismatch, and a small allocation experiment that preserves its real costs. Allow about 25 minutes for the supplied fictional case. A live route uses three brief reconstructions over comparable days and one later attempt; it does not require continuous tracking or installing monitoring software. Paper, voice notes, icons, or a private text editor are sufficient.

The default studies only **18:00–21:00 on three days**, not all waking time or a whole life. All times in its packet are stipulated fictional records. For a real audit, uncertain memory should stay approximate. This is not a medical measure of fatigue, a diagnostic assessment, or a score of productivity or worth.

## Reference: four distinctions before counting

**Planned versus actual:** “I reserved 30 minutes for reading” is a plan. “I rested during that interval” is the activity record. Keep both rather than rewriting one to match the other.

**Time versus usable energy:** Record whether the intended activity was accessible at that time, using your own words or low/medium/high. Those are ordered personal descriptions, not quantities that can be multiplied by minutes to produce scientifically measured “energy units.” Different tasks can be accessible under different conditions.

**Choice versus constraint:** Separate required responsibilities, protected recovery, flexible choices, and unknown time. The categories describe the current situation, not a ranking of moral value. A freely chosen leisure activity may be worthwhile. A genuinely constrained period cannot be reclaimed by renaming it.

**Coverage versus certainty:** State the window and account for every interval exactly once. Concurrent activities belong in the same clock interval, with one primary category and an optional secondary note. Missing activity is unknown, not zero. A long record is not automatically accurate or representative of another week.

## Different worked example: a commute overlaps listening

A person travels from 08:00 to 08:30 and listens to a lecture from 08:10 to 08:30. The elapsed window is 30 minutes, not 50. The 20 minutes of listening describe an activity within the commute, not a second free block. If attention to travel must take priority, do not infer that this is usable time for every learning task. A bounded audit preserves both the overlap and the constraint.

### Action 1 — Reconcile the record before interpreting it

For each day, confirm that the non-overlapping intervals cover 180 minutes. Aggregate required time, protected recovery, known flexible activity, and unknown time across the three days. Keep the 30 unknown minutes visible in the denominator rather than deleting the day.

Record the observed reading minutes and the number of evenings with a confirmed ten-minute reading attempt. Explain what the unknown block prevents you from concluding. Distinguish “only ten minutes are recorded as reading” from “exactly ten minutes of reading certainly occurred.” Do not treat a missing entry as either proof of success or proof of failure.

Use this private record form for a live route:

`Day and sampled window | planned activity, if relevant | actual interval/activity | estimate or range | usable energy for the priority | required/protected/flexible/unknown | overlap or interruption | confidence in recall`

For a full-day adaptation, cover the day with major blocks including sleep, care, work, travel, meals, maintenance, recovery, and leisure. Avoid fictitious precision. When a block's endpoints or duration are uncertain, record a range and do not add incompatible high endpoints as if they were one observed day.

### Action 2 — Name one controllable mismatch and its tradeoff

Compare the reading intention with both time and energy. D1's late planned slot became rest; that does not establish that the person should sacrifice recovery. D1 and D2 contain earlier known flexible medium-energy video blocks. Identify a ten-minute substitution within one such block, leaving care, meals, cleanup, and protected recovery unchanged.

Write the displaced benefit honestly: ten fewer minutes of chosen leisure, not ten minutes rescued from an activity declared valueless. Specify the planned trigger, duration, access support, and stop/revise condition. One suitable default is, “On the next comparable evening, after personal administration and while reading is accessible, use the first ten minutes of the video block to read; retain recovery and stop or shorten the trial if a required responsibility intervenes.” This is a trial, not a command for every day.

Acknowledge D3's constraint. It does not contain evidence of a comparable available block. A person with no discretionary capacity may complete the analysis by identifying an external constraint and a legitimate support or renegotiation need; inventing free time would be the error.

### Action 3 — Attempt or inspect the trial and include its cost

Save the proposed allocation before opening **later-packet.md**. The supplied follow-up is a fictional trial result, not a record of your behavior. Compare it using the same activity definitions. Inspect actual reading, its displaced activity, required time, protected recovery, and changed context. Do not call a seven-minute attempt a completed ten-minute target.

Choose to keep, shrink, move, or reverse the allocation with a reason. A later day may differ in care demands; record that difference rather than claiming the allocation alone caused the outcome. If using a live route, retain the actual attempt and any missing observation. Stop unnecessary logging when it ceases to help; the aim is one usable decision, not surveillance.

## Fresh checks and a completion boundary

Attempt **check-prompts.md**, then consult **check-answers.md**. One case tests overlap and missing time; the other contains no discretionary block. Both require conclusions that respect what was actually observed.

Completion of this bounded exercise means a reconciled record, a priority/time/energy mismatch or justified no-capacity finding, a tradeoff, and a trial review or honestly untested plan. A fictional success does not establish improved personal allocation. A plan alone does not establish the live progress indicator.

## Accessibility and progression

Use broad blocks, a few symbols, voice notes, or a support person who records rather than judges. Keep established accommodations. Compare similar-capacity days when conditions vary; do not force a “normal day” that does not exist. Energy descriptions may be task-specific or simply accessible/not accessible/unknown.

For a more demanding route, compare a workday and non-workday, or add an interruption category while preserving non-overlap. Separate your choice from costs moved onto another person. Any proposed reassignment of shared responsibilities requires agreement, not an accounting trick.

For later transfer, repeat the small audit after a meaningful change in responsibilities. Do not promise that a three-day sample identifies a permanent routine or that time management can remove structural overload. Background research concerns broad associations, not validation of this original case or a guarantee of individual benefit; see [source notes](../SOURCES.md).

**Supportive:** The record exposes an earlier usable block, the chosen tradeoff preserves obligations and recovery, and the follow-up reports the actual duration and remaining constraint.

**Mixed:** Reading occurs, but a displaced task silently moves to another person or the shortened attempt is reported as meeting the original target.

**Contradictory:** Required care, access needs, or recovery are labeled wasted time; unknown time is relabeled free; or overlapping activities are counted as extra capacity.

**Inconclusive:** An ideal schedule is drawn without any actual record or a comparison to the endorsed priority.

**Final review:** Did the change provide usable attention without hiding its cost, and which part of the mismatch is genuinely within your control?
