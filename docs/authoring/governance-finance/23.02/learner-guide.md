# 23.02 — Budgeting and cash-flow control

Build a two-cycle spending calendar that works on the dates money is needed. Allow forty minutes for the calendars and ten for review. These invented amounts are not recommended percentages. The exercise tests obligations and chosen priorities together: resilience, goals, generosity and enjoyment all remain visible.

## A monthly surplus can arrive too late

A budget allocates money across purposes; a cash-flow calendar tests whether available money covers each dated movement. The month's total can look comfortable while a bill arrives before the next payment. A repair must change an actual resource or a feasible payment arrangement before the shortfall occurs. Deleting a later optional purchase does not create earlier cash.

The [CFPB cash-flow worksheet, S06](../SOURCES.md#S06) uses a carried starting balance and incoming and outgoing money to expose short periods. Here the same principle is applied to individual days in an original case. Moving money into a separate savings pot reduces the amount available in the spending pot, but it is not consumption or a loss of household wealth. Do not count the same transfer twice when reconciling the whole household.

## Complete first-cycle calendar

Start with **100** available cash. Income is **1,200 on day 1** and **1,200 on day 15**. Every movement below is from or into the spendable pot; separate reserve and goal pots begin at zero. There are no other movements, interest or fees.

| Day | Movement | Amount out unless labeled income |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Income | +1,200 |
| 2 | Rent | 1,000 |
| 8 | Essentials | 400 |
| 15 | Income | +1,200 |
| 18 | Utilities | 150 |
| 22 | Transport | 100 |
| 25 | Debt payment | 100 |
| 28 | Enjoyment | 150 |
| 28 | Generosity | 50 |
| 29 | Reserve transfer | 100 |
| 29 | Goal transfer | 100 |

Outflows total **2,150**, leaving an arithmetic ending balance of **100 + 2,400 − 2,150 = 350**. But after day 1 the balance is 1,300; after rent it is 300; after essentials it would be **−100 on day 8**. This is an infeasible point in the original plan, not permission to overdraw or evidence of an actual bank transaction. Mark it before carrying the arithmetic forward.

## Use the supplied agreement, not imagined consent

The case includes one confirmed fictional repair: the rent provider agrees to **800 on day 2 and 200 on day 16, with no fee or other consequence**. This is a supplied fact for the exercise. An actual person would need an appropriate confirmed agreement before relying on a changed due date. The worksheet itself cannot renegotiate a contract.

With the split, available cash is **1,300 after day 1, 500 after day 2 and 100 after day 8**. The second income raises it to 1,300 on day 15; the remaining rent leaves 1,100 on day 16. Utilities, transport and debt reduce it to 950, 850 and 750. Day-28 allocations leave 550; the two saving transfers leave **350**. The lowest revised balance is **100**, including the opening position. Total rent and month-end spendable cash are unchanged; timing is what repaired the failure.

The optional enjoyment and generosity happen on day 28. Canceling them alone would increase the end balance but would not remove the original day-8 shortage. Nor may you remove essentials from the case because they are inconvenient. If a different earlier optional expense existed, reducing it might help, but none is supplied. State that limitation rather than inventing a hidden source of money.

## Make priorities explicit enough to revisit

Obligations protect immediate commitments. Reserves can absorb later disruptions; goal saving serves a chosen future use. Generosity and enjoyment can be legitimate priorities alongside both. A workable plan gives each a reason and an adjustable boundary. It does not demand that a person prove seriousness by eliminating every pleasure or gift. The fictional allocation is one choice, not a universal ideal.

Review matters because even a careful calendar can become stale. A due-date change, reduced pay, extra care cost or delayed transfer can remove the buffer. A useful review asks what changed and whether the next required movement is covered. It need not track every private detail in a shared app. When essentials exceed resources, help with assistance, negotiation or stabilization may be needed; a balanced-looking sheet cannot create income.

## Produce two calendars and a review rule

1. Enter every first-cycle movement in order and calculate the running balance. Label obligations and each chosen purpose. Flag the original day-8 shortfall even though the total ends positive.
2. Rebuild the calendar using the confirmed rent split. Show why the lowest balance is 100 and why the end is still 350. Explain why canceling day-28 spending does not repair day 8 in the original calendar.
3. Prepare the next cycle using the carried **350**, the same revised outflows and first income on day 1. Move the second 1,200 payment to **day 17**. Calculate the balance before and after day-16 rent rather than assuming the prior solution still works.
4. Choose a weekly review point, such as day 7 before essentials. Name an early trigger: expected available cash below a required upcoming payment, or income not confirmed before a dependent due date. Optional allocations may be reconsidered with reasons; a revised obligation requires an authorized arrangement.
5. Save your calendars before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md). Distinguish a plan, a simulated movement and a later actual spending record. Two simulated cycles cannot establish real adherence.

For support, use a tactile calendar or one event per card and calculate together, recording the help. For a harder case, delay the second-cycle income past utilities as well; identify the first point requiring a new plan rather than assuming another agreement. Optional actual transfer is a private comparison of a real plan with subsequent movements, with no linked account requirement. Retain mismatches and resource constraints. A useful budget is one you can revise honestly, not a test of personal worth.
