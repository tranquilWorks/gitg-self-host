# 22.14 — Institutional capture and concentrated power

Compare three ways an institution can become dependent on the people it should assess. Allow thirty minutes to map the cases and ten to pressure-test your safeguards. The result is a mechanism-and-checks analysis, not a list of unpopular people or a claim that every concentrated interest is corrupt.

## Follow the dependency to the decision

An institution can retain its formal title while its practical choices narrow. Ask who supplies the money, information, appointments, access or career opportunities on which decision-makers depend. Then ask how that dependence could divert the institution from its duty. Influence becomes a capture concern when a particular interest persistently controls the process or its outputs at the expense of that duty. A donor, specialist or experienced leader may also provide legitimate resources. Their involvement alone does not establish capture.

The [OECD report abstract, S02](../SOURCES.md#S02) frames policy capture as repeated diversion from public purposes and identifies participation, transparency, accountability and integrity safeguards. Only the landing abstract was inspected, not the full report or its case studies. The three cases below are original teaching materials. Their mechanisms and counterfactual tests are analytical proposals, not OECD findings about actual institutions.

Boards may depend on a major donor; regulators may rely on industry expertise; professional bodies may allow insiders to select their own reviewers. Parties can face a similar problem if funders or gatekeepers narrow who can stand or which interests are heard. A leader can concentrate appointment and appeal powers even without taking money. Keep these routes distinct: disclosure about funding cannot by itself repair an exclusive information channel, and rotation without a fair appointment process can replace one loyal group with another.

## Three complete fictional case cards

| Case | Assigned duty and observed arrangement | Uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hall board | Allocate community rooms using published access criteria. One donor pays 60 of the board's 100 budget units and asks the chair to remove evening access from next month's agenda. The chair removes it without a recorded board decision. Four other board members may restore agenda items by majority. | The donor's reasons, other members' views and the final access outcome are unknown. |
| Water review office | Assess a fictional monitoring method impartially. The regulated firms fund and supply every technical briefing. The office has no independent testing budget. Its director can commission an external review using 8 discretionary units, but has not done so. | The supplied technical claims have not been verified; no unsafe water or false report is established. |
| Design profession panel | Select training reviewers for competence and independence. The panel leader appoints all five selectors, including three former campaign supporters. Applicants receive no selection reasons and appeals return to the leader. The elected council can revise appointments and appeals next month. | Loyalty does not establish incompetence; the effect on past selections is unknown. |

For the hall, an alternative 10-unit unrestricted grant is available only next quarter. It cannot replace 60 units immediately. For the office, the external reviewer has relevant expertise but must disclose other industry engagements; independence cannot be assumed from the word external. For the panel, two rotating seats could be filled through published criteria, but the current leader would still choose the shortlist unless that power changes. These constraints are part of the supplied work.

## Worked mechanism, including a plausible innocent explanation

Start with the hall. A donation can legitimately support room provision, and a donor may flag genuine scheduling problems. The observed issue is narrower: a funding request has removed an access question without the board's prescribed decision. Map the chain as funding dependence, chair agenda control, excluded deliberation and possible departure from access duty. Evidence establishes the request and removal, not a recurring bargain or donor intent. A stronger inquiry would ask for the authorized agenda record and funding conditions; it would not secretly investigate the donor.

A proportionate immediate safeguard is a recorded board decision on restoring the item, with the donor interest disclosed and affected users able to contribute. Longer-term diversification reduces dependence, but the 10-unit grant still leaves a large gap. Record the service costs of refusing funding rather than pretending that integrity has no tradeoffs. A transparent donor agreement can clarify limits on influence; it cannot guarantee that informal pressure disappears.

## Build and challenge three maps

1. For every case, state the institution's duty, dependent resource, influence route, missing check and precisely observed evidence. Separate suspected effects from established actions. Include a legitimate reason for stakeholder involvement.
2. Propose one immediate check and one structural safeguard per case. For each, name the supplied authority that could act, the resources needed and what remains outside its control. Keep unapproved reforms labeled proposals.
3. Test the office's information dependence. An independent technical review, publication of methods where lawful, competing expertise and documented responses could improve challenge. Disclosing the firms' funding is helpful but insufficient if nobody can test their claims.
4. Test the panel's appointment dependence. Require criteria, conflict handling, reasoned decisions and an appeal reviewer outside the original chain. Ask who appoints that reviewer and can remove them. Rotating seats without changing the shortlist may preserve the same gatekeeper.
5. Write a pressure response for donor withdrawal, a firm disputing an external report and the panel leader objecting to independent appeal. Specify which decision pauses, what evidence is needed and who retains lawful authority. Save your maps before the [later packet](later-packet.md).

Checks create their own risks. Public minutes may need redaction to protect applicants. Multiple reviews can delay a decision or exhaust a small organization. A new funder can create a new dependence. Countervailing power needs defined responsibility so that no faction gains an unchecked veto. The goal is an institution able to hear, test and correct important claims while continuing its legitimate work.

## Evidence and transfer

For a supported attempt, use the hall model to label the five columns, then generate the office and panel maps yourself. For a harder pass, combine funding and appointment dependence: the donor offers replacement money only if allowed to appoint the appeals reviewer. Identify why this exchanges one problem for another rather than solving both.

Optional actual transfer is a review of public governing documents for a low-stakes group. Report only document features and unanswered questions. Do not infer personal corruption, collect private political affiliations or pressure members to disclose relationships. A record of a real challenge reaching an independent authorized review would be stronger than a tidy diagram, but even that would not establish whole-institution freedom from capture. Actual legal, regulatory or governance reform needs responsible authority and qualified advice.
