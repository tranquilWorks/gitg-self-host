# 23.04 — Debt and credit

Compare two fictional debts by purpose, total obligation and a repayment exit that survives a missed assumption. Allow thirty minutes for comparison and twenty for stress testing. No credit application, guarantee, refinancing or balance transfer is part of this exercise. A decision to defer can be the most accurate result.

## Borrowing changes both today's options and tomorrow's claims

Credit lets a person use resources before paying for them in full. It also creates an obligation with dates, conditions and consequences. Whether the purpose is worthwhile and whether repayment is feasible are separate questions. A useful purchase does not automatically make its financing affordable. Approval or a high credit score likewise does not show that a proposed payment fits the borrower's actual constraints.

Interest is one part of cost. Fees and other terms can change the comparison. The CFPB's [interest-rate and APR explanation, S09](../SOURCES.md#S09) distinguishes a rate of interest from a broader annual percentage measure incorporating relevant fees. The inspected page is in a U.S. auto-loan context. The simple rates below are **nominal interest assumptions, not disclosed APRs**. Do not calculate or claim regulatory APRs from this abbreviated teaching file.

Leverage magnifies the consequence of an assumption failing because repayment remains due even if the financed plan disappoints. A balloon loan concentrates the obligation at one date. Saving toward that date can create a path, but the path fails if contributions are missed and no other dependable resource closes the gap. Collateral adds a possible loss of a pledged asset; its exact enforcement consequences require the actual terms and applicable law.

## Complete teaching offer cards

Both loans provide **1,000 principal now for exactly one year**, with **no interim loan payments**. Simple interest and any fee are paid at the end. No compounding or other charge is included in the base arithmetic. Neither base loan has collateral. These invented cards are not current offers.

| Term | A | B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Principal received | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| Nominal simple interest for the year | 10% | 12% |
| Fee due at the end | 50 | 0 |
| Interim loan payments | None | None |
| Final due date | Exactly one year | Exactly one year |

The fictional purpose is a 1,000-unit training course that may expand future work options, with no promised job or increased earnings. A free introductory course is available now but covers fewer topics. Deferring the paid course to its next intake means waiting a year; no deposit or cancellation loss is supplied. The household can set aside **94 monthly** only if income and essential costs remain unchanged. Those savings are not payments to the lender and do not reduce the balloon before its due date.

## Worked cost comparison

A's interest is **1,000 × 10% = 100**, so its total is **1,000 + 100 + 50 = 1,150**. B's interest is **120**, giving **1,120** total. A has the lower nominal rate but costs **30 more** under these terms. A low monthly payment would also be misleading here because neither loan requires one; the whole amount is due at the end.

For B, twelve savings contributions give **94 × 12 = 1,128**, leaving only **8** beyond the 1,120 obligation. The base path works arithmetically but has little margin. It does not establish that the household will make every contribution or that the course will provide a financial return. If the household has no dependable way to absorb missed saving, choosing the cheaper loan still leaves the central affordability concern unresolved.

The real-loan questions exceed these cards: exact payment dates, interest method, financed fees, optional products, prepayment treatment, late charges, collateral, default consequences and any conditions on the advertised offer. Do not fill these gaps from an unrelated product. An actual comparison needs the applicable official disclosure, with independent qualified assistance where appropriate. A claim that debt can always be refinanced or forgiven is not an exit plan.

## Test the path before making a conditional decision

1. Write the purpose, expected benefit, uncertainty and non-borrowing alternative. State what deferral costs in this case without inventing lost wages or a guaranteed career gain.
2. Calculate both total repayments and identify why the lower interest rate does not win. Label each rate correctly and list the missing real-loan information separately from the complete toy arithmetic.
3. Make a twelve-month contribution tracker for B. Stress it by omitting **month 4 and month 7**. At month 4, the first missed contribution already reduces the projected twelve-month total to **1,034** if the remaining 94 contributions occur, a projected shortfall of **86**. Do not wait for the final due date to notice.
4. After the second missed contribution, compute the revised saved total and final gap. Name an early contact route: the actual creditor through its official channel for possible arrangements, or reputable independent debt guidance. No agreement, new loan or forgiveness is assumed.
5. Consider a separate collateral variant: B would require a necessary work tool as security. Describe the asset potentially at risk and the added consequence of failure without guessing legal recovery rights. Do not pledge anything.
6. Choose borrow conditionally, defer, seek another option or decline. State a maximum total obligation and the conditions for accepting it. One defensible teaching decision is to **defer any new debt**, with a maximum currently accepted obligation of **0**, while exploring the free course and dependable funding. A conditional comparison ceiling of 1,120 is not acceptance of B. Save your decision before the [later packet](later-packet.md).

## Track an exit, not just an intention

If borrowing were genuinely chosen after appropriate review, an exit record would show the obligation, due date, funds actually reserved, forecast gap, review dates and response to changed capacity. The forecast should update after each contribution or missed contribution. An intention to work more is not an earned resource. Promised help needs verification before it supports a repayment claim.

For support, calculate interest with counters or a calculator and keep the cards alongside the totals. For a harder pass, add a financed fee and explain why the original arithmetic no longer applies until the charging basis is known. Optional actual transfer is a private reading of a disclosure already available to you, with no transaction. Real hardship may require reputable debt, legal or benefits support; verify credentials and fees. A person who declines an unaffordable obligation has not failed this practice.
