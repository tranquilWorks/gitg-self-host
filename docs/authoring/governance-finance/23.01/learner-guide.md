# 23.01 — Personal financial literacy

Explain one fictional household's position without confusing the month's movements with what is owned or owed. Allow thirty minutes with paper or a calculator, then ten for a six-sentence explanation. All amounts are invented currency units. You need no account access, tax identifier or personal financial disclosure.

## Keep a flow and a balance in different columns

Income and spending happen over a period. Cash and debt balances describe a point in time. The same household can have a positive cash balance, owe money and spend more than it earns in a particular month. These are compatible facts. A large credit limit is permission to borrow under terms; it is not earned income, an asset free of obligations or proof that repayment is affordable.

Gross pay is the amount before deductions. Take-home pay is what enters the spending plan after those deductions. Withholding is not automatically the final tax bill. The IRS's [withholding explanation, S15](../SOURCES.md#S15) describes U.S. pay-as-you-go payments and possible under- or over-withholding. It is used only to explain that distinction, not to calculate a tax return or recommend a withholding change. Real tax and benefits treatment requires current local information.

A debt payment is a cash outflow, but the amount reducing principal depends on interest, fees and allocation. You cannot infer the closing debt balance just by subtracting the entire payment from principal unless the terms establish that treatment. Similarly, spending less than income does not establish resilience if large bills, unstable pay or inaccessible savings are omitted. The [CFPB cash-flow tool, S06](../SOURCES.md#S06) supports separating starting cash, incoming money and outgoing money before carrying a balance forward. Its worksheet is an educational aid, not a credit or stability assessment.

## Supplied month L1

| Item | Amount | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross pay | 3,000 | Income before withholding |
| Withholding | 600 | Deduction from gross pay |
| Take-home pay | 2,400 | Cash inflow |
| Obligations | 1,500 | Cash outflow |
| Variable spending | 500 | Cash outflow |
| Debt payment | 100 | Cash outflow; allocation unknown |
| Opening accessible cash | 400 | Starting cash balance |
| Opening debt | 1,200 | Starting amount owed |

No other cash movements occur in L1. Payment dates within the month, final tax liability, the debt's fee and interest allocation, and other assets are not supplied. Do not invent them. Separately, use a toy interest calculation of **1% for one month on 1,200 before any payment**, and a basket that changes price from **100 to 105**. These independent teaching examples are not the household's actual loan terms or a current national inflation statistic.

## Reconcile the position before interpreting it

Gross pay less withholding is **3,000 − 600 = 2,400**. Outflows are **1,500 + 500 + 100 = 2,100**. The period's surplus is **300**, and closing accessible cash is **400 + 300 = 700**. Starting cash is not added to income when describing earnings. The opening 1,200 debt is not subtracted again as a monthly expense. The 100 payment has already appeared once in cash outflow.

Closing debt remains unknown because payment allocation and any charges are not given. The separate interest example is **1,200 × 0.01 = 12**. It would be wrong to silently attach that 12 to L1, assume 88 principal repayment and announce a closing debt of 1,112. That could be correct under additional terms, but those terms are absent. A sound explanation leaves an important number unresolved rather than making the table look complete.

The basket rises by 5 currency units, which is **5 / 100 = 5%**. The original 100 now buys less of that same basket; purchasing power has fallen relative to those items. This does not show that every item rose equally, that wages changed or that next year's prices will follow the same path. A percentage needs its base and period, just as an interest rate needs its charging method and timing.

## Compare what the surplus could do

For the fictional 300 surplus, write three conditional options. Keeping it accessible could help with a disruption, while forgoing an immediate debt reduction or goal purchase. Applying it to principal could reduce future interest if the real terms permit, while leaving less readily available cash; allocation and prepayment terms must be checked. Using it for a chosen goal could deliver a valued benefit while delaying reserve growth or debt reduction. The question is what is given up, not which option proves better character.

Risk includes both loss and the inability to meet an obligation at the needed time. A positive month-end balance does not answer every timing question. An information-gathering step can therefore be more responsible than selecting a product. For this case, the loan statement showing payment allocation would resolve a specific debt unknown. A payslip and relevant tax information may clarify withholding, but the payslip alone does not determine final tax liability.

## Produce and check the explanation

1. Create a flow column and opening/closing balance columns. Reconcile gross to net, total outflows, surplus and accessible cash. Leave closing debt explicitly unknown.
2. Show the separate 12-unit interest and 5% basket calculations with their assumptions. Mark them as examples, not additional household movements.
3. Compare the three uses of 300 using benefit, foregone alternative and missing information. Name why credit availability does not remove repayment risk.
4. Explain the case in six sentences: net income; outflows; cash change; amount owed and its date; unresolved tax or loan detail; most material vulnerability. Use a private voice note or willing listener without personal identifiers. Record any correction rather than only reporting that the explanation felt clear.
5. Save your attempt before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md). Recompute the next month from its changed income and carry forward actual supplied cash, not a desired target.

For support, use counters or spoken arithmetic and have a helper label stocks versus flows before you calculate. For a harder pass, ask what dated bills you would need before declaring the 700 accessible throughout the month. Optional actual transfer is a private, redacted review of your own figures, with qualified help for tax, credit or benefits questions. Do not assess another person's competence from their income or savings. Understanding a shortfall can be progress even when resources have not improved.
