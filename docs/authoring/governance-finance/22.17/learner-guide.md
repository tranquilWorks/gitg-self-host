# 22.17 — Abuse response and institutional repair

Prepare responses to two fictional institutional reports without investigating a person. Allow thirty minutes for the response map and ten for the later findings. The goal is to protect affected people, preserve fair process and repair conditions that could allow recurrence. Reputation management is not a substitute for those responsibilities.

## Separate protection, determination and repair

An initial report can warrant protective action before the institution knows every fact. Interim measures should be authorized, proportionate and reviewed; they should not quietly penalize the person who raised the concern or announce a final finding against the respondent. Determining what occurred requires competent, independent procedures that can assess relevant evidence and hear responses. Remediation then addresses identified harm and failures, while structural repair changes the conditions that enabled them.

Harassment, discrimination, violence, fraud and cover-up may require different expertise and reporting routes. Harassment or discrimination concerns need appropriate safeguarding or workplace response; actual violence or imminent danger requires urgent safety help; possible fraud needs qualified financial and legal handling; interference with records or reports needs independent oversight. Do not flatten these into interpersonal misunderstandings or assume every dispute requires mediation. Never require an affected person to confront an alleged abuser as an exercise.

The EEOC's [2017 promising-practices document, S05](../SOURCES.md#S05) supports multiple complaint routes, neutral investigation, privacy limits, anti-retaliation follow-through and accountable correction. It is historical, nonbinding U.S. technical assistance, not a current legal ruling for every jurisdiction. Only the identified response principles are used here. Current reporting duties, employment rights, safeguarding rules and specialist procedures must be verified for actual cases.

## Two original report cards

**H: conduct report.** A fictional program assistant, **Ivo**, reports repeated demeaning remarks by program lead **Tess** during planning sessions and an unwanted personal message after Ivo asked for it to stop. No graphic detail is supplied or needed. Ivo requests no direct meeting with Tess and wishes to keep an existing paid shift. Tess controls the usual reporting inbox and shift allocation. An independently appointed safeguarding officer, **Wen**, can receive reports, arrange support and recommend interim measures to an uninvolved authorized director, **Uma**. A qualified external investigator can be commissioned by Uma if appropriate. There is no reported immediate physical danger in this card.

**F: financial report.** Bookkeeper **Ren** notices the same fictional receipt reference, R17, attached to two expense entries of **80 units each**. Ren is authorized to see the ledger and reports the discrepancy. Payment status and whether it is a duplicate entry, erroneous attachment or deliberate claim are unknown. Finance lead **Cal** asks Ren to delete one entry “so auditors do not get confused.” The independent audit contact, **Dee**, is authorized to receive the concern and arrange competent review; Cal cannot determine the outcome of a report about Cal's instruction. No real receipt, account or transaction is included.

Both cards use fictional policy A7. It offers need-to-know handling subject to investigation and legal duties, an alternate independent oversight route if the assigned receiver is implicated, acknowledgment within two working days, a named contact for updates, and a prohibition on retaliation with a route to report it. The acknowledgment standard is not a legal deadline. Qualified responsible people must determine any mandatory reporting or preservation duties; the learner must not invent confidentiality promises or decide that reporting can wait.

## Worked first response to H

An unsent rehearsal response could say: “Thank you for telling me. You do not need to meet Tess for this intake. Wen can explain support and reporting options, including privacy limits, and Uma can consider interim arrangements that preserve your shift where possible. We will identify who can update you and when.” This acknowledges the report without promising absolute secrecy, guaranteed job protection or a finding before investigation.

Wen should establish immediate needs through a limited, appropriate intake rather than demand repeated retelling. Uma can consider removing Tess from decisions about Ivo's shifts while the matter is assessed, subject to actual authority and review. The institution should preserve relevant records through lawful qualified procedures; the learner does not gather messages, interview witnesses, demand medical information or decide credibility from demeanor. Tess's opportunity to respond belongs in the fair investigative process, not in a forced encounter with Ivo.

## Write two response maps

1. For H and F separately, record the report as an allegation or discrepancy, who may be exposed to continuing harm, the immediate safety question and who has authority to act. Identify the conflicted ordinary route and the supplied independent alternative.
2. Specify a bounded intake, support contact, privacy explanation and update point. For H preserve Ivo's refusal of direct contact. For F preserve the original record history rather than quietly deleting the duplicate-looking line.
3. Assign investigation and lawful evidence preservation to competent authorized people. List unresolved reporting-duty questions for qualified review. Do not decide legal guilt, discipline, mandatory reporting exemptions or retention periods yourself.
4. Separate interim measures from final consequences. Define how the respondent can be heard by an impartial process and how affected people can report retaliation or a failed protective measure. A no-retaliation policy is a commitment requiring follow-through, not proof of safety.
5. For each possible supported finding, propose a proportionate remedy and a structural change. H may expose a reporting inbox controlled by the person reported; F may expose weak duplicate detection and edit history. Assign owners, an authorized review date and evidence that could show whether a repair works.
6. Draft a limited communication: what process is underway, what people can do and when the next update is expected. Do not publish identities or reassure the public that everything is resolved. Save both maps before the [later packet](later-packet.md).

Repair should be assessed by affected people's practical protection, correction of supported failures and reduced opportunity for recurrence. A lower complaint count alone is ambiguous: it might reflect improvement, discouragement or an inaccessible channel. Ask whether reports can reach an independent receiver, interim measures remain usable and corrections survive leadership changes. Do not require a complainant to validate the institution's success narrative.

## Supported and actual routes

For support, complete H using the worked response and then generate F independently. A harder pass makes Uma implicated; use A7's alternate oversight route and record any authority or resourcing gap. Do not turn the exercise into a simulated interrogation. Optional actual transfer is an authorized review of a blank response policy for conflicting roles and missing follow-up, without accessing case files. Real abuse concerns require appropriate professional and urgent support. A polished plan, one finding or a revised inbox does not prove that harm has ended.

## Scope and evidence boundary

The canonical record has no professional_boundary field. Practical safeguards do not add canonical metadata. Applicability and normative status remain role_conditional. A leadership role is not compulsory. Use actual authority and willing participation; keep protected records outside app evidence. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Human dignity is never scored.
