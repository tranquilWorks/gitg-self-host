# 22.15 — Whistleblowing and protected dissent

Prepare a defensible escalation plan from a fictional discrepancy. Allow twenty-five minutes for the evidence map and ten for the later response. You will distinguish what is known, who can receive it and what must be checked before disclosure. No report is filed and no allegation is sent.

## Separate the concern from the conclusion

A troubling record can justify a question before it justifies an accusation. Direct observation is something you saw or read within your authorized access. A secondhand account is someone else's report. An inference is your explanation of those facts. Preserve these distinctions so a reviewer can assess both the concern and alternative explanations. Accurate qualification strengthens a report; it does not require waiting until you can prove an entire case yourself.

Severity and urgency affect the route. A discrepancy in a completed checklist may need prompt specialist review. An immediate threat to life or safety needs the appropriate urgent-help or responsible safety route, not completion of a learning worksheet. An internal channel can offer a remedy, but a conflicted recipient or ignored concern may require an independent channel. None of these observations determines which law covers a particular worker, allegation or disclosure.

The [OSHA complaint page, S03](../SOURCES.md#S03) describes a U.S. retaliation-complaint process under specified laws. It is distinct from reporting the underlying safety hazard. Its deadlines vary by applicable statute, and it says retaliation complaints cannot be anonymous. If an investigation proceeds, the employer is notified and may respond. These details illustrate why “protected” cannot mean universally anonymous, confidential or immune from consequences. They do not establish your jurisdiction, coverage, filing deadline or legal protection. Seek qualified, jurisdiction-specific advice promptly when those questions matter; an internal process must not be assumed to pause an external deadline.

## Supplied file Q8: no actual hazard exercise

In a fictional training organization, **Ari** is authorized to view a routine equipment-check status page. The page at **09:10 Tuesday** marks room K's inspection “complete.” Ari also sees an authorized task card created at **08:50 Tuesday** that says “final sign-off awaited.” Neither record specifies whether completion means administrative entry or technical approval. Ari does not have access to the technical inspection file and must not obtain it for this exercise.

Colleague **Bo** says, “I think the sign-off was never done.” Bo has not supplied a basis. Ari infers that somebody may have closed the record early. That remains an inference. Room K is closed for scheduled maintenance and there is **no reported immediate exposure** in this case. Users could be affected if a room were reopened on an incorrect status, so the discrepancy deserves clarification without a technical safety judgment by Ari.

The fictional Q8 route card assigns routine clarification to the records supervisor, **Mina**, who also entered the status. It separately authorizes independent safety reviewer **Sol** to receive concerns about unresolved sign-off, including concerns involving Mina. Restricted technical records stay in the authorized safety system. The card promises need-to-know handling subject to investigation and reporting duties, not absolute secrecy. Sol acknowledges intake within **two working days** under this fictional service standard; it is not a legal filing deadline. If Sol is unavailable or implicated, the independent oversight desk is the alternate authorized intake route. No actual address or form is supplied.

## Worked neutral inquiry

An unsent rehearsal draft reads: “The status page at 09:10 says complete; the 08:50 task card still says final sign-off awaited. I may be missing an update. Could the responsible reviewer reconcile the records and confirm who can authorize reopening?” It identifies the discrepancy, leaves room for correction and asks for an authorized action. “Mina falsified a safety certificate” adds both a document type and an intent that the supplied facts do not establish.

Because Mina entered the disputed status, Ari can use Q8's independent reviewer rather than ask Mina to assess her own possible error. That is a route choice based on the supplied policy, not a universal rule that every routine correction is whistleblowing. Ari records which authorized items were observed and when. Ari does not download a restricted file, photograph private screens, pressure Bo for witness names or post the concern publicly.

## Build the plan in a safe sequence

1. Make an evidence ledger with separate direct, secondhand and inferred rows. Add the record times, access basis, uncertainty and lawful storage location. State what is missing without turning the gap into evidence of concealment.
2. Describe possible affected people, severity and current exposure. In Q8, no immediate exposure is reported; reopening authorization remains for the responsible safety role. Write the different urgent route you would use if actual immediate danger emerged, without staging it.
3. Compare the records supervisor, independent reviewer and alternate oversight desk for remit, conflicts, privacy limits and response expectations. List legal questions separately: jurisdiction, worker status, covered subject, protected route, deadlines and available support. Do not answer them from this fictional card.
4. Save the neutral inquiry as an **unsent draft**. Describe only lawfully accessible facts and the action requested. Ask the proper recipient how sensitive material should be transmitted before sharing it. Do not put restricted, classified, privileged or personal records into an ordinary portal or app evidence field.
5. Specify what happens if the fictional response standard passes without acknowledgment: record the absence, use the authorized alternate and seek timely qualified guidance where rights or duties may be affected. Include a way to document possible retaliation through appropriate lawful channels. Do not promise that this prevents retaliation.
6. Save your first plan before the [later packet](later-packet.md). Later, distinguish acknowledgment, investigation, correction and safety clearance; none substitutes for the others.

## Practice without exposing a person

For support, label the three supplied evidence rows with a helper before drafting your own inquiry. A harder variant makes the alternate desk report to Mina. The plan now needs verification of a genuinely independent authorized route and prompt advice about outside options; inventing a recipient or publishing the allegation is not the solution.

Optional actual transfer is to locate a public reporting policy and list questions about its remit and confidentiality. Do not use a real suspected violation as practice, collect new evidence or contact implicated people. If an actual concern is present, use responsible professional support rather than this simulated script. A good plan respects affected people, possible respondents and the integrity of records. One acknowledgment does not prove protected dissent is safe or that an institution acted correctly.

## Scope and evidence boundary

The canonical record has no professional_boundary field. Practical safeguards do not add canonical metadata. Applicability and normative status remain role_conditional. A leadership role is not compulsory. Use actual authority and willing participation; keep protected records outside app evidence. Fiction and no attempt are legitimate. Human dignity is never scored.
