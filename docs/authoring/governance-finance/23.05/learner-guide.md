# 23.05 — Saving, investing, and retirement

Write two provisional policies before choosing any product. Allow forty minutes for goals, disclosures and arithmetic, then fifteen to test a loss scenario. This is financial education using original fictional figures, not personal asset allocation, retirement sufficiency analysis or an instruction to trade.

## Give each amount a job and a deadline

Money needed for a necessary payment in six months has a different job from money for optional spending twenty years away. A person may feel comfortable with price fluctuations yet be unable to absorb a loss before rent, care or another obligation. Risk willingness describes preference; loss capacity describes what the financial position can withstand. A long horizon does not remove all risk, guarantee recovery or make every investment suitable.

The SEC's [asset-allocation guide, S10](../SOURCES.md#S10) relates investment choices to horizon and risk and distinguishes diversification within and between asset categories. Its educational example contrasts a broadly held stock fund with a narrowly focused sector fund. Owning a fund does not automatically remove concentration. No numeric allocation formula, historical return prediction or particular fund recommendation is adopted here.

An account and the assets inside it are also different. A retirement or tax-advantaged account may have eligibility, contribution, tax and withdrawal rules; a familiar account name does not answer which holdings it contains or when money can be accessed. Actual rules depend on jurisdiction and circumstances. Keep those questions open for current official information and appropriately qualified advice.

## Supplied goals and original disclosure cards

For comparison, use **1,000 starting units for each separate hypothetical goal**, not a real combined portfolio. Goal N is a necessary **1,000 payment in six months**, with no other supplied resource to absorb a shortfall. Goal L is **optional spending twenty years away**; its amount and date can be revised. Both require a policy page covering deadline, loss consequences, liquidity, diversification, fees, tax questions and review triggers.

The original teaching cards below contain all holdings in two toy sector funds. They are **not actual products or regulatory disclosures**. The inspected SEC educational example supplies the general concentration principle; these cards make the overlap calculation self-contained.

| Feature | Fund P | Fund Q |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holdings by value | Alpha 60%; Beta 40% | Alpha 60%; Beta 40% |
| Exposure | Two firms in one sector | The same two firms in the same sector |
| Annual fund operating expense | 0.20% of assets | 0.80% of assets |
| Account maintenance | 0 in this sample | 0 in this sample |
| Purchase/sale fee | 0 in this sample | 0 in this sample |
| Taxes, dealing spread and settlement/access terms | Not supplied | Not supplied |

Splitting 1,000 equally across P and Q still leaves **600 in Alpha and 400 in Beta**. It doubles the number of fund labels without broadening the underlying holdings. On a frozen 1,000 balance for a one-year fee illustration, the respective operating-expense amounts would be **2 and 8**. Actual dollar charges change with asset values and terms. The unprovided costs and access rules remain questions, not assumed zeros. Neither fund is recommended.

The SEC's [fees bulletin, S11](../SOURCES.md#S11) explains ongoing and transaction charges and the importance of checking disclosures. Its general cost mechanism supports this comparison; no actual prospectus, adviser fee agreement or account statement was inspected. A cheaper concentrated holding remains concentrated. Cost is one constraint alongside suitability, access and risk.

## Separate compounding arithmetic from a forecast

For arithmetic only, compare two years of constant **net** annual growth at 5% and 4%, with no additions, withdrawals or taxes. The results are **1,000 × 1.05² = 1,102.50** and **1,000 × 1.04² = 1,081.60**, a **20.90 difference**. Each second year's growth applies to the first year's closing amount. Net means the assumed return already reflects whatever costs are embedded in that rate; do not subtract the toy funds' expense ratios again.

These paths are independent examples, not projections for P or Q and not a twenty-year retirement forecast. The one-percentage-point difference illustrates how less retained growth leaves less to compound. Actual returns may be negative or variable, and taxes can change results. A neat curve does not establish a dependable future balance.

## Produce two bounded policies

1. For N and L, state the deadline, consequence of loss and access need. Distinguish willingness from capacity. N cannot assume it can replace a shortfall; L has flexibility but still needs an explicit understanding of possible loss.
2. Inspect the source's broad-versus-sector example and the complete toy holdings and fee cards. Calculate the combined Alpha/Beta exposure and identify all unverified disclosure fields. Explain why two names do not create diversification and why diversification cannot guarantee against market loss.
3. Show both compound calculations and the 20.90 difference. Keep net rates, time horizon and excluded taxes beside the results. Do not imply either rate is expected.
4. Write a constraints-first N policy: preserve ability to meet the required amount by its date, require verified access and relevant protection, and reject reliance on uncertain market appreciation. Do not name a product or declare an unverified account safe.
5. Write an L policy: assess capacity before taking investment risk, seek understandable diversified exposure, compare complete costs, resolve account and tax questions, and review on an agreed schedule or material life change. Leave allocation percentages undecided because suitability inputs are incomplete.
6. Stress both policies with a **20% loss just before the required date**. A risky 1,000 holding would become 800; ask which goal can absorb the 200 loss or delay and what would need revision before implementation. Save your policies before the [later packet](later-packet.md).

Review rules should react to changed goals, capacity, access and costs, not just recent performance or a confident prediction. Retirement planning also involves future needs and account rules that this small case does not establish. A simple policy can be a useful starting point while those questions remain unresolved.

For support, use a calculator and two separate goal cards. For a harder pass, move L's date forward and ask whether the previous capacity assumption still holds. Optional actual transfer is to read official education or a public disclosure privately, recording uncertainties without publishing holdings or opening an account. No purchase, sale or account transfer demonstrates completion. Having no investable surplus is compatible with learning the distinctions.
