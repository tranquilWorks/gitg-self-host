# 12.05 — Build one capability-fit strength pathway with technique, recovery and progression evidence

Map the major strength patterns, choose an entry, established-plan or instruction-first route, and compare two bounded sessions before making one justified progression decision.

This practice teaches strength-program reasoning and observes one already-safe movement. It is not medical clearance, rehabilitation, a maximal-strength test or a universal prescription. Two sessions cannot establish measurable strength gain across major patterns.

Use the supplied pattern map, capability screen, two-session record and separately revealed decision checks. Select one route. The entry route uses a familiar supported movement such as the stable-chair rise default. The established-plan route uses one movement already present in a qualified or competently established program. The instruction-first route prepares a useful consultation instead of attempting an unfamiliar movement. No maximum effort, forced fatigue, new ballistic exercise or removal of ordinary support is required.

About thirty to forty-five minutes of planning, two short sessions on separate suitable days, and a ten-minute recovery and progression review.

Use seated, assisted, isometric, band, machine, bodyweight or other familiar equivalents already appropriate to the person; support is a legitimate condition, not a failed version. Use the instruction-first route when safety, technique, equipment or progression is uncertain. Preserve a rehabilitation or clinician plan and do not practice through injury, remove fall support, reveal private health information or use exercise as a test of worth.

## 1. Map the patterns and choose a capability-fit route

Open the guide and map squat or rise, hinge, push, pull and carry to a familiar example, accessible equivalent, not-currently-relevant status or instruction-needed status. Complete the capability screen before selecting one pattern. Choose entry, established-plan or instruction-first; do not select by pride or peer comparison. Record the movement, support, planned dose already known to be tolerable, four technique and boundary criteria, recovery interval and stop conditions. If the route is instruction-first, complete the supplied consultation brief and do not improvise a physical test.

- All five patterns receive an honest route or status
- The selected route matches current experience and support
- Dose criteria recovery and stop conditions are fixed before action
- Instruction-first produces a complete consultation brief without a physical attempt

## 2. Record one controlled session against fixed criteria

For an entry or established-plan route, perform only the predeclared familiar dose with the same equipment, range and support. Record completed work, the four selected technique or boundary criteria, perceived effort in ordinary words, and any symptom or equipment change. Stop at the preset boundary or earlier when technique, control or normal tolerance changes; an incomplete session remains valid evidence. For instruction-first, use the guide to ask the prepared questions of an appropriately qualified person and record only the resulting category-level decision, not private health detail or credentials.

- The attempt follows the selected route and predeclared boundary
- Observations use the same four criteria fixed before the session
- Support symptoms and any early stop remain visible
- No maximum fatigue challenge or unplanned escalation is added

## 3. Recheck recovery and make one progression decision

After a suitable recovery interval, record whether ordinary function returned to its prior state and whether delayed symptoms or unusual fatigue appeared. Repeat the same dose only when it remains appropriate under the selected route. Compare technique, effort, support and recovery, not merely repetitions or load. Choose exactly one disposition—repeat unchanged, regress one variable, progress one variable inside the established plan, seek instruction, or stop and use appropriate care. Open the progression check only after recording the reason. Do not label two successful sessions as measurable strength improvement.

- Recovery is checked before any repeat or progression
- Comparable sessions preserve technique support and dose unless a regression is declared
- One disposition follows the observed technique effort and recovery evidence
- Progression stays inside established guidance and no strength-gain claim is inferred

What strength practice is and is not

Strength is the capacity to produce force against resistance. Muscular endurance concerns sustaining or repeating force, and power concerns producing force rapidly; those outcomes are related but not interchangeable. A useful strength pathway considers the movement being trained, current experience, technique, dose, recovery and progression. A supported chair rise can be an appropriate entry or adapted observation, but it cannot represent every major pattern or prove a strength increase. Current professional guidance supports individualizing resistance training to goals, enjoyment and safety and distinguishes ordinary general participation from sport-specific programming. This guide therefore supplies decision tools rather than a universal load or repetition prescription.

Five-pattern capability map

Make five rows: squat or rise | hinge | push | pull | carry. For each row record: a familiar current example or accessible equivalent | current route [entry / established plan / instruction first / not currently relevant] | equipment and support | what competent technique would require | what remains unknown. Examples are descriptive only: rising from a stable chair, moving an object from a raised surface with an already learned hinge, a wall or machine push, a band or machine pull, and carrying an ordinary object with a usual aid. Do not attempt an example merely because it appears here. The map is complete when every pattern has an honest status; it is not a requirement to train all five during this practice.

Capability and boundary screen

Before physical action record yes, no or unknown: the exact movement is already familiar; the equipment and environment are available and stable; required support can remain; the planned dose has been tolerated or belongs to an existing plan; technique criteria are known; a suitable recovery interval is available; and stop conditions are understood. Any no or important unknown moves the route to instruction-first or defer. Stop the attempt for chest discomfort, faintness, new or increasing pain, unusual breathlessness, loss of control, equipment instability or any existing clinician or rehabilitation stop rule. This screen does not provide medical clearance.

Choose one of three complete routes

Entry or adapted route: choose one already-safe, familiar movement and a modest dose known to be tolerable. The supplied default is three controlled rises from a stable chair that cannot slide, using usual hand support, only for someone who already performs that movement safely. Established-plan route: select one familiar movement and dose already present in a qualified or competently established program; record the plan source and do not change load, volume, range, equipment or tempo during the comparison. Instruction-first route: do no new physical test. Prepare the consultation brief, identify who is qualified for the uncertainty, and obtain a route decision. Any route may end in defer or stop without penalty.

Instruction-first consultation brief

Prepare: the function or goal sought; current experience; the exact movement considered; available equipment, support and access needs; existing restrictions or plans stated only as needed; and what is unknown. Ask: Which movement or prerequisite should come first? What technique cues can be observed? What starting dose and recovery fit this person and goal? Which one variable could later change, under what criterion? What warning signs or equipment conditions require stopping or referral? Record only one category-level result: appropriate as proposed | appropriate with modification | different prerequisite first | needs another qualified route | defer. The consultation informs a plan; it is not evidence that strength improved.

Worked example with no automatic progression

A person already uses a supported cable row in an established plan. They map the other four patterns but select only the row. Their criteria are stable seated support, planned range, controlled path and completion before technique loss. Both sessions meet those criteria with ordinary recovery. They repeat the same dose next time because the plan has not yet reached its progression condition. This is supportive evidence for executing that movement under recorded conditions; it is not a measured strength gain. If the second session instead produced unusual delayed symptoms, the proper record would be mixed and the next step would be instruction, not added load.

Progress one variable only when the route supports it

A progression decision follows the established plan or qualified instruction and the observed technique and recovery criteria. Change only the authorized variable, such as load, repetitions, range, support or movement complexity, while retaining the other comparison conditions. If the default is well below present ability, use the established-plan route rather than manufacturing fatigue. If technique, recovery or route authority is uncertain, repeat, regress or seek instruction. A future measurement needs comparable conditions and sufficient time; this practice does not award a strength-gain claim.
