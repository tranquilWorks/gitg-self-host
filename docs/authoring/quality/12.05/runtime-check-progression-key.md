Decide what the evidence permits

Case: Session 1 used the planned familiar dose and met T1, T2 and T4, but control criterion T3 was mixed. The person repeated after ordinary recovery under the same conditions; T3 was again mixed. They feel eager to add load. Choose one disposition from repeat unchanged, regress one variable, progress one variable inside the established plan, seek instruction, or stop. Explain what evidence supports the decision and what the two sessions cannot establish. Preserve the answer before opening the check.

Check your attempt: Check the progression decision

Progress is not supported in this case because the same control criterion remained mixed twice. A defensible disposition is repeat unchanged with a correction only if the established plan supplies one, regress one variable, or seek instruction; the exact choice depends on the recorded cause and plan. Adding load contradicts the evidence. The two sessions cannot establish measurable strength improvement, broad pattern competence, cause, or safety for a different movement.

- The unresolved repeated criterion prevents automatic progression.
- The disposition is one of the declared options and cites observed evidence.
- Load, repetitions or complexity are not added to prove effort.
- The conclusion remains limited to the selected movement and recorded conditions.
