# 10.01 — Learn six ideas through recall, correction and a delayed transfer test

Use retrieval, spacing, mixed practice, explanation and feedback to learn a small set of ideas, then test retention and a new application after a delay.

This is a learning-method trial, not an intelligence test or proof that one schedule suits every learner. Ordinary sleep and access supports remain protected.

Use the supplied learning guide, paper or an accessible note tool. It contains six definitions, two worked examples, and separate first, second and delayed prompts with matching checks. Study the teaching first; open only the prompt for the current session. Keep your original response before revealing its check. No purchased materials or private information are needed.

Fifteen minutes on day one, ten minutes on day two, and fifteen minutes about a week later.

Use audio cards, large print, tactile counters or supported recall. Adjust the spacing to available capacity and report the actual delay. A same-day repetition is useful practice but must not be labeled a week-delayed retention test.

## 1. Study an example and retrieve before looking again

Read the six definitions and both worked examples in the learning guide. Explain why the mean uses the count and why the median can differ from it. Close the teaching, open First recall and application, and answer all six ideas plus the application before opening its check. Preserve your original attempt, compare it with the supplied key, and write the corrected reasoning for a specific mistake. Record any cues or lookups honestly.

- All six ideas receive an initial recall attempt
- A worked example and a contrasting example are used
- Errors are compared with an available answer source
- Explanation goes beyond copying the definitions

## 2. Space and mix the second practice instead of repeating recognition

On the next day when feasible, open Second mixed practice before rereading the teaching. Answer the six mixed questions, switching between recall, calculation and explanation. Keep your original response, then explicitly reveal that session’s check. Identify an error and use feedback to correct the concept or method. Record the actual separation between sessions and supports used. Stop at the planned time; preserve ordinary sleep, rest and necessary access supports.

- The second retrieval is separated by a day when feasible
- Definition and application questions are interleaved
- Feedback corrects specific missed ideas
- The plan preserves sleep rest and access supports

## 3. Test delayed retention and unfamiliar application

About a week after the first session, open Delayed recall and new application without reopening the teaching or earlier keys. Record the actual delay, answer the six recall questions, and solve the supplied unfamiliar application. Keep the original response before revealing its separate check. Label unaided, cued, looked-up and open-explanation work distinctly. Use a specific retained idea or error to choose the next review; same-day practice does not establish delayed retention.

- Delayed recall is attempted before looking up answers
- A new application differs from the worked example
- Unaided cued and looked-up answers are distinguished
- The next review is based on specific retention or transfer errors

What this practice can show

This is a small learning-method trial. Reading a solution, retrieving it from memory, explaining it with help, and applying it to unfamiliar material provide different observations. None alone establishes broad learning ability. Use each prompt once before opening its check, keep the first response, then correct a specific error. The intended schedule is day one, the next day, and about a week later; record what actually happened. A gap on a calendar does not prove retention.

What each learning method asks you to do

Retrieval means producing an answer before reopening its explanation; recognizing a familiar sentence is different. Spacing means returning after a recorded interval rather than doing every attempt together. Interleaving means switching between question types so you must choose the method, rather than applying the same operation repeatedly. Elaboration means explaining why a method works and when it would be wrong. Worked and contrasting examples let you compare those choices. Feedback is checking the preserved attempt and correcting a specific error, not replacing the first response with a neat copy. Keep ordinary sleep, rest and necessary access supports; this exercise neither measures sleep nor prescribes a sleep schedule. The sessions below put these methods into a small trial; they cannot determine which method caused any change.

Six ideas to learn

Mean: add the values and divide by their count. Median: sort the values; select the middle value for an odd count, or average the two middle values for an even count. Range: subtract the smallest value from the largest. Numerator: the number above the fraction bar; in a parts-of-a-whole example it counts the chosen equal parts. Denominator: the number below the bar; in that example it states how many equal parts form the whole, and it cannot be zero. Unit: the named quantity scale, such as minutes or meters; it gives a measured number meaning.

For the mean, a fair-share interpretation helps: redistribute the total equally across the recorded items. Sorting changes order, not the values. A median uses position after sorting; the mean uses every value. A range describes the distance between the extremes, not the typical value. These are definitions and a bounded teaching example, not a claim that this exercise is a validated intervention.

Worked example: an even set

Four waiting times are 2, 4, 6 and 8 minutes. Their sum is 20 minutes and count is 4, so the mean is 20/4 = 5 minutes. The middle values are 4 and 6, so the median is (4+6)/2 = 5 minutes. The range is 8−2 = 6 minutes. In 3/4 of a one-meter strip, 3 is the numerator and 4 is the denominator; the length is three quarters of a meter.

Worked example: the summaries differ

For 1, 2 and 9 minutes, the sum is 12 minutes and count is 3: mean 4 minutes. The sorted middle value gives median 2 minutes. Range is 9−1 = 8 minutes. The mean and median answer different questions; an unusually large value affects the mean while it may leave the middle position unchanged. If you obtained median 4, check whether you averaged when the question asked for the sorted middle.

Choose an honest way to attempt

Audio, large print, accessible input and tactile counters can remain available throughout. Distinguish access support from content assistance. Unaided means the response preceded content cues or answer access; cued means a hint was used; looked-up means the reference supplied information; open explanation means you intentionally reasoned with the reference available. Do not erase supported responses. If recalling math definitions is already easy, use six unfamiliar ideas from a source you can actually inspect, predeclare an equivalent unseen application and a reliable checking method, and preserve those materials; the supplied arithmetic route remains the complete default.

Choose the next review from the error

Privately note idea, original response, support, check result, corrected reasoning and next review. A complete answer with a wrong method needs a method correction; a correct definition with a wrong application needs a new example. Missing units need an explicit quantity check. Keep one specific unresolved point for another spaced attempt and record the interval you can actually use. The exercise observes these responses; it does not establish long-term change beyond the sessions performed.
