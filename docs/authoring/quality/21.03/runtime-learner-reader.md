# 21.03 — Produce three competent examples of one foundational craft task

Identify the core knowledge, tools, standards and safety limits of a small craft task and demonstrate repeatable acceptable work rather than one impressive attempt.

Choose a safe non-regulated component. This does not authorize professional practice, hazardous equipment use or a claim of competence in an entire occupation.

Use the three complete fictional event briefs in the learning guide: Neighborhood Seed Swap, Repair Café Taster and Evening Sketch Walk. Use paper or a familiar text editor. Prepare one notice from each brief and check against the supplied F1–F5 accuracy and U1–U2 usability criteria. No event, contact route or address is real; keep the notices as practice artifacts. The guide supplies the facts, layout example, matching checks and a fresh contact-change retest.

Twenty minutes to define the standard, three short attempts and a 15-minute comparison.

Use speech, large print, accessible digital text or another safe foundational task with a real standard. Necessary supports are legitimate work conditions. Tasks involving clinical care, driving, weapons, hazardous tools or licensed work require appropriate supervision and are not default practice material.

## 1. Identify the field’s basics and bound the sample task

Read the guide’s event-notice preparation card, three briefs and F1–F5/U1–U2 standard. Identify the knowledge, tool and access method needed to turn supplied facts into a readable notice. Use this supplied product specification for the default. Separate mandatory factual accuracy and reading access from optional decoration. State that this samples a small communication-design component, not an entire profession, a legal notice or regulated work.

- Knowledge tools standard and safety limits are identified
- Mandatory accuracy and access are separated from polish
- The sample task is bounded within a broader craft
- Unassessed professional capability is explicitly excluded

## 2. Complete three independent attempts against the same standard

Create a separate notice from each of the three supplied briefs. Keep comparable format, tools and support conditions, and verify which brief you are using before entering its facts. Preserve the original attempt before checking. Mark an incorrect, omitted or uninspected criterion explicitly instead of silently fixing the first result. You may use the blank layout pattern, but each notice must use its own facts. These fictional notices are not for real publication.

- Three distinct fact sets are attempted
- The same standard and comparable conditions are used
- Errors remain visible in the attempt record
- No inaccurate notice is put into real use

## 3. Have the work checked and target the unstable basic

After preserving all three attempts, reveal each matching check or ask a freely willing intended reader to locate the five facts using the supplied standard. Label self-check, external review and untested access conditions separately. Inspect F1–F5 and U1–U2 for every output; attractive formatting cannot compensate for an incorrect date or missing contact. Identify an unstable basic, apply one specific correction and attempt the supplied contact-change retest. If no error was found, use the retest to check that a change is propagated accurately. Report only the component and conditions actually tested.

- All three outputs receive a consistent check
- Self-review and external review are distinguished
- A repeated weakness receives a concrete correction and retest
- The claim remains limited to the sampled component

Produce and check three dependable event notices

Learning guide — unscored

Turn a brief into a dependable notice

The component is extracting required facts, arranging them for a reader, and checking the result against a fixed specification. Know which source controls each fact, use a tool whose reading order you can inspect, and distinguish accuracy from decoration. A repeatable process is: identify the assigned brief; enter its five facts; preserve the first output; check each fact against that same brief; inspect reading order and access; then correct and retry. The supplied standard is a product specification for this exercise, not a professional credential or an accessibility certification.

A reusable layout, with new facts each time

Use a heading for the event name, then labeled lines for date, start–end time, full location and contact. Blank pattern:
EVENT NAME
Date: [day month year]
Time: [start–end]
Location: [venue and address]
Contact: [route]

For an unrelated illustration: Pocket Poetry Hour; 8 August 2027; 14:00–15:00; Maple Reading Room, 3 Orchard Road; poetry@events.example. The facts are fictional. In digital text, keep this order in the underlying text as well as visually; an image-only notice needs an equivalent text version for a text-reading route.

The checking standard

F1 — Event name matches the assigned brief.
F2 — Day, month and year match the assigned brief.
F3 — Both start and end times match the assigned brief.
F4 — The full venue and address are preserved.
F5 — The contact route is present and matches the assigned brief.
U1 — A reader can follow the event name, date, time, place and contact in a clear reading order.
U2 — The selected format remains legible and usable with the intended access method; record what was actually inspected.

For each criterion record met, not met, or uninspected, with a short fact or location in the output. Any F1–F5 failure prevents an accuracy pass, regardless of polish. U1 or U2 uninspected means usability remains unverified, not passed. Inspect text order by reading or listening from start to finish. For U2, use the format you actually chose: appropriate zoom or text-reader access for a digital notice, or the selected large-print/speech method. Record that specific condition. A text comparison alone cannot certify every device, reader or print condition. Do not invent a fee, booking rule or access claim that the brief does not supply.

Inspect the format you actually used

For digital text, read or listen from the event heading through the contact line.
Check that every fact occurs once in a sensible sequence. Enlarge the text to
200 percent using your usual application controls and inspect every line for clipping
or overlap. If a text-reading route matters for your use, inspect the reading order
with that route too; visually positioned boxes may be read in an unexpected order.
An image-only notice needs a matching text version. Record the application, enlargement
and reading route actually checked; another device or assistive tool remains untested.

For paper, use the intended print size, lighting and reading distance. Read all five
facts without guessing at cramped or faint characters. Enlarge or simplify the notice
if that fails, preserve the earlier version, and inspect the revision. A screen preview
does not establish that the printed copy is usable.

For speech, state the labeled facts in order and allow a replay. Have a freely willing
listener repeat back the date, both times, full place and contact if one is available.
Otherwise record a self-check and leave another listener's usability untested. Necessary
supports are legitimate conditions; these checks are not an accessibility certification.


Use an appropriately demanding component

The notice route is complete and suitable for practicing a small accuracy-and-presentation task. Keep necessary supports present in all attempts. If this is already routine, choose a safe component from a craft you actually practice, with three comparable input sets and a real recipient or published standard available before beginning. Identify its tools, knowledge, checking method and limits; do not substitute a vague goal such as be professional. This alternate route requires those real materials and their review, so the supplied notice checks cannot certify it. Work requiring licenses or hazardous equipment needs its appropriate established supervision; this route grants no authority.
