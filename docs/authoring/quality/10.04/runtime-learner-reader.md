# 10.04 — Build and audit one claim-by-claim research answer another reader can trace

Search beyond a first summary, distinguish primary and expert sources, compare conflicting information and attach each conclusion to an identifiable source.

This is a bounded research workflow, not expert certification. Absence of a source is an uncertainty, and a polished summary cannot compensate for unverifiable provenance.

Use the supplied fictional lending-service question, three complete source cards, research-plan worksheet, claim matrix and audit prompt. No live search is required. An optional live route may use current public documents, but every material claim must retain issuer, title, publication/effective date, locator, access date and a stated authority/fit limit.

Forty-five minutes, including a ten-minute provenance check.

## 1. Plan a search that can find more than the expected answer

Open the fictional question and source cards. Split the decision into eligibility, item limit, loan period, effective date and exceptions. For each information need name the source type and authority that would fit it, then record issuer, title, publication date, effective date, locator and access date. Complete the source-role prompt before opening its key; do not infer that a polished or recent document has authority over a rule it does not issue.

- The research question is divided into answerable subquestions
- A primary source is sought rather than only a summary
- At least one alternate perspective or interpretation is considered
- Source title issuer date and locator are recorded

## 2. Compare claims and preserve unresolved discrepancies

Create one claim-matrix row per subquestion and attach exact source codes. Compare each source’s scope, authority and effective date. In the fictional case, determine which rule P2 changes, which P1 rule remains, and why P3 cannot override either official card. Record the missing eligibility and exception information as unknown. Complete the conflict prompt before revealing its key, preserving rejected claims and the reason for rejection rather than silently deleting them.

- Each factual claim points to a source code
- Publication and effective dates are distinguished
- Conflicting information is resolved by relevant authority and context
- Unanswered subquestions are not silently filled in

## 3. Write and audit a concise answer

Write the supplied answer template with confirmed rules, bounded interpretation, unresolved questions and scope/date checked. Place a source code immediately after each material claim. Run the audit by reopening all three cards, checking claim wording, number, date, authority and omission. A claim passes only if its cited source supports that wording. Correct unsupported text while retaining the original audit result and one specific official clarification question.

- The summary distinguishes facts interpretation and unknowns
- Every material claim survives a source recheck
- The answer includes its checking date and scope
- One unresolved issue has a concrete clarification route

Use a librarian, screen reader, printed policy or accessible official phone channel. Keep the same source-to-claim trace when using AI to summarize; generated citations must be checked directly. The offline case is research rehearsal, not advice about an actual service.

Plan, trace and audit a bounded research answer

Learning guide — unscored

Match the source to the claim and decision

Research is an iterative inquiry, not link collection. Start with the decision and its subquestions. A primary source is closest to issuing the rule, measurement or original record; an expert source interprets within demonstrated relevant expertise; an affected-person or community source can reveal experience and missing perspectives. None is automatically best for every claim. Authority is contextual: a service policy fits the service’s own current borrowing rule, while a user post may identify a practical concern but cannot amend that policy. Currency means the version effective for the decision, not merely the newest timestamp. Preserve uncertainty when authority, scope or evidence is absent.

Research plan and stopping rule

Decision: __. Subquestions: eligibility | limit | duration | effective date | exceptions. For each write: evidence needed | likely issuing body or expert | search terms or offline card | alternate perspective | consequence if wrong. Stop when every material claim has a fitting inspectable source or is explicitly unknown, the decision can be made within its stated scope, and one additional search is unlikely to change it. Stop earlier for unauthorized access, private data, paid access outside the plan or stakes requiring qualified advice.

Fictional lending-service source cards

P1 — Northstar Tool Library, “Standard Borrowing Policy,” issued 10 January 2026, effective 10 January 2026, locator northstar.example/policy. Text: members may borrow one item at a time for seven calendar days. The card does not define member eligibility or exceptions.

P2 — Northstar Tool Library, “April Limit Update,” issued 15 March 2026, effective 1 April 2026, locator northstar.example/april-update. Text: beginning 1 April, the simultaneous item limit changes from one to two. All other Standard Borrowing Policy terms remain unchanged. The card does not define eligibility or exceptions.

P3 — River District Neighbors forum, post by “HelpfulNeighbor,” issued 20 February 2026, no official effective date, locator forum.example/thread-44. Text: “I think returns are monthly now.” The writer provides no policy link or role. These are fictional records; the locators must not be opened or treated as real rules.

Source and claim matrix

One row per claim: C-ID | exact claim | source code and locator | issuer/author | source role [primary / expert / community] | publication date | effective date | relevant passage in your own words | authority for this claim | conflict or limitation | disposition [supported / qualified / rejected / unknown]. Keep rejected and unknown rows. An answer is traceable only when another reader can locate the same source and see why it does or does not support the exact claim.

Bounded research answer and audit log

Question and decision context: __. Checked on: __. Confirmed: [claim plus source code]. Interpretation: [reasoning clearly labeled]. Unknown: [missing fact and consequence]. Before acting: [specific clarification route]. Scope limit: __. Audit one row per material claim: wording matches source [yes/no] | number/date matches [yes/no] | source has authority for this claim [yes/no] | current/effective for context [yes/no] | uncertainty retained [yes/no] | correction. A clean citation format cannot rescue a source that does not support the claim.

Worked claim trail for the fictional case

For borrowing on or after 1 April 2026, the simultaneous limit is two items [P2]. The seven-calendar-day period remains because P2 leaves other P1 terms unchanged [P2; P1]. The cards do not state who qualifies as a member or what exceptions exist, so both are unknown. P3’s monthly statement is rejected for the policy claim because it has no issuing authority or supporting policy. Before acting on an exception, ask the service through its official public contact route. This answer applies only to the fictional cards and is not advice about a real library.

Move from the packet to a live or advanced question

Live route: replace each fictional card with an inspectable current public source and retain its provenance. Advanced route: add scholarly or technical claims, searching more than one database or vocabulary and comparing methods, populations and limitations rather than counting sources. Use a librarian or qualified expert when the domain or access path is unfamiliar. AI may suggest search terms or summarize inspected text, but generated citations and quotations are unverified until opened and checked against the original.

Progress by increasing decision fit and auditability

Transfer the same research plan, matrix and audit to a different low-stakes decision. Increase difficulty through a more ambiguous source set, competing expert interpretations or a version history—not through higher consequences. One correct fictional policy summary demonstrates a bounded traceable answer, not expertise in a domain or general immunity to misinformation.
