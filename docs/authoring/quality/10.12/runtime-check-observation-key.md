Learning guide — unscored

Complete loose-cord bow reference

Use about 80 cm of soft cord loose on a table. Cross the ends, pass one end under the crossing and pull gently into a flat first knot. Fold one end into a loop. Wrap the other end around the loop's base, fold a bight of that end and push the folded bight through the opening behind the first loop. Hold both loops and pull them gently apart; leave free tails rather than pulling a tail through. Release by pulling one free tail. A willing demonstrator may show their equivalent method. Keep the cord away from bodies, animals, machinery and loads. This is not restraint, climbing, rescue or a safety knot. Stop for strain or confusion and ask the teacher to demonstrate again.

Separate the step from the judgment

Before revealing the check, classify what the bow card supplies and what you must elicit from a teacher. Ask what they notice before tightening and when they stop. If no teacher is available, analyze this fictional demonstrator statement instead: compare loop sizes before tightening; stop while both free tails remain longer than a finger width. Record one written step, one demonstrated judgment cue, and which live evidence remains untested. The simulation is analysis only.

Check your attempt: Check the step and judgment distinction

The written-step example is wrapping the second end around the loop base. The fictional judgment is comparing loop sizes before tension and preserving visible tails when stopping. Naming the next motion alone misses that judgment. The simulation supplies words to analyze, not an observed normal/slowed demonstration, learner imitation or live feedback. On a live route, cite what the teacher actually demonstrated rather than substituting the fictional statement.
