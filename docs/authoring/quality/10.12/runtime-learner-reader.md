# 10.12 — Learn one tacit adjustment from a willing skilled person

Observe a safe skill, imitate it, obtain correction on an otherwise hidden judgment and repeat with that correction in view.

This is a small apprenticeship encounter, not professional qualification. Written steps or a video can prepare observation, but they are not live guided correction or community membership.

Default skill: tie and release an ordinary shoelace-style bow in loose cord lying on a table. Use no body, load-bearing line, animal, machinery or safety function. Ask a willing person who already does this comfortably to demonstrate slowly and watch one attempt. When hand movement is inaccessible, use an equally low-risk skill selected with the teacher, such as arranging a simple visual layout.

A voluntary twenty-to-thirty-minute demonstration and practice, followed by ten minutes to record the learned adjustment.

## 1. Observe the judgment that a written sequence leaves out

Agree on the safe task and permission to ask questions. Watch one normal demonstration and one slower pass. Ask what the teacher notices about loop size, tension and when to stop pulling; distinguish those judgments from merely naming the next hand movement. Do not film without permission. Identify one cue that you would have missed from reading a sequence alone.

- The task and teacher participation are freely agreed
- Both normal and slowed demonstrations are observed
- A question elicits a judgment cue rather than only a step
- Recording and privacy boundaries are respected

## 2. Imitate once and invite a specific correction

Attempt the bow with loose cord on the table while the teacher observes. Ask for one correction tied to the agreed cue, such as keeping the loops similar in size before tightening. Repeat the cue back in your own words and ask whether that matches the teacher’s meaning. Stop if the method causes strain or the task moves beyond the safe setup; correction never requires humiliation.

- A real imitation attempt occurs
- The correction concerns a specific observable cue
- The learner checks their understanding of the correction
- The teacher’s authority does not override safety or dignity

## 3. Repeat with the correction and identify the remaining dependence

Make another attempt using the cue, then release the bow to check that it functions for this harmless purpose. Ask what still needs practice and what circumstances would require a different method. Record the cue, one mistake and the improvement in a private learning note with the teacher’s permission. Without a willing teacher, use a lawful recorded demonstration and label guided feedback untested.

- The corrected attempt is actually performed
- The task’s harmless functional check is completed
- Remaining dependence and context limits are named
- Recorded observation is not claimed as live guided feedback

Choose an accessible low-risk skill with the teacher, allow assistive methods and shorten the session. Defer when no willing skilled person or suitable task is available. Never turn this exercise into rope restraint, climbing, rescue or load-bearing knot practice.

Observe, imitate and check one tacit adjustment

Learning guide — unscored

A sequence is not the whole judgment

Written motions say what comes next; demonstration can expose tension, timing and stopping cues. Ask a willing skilled person to show their judgment at normal and slower pace, then observe your imitation and correct one cue. A recording or the supplied fictional case can prepare questions but cannot establish live guided participation, community immersion or competence. Correction concerns a movement, never your worth; consent can be withdrawn.

Complete loose-cord bow reference

Use about 80 cm of soft cord loose on a table. Cross the ends, pass one end under the crossing and pull gently into a flat first knot. Fold one end into a loop. Wrap the other end around the loop's base, fold a bight of that end and push the folded bight through the opening behind the first loop. Hold both loops and pull them gently apart; leave free tails rather than pulling a tail through. Release by pulling one free tail. A willing demonstrator may show their equivalent method. Keep the cord away from bodies, animals, machinery and loads. This is not restraint, climbing, rescue or a safety knot. Stop for strain or confusion and ask the teacher to demonstrate again.

Different example — align before creasing

A learner folds a paper strip into thirds but presses the first crease before comparing panel widths. The teacher slowly brings the two loose folds together, checks the three widths, then presses. The correction is to align before pressure, not to be a more talented folder. The learner restates the cue and repeats while the teacher observes. A neater strip with that prompt is a supported retry, not evidence of independent performance on every folding task.

Written step: __. Judgment cue: __. Preserved first attempt: __. Exact correction: __. My restatement: __. Teacher confirmation or untested: __. Retry and supports: __. Release result: __. Remaining dependence: __. Permission to keep this note: __. Leave unobserved fields untested; never invent a teacher response.

Preserve the error so the correction has a target

Keep the first attempt description before changing it. Request one observable cue, restate it and ask the teacher whether the restatement is accurate. Retry and check release. Merely repeating the teacher's words does not show the adjustment was made. One successful guided attempt supports a narrow result; partial success still needing prompts, strain or coercion, and a recording without feedback are different outcomes, not equivalent evidence.

Keep the participation voluntary and the task harmless

Use thicker high-contrast cord or agree another accessible low-risk task with the teacher. Assistance may stabilize the material but must be recorded; do not claim unsupported execution. Stop rather than force painful movement. Without a willing teacher, finish the supplied classification exercise and mark live demonstration, imitation and correction untested. A lawful recording is preparation, not membership in a community of practice.

Return to a guided attempt with one question

Ask the teacher to observe a later safe attempt with the same supports and one question about when the cue changes. Record any continuing dependence. If only simulation was possible, the next step is arranging voluntary live guidance, not claiming apprenticeship complete. Broader community immersion and professional qualification remain outside this small encounter.
