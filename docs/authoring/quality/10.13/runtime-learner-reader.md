# 10.13 — Preserve a piece of lived knowledge with its holder’s permission

Document or teach one practical story, procedure or tradition through a relationship, verify it with an experienced holder and preserve the agreed limits on sharing.

This preserves one contribution to communal memory. It does not make the participant the owner of another person’s knowledge or authorize disclosure of sacred, private, restricted or commercially protected material.

Default: ask a willing experienced person how a small group welcomes a new member or hands over a shared task. Use a page with purpose, steps, reasons, exceptions, source holder and sharing permission. The holder may choose a different ordinary practice or decline. Do not request family secrets, protected work procedures or cultural knowledge they are not authorized to share.

Ten minutes to agree scope, a twenty-minute demonstration or conversation, and fifteen minutes for a verified revision.

## 1. Agree what may be learned recorded and passed on

Explain that you want to prevent one useful practice from being lost. Ask what may be written, whether attribution is wanted, who may receive it and what should remain unrecorded. Identify the holder’s relationship to the practice without assuming that age or status alone guarantees accuracy. Agree on a small scope, such as the first ten minutes of welcoming a newcomer.

- The holder freely chooses the scope
- Recording attribution and sharing permissions are explicit
- Restricted or private knowledge is excluded
- The holder’s experience is described without treating status as infallible

## 2. Learn through a demonstration or concrete story

Ask the holder to walk through an actual or fictionalized example. Record not just what happens but why a step matters, what changes with context and which mistake a newcomer commonly makes. Participate in a safe demonstration when invited. Distinguish remembered fact, personal interpretation and a version that differs across families or groups; do not flatten variation into a single official tradition.

- A concrete example or demonstration is obtained
- Reasons exceptions and a common mistake are captured
- Observation and interpretation remain distinguishable
- Legitimate variation is preserved

## 3. Return the record for correction and make an agreed handoff

Show or read the concise draft back to the holder. Ask what is inaccurate, missing, too private or overgeneralized, then revise it before sharing. Preserve only the attribution and audience permission they approved. Teach or hand the final version to a willing intended recipient when authorized, or store it where agreed with a review date. An unverified draft is not presented as the holder’s endorsed account.

- The holder reviews the draft for accuracy and permission
- Corrections are incorporated before any handoff
- The final audience and attribution match consent
- Unverified material is not represented as endorsed communal knowledge

Use audio only with permission, a large-print draft, an interpreter or a jointly drawn sequence. A public archive can support source study when no holder is available, but it does not supply personal permission or verification that did not occur.

Preserve knowledge without taking over its authority

Learning guide — unscored

Preserve reasons, relationships and permission

Knowledge transmission preserves why a practice works, when it changes and who may correct or receive the account. It is not extraction of a story into unrestricted content. An experienced holder can decline, restrict recording or correct the draft. Age alone does not make one account infallible. A useful record retains variation and provenance instead of declaring one official tradition. The supplied packet is fictional and cannot provide actual holder verification.

H0 — fictional tool-library holder packet

H0 is an experienced volunteer describing this shift's first ten minutes with a newcomer. Permission is notes yes, audio no; attribution is experienced volunteer; audience is the volunteer team only, not the public. Do not include member names. Five actions are greet the newcomer; ask their preferred name and pronunciation; explain the checkout board; offer a quiet walk or paired tour; arrange a willing buddy if requested. The reason is to avoid overwhelming a new person. Exception is a return-only visit, when the tour is skipped. A common mistake is lecturing about tools before asking what the person needs. Another shift starts at the return desk. These are fictional practice facts, not universal tool-library policy or a living community's testimony.

Different example — a garden handoff

A fictional garden note says water every morning. Its holder corrects it to check soil first and skip watering when already wet; that exception prevents the bare schedule from replacing judgment. The note is for plot stewards only, with no public attribution. A repaired instruction can be accurate yet still unauthorized for a public blog. The writer returns both content and destination for confirmation; a missing reply is not approval.

Accuracy and distribution are separate checks

Ask what is inaccurate, missing, too private or overgeneralized, then revise and confirm the intended audience. Consent to a conversation is not publication consent. An accurate draft awaiting holder response is pending; a verified team-only record posted publicly is adverse, not a successful transmission. A public archive can support source study but cannot supply personal permission or a verification conversation that never occurred.

Let the holder choose a usable and bounded process

Offer readback, large print, a jointly drawn sequence or a mutually agreed interpreter. Audio requires separate permission; refusing it does not obstruct participation. Stop if material becomes sacred, private, commercially protected or outside the holder's authority. If no holder is available, complete H0 analysis as simulation, not community endorsement. A later real encounter may preserve legitimate disagreement rather than force consensus.

Complete only the permitted handoff

After actual holder confirmation, offer the corrected record to a willing recipient in the agreed audience or store it at the agreed location with a review date. Invite a recipient question and return uncertain points to the holder. For advanced practice, compare a second willing holder's version with separate consent and preserve both contexts. The simulation does not authorize contacting, recording or publishing a real community.
