# 10.14 — Remove a learning barrier while keeping the real standard

Adapt pace, medium, environment or response method to a learner’s stated needs, then check whether the underlying skill is more accessible without changing what the skill means.

This is an access-focused teaching trial, not diagnosis or proof of a fixed learning style. One person’s preferred adaptation should not be generalized to everyone with a similar label.

Default task: follow a three-position, two-move route on a paper grid from A1 to A2 to B2. The learning goal is interpreting ordered route instructions, not reading tiny print, speaking quickly or using a particular hand. Invite a willing adult to identify an unnecessary barrier, or examine a barrier you personally encounter. Use large print, tactile markers, audio or a slower presentation as appropriate.

Ten minutes to agree the goal and barrier, two short attempts and fifteen minutes to compare.

## 1. Separate the skill from the barrier around it

State the skill criterion: follow the sequence to the correct destination and explain the order. Ask what makes the instruction hard to access—language, visual size, pace, noise, working load or response format. Do not infer a diagnosis. Identify which demand is essential to the skill and which is incidental, such as tiny type or a speed limit that the task does not require.

- The underlying skill has a clear criterion
- The learner identifies or confirms the barrier
- Essential and incidental demands are separated
- No diagnosis or fixed learning-style label is assigned

## 2. Agree an access adaptation and attempt the same kind of task

Agree on one adaptation package, recording each changed component, such as presenting each direction as an audio step with a repeat option. Keep route complexity and the success criterion comparable; preserve other established supports. Use a new route of the same length and ask the learner to attempt it without answer-revealing hints. Record whether the change reduced the identified barrier and whether it created a different one.

- The adaptation is chosen with the learner
- The skill standard and route complexity remain comparable
- A real adapted attempt occurs
- New barriers and support use are recorded

## 3. Review access and learning as separate questions

Ask whether the instructions became usable and check the actual route result. A comfortable experience may still reveal a concept error, while a correct result may still impose excessive burden. Revise the teaching or adaptation accordingly and agree whether to keep it. Record the specific learner and task boundary without collecting sensitive identity details or claiming population-wide effectiveness.

- Usability and skill performance are reviewed separately
- The learner’s feedback informs the next version
- The retained adaptation has a specific reason
- One trial is not presented as a universal accessibility solution

Use the learner’s chosen communication method and allow stopping without penalty. A self-test is valid for your own access barrier but not an independent test with another learner. Seek qualified educational or accessibility support for persistent or complex needs.

Reduce a barrier while preserving the learning target

Learning guide — unscored

Change access demands without replacing the construct

The construct is the actual skill to be demonstrated. Here it is interpreting an ordered route and explaining its order, not reading tiny print, responding quickly or moving a particular hand. Ask the learner which barrier matters. An adaptation is a testable design choice, not a diagnosis or proof of a fixed learning style. Preserve required supports and the meaningful target; comfort, correctness and burden are separate observations.

Complete grid and two comparable routes

Draw nine cells with columns A, B, C from left to right and rows 1, 2, 3 from bottom to top. Bottom row is A1 B1 C1, middle A2 B2 C2, top A3 B3 C3. R1 is A1, A2, B2. Fresh R2 is C1, C2, B2. Each has three positions and two moves, first upward then horizontal. Criterion is to visit every position in order and explain the order; reaching B2 alone is insufficient. Use chosen speech, pointing, tactile markers or AAC without helper answer selection. No speed criterion applies. Keep reference and other established supports available on both attempts.

Different example — usable shape cards can still be in the wrong order

A learner must place circle, square, triangle in that order. Larger separate cards become easier to see, but the learner places circle, triangle, square. Usability improved by their report; sequence performance still misses the target. Keep the helpful size while explaining order and checking a fresh comparable case. Declaring success solely because the cards feel comfortable would discard the learning standard.

Record usability and performance without claiming causation

A fresh route reduces copying of the old answer but does not remove effects of practice, familiarity or support. Matched route length and criteria help comparison; they do not make one trial a controlled experiment. Larger labels and chunking change two components together, so this package cannot isolate which mattered. Correctness with excessive burden needs further adaptation; greater comfort with a sequence error needs further teaching. Preserve the learner's feedback rather than infer it from the output.

Offer chosen supports without choosing the answer

Use large print, tactile cells, sequential audio with repeat, AAC, quiet space or more time as chosen by the learner. A helper may operate a marker at the learner's direction but must not choose the next cell. Record assistance and new barriers. Stop without penalty; persistent or complex needs can require qualified accessibility or educational support. A self-test concerns your own barrier, and a simulation supports planning only, not an independent learner result.

Keep helpful access and check another comparable case

Retain supports the learner finds useful and agree a later fresh route with the same criterion, such as A3, A2, B2 with its changed initial direction recorded. If testing a new component, change and record that component while retaining needed aids; do not remove accommodations to prove independence. Ask about fatigue and usability again. Success on one supported route does not establish durable learning, a diagnosis or a population-wide accessibility solution.
