Complete grid and two comparable routes

Draw nine cells with columns A, B, C from left to right and rows 1, 2, 3 from bottom to top. Bottom row is A1 B1 C1, middle A2 B2 C2, top A3 B3 C3. R1 is A1, A2, B2. Fresh R2 is C1, C2, B2. Each has three positions and two moves, first upward then horizontal. Criterion is to visit every position in order and explain the order; reaching B2 alone is insufficient. Use chosen speech, pointing, tactile markers or AAC without helper answer selection. No speed criterion applies. Keep reference and other established supports available on both attempts.

Identify the barrier and preserve the standard

In this fictional case the route is printed in crowded 6-point type with all arrows together. The learner asks for bigger labels and one instruction at a time. Before opening the check, separate the essential skill from incidental demands and propose a presentation adaptation. State what stays comparable across R1 and R2, which existing supports stay available, what the helper may do, and why a correct destination alone is insufficient. Treat larger labels plus chunking as a two-component presentation package, not an isolated causal variable.

Check your attempt: Check the access design

The essential demand is preserving all three positions and explaining their order. Tiny print, crowded simultaneous arrows, speed and a specific response hand are incidental here. The requested presentation package can use larger labels with one instruction at a time and optional repeat; preserve established supports, route length and criterion on both attempts. A helper can present material but cannot select positions. A shared B2 destination does not validate an incorrect intermediate cell. Two changed presentation components do not isolate a single cause, and the fictional request is not a diagnosis.

- Construct and incidental barriers distinguished
- Learner request informs adaptation
- Criterion and supports preserved
- Package and causal limits explicit
