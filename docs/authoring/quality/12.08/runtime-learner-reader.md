# 12.08 — Separate power, speed, coordination and agility into safe capability-fit learning routes

Classify four distinct movement capacities, select a route whose prerequisites are already met, and compare one bounded practice component without treating a coordination drill as proof of athletic power, speed or agility.

The complete default is a low-risk tabletop coordination and reaction exercise. Speed, power and agility routes use only an existing qualified or competently established plan, or instruction-first preparation. No sprint, jump, loaded explosive movement, fall response or rapid direction change is prescribed by this practice.

Use the supplied component cards, route matrix, prerequisite screen and session record. The default requires three large labeled cards on a stable table within comfortable reach and an optional willing cue giver. A person with an existing suitable program may instead document one already-prescribed speed, power or agility component without changing its dose. Otherwise prepare the supplied qualified-instruction brief. Accuracy, control, recovery and stop rules take priority over speed or force.

About thirty minutes for classification and route planning plus two five-to-ten-minute bounded sessions on separate suitable days, or one instruction-first consultation.

Use eye gaze, a switch, larger targets, verbal selection, slower cues or an existing therapist-approved movement for the coordination route. Use a seated or equipment-supported route when it preserves the selected component. A support changes conditions but does not reduce dignity. Never simulate disability, catch a falling person, improvise a sprint, jump, loaded explosive movement or rapid direction change. Existing clinical, rehabilitation and sport plans control physical progression.

## 1. Classify the four capacities and select one honest route

Open the component cards and classify the supplied cases before revealing the key. Map the chosen real-world goal to coordination, reaction, speed, power or agility without assuming they are interchangeable. Complete the prerequisite screen. Select tabletop coordination, existing-plan speed, existing-plan power, existing-plan agility, or instruction-first. Fix the exact component, materials, environment, support, known dose, accuracy or technique criteria, recovery and stop conditions. The tabletop route cannot be relabeled as athletic testing, and missing prerequisites require instruction or defer.

- Power speed coordination reaction and agility are not treated as synonyms
- The chosen route has its stated prerequisites and authority
- Materials criteria support recovery and stop conditions are fixed before action
- A tabletop sequence is never represented as a power speed or agility test

## 2. Practice one component and correct one observed error

For the tabletop route, attempt three unhurried left-center-right-center sequences with rest, record exact target or order errors, change one cue, and retry at the same comfortable pace. For an existing-plan route, perform only the already-authorized movement and dose while recording its existing technique and recovery criteria; do not add timing, force, direction changes or volume. For instruction-first, use the prepared questions and record the route disposition instead of performing the movement. Stop for pain, fatigue, loss of control, unsafe surface or equipment, or any existing plan's stop rule.

- A bounded attempt consultation or appropriate stop is recorded
- The observed error belongs to the selected component and fixed criteria
- Only one cue or plan-authorized variable changes
- Accuracy control recovery and stop limits take priority over speed or force

## 3. Compare a second exposure and choose a component-specific next step

After suitable recovery, repeat the tabletop sequence with only the label order or an unhurried external cue changed, or repeat the exact existing-plan component only when its plan supports it. Compare component-specific accuracy, technique, cue response, support and recovery. Decide among repeat, regress, plan-authorized progression, instruction or stop. A faster time is not required and cannot compensate for an order, technique or safety error. State which of the other three capacities remain untested and open the comparison check only after preserving the decision.

- The second exposure preserves comparison conditions or declares the change
- Evidence is interpreted only for the selected component
- One next step follows accuracy technique recovery and prerequisite evidence
- Every untested capacity remains explicit rather than inferred

Four capacities and one related cue skill

Coordination organizes body parts or actions into an accurate, controlled sequence. Reaction is initiating an appropriate response to a cue. Speed is completing a defined movement or distance quickly. Power is producing force rapidly, so force and time both matter. Agility is rapid whole-body movement or change of direction in response to a task or stimulus; a preplanned change-of-direction drill does not capture every reactive-agility demand. These capacities interact, but one result does not stand in for the others. Touching cards can expose order, target and cue-response errors. It does not measure sprint speed, high-force power, landing ability or athletic agility.

Five route choices

Tabletop coordination and reaction: use the supplied cards, comfortable reach, accuracy criteria and unhurried cues; no athletic claim. Existing-plan speed: use only a movement, surface, dose, recovery and timing method already established for the person; no maximal trial is added. Existing-plan power: use only a qualified or competently established power exercise with its strength, technique, equipment, landing or force-absorption prerequisites already met; this guide supplies no load or jump. Existing-plan agility: use only an established task with known deceleration, surface, footwear, space and cue rules; do not infer reactive agility from a preplanned route. Instruction-first: prepare the supplied brief and obtain a route decision without performing the uncertain movement.

Prerequisite and boundary screen

Record yes, no or unknown for: the selected component is defined; the movement and technique are already familiar; the environment, surface and equipment are suitable; required mobility, strength, balance, deceleration or force-absorption prerequisites are established where relevant; the dose and recovery come from an existing plan; ordinary access support remains; and stop conditions are known. A no or important unknown means instruction-first or defer. Stop for pain, faintness, chest discomfort, unusual breathlessness, loss of control, unsafe surface or equipment, collision risk, or the existing plan's stop rule. This screen is educational and is not medical or sport clearance.

Complete tabletop coordination default

Place three large cards labeled L, C and R on a stable table within comfortable reach. Use a finger, pointer, eye gaze, switch or verbal selection. Fix four criteria: correct order; intended target; controlled initiation and stop; comfortable reach without symptom-provoking effort. Attempt L-C-R-C three times at an unhurried pace with rest. Record the exact missed contact or transition. Add one cue, such as numbers 1-2-3-2 or speaking the sequence, and retry without increasing pace. At the second session, change only the label order or use one willing cue giver. Timing is optional and is not a completion criterion.

Speed, power or agility instruction brief

Prepare: the exact goal and component; current training experience; relevant movement and equipment; surface, space and access conditions; existing dose and recovery; known technique; and each unknown prerequisite. Ask: Is the goal actually speed, power, coordination, reaction, change of direction or reactive agility? Which prerequisites and technique criteria should be established first? What starting task and dose fit the current plan? What recovery and stop rules apply? Which single variable could progress, and what evidence would permit it? Record only: appropriate as proposed | modify | prerequisite first | another qualified route | defer. The answer prepares training; it does not prove capacity.

Worked example that keeps the component boundary

Three unhurried L-C-R-C attempts contain two R-to-C order errors. The learner adds spoken numbers and retries at the same pace with no order errors and controlled contacts. On the second day, a willing partner gives the same four cues in an unhurried order; one target error returns. The evidence supports improved performance with a self-spoken cue and mixed transfer to an external cue. It does not show increased hand speed, rapid force, running speed, deceleration or agility.

Choose the next component from evidence, not ambition

Repeat when the selected criteria need another comparable exposure. Regress when control, recovery or prerequisites are insufficient. Progress one variable only when the existing plan or qualified instruction permits it. Seek instruction when component choice, technique, dose, surface, equipment or readiness is unknown. Stop when safety or recovery rules require it. Keep coordination, reaction, speed, power and agility findings separate; broader athletic capability requires separately appropriate evidence.
