Classify five movement cases

Classify the main observed capacity in each case and state one capacity the case does not establish. A: touch L-C-R-C in the correct order at a comfortable pace. B: begin the correct touch after an unpredictable L, C or R cue. C: cover a fixed straight distance in less time under comparable conditions. D: move a known resistance rapidly with established technique. E: change whole-body direction in response to an opponent or unpredictable signal. Explain why a quick tabletop sequence cannot by itself establish D or E. Preserve the answers before opening the check.

Check your attempt: Check the component classifications

A primarily observes coordination. B primarily observes reaction choice while still requiring coordination. C is a speed comparison. D is a power task because resistance is moved rapidly. E is reactive agility because whole-body direction changes in response to an unpredictable signal. Any case can depend on other capacities, but it does not establish them automatically. A quick tabletop sequence has negligible relevance to high-force output, deceleration and whole-body direction change, so it cannot establish D or E.

- Each case has one main capacity and one explicit evidence limit.
- Reaction is separated from movement speed and coordination.
- Power includes rapid force rather than speed alone.
- Agility includes whole-body direction change and task or stimulus response.
