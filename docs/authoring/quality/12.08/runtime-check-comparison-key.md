Interpret a changed-condition comparison

Case: An existing-plan change-of-direction task met three of four technique criteria on the first exposure. On the second exposure the athlete changed shoes and surface, received extra cueing, and completed it faster while still missing the same deceleration criterion. Decide whether the evidence supports progression and explain what is comparable, what is confounded and which capacities remain untested. Preserve the answer before opening the check.

Check your attempt: Check the changed-condition decision

Progression is not supported. Shoes, surface and cueing changed, so the faster result is not comparable, and the same deceleration criterion remained unmet. The record can show that the second exposure occurred under changed conditions and that one criterion remained a concern. It cannot establish improvement, cause, power, straight-line speed, coordination across other tasks or safe reactive agility. A defensible next step is to restore comparable conditions, regress, or seek plan-specific instruction before progression.

- Changed surface footwear and cueing are identified as comparison limits.
- Faster time does not override the repeated technique defect.
- Progression is withheld pending comparable or qualified evidence.
- Untested capacities remain explicitly unknown.
