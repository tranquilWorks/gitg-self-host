# 10.03 — Turn one puzzling observation into a precise, testable question backlog

Distinguish a vague curiosity from a precise descriptive, causal or evaluative question, investigate one and keep a useful backlog of unresolved questions.

This is inquiry practice around a harmless observation. A good question need not have an immediate answer, and one observation cannot establish a causal explanation.

Use the supplied synthetic notice-board brief, question ladder, evidence-match table and backlog template. The board has twelve labeled rectangles, five overlaps, four designated posting zones and no personal content. A live board is optional and may be used only when viewing it is permitted; record counts and layout categories rather than names, messages or photographs.

Ten minutes for observation, twenty minutes for one inquiry and ten minutes to refine the question backlog.

## 1. Describe the puzzle and generate different kinds of question

Read the supplied board brief without opening the classification key. Record observation, expectation and contradiction in separate fields. Write at least one descriptive, causal and evaluative question. Replace vague terms such as bad, cluttered or works with a named population, object, measure, comparison or criterion. Complete the classification prompt, preserve the first answer and then use its key to correct any question whose type or observable terms were unclear.

- The observation is separated from an immediate explanation
- Descriptive causal and evaluative questions are distinguished
- Vague terms are replaced with observable meanings
- The surprising expectation or contradiction is stated

## 2. Choose one question and obtain a relevant piece of evidence

Use the evidence-match table to rank questions by relevance, answerability, consequence, access and twenty-minute fit. Select one question and predeclare the evidence that could answer it, what result would remain ambiguous and one privacy boundary. For the complete default, count the supplied overlap and zone-placement observations. Explain why those counts can answer a descriptive question but cannot establish motive or causation. Preserve the result and any missing evidence.

- The selected question has a reason for priority
- The evidence actually bears on that question
- Observation respects access and privacy
- A descriptive result is not promoted into an unsupported cause

## 3. Resolve refine or defer rather than filling the gap with a story

Enter every question in the supplied backlog with type, exact wording, status, evidence seen, uncertainty and next check. Mark the investigated question answered within scope, partly answered or unresolved. Refine one causal question into a safe comparison, then complete the resolution prompt before revealing its key. Keep unanswered questions visible with a revisit trigger; do not invent a cause or close an entry merely to make the backlog look complete.

- The question receives an honest resolution status
- The answer cites the observed evidence
- One refined next question has a feasible check
- Unanswered questions remain visible without invented closure

Use spoken questions, sketches or a private synthetic example. Curiosity about people is not permission to investigate their private lives. When no live observation is accessible, the supplied notice-board case supports question formation but not a real-world finding.

Observe, form and refine questions without inventing answers

Learning guide — unscored

A question is a tool, not an answer in disguise

Curiosity becomes investigable when observation, interpretation and uncertainty remain separate. An observation reports what the available record shows. An interpretation proposes meaning. A question identifies what is still unknown. Descriptive questions ask what, where, when or how much; causal questions ask what contributes to a change and require a defensible comparison; evaluative questions ask how something performs against explicit criteria for a named user or purpose. A precise question names its object, scope and evidence need. Precision does not require premature certainty, and one count cannot establish motive or cause.

Question ladder and evidence-match table

For each candidate write: observed fact | expected pattern | contradiction or gap | question type | exact question | evidence needed | evidence available | what that evidence cannot show | privacy or access limit. Test the wording: Can two readers identify the same object and measure? Is the population or setting bounded? Does the proposed evidence match the type? Is the answer useful enough to justify the effort? Rank candidates with high, medium or low for relevance, answerability, consequence, access and time fit. A high-consequence question requires stronger authority and is outside this low-stakes default.

Synthetic notice-board evidence packet

The fictional board is divided into four equal posting zones, Z1 through Z4. Twelve same-size notices are present. N1-N4 are in Z1, N5-N7 in Z2, N8-N10 in Z3 and N11-N12 in Z4. Overlap pairs are N1/N2, N2/N3, N5/N6, N8/N9 and N9/N10: five overlap pairs. Seven notices do not overlap another notice. Ten notices are fully inside a zone and two cross a zone boundary. A weekly log says expired notices were removed on schedule for four weeks. It contains no measure of readability, posting motives, board traffic or results from a board with clearer zones. This packet permits counting and question design; it does not establish why overlap occurs or what readers prefer.

Private question backlog

Use one row per question: Q-ID | exact wording | descriptive / causal / evaluative | priority reason | status [unexamined / investigated / answered within scope / partly answered / unresolved / deferred] | evidence and date | strongest limitation | next safe check | revisit trigger. Keep unresolved entries rather than converting uncertainty into a confident story. Store no names, message content or other private detail.

Worked refinement from vague concern to testable inquiry

“Why is the board bad?” hides both the criterion and the proposed cause. A descriptive refinement is “How many of the twelve notices overlap at least one other notice in the supplied packet?” A causal refinement is “Under comparable posting traffic, does a board with visibly marked zones have fewer overlap pairs than a board without them?” An evaluative refinement is “Can intended readers identify current event date and contact details at the agreed viewing distance?” The packet answers only the supplied overlap count. The causal comparison and actual-reader criterion need new evidence.

Choose a fitting inquiry route

Default route: use the complete fictional packet. Live-observation route: inspect only a place you may view and retain category-level counts, not personal content. Advanced route: use a real low-stakes technical or civic question, but predeclare the authority, evidence and stopping boundary before searching. Spoken, signed, audio, tactile and supported responses are equivalent when the question structure remains observable. Curiosity about a person never grants access to that person’s private life.

Progress by improving evidence fit

Progress is not asking more questions. It is forming a question whose wording, consequence and evidence need are clearer, then revising it when inquiry exposes a gap. Transfer by applying the same observation-question-evidence-status record to a different harmless context. A completed packet shows one bounded inquiry cycle, not a stable trait or broad research competence.
