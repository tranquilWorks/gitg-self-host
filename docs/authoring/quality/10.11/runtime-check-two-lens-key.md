Supplied fictional waiting-area records

W0: six seats occupy a 4-by-6-meter room; four receive afternoon shade; three sound readings are 70, 72 and 71 dB at the same marked point, with no comfort threshold supplied. H1: a 2024 memo says the stop moved indoors after summer heat complaints; it does not explain who was consulted. D1: the route display uses 8-millimeter characters and refreshes each minute; no legibility standard is supplied. E1: the improvement budget is $2,400; either eight $300 high-contrast signs or four $600 shaded seats fit before maintenance, but mixed options are allowed. U1: in a voluntary fictional test at the intended distance, two of two readers could not identify the route number; the sample cannot represent all users. A1: one designer describes the current gray surface as calm; one rider describes it as hard to distinguish. S1: a meeting roster lists operations and one designer but no riders or disability advocate. These records are synthetic and contain no real people.

Connect display technology and social access

Use D1 and U1 to write a five-part synthesis: exact supported technical fact; exact observed access result; how the access result changes the display-design question; one bounded next decision; and three important unknowns. Then state one tension introduced by E1 without pretending the budget record identifies the best option. Name the five lenses not investigated beyond the map and the source, specialist or affected voice needed before a consequential redesign. Cite record codes after every factual claim and preserve the draft before opening the key.

Check your attempt: Check the cross-field inference and retained limits

D1 supports that the fictional display uses 8-millimeter characters and one-minute refreshes. U1 supports that two willing readers in this fictional test could not identify the route number at the intended distance. Together they justify treating legibility as an unresolved design requirement and testing a bounded high-contrast or larger-character alternative. They do not show that character size caused the failures, represent other users, identify an applicable standard or choose the best redesign. E1 introduces a $2,400 allocation tension between signs and shade but omits maintenance and mixed-option performance. History, economics, humanities, arts and society remain only mapped unless separately investigated; a correct synthesis names those limits and the next fitting expertise.
