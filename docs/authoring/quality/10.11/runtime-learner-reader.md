# 10.11 — Connect seven fields around one ordinary public-space question

Use foundational questions from several disciplines, investigate two connections and mark the boundary between useful breadth and genuine subject expertise.

The seven-lens map is a product-authored learning device. It is not a complete account of any discipline or permission to make professional claims outside your knowledge.

Question: how should a small public waiting area be improved? Use seven lenses: science asks what can be measured; history asks what changed and why; technology asks how a designed system works; economics asks about scarce resources and tradeoffs; humanities asks about meaning and justified values; arts asks how form shapes experience; society asks who participates and who decides. Use a fictional waiting area when no safe public case is available.

Twenty minutes for the map, thirty minutes for two source checks and ten minutes for synthesis.

## 1. Generate one concrete question from each lens

Apply the seven supplied definitions to the waiting area: measure shade or noise; trace a past relocation; inspect the information display; compare maintenance tradeoffs; examine dignity and fairness; consider legibility and atmosphere; identify whose access needs are unheard. State what kind of evidence each field would need. Do not use a discipline’s name as a substitute for an actual question.

- All seven fields receive a concrete question
- Each question names a relevant kind of evidence
- At least one value question is distinguished from a measurement
- The map does not claim expertise from terminology alone

## 2. Investigate two connected questions with traceable evidence

Choose two lenses that affect the same decision, such as display technology and access. Consult a public specification, historical record, scholarly introduction or experienced professional appropriate to each. Offline alternative: use these fictional records—display D1 requires reading small print; user test U1 reports that two willing readers could not identify the route number at the intended distance. Explain what the pair supports and what it leaves unknown about other users or designs.

- Two distinct lenses receive actual evidence checks
- Sources or fictional records are identified
- The connection affects a concrete decision
- Small or synthetic evidence is not generalized to a whole population

## 3. Write a synthesis that keeps disciplinary limits visible

Explain one insight gained by connecting the two fields and one tension that remains, such as a visually attractive display that is difficult to read. Name the five lenses not investigated beyond the initial map and the expert or source needed before a consequential decision. Propose one small next learning step rather than claiming that interdisciplinary vocabulary has solved the waiting area.

- The synthesis contains a genuine cross-field connection
- A tradeoff or disagreement remains explicit
- Uninvestigated lenses are honestly identified
- The next step names an appropriate source or expertise

Use a spoken mind map, cards or the fictional records. A library or accessible introductory resource can replace internet access. Do not photograph or profile public-space users without appropriate consent.

Connect seven disciplinary lenses without pretending depth

Learning guide — unscored

Breadth changes a question; labels alone do not

Interdisciplinary breadth uses each field to ask a different concrete question, identify a fitting kind of evidence and show how at least two lenses change one decision. Science asks what can be observed or measured; history asks what changed over time and why accounts differ; technology asks how a designed system works; economics asks about scarcity, incentives and tradeoffs; humanities examine meaning, interpretation and justified values; arts examine how form shapes experience; and society asks who participates, is affected and decides. Naming fields is not expertise, and combining weak evidence does not make it strong.

Supplied fictional waiting-area records

W0: six seats occupy a 4-by-6-meter room; four receive afternoon shade; three sound readings are 70, 72 and 71 dB at the same marked point, with no comfort threshold supplied. H1: a 2024 memo says the stop moved indoors after summer heat complaints; it does not explain who was consulted. D1: the route display uses 8-millimeter characters and refreshes each minute; no legibility standard is supplied. E1: the improvement budget is $2,400; either eight $300 high-contrast signs or four $600 shaded seats fit before maintenance, but mixed options are allowed. U1: in a voluntary fictional test at the intended distance, two of two readers could not identify the route number; the sample cannot represent all users. A1: one designer describes the current gray surface as calm; one rider describes it as hard to distinguish. S1: a meeting roster lists operations and one designer but no riders or disability advocate. These records are synthetic and contain no real people.

Example connection on a different public library question

A library-entry question connects technology—whether an automatic door’s sensor detects a slow approach—with society—whose mobility patterns were included in testing. The technical specification may describe the detection range, while a small user observation reveals one missed condition. The connection changes the decision: a range number alone is insufficient if the test population omits relevant approaches. It still does not answer historical preservation, budget or aesthetic questions, and one observation does not establish population-wide performance.

A connection must alter interpretation or action

Two adjacent summaries are not yet a synthesis. A real connection shows that one lens changes the question, interpretation, criterion or action under another. D1 says how the display is built; U1 shows a small access problem at the intended distance. Together they justify further legibility testing or a bounded redesign, not a claim about all riders. E1 adds a cost tradeoff but cannot decide which option is fair without stated values and affected voices. Retain disagreements and unknowns.

Use the fictional case or a permitted public record

Use spoken mapping, cards, large text, audio or a supported scribe. The complete fictional packet requires no internet or observation. A live route may use public specifications and records but must retain provenance, access permission and population limits; do not photograph or profile people without consent. An advanced route may add a third lens only when it changes the decision, not to make the map look comprehensive. Consequential design needs relevant specialists and affected users.

Deepen one boundary rather than adding more labels

If the two-lens synthesis is supported and limited, choose one missing evidence item such as a larger voluntary legibility check or the applicable display standard. If the connection is only adjacent summaries, rewrite how one finding changes the other question. If a value judgment is hidden as a measurement, label it and identify whose reasons matter. Transfer the seven-lens map to another harmless decision without claiming disciplinary mastery.
