# Later packet — revise an inference, keep an honest commitment

Supplied fiction, not actual participant evidence.

A fictional reader reviews the learner's initial statement: “I want an exclusive partnership. Rae probably has a different kind of arrangement, but can come to the picnic anyway.” The reader points out that Rae's orientation does not establish the relationship structure, and “anyway” makes ordinary inclusion sound like reluctant permission. The learner's preference for exclusivity is not challenged.

The revised statement reads: “I would choose exclusivity in my own partnership. Rae asked about the picnic; I can share its time and access details without assuming anything about Rae's private agreements.” The reader says this now represents the supplied facts accurately. Rae has not received the reply and no picnic has occurred.

Credit the specific correction and fictional reader confirmation. Do not claim actual friendship maintenance, changed prejudice, approval of every possible structure or an independent human review. The learner can retain a personal commitment while withdrawing an unsupported inference. In C, any new invitation still requires attention to the existing notification and privacy commitments; the structure's label does not answer that question.
