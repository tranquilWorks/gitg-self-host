# Corrective answers

1. Nothing about exclusivity follows from orientation alone. Structure and actual agreements need their own facts, and you may have no legitimate reason to ask. Treat ordinary social requests on their relevant merits.
2. No. Respect does not create an obligation to participate. A new arrangement needs freely given agreement, and an existing promise remains relevant until legitimately changed. Name the pressure without degrading people who freely choose that form.
3. Yes. State the personal commitment as a boundary on your own participation, avoid claims of lesser dignity or invented character defects, and respect the friend's actual requests and responsibilities. Honest disagreement does not require contempt or false agreement.
