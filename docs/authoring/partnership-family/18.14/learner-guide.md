# 18.14 — Reproductive intentions and partnership communication

Clarify present wishes, uncertainty, bodily boundaries and practical responsibilities before assumptions become a crisis. Allow twenty minutes of private preparation, a willing adult conversation of similar length, and one actual information inquiry or agreed follow-up if appropriate. Parenting and partnership are optional. Single life, not wanting children, uncertainty and declining personal disclosure are legitimate. Use the complete fiction without revealing reproductive history.

## A wish is not a forecast or a promise on another person's behalf

Wanting children, not wanting them, being unsure and being open only under certain conditions are different current positions. “Maybe someday” can conceal a timing conflict or simply express uncertainty. Ask what the phrase means now rather than converting it into a yes. People may change their minds; that possibility does not justify ignoring a clear present boundary or promising children merely to avoid a difficult conversation.

A reproductive intention is distinct from a fertility prediction. Appearance, cycle assumptions or a previous outcome cannot establish an individual's future fertility through this exercise. Pregnancy risk, contraception, health history, fertility questions and relevant treatment belong with current qualified guidance. The conversation can identify what needs asking without selecting a method, interpreting a test or guaranteeing an outcome.

Timing involves practical circumstances as well as desire: work, finances, care capacity, housing and each person's values may matter. Partners can share information gathering, costs, appointments or future care planning when freely agreed and authorized. Shared responsibility does not confer control over another person's body, medication, pregnancy decisions or access to care. Interfering with contraception, withholding material information or using threats is not negotiation.

A genuine mismatch can remain after a kind, clear conversation. Do not require one person to surrender a core intention, or use an inquiry as evidence they have accepted a future path. Adoption or other lawful routes may raise their own legal and practical questions; they are not automatic solutions to an unexamined disagreement. Qualification and local context matter.

## Worked example

One partner says “I might want children in a few years,” while the other means “I want to begin trying next year.” Repeating “we both want children” hides a consequential difference. A useful account records both timing positions and any conditions, then identifies what remains undecided. Booking a medical discussion may improve information later, but it does not reconcile timing or prove that pregnancy will be possible.

## Supplied fictional task

Adult partners Asha and Luca have used the phrase “maybe someday.” On 5 October Asha explains that they are currently uncertain about wanting children and do not want a decision this year. Luca says they presently hope to parent and would like a clearer discussion about timing within six months. Neither has promised to try for pregnancy. No fertility status, pregnancy, method details or medical diagnosis is supplied.

Both want truthful information and no contraceptive interference. Asha is willing to ask a qualified clinic what a discussion of individual reproductive questions would involve, but does not authorize Luca to access clinical records or choose care. Luca offers to research public appointment costs, within thirty minutes, if Asha wants that help. The offer has not yet been accepted. Both agree to a nonsexual conversation; neither has accepted a reproductive outcome or a six-month deadline for changing their mind.

## Clarify without coercion

1. Make separate entries for present wish, timing, conditions, boundaries and unknowns for each person. Write “uncertain” for Asha rather than “eventual yes”; write Luca's current hope without making it a command. Output: two distinguishable positions and one explicit unresolved mismatch.
2. Separate personal decisions from questions requiring information. Draft one qualified-service inquiry about how to discuss individual reproductive concerns and available consultation options. Do not request a fertility prediction from the fictional facts. Output: a question list with clinical and legal limits, not advice on a method or treatment.
3. Draft the conversation opening and an accurate reflection from each person. Asha can acknowledge Luca's hope without promising children; Luca can acknowledge Asha's uncertainty without treating it as manipulation. Output: two fair summaries and any agreed review date, if actually accepted.
4. Address responsibility and authority. Ask whether the public-cost research is wanted; preserve Asha's control over private care information. Identify support someone can offer without assuming access to records, appointments or bodily decisions. Output: accepted tasks with limits, and unaccepted offers still marked as offers.
5. Save the map and open the [later packet](later-packet.md). Check what was asked, answered or booked, and whether the core intentions changed. Output: a status review and a next question that does not manufacture consensus.

## Supported, harder and live routes

For support, use two fictional statements and underline wish, timing and uncertainty in different formats. No one must produce an intimate autobiography. For a harder case, introduce a looming housing decision and distinguish its deadline from permission to pressure a reproductive decision. Consider whether the shared housing plan should remain unmade while the mismatch is unresolved.

For optional real transfer, invite a calm discussion only if welcome and safe. Use current individual information from qualified professionals where needed. Make one actual authorized inquiry or hold the agreed later conversation, recording only its status in learning notes. An unread webpage is not an inquiry, a clinic booking is not attendance, and attendance is not a particular medical answer. Reproductive coercion or abuse calls for appropriate safety support, not a more persuasive script.
