# 18.09 — Sexual ethics and consent

Use adult fictional cases and a no-contact rehearsal to recognize freely chosen permission and respond properly to refusal or uncertainty. Allow twenty minutes for classification and ten for optional spoken or written practice. There is no sexual activity assignment, touch demonstration or requirement to disclose experience. Single life, abstinence and declining rehearsal remain legitimate. An observer can review fictional words only, without being cast as a sexual partner.

## Consent is a decision with conditions

Consent concerns a specific activity in a particular situation. Agreement to one form of closeness does not authorize another. Previous participation, a relationship title, marriage, gifts or a paid expense cannot settle a present decision. A person can change their mind after agreeing or after an activity begins. Stopping is the correct response to withdrawal; completing a planned sequence is not a competing obligation.

Freedom matters alongside words. A yes obtained through repeated pressure, threats, dependency or a penalty for refusal does not become freely chosen because the asker finally hears the desired syllable. Silence, stillness and nonresistance do not supply missing permission. Uncertainty calls for stopping or not starting, rather than trying to construct a technically defensible yes.

Capacity and access are different questions. A sleeping person or someone too impaired to understand and choose cannot provide the needed current agreement. A person using an established communication device may communicate a clear decision. Do not treat disability, quiet speech or an unfamiliar expression as automatic incapacity. Equally, do not ignore an actual inability to understand or communicate a choice. Relevant legal questions need qualified local guidance; this exercise does not determine legal capacity or age rules.

Informed choice also depends on honesty about material circumstances: what is being proposed, relevant health and prevention questions, commitment expectations, privacy and foreseeable consequences. Do not hide a consequential fact to preserve a desired answer. Discuss uncertainty plainly and seek suitable professional information where needed. Ethical responsibility extends beyond obtaining permission: dignity, care and keeping actual promises still matter.

## Worked example

Two adult peers agree to sit close while watching a film. One asks about a different kind of contact. The other says, “No, just sitting together.” A sound response is “Okay,” followed by no further attempt at the refused contact. Asking why until the answer changes, sulking as punishment, or claiming the earlier closeness made refusal unfair would undermine the freedom being respected. Continuing the originally welcome activity is also optional; neither person must stay to soothe disappointment.

## Four supplied adult cases

All people below are fictional adults. No physical enactment is required or permitted by the task.

- **A, specificity:** Robin agrees to hold hands with Casey and explicitly declines kissing. Casey says the handholding shows romantic interest and asks whether a kiss is now permitted. No changed answer is supplied.
- **B, withdrawal:** Jo initially agrees to a particular intimate activity with Ari, then says “Stop; I don't want this now.” Ari can hear and understand the request. No reason is offered or required.
- **C, pressure:** Pat declines. Ren asks repeatedly, says the evening cost a lot, and threatens to end the relationship if Pat will not agree. Pat eventually says “Fine.”
- **D, capacity:** Adult partners Quinn and Lee had earlier discussed intimacy. At the later moment Quinn is asleep. Lee remembers the earlier conversation. There is no present communication.

The cases intentionally supply enough information to decide whether the proposed continuation is authorized. Do not add a hidden enthusiastic yes, assume a refusal is playful, or invent a rule that partnership replaces present choice.

## Classify and rehearse

1. For each case write the proposed activity, the actual communication, any pressure or capacity concern, and the immediate permissible response. Output: four decisions with reasons tied to the facts. A authorizes only the stated handholding; B requires stopping; C does not supply freely given agreement; D supplies no current consent.
2. Write one respectful ask, one plain refusal, and one response to withdrawal. Use your own language or accessible communication format. The response must not require explanation, bargaining, reassurance or a promise for later. Output: three usable lines, rehearsed without contact or intimate detail.
3. List questions that should be settled before any real relevant decision: boundaries, material health/prevention information, actual commitments, privacy expectations and consequences. Mark which are personal choices and which need qualified medical or legal information. Output: a question list, not a self-issued certificate of safety or a prescribed prevention method.
4. Save your decisions before opening the [later packet](later-packet.md). Check whether the supplied conduct respects the original boundary. A renewed question after pressure is not automatically an ethical reset. Output: a correction wherever actions fail to match the decision.

Do not evaluate success by how comfortable an observer feels with sexual discussion or how visibly enthusiastic someone appears. The inspectable skill here is recognizing permission limits and choosing respectful conduct. A fiction response shows reasoning in rehearsal, not actual sexual behavior or legal compliance.

## Supported and harder routes

For support, use only A and B first, in writing or with communication aids, then return to pressure and capacity. A survivor need not disclose why they prefer fiction, omit a case or stop. For a harder fictional check, have someone agree to one activity while reserving a relevant health question; identify what remains unresolved without treating willingness as blanket permission.

Real transfer is optional and consists of applying respectful communication only in a genuinely wanted adult context. It creates no sexual performance requirement and no intimate app evidence. If coercion, assault concerns or immediate danger are present, use suitable specialist, medical or safeguarding resources; do not practice with the coercive person as a condition of getting help.
