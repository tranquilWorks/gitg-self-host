# Corrective answers

1. Neither. Communication access does not itself establish incapacity, and specific agreement remains limited to what was actually chosen. Attend to understanding, voluntariness and the stated boundary; seek qualified guidance for genuine capacity or legal uncertainty instead of inferring it from disability.
2. Do not proceed. A previous promise does not override present uncertainty, and insistence adds pressure. Allow refusal or space without bargaining; a desired outcome is not evidence of agreement.
3. Stop. Consent is reversible, including after starting. No explanation, compensation or later substitute is owed as the price of stopping. Offer only welcome practical support, without making the person manage the other's disappointment.
