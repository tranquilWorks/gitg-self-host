# 18.07 — Shared expectations, roles, and labor

Make the work behind one ordinary shared responsibility visible, agree who owns each part, and examine one full cycle. Allow twenty minutes to map it, twenty for a willing conversation, and time for the task itself. This optional adult-partnership route does not make partnership a requirement; single life is equally compatible with dignity. Use the fiction if no suitable shared arrangement exists.

## See the whole responsibility

Doing the visible task is only part of responsibility. Someone notices it is needed, discovers constraints, chooses among options, obtains resources, performs it, checks completion and handles surprises. “Tell me what to do” can leave the planning and monitoring with the person already carrying them. Ownership must say which of these parts is included and when to ask for help.

Negotiation spans money, home, time, sex, family, career, caregiving, privacy and decision authority. These areas interact without becoming interchangeable currencies. Working more hours does not purchase a partner's sexual consent. Paying a bill does not automatically authorize reading messages. Caregiving obligations cannot be dropped merely to demonstrate unfairness. Identify which decisions are shared, personal, delegated or constrained by an existing duty or qualified legal arrangement.

Fairness may involve unequal minutes because people have different availability, capacity or preferences. It still requires informed willingness, a visible burden and an opportunity to revise. Gender, income, disability or being “better at organizing” does not silently assign permanent service. Notice whose rest, career flexibility and social relationships absorb the hidden cost. Someone's voluntary preference today is not their irrevocable role.

A useful agreement names a result, limits, resources, ownership and change route. “Own the meal” is incomplete if the person lacks permission to spend the budget or ask guests about dietary needs. “You can spend up to the agreed amount and ask only the necessary food question” provides bounded authority. It does not authorize collecting private health histories.

## Worked example

Lee agrees to arrange a grocery order but assumes Ari will supply a list. Ari thinks arranging includes checking what is needed. The groceries arrive, yet Ari spent twenty minutes planning and sent three reminders. Calling the task fully transferred would hide that labor. They can acknowledge the order was placed while renegotiating whether Lee owns stock checking and scheduling. Blame is less useful than a precise interface.

## Supplied fictional task

Adult partners Dani and Jules will host two relatives for a simple meal on 10 October. The total agreed food budget is 60 units. Dani offers 45 minutes on Friday; Jules offers 60 on Saturday. They agree to ask guests only about food requirements relevant to the meal, without storing medical details. Both want a modest visit and neither has agreed to elaborate decoration.

Dani offers to check ingredients, ask the food question, select a menu within budget and order supplies. Jules offers to prepare the meal, welcome the guests and clean the kitchen afterward. The guests leave at 18:30. A delivery must arrive by Saturday 15:00 to allow preparation. The supplier's delivery confirmation is not yet available. No one has yet agreed who will track it or handle a delay. Dani also wants uninterrupted Friday work time after 17:00; Jules cannot leave home to collect groceries on Saturday.

## Negotiate and run a complete cycle

1. Make nine headings for the scope areas. Under home, time, money and family put the relevant meal facts. Mark sex, career, caregiving and privacy as no new agreement or private where appropriate, while noting Dani's existing work boundary. Output: the applicable decisions and explicitly unassigned areas, without forcing disclosure in every category.
2. Break the meal into noticing, constraints, choices, supplies, execution, checking and cleanup. Attach each to a willing owner and feasible window. Count the unassigned delivery check rather than assuming Dani will remember. Output: a responsibility map with one visible gap.
3. Write a proposal that closes that gap with permission: Dani can check delivery before 17:00 Friday and tell Jules the result; if it cannot be confirmed, both choose an available simpler menu within budget. This is a proposal until agreed. Include a completion signal and a way to renegotiate reduced capacity. Output: a bounded whole-task agreement, not a command to rescue the meal at any cost.
4. Save the agreement and open the [later packet](later-packet.md). Trace what each person actually did, including reminders and unexpected coordination. Calculate actual spending and remaining budget without interpreting money saved as proof of fairness. Output: one-cycle review and a specific revision for the next event.

A successful meal can coexist with an unfair handoff. Equally, asking one agreed clarification is not proof that someone failed at ownership. Check the agreed scope and actual burden. If the task exceeded either person's offered time, record that rather than silently resetting the estimate to make the plan look successful.

## Adapt and transfer

For support, choose a simpler shared task with three parts and use a visible list, voice note or accessible reminder system. The tool should support the agreed owner, not make the other person the permanent notification service. For a harder fictional attempt, remove the delivery option and require explicit renegotiation; do not assign collection to Jules against the stated constraint.

For real transfer, select a low-stakes responsibility both want to revisit. Agree authority and resources before acting, observe one actual cycle, and invite each person's view of hidden effort. Keep only a minimal task-level record. Revisit recurring patterns over time before claiming the overall division is fair. Formal financial, employment or care responsibilities may require qualified advice and cannot be rewritten through a household worksheet.

Consent, health, abuse, and legal concerns require appropriate medical, legal, or safeguarding resources.

This is role-conditional learning. Fiction is preparation, not actual participant evidence. Keep private identifying, intimate, medical and family details out of app records.
