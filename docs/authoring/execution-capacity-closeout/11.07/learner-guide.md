# 11.07 — Pacing and sustainable intensity

## Choose effort from the whole consequence

Pacing means matching effort to urgency, duration, health/capacity, quality and the importance of the result. The fastest visible output can leave errors, rework or another duty unpaid. A sprint, maintenance pace and recovery serve different situations; none is a rank of personal worth.

This guide uses a fictional room inventory to practice choosing a pace and reviewing its consequences. Allow 15 minutes for the decision and paper work, then 10 for the later outcomes and fresh checks. It requires reading short records and counting correct entries. Use paper, audio or a text editor; no physical exertion, live inventory, diagnosis or purchase is required. The case's work periods are scenario facts, not a direction to force yourself to work for that length.

A live route applies only to ordinary safe work within existing restrictions and supports. It is not treatment for fatigue or a rehabilitation plan. Illness, pain, dangerous sleepiness, worsening symptoms or unsafe conditions override output targets; use appropriate existing support. Do not test deprivation, remove aids or work through a warning sign for the exercise.

## Reference: pace includes what happens afterward

**Maintenance** is a repeatable allocation sized to current capacity and ordinary demands. **Sprint** is an exceptional bounded effort for an important, time-sensitive result, with a stop, quality check, recovery and outstanding-duty plan. **Recovery** reduces optional demand while preserving essential duties through suitable support. Recovery is not a debt to repay with extra work.

Separate four observations: attempted quantity, accepted output, rework and later burden. An entry counts as accepted only after its fixed criteria pass. Rework consumes real time; do not count the corrected item as a second new item. Later burden includes postponed duties and work shifted to others without agreement. A private rating or an invented energy score is not a physiological measurement.

Choose the pace before seeing the outcome. Record the real deadline and consequence, the task's importance, current capacity, existing restrictions, the stopping boundary and the next responsibility. The same task may merit a different choice on another day. Pressure alone does not establish urgency. A deadline also cannot authorize unsafe intensity.

## A different worked example

A non-urgent box-labeling task has eight labels. One supplied record shows eight attempted, five correct, and three still needing repair; another shows six attempted and six correct. The first has a larger attempt count but less accepted output. If repair later takes nine minutes, include those minutes in its cost. Neither record tells you whose health improved or that the second method caused the difference. Compare conditions before choosing a next pace.

### Three decision cards

- **Maintenance:** one section in each ordinary opportunity, with the protected transition/recovery and care duty retained. Review accuracy before the next section.
- **Sprint proposal:** do both sections now in a single 40-minute block. This exceeds today's stated 20-minute availability and would consume recovery and care time. It is not feasible in this case. A valid sprint in another case would need actual capacity, a bounded important deadline, preserved supports and a recovery/remaining-duty plan.
- **Recovery:** no optional inventory period today; preserve essential duties using existing supports. Keep inventory pending and review at the next suitable opportunity. If this threatens Day 3, communicate the actual limitation rather than promising compensatory overrun.

### Action 1 — Make a justified pace decision

Before opening the later packet, write `deadline and consequence | importance | capacity/restrictions | pace | output limit | quality criterion | stop condition | next duty | next decision`.

For the supplied baseline, maintenance fits the low urgency and two available periods. Recovery becomes appropriate if current safe capacity is unavailable. Do not “solve” that branch by dropping care or declaring a universal recovery deadline. A sprint cannot be justified from the optional catalog's importance alone.

Set your review rule now: retain maintenance provisionally only when entries meet the accuracy criterion and the protected periods/duties remain intact. Revise the task size or seek agreed help if rework or later burden exceeds the planned capacity. Stop optional work if a warning sign appears. Return from recovery according to safe actual capacity and existing restrictions/supports, not punishment for low output.

### Action 2 — Produce and inspect a paper inventory

Copy section A into your own inventory. Compare each row against the reference and retain any correction. Then, at your next chosen reading break or now if appropriate, copy and check B. Record which route you actually used; do not invent a two-day attempt or a physical recovery experience. These are actual paper outputs about fictional objects.

Open [later-packet.md](later-packet.md) after saving the pace decision. It contains alternative simulated two-period records with quality and aftermath. For each, calculate attempted entries, accepted entries, rework and outstanding items. Keep attempted output before correction separate from accepted output after correction. Do not mix the successful recovery of one branch with the errors of another.

### Action 3 — Decide the next pace from output and aftermath

Append `what met the standard | rework minutes | later duty/burden | differing conditions | next pace | concrete next action | what remains unknown`. A useful result may be a slower next period, agreed help or a recovery decision. “Try harder” is not a changed pace. A sprint's accounting remains open until its promised recovery and outstanding obligations are addressed.

## Access, greater challenge and transfer

A reader may dictate rows or use a large-print template; record assistance without treating it as a deficit. If counting is the access barrier, have an aid count while you decide what the quality and burden evidence permits. The supported route still exercises pace judgment, but not unaided counting. No unsupported physical task is required.

For a more demanding case, use fresh check 1's feasible sprint and add uncertainty: its four items may need 5–8 minutes each. Four upper bounds require 32 minutes before the 10-minute review; the 40-minute task window is then two minutes short. Reduce scope with agreement or seek another feasible arrangement; never turn an upper estimate into safe capacity by assertion.

For later transfer, choose two naturally available periods of one low-stakes task. Preserve existing supports, inspect accuracy and actual aftermath at the next ordinary responsibility, and record changed conditions. No observed burden is not proof of physiological benefit; unexpected worsening is a reason to stop or adjust, not evidence of inadequate character.

**Supportive:** Accurate work fits the chosen periods, recovery occurs and the next duty remains covered.

**Mixed:** The target is reached but corrections and later burden exceed the planned allocation.

**Contradictory:** Warning signs are ignored or recovery is withheld until a quantity target is reached.

**Inconclusive:** Only intended intensity is recorded, with no inspected output or later information.
