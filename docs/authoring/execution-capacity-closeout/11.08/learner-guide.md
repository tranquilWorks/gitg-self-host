# 11.08 — Follow-through and accountability

## Keep the promise visible until it is resolved

Accountability lets another person and your future self plan around what you actually committed to do. It preserves the original promise, checks risk early, communicates while alternatives remain useful, and follows delivery through to receipt or explicit uncertainty. Sending a proposed change is not an accepted renegotiation. Attempting delivery is not confirmed receipt.

Use the fictional borrowed-book case to practice the full chain without contacting anyone. Allow 10 minutes for the promise and risk decision, two five-minute updates and 10 minutes for checking. Paper, dictation or a text editor are sufficient. No real account, travel, book, recipient or private disclosure is required. You will create a promise record, messages and a branch-specific closure ledger. These are simulated communication artifacts, not live reliability evidence.

Use a live route only for a small promise within your authority and capacity. Reliability does not mean unlimited availability. Unsafe contact, essential care, professional obligations or consequential disputes need the appropriate existing support/process; this exercise does not settle them.

## Reference: agreement, action and receipt are different states

Record the promised object/result, recipient, deadline, method and any acceptance condition in words the recipient would recognize. Clarify ambiguity before relying on it. “Friday” may mean before a trip, not late evening. A risk checkpoint should precede the latest useful opportunity to act; a reminder after all alternatives close is too late.

A **proposal** requests a specific alternative. An **accepted revision** needs the affected person's clear agreement to its material terms. A vague reply, reaction icon or silence leaves the proposal pending. You can decline a new optional request without the requester's approval; that differs from revising an existing promise on which someone relies.

A **delivery attempt** records what you did and when. **Receipt** records evidence that the agreed recipient or authorized receiving route actually received the promised item. Where the agreed method itself defines receipt (for example a named staffed desk receipt), use that definition; otherwise do not invent it afterward. A read indicator proves neither acceptance of changed terms nor physical delivery.

If the deadline passes unmet, preserve the missed promise and acknowledge its impact. A later successful return can close the remaining obligation without turning it into on-time delivery. Repair should be proportionate, specific, safe and within your control; another person may decline your proposed repair.

## A different worked example

You promised a two-page agenda by Tuesday noon. Monday you send, “Could I send one page on Wednesday?” The recipient replies, “Thanks for telling me.” That acknowledges the message, not the new scope or deadline. Ask a concise clarification while keeping Tuesday noon as the original commitment. If noon passes, mark the original missed; do not edit it to Wednesday and call the record accurate.

## Complete fictional promise packet

All times use the same local clock, with no time-zone conversion. The book is *Blue Atlas*, one undamaged copy. The original exchange is preserved:

- Monday 09:00, borrower: “I will return Blue Atlas to you at the hobby room Friday by 17:00, before your 17:30 departure.”
- Monday 09:10, owner Ren: “Agreed. Please hand it to me; don't leave it outside or send it through someone else without checking with me.”
- Monday 09:15, borrower: “Confirmed. I will check transport Thursday at 12:00.”

The book is ready and undamaged. Direct return would require leaving home Friday by 16:20 for a 30-minute trip plus a 10-minute arrival margin. The margin is not a guarantee against all travel disruption. On Thursday at 12:00, the scheduled service for that trip is confirmed cancelled and no other direct route is available within the borrower's capacity. No new paid transport is part of the default.

A feasible **proposed** alternative is available: Jo, an adult known to both people, has explicitly agreed in this fictional case to pick up the book Thursday at 18:00 and hand it to Ren Friday at 09:00 at the hobby room. Jo is not yet authorized by Ren. Ren can check messages Thursday until 16:00. If no clear reply arrives, the alternative is not approved. One clarification before 16:00 is possible; repeated pressure is not required.

### Action 1 — Freeze the actual promise

Create `original promise | agreed at | recipient | result | due | method | latest useful departure | risk checkpoint | status`. Preserve the Monday exchange and Friday 17:00 due time. Explain why Thursday noon is useful: it leaves time for a clear response before the 16:00 message window and proposed 18:00 pickup. The Friday 16:20 departure boundary is the latest useful start for the original route, not the time to start thinking about alternatives.

Keep real identifying details in private working notes on a live route, not public evidence. The fictional names here need no private disclosure.

### Action 2 — Communicate the threatened miss and a feasible alternative

At the stipulated Thursday checkpoint write a message with the original commitment, the concrete risk, a brief non-sensitive reason and the alternative needing agreement. For example: “I promised Blue Atlas to you Friday by 17:00 in person. The return service is cancelled, so I cannot currently make that route. Jo has agreed to collect it Thursday at 18:00 and hand it to you Friday at 09:00. Would you accept that? I will keep the original promise recorded until you confirm.”

Record the message as drafted in this exercise. In the later simulation, supplied messages may be analyzed as fictional sent events, never reported as your actual contacts. Preserve the latest decision point: if agreement is still unclear before Thursday 16:00, record the unresolved original and report the likely miss. Do not pass the book to Jo without Ren's approval.

Open [later-packet.md](later-packet.md) after saving the original and draft. Work each branch independently. A clear acceptance in A cannot be borrowed to resolve B or C.

### Action 3 — Record receipt or repair without changing history

For each branch produce an ordered ledger: `event time | observed/supplied event | agreement state | delivery state | original deadline status | next action`. Preserve proposed and accepted terms separately. A transfer to an authorized intermediary is a delivery step; it is not necessarily final recipient receipt.

When receipt is supported, record the exact agreed result and the supporting event. If receipt is uncertain, say what is unknown and choose an appropriate low-burden check. If delivery fails, acknowledge the miss and stated impact, propose a feasible repair and retain it as proposed until accepted. Carry out only the action you can actually perform on a live route; a sentence promising repair is not repair completed.

Attempt [check-prompts.md](check-prompts.md) before [check-answers.md](check-answers.md). A complete learning artifact preserves the promise and all branches, distinguishes acceptance from acknowledgment, and contains a repair decision. It adds no application actions or completion rules.

## Access, challenge and later transfer

Use accessible reminders, written/augmentative communication or agreed transport support. Record actual support as support; accepting help can make a commitment more reliable. Where direct contact is unsafe, use an authorized intermediary or appropriate support rather than the practice script.

For a harder supplied variation, Ren approves Jo only for Friday 09:00, then Jo reports Thursday 17:00 that pickup is impossible. The first acceptance does not approve a different courier. Reopen the delivery risk, preserve the accepted revision, seek another explicit agreement and report a missed revision if it remains unmet. Do not silently fall back to whichever date is easiest.

Later, follow one real modest promise through its actual checkpoint, delivery route and receipt. Preserve a missing reply or failed promise when that is what occurs. Independent observation exists only if someone actually observes and consents to the appropriate reporting; self-attestation is not that evidence. Use the result to change one reminder or pre-promise capacity check.

**Supportive:** Risk is communicated early, revised terms are explicitly accepted and receipt is confirmed under those terms.

**Mixed:** The item arrives but warning was too late to avoid disruption, or the item reached an intermediary while final receipt remains unconfirmed.

**Contradictory:** The original deadline is silently rewritten, silence is treated as consent or another person is blamed for relying on the promise.

**Inconclusive:** A reminder and proposal exist, with no acceptance, delivery or receipt evidence.
