# 11.10 — Saying no and ending responsibly

## Reclaim attention while closing what you owe

Saying no concerns an optional demand. Ending responsibly also asks what promises, resources and dependencies remain when a goal, project or relationship stops. You can release an obsolete goal without pretending it was achieved. You can set a legitimate boundary without another person's agreement. An existing promise may still need completion or an agreed revision.

This guide teaches those distinctions through a complete fictional hobby-group case, an obsolete-project release and a safe optional relationship ending. Allow 20–30 minutes for the paper route and 10 minutes for the separate checks. Use paper, dictation, signed/augmentative or supported communication. No real confrontation, purchase, account, private disclosure or contact is required. Your default output is a limit, notice, handoff plan and branch-specific closure record; rehearsal is not a communicated live boundary.

Choose only a low-stakes situation where your own participation is within your control and direct communication is reasonably safe. Do not use this exercise for abuse, coercive control, likely retaliation, stalking, unsafe dependency or material danger. Employment, housing, essential caregiving and consequential relationships may need organizational, trusted or qualified support. Do not withdraw essential care or encourage unsafe confrontation to finish a lesson.

## Reference: refusal, renegotiation and closure

A **refusal** says an optional new request will not be taken on. A **boundary** states your involvement and the proportionate action you control. It does not require the other person to approve, become pleased or stop asking. Avoid threats, punishment, silent tests and demands to control their response.

A **renegotiation** changes an existing accepted promise and needs the relevant agreement. If you promised one hour and want to leave after 30 minutes, “my boundary changed” does not itself settle the missing half-hour. Where a serious safety issue arises, prioritize the appropriate safety response and record the unresolved duty; do not force continued exposure for tidy paperwork.

An **obsolete goal** is no longer useful or appropriate. Record why it ends, what will no longer be done and who or what may still rely on it. Sunk effort alone does not require continuing an optional goal. **Responsible closure** accounts for notice, remaining promises, borrowed resources, the handoff and unresolved items. Closure need not mean universal agreement, emotional resolution or no discomfort.

A relationship ending can concern a voluntary, low-stakes activity partnership. Be direct about ending that activity and settle concrete shared obligations. This practice does not instruct separation from a dependent, termination of essential support or ending a dangerous relationship without appropriate help.

## A different worked example

You agreed to lend a puzzle until Saturday and now want to stop lending it in future. “Please return it Saturday as agreed; I won't lend it again after this” preserves the current promise while declining future lending. Demanding an immediate return as punishment changes the arrangement. When the puzzle returns, record receipt; the other person's dislike of your future limit does not by itself make that limit invalid.

## Complete practice packet: one agreed hour, no extra setup

The fictional hobby group meets Friday. You agreed on Monday to set out the paper materials from 17:00 to 18:00. Your promised result is six tables each receiving one blank-card pack by 18:00. The organizer has promised to supply all six packs at the start; actual supply and placement are established by the later branches. This is ordinary optional hobby work, with no essential care or safety role. You may complete it seated or with already available assistance. No further tasks were promised.

At 17:15 the organizer asks you to stay until 19:00 and decorate an additional display. That request is optional; nobody has agreed you will do it. You intend to leave at 18:00. You hold the group's labeled storage key temporarily and must return it. The agreed receiving arrangement is the organizer in the room at 17:55; if they are unexpectedly absent, a designated labeled return envelope in the room is explicitly authorized for the key. The organizer has agreed to collect that envelope. The case does not require a stranger to accept custody.

The supplied low-stakes facts rule out known retaliation or unsafe dependency. In a real situation, recheck rather than borrowing that fictional assurance. Your goal is to complete the agreed hour/result, give notice that the extra hour is unavailable and return the key. The organizer's agreement to the new refusal is not a completion condition.

### Action 1 — Define the limit and the duties that remain

Write `optional request | existing promise | own limit | proportionate follow-through | resources | notice | handoff/fallback | unresolved item`.

An appropriate limit is: “I can finish the agreed setup until six; after that I will leave. I cannot take on the additional display.” The follow-through is your own departure, not making the organizer cancel the display or agree with you. Keep all six packs and key return in the responsibility record. A request you dislike is not automatically optional; here optionality is explicitly supplied by the case.

Also examine the obsolete goal and relationship mini-cases below. Each needs a different closure artifact, not the same refusal with a noun changed.

### Action 2 — Give useful notice without forcing approval

Draft a direct statement at 17:15 while the organizer can still plan: “I can complete the agreed six-table setup by six, then I will leave. I can't stay for the display. I'll return the key at 17:55 using our agreed arrangement.” If a promised detail is genuinely uncertain, clarify that detail without turning your availability into unlimited bargaining.

This paper script is not a sent statement. Save it, then open [later-packet.md](later-packet.md) and analyze the supplied responses. The relevant evidence is what the person safely said and did, including handoff; another person's disappointment is not an outcome to force away.

### Action 3 — Follow through and account for closure

In each fictional branch record `notice event | agreed work result | limit maintained | key route/receipt | open duty | next action`. If the agreed work is incomplete, retain that fact and propose an appropriate repair without pretending the promise vanished. If a resource return is unknown, leave that obligation open. Emerging danger overrides the exercise and calls for appropriate support; a completed worksheet is never more important than safety.

On the optional live route, the existing boundary practice spans ten days, with about 20–40 minutes plus the naturally occurring interaction. Define, state and follow through within its existing action windows: define within three days and follow through within seven days when the relevant situation arises. Do not invent an opportunity or reset dates to manufacture follow-through. The actual application retains its own existing instructions and evidence requirements; these reading checks neither replace them nor add new requirements.

## Complete mini-case: release an obsolete project

You planned to make ten paper tickets for a now-cancelled optional game. Three blank ticket drafts exist; nobody paid or relied on receiving them. You borrowed a template sheet, due back Saturday by noon. The owner has already agreed you may return it through their labeled indoor return box and record that placement; no other resources were borrowed.

Write a cancellation/closure card: why the game-ticket goal no longer serves its purpose, which work stops, what draft material is retained or discarded under your authority, and the template-return obligation. A goal released is not ten tickets completed. In simulation, write the return action as pending until a supplied or actual event supports it. Do not claim the actual return of a fictional object. Reclaimed attention can be described as future work no longer undertaken, not a measured emotional benefit.

## Complete mini-case: end an optional activity partnership

Two fictional adults, you and Sam, have an optional monthly puzzle exchange. There is no dependency, paid agreement, shared account, care duty or known safety concern. You no longer want to continue. Sam's puzzle is in your possession under an agreed return next Saturday at 11:00 in the hobby room. No other item is owed. Sam has not been promised another exchange after that return.

Draft a clear ending of the exchange and preserve the return: “I am ending the monthly puzzle exchange. I will return your puzzle Saturday at eleven as agreed; I won't arrange another exchange.” You need not obtain permission to stop future optional meetings. You do need to keep or properly address the existing return arrangement. Do not demand a reply, forgiveness or a matching explanation. A missing reply leaves any new proposed return method unaccepted, but does not create an obligation to keep the optional exchange going. A real emotionally consequential ending deserves context-specific care beyond this bounded example.

Attempt [check-prompts.md](check-prompts.md) before [check-answers.md](check-answers.md). Finish with distinct closure records for setup, obsolete tickets and the exchange, including what remains open. These are unscored learning materials and add no completion rules or action IDs.

## Supported routes, challenge and later transfer

Use culturally suitable timing and directness, written or augmentative communication and an agreed support person. Private rehearsal is the appropriate route when there is no safe opportunity; label it preparation. It does not become live follow-through by being detailed. A support person cannot consent to an unrelated person's changed promise on their behalf without real authority.

For greater challenge, add the later packet's unfinished-duty branch and choose a proportionate repair. Keep the optional extra display separate from the incomplete agreed setup. This tests whether a clear refusal can coexist with accountability. Do not broaden the challenge into high-stakes conflict.

For transfer, use one naturally occurring safe optional request or one obsolete personal goal. Preserve actual notice, action and resource disposition. Review whether attention was freed without unannounced duties being abandoned. Do not infer relationship health, general assertiveness or freedom from guilt from one record.

**Supportive:** The extra request is declined, agreed setup and key return occur, and the person leaves as stated without forcing approval.

**Mixed:** The limit is maintained but a promised item or return remains unresolved and has a repair action.

**Contradictory:** Essential care is withdrawn, a silent test punishes someone, or the original promise is erased under a boundary label.

**Inconclusive:** Only a private script exists, no suitable live opportunity arose or safety uncertainty prevents use.
