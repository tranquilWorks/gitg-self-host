# 11.09 — Review, measurement, and adaptation

## Make a review change the next attempt

A review compares the plan you actually made with outcomes and costs, then changes a method you can control. It keeps explanations provisional. A good-looking reflection is not a closed loop until the changed method is tried and its result affects the next decision.

This guide uses a complete fictional three-day lunch-preparation record. Allow 20 minutes for comparison and a trial plan, then 10 for later results and fresh checks. Use paper, dictation or a text editor. No food preparation, purchase, private diary, account or participant is required. The default is a desk exercise about supplied records, not evidence that your routine changed.

A live route concerns an ordinary low-stakes routine that fits your existing food, health, access and care needs. It is not dietary or clinical advice. Keep those needs and supports in place; do not sacrifice meals, sleep or essential duties to improve a metric.

## Reference: outcome, cost and explanation

Preserve the **planned result** and its time boundary. Record the **actual outcome** against that same definition, including partial work. Keep **costs** such as active effort, rework and burden on others separate from the success count. An item bought is an activity; the required lunch being available at departure is the outcome.

An explanation can be supported without being proven causal. “An ingredient was unavailable” may be directly recorded. “I am lazy” is not an observation of a mechanism. Distinguish conditions you can change, external events and assumptions. List at least one plausible alternative and say what evidence would help distinguish it.

Choose **one controllable method change**, define a comparable next opportunity, state the predicted result and decide in advance what will make you retain, revise or reject it. Compare as consistently as practical and record changed conditions. Multiple simultaneous changes make attribution difficult. A positive result in a short comparison is provisional evidence for this use, not proof of general effectiveness.

Use an explicit **inconclusive** disposition if the follow-up or cost data are absent or the conditions prevent a fair comparison. Unknown is not zero. Do not silently shrink the denominator to remove missed days or alter the original target after seeing results.

## A different worked example

A person plans to return three library cards to their folder after use. One is returned, one remains on the table and one was not used. A review preserves the planned three and records the actual opportunities. Moving the folder closer is one testable method change; “be more organized” is only an intention. If the next record has all cards returned but a helper did the returning without that support being recorded, the evidence does not establish the person's changed behavior.

## Complete baseline packet: lunch available at departure

The fictional plan was saved before Week 1: “Have my usual suitable lunch packed and available by 08:00 on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. At most eight active preparation minutes per day, no transferred care work, and no delayed sleep.” The planned total active allowance is 24 minutes. Shopping time is outside this bounded preparation measure and must be disclosed if a later method changes it.

The case uses the person's already suitable lunch and existing safe storage arrangements. No recipe or change of diet is required. The only method variable considered is when the dry, shelf-stable portion is placed in its bag. All other lunch components and safe handling follow the existing arrangement.

| Week 1 day | Lunch by 08:00 | Active minutes | Recorded event | Other burden |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | yes | 8 | All required components available | none recorded |
| Wednesday | no | 6 | One required component unavailable; not checked earlier | none recorded |
| Friday | no | 7 | Seven-minute unavoidable interruption in the preparation window | none recorded |

The seven interruption minutes are elapsed disruption, not part of the seven active preparation minutes on Friday. Do not add them into active cost or remove them from elapsed feasibility. There are no actual meal, sleep or medical observations beyond this fictional record.

### Action 1 — Compare without rewriting

Write the original target beside the three outcomes and costs. Calculate outcome success and active effort. Preserve Wednesday and Friday as different misses. For each, distinguish supported observation, inference and alternative. Example: the missing component is observed; an earlier check might have exposed it, but packing an existing dry portion earlier cannot guarantee that component becomes available. Friday's interruption is recorded; assuming next Friday has the same interruption is a planning assumption.

Use `planned outcome | actual outcome | active and other cost | supported event | possible mechanism | alternative | controllable part`. Do not convert the record into a personal character verdict.

### Action 2 — Prespecify one change and a fair comparison

The default candidate method is: place the already available dry portion in its bag the preceding evening, while retaining the usual remaining morning preparation. Keep shopping, lunch requirements, supports and care arrangements unchanged. This may free morning time; it cannot create a missing component. Estimate four active minutes in the evening and four in the morning per day, labeling those as trial assumptions rather than measurements.

Compare the same Monday/Wednesday/Friday departure opportunities next week. Save this prediction before opening the later packet: “At least two of three lunches available by 08:00; no more than 24 combined evening-plus-morning active minutes; no care shifted or sleep delayed.” The original aspiration of three lunches is retained; two is a provisional improvement threshold for deciding whether the method deserves another trial, not retrospective completion of the original goal.

Also compare each lunch's combined evening-plus-morning cost with the original eight-minute daily cap. Meeting the trial's 24-minute total does not itself meet or rewrite that daily cap. Report any daily overrun separately, even when the provisional trial rule supports another attempt.

Apply these case decision rules:

- **Retain provisionally:** at least two of three lunches available, total active cost at most 24 minutes, no shifted care or delayed sleep, and comparable conditions with complete records.
- **Revise:** at least two available but active cost exceeds 24, with no harmful burden; change one feature for the next trial. Do not call the current method successful on cost.
- **Reject this version:** at most one available under comparable fully recorded conditions, or shifted care/delayed sleep occurs. Return to an appropriate prior arrangement or a different method with its own trial.
- **Inconclusive:** outcome/cost records are missing or a material condition differs so this comparison cannot answer the planned question. Retain the observations but repeat or redesign before an effectiveness claim. A known adverse burden still stops that version even if other data are missing.

These are original teaching thresholds for this case, not a validated assessment or universal rule. Do not average away an essential-duty violation.

### Action 3 — Study results and make the next action concrete

Open [later-packet.md](later-packet.md). Analyze each branch as an alternative follow-up, calculating both evening and morning costs. State whether the trial supports the prediction, apply the rule and specify the next action. “Retain” means schedule another bounded comparable trial with the same monitoring, not pronounce the method proven. “Revise” must name an actual changed step. “Reject” must leave a usable alternative or reasoned stop, not simply judge the week.

Complete [check-prompts.md](check-prompts.md) before [check-answers.md](check-answers.md). Your finished desk artifact is a preserved comparison, a pre-result prediction, branch calculations and concrete next actions. The supplied follow-up is simulated; no real week has elapsed because you read it.

## Access, deeper work and transfer

For a future supported trial, choose one day rather than a week when recall or writing burden is too high, stating that prospective denominator and its narrower conclusion. Keep this supplied three-day record intact; do not delete its missed days. An aid may total minutes or transcribe; the learner can still distinguish mechanism, evidence and decision. Approximate real timings may be ranges; if a range crosses the cost threshold, record the decision as uncertain rather than choosing its convenient end.

For greater challenge, use the harder check's differing-support case and design a second comparison that preserves the access support while testing the person's method. Do not remove necessary assistance for experimental purity. Extend to several suitable cycles only if the routine remains useful and the measurement burden is proportionate. Keep unchanged or worse outcomes in the record.

For actual transfer, choose one safe routine, save a small prediction, attempt the method at the next comparable opportunity and append what happened. Record external changes. An actual supported attempt differs from this simulation; independent observation differs from self-review. A real closed loop can end in a justified stop or release of an obsolete target.

**Supportive:** A changed method is tried, its measured result and cost meet the saved rule, and a specific next trial follows.

**Mixed:** Availability improves but total burden exceeds the plan; revise without hiding the cost.

**Contradictory:** The original target is rewritten, missed days vanish from the denominator or harm to other duties is ignored.

**Inconclusive:** Reflection produces an intention but no attempted change, or follow-up information cannot answer the comparison.

The broad small-test-and-adapt principle is discussed in [IHI background and source limits](../SOURCES.md). These original cases do not reproduce a validated intervention or demonstrate causal improvement.
