# 11.05 — Scheduling and realistic capacity

## Make the plan fit the day

A usable schedule assigns work to times when its prerequisites, access and your capacity are present. Adding durations is the first check; fitting them into actual intervals is the second. A spare half-hour at noon cannot rescue a form due before an appointment at ten.

This guide practices allocating duration ranges, energy, transitions, recovery, buffers and competing responsibilities. It is suitable for an ordinary day you can partly plan. It does not make imposed overload, changing disability-related capacity or insecure work your fault. Use the complete fictional morning below when there is no suitable live day. No calendar account, purchase, personal disclosure or manufactured interruption is needed.

Allow 15 minutes to make the paper plan, 10 to analyze the update, and 10 for the separate checks. Paper, a text editor, dictation or movable labeled cards suffice. You need to add minutes and read a clock; the reference below supports both. This simulation produces a baseline, revised schedule and communication decision. It cannot establish that you used a schedule in life.

## Reference: capacity is located, and uncertainty stays visible

A **duration range** records uncertainty, not permission to pick the smaller number when the plan is crowded. Use an earlier comparable attempt where available and label guesses. Here the upper bounds are stipulated case assumptions, not population estimates. Reserve the upper bound for commitments, then release unused time only after work actually finishes.

A **fixed interval** cannot move without the relevant agreement. An **access window** says when a place, resource or input is usable. An **energy window** is a person's stated fit for a kind of work, not a universal morning preference. **Transitions** include preparing, changing location or switching equipment. **Recovery** meets a real need and remains protected. A **buffer** is uncommitted capacity reserved against uncertainty; it is not another task to finish or an extra copy of time already assigned.

Use half-open intervals: 09:00–09:05 occupies five minutes and may be followed immediately by 09:05–09:20. A task requiring 30 uninterrupted minutes cannot fit in two separate 20-minute gaps unless splitting is explicitly allowed. Two independent tasks still compete for one person's attention.

For a task with deadline D, upper duration U and required final transition T, the latest useful start is D minus U minus T, then checked against access and energy. If prerequisites cannot be ready by that start, communicate as soon as that becomes apparent. Waiting until the deadline removes useful alternatives. A promise changes only through the appropriate agreement; changing your own optional plan needs no invented acceptance.

## A different worked example

A label must reach a desk by 14:00. Making it takes 15–20 minutes and walking to the desk takes five. The latest useful start is 13:35: 20 + 5 = 25 minutes before 14:00. Access to the printer starts at 13:45, so the proposed plan is infeasible even though the afternoon has 90 free minutes. Ask about an authorized alternative format or a changed deadline before promising delivery. Removing a needed break does not open the printer sooner.

## Complete practice materials: one fictional morning

You are planning 09:00–12:00. You are the sole worker. At 12:00 an existing care duty begins; it cannot be delayed. The person has stated that the form is suitable between 09:00 and 10:05. Keep their existing support and recovery needs. The form's inputs are all available at 09:00. Completion means all pages checked before leaving for the appointment, not merely opening the form.

| Item | Duration minutes | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare form materials | 5 | Before form |
| Complete and check form | 40–60 | May pause; finish by 10:05; suited to 09:00–10:05 |
| Move to appointment | 5 | After form, arrive by 10:10 |
| Fixed appointment | 30 | 10:10–10:40; already agreed |
| Recovery | 15 | Immediately after appointment; protected |
| Move to errand desk | 5 | After recovery, before errand |
| Optional errand | 20–30 | One continuous block; desk open 11:00–11:45 |
| Reserved buffer | 30 | Uncommitted time; use at most once |

The errand is optional and has no recipient relying on completion today. If it moves, record “pending, next suitable window to be chosen,” not “done.” All durations include the stated work; do not add unlisted travel. There is no travel back after the errand in this toy case. In life, explicitly include such travel if needed.

### Action 1 — Preserve a feasible baseline

Calculate the upper-bound demand, then place every item. Save your attempt before comparing with this worked baseline:

| Baseline interval | Allocation | Minutes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 09:00–09:05 | Prepare form materials | 5 |
| 09:05–10:05 | Complete and check form | 60 |
| 10:05–10:10 | Move to appointment | 5 |
| 10:10–10:40 | Fixed appointment | 30 |
| 10:40–10:55 | Recovery | 15 |
| 10:55–11:00 | Move to errand desk | 5 |
| 11:00–11:30 | Optional errand | 30 |
| 11:30–12:00 | Reserved buffer | 30 |

The sum is 180 minutes. Every dependency fits, the errand ends before 11:45, and the form fits the stated energy window. The upper-duration latest starts are 09:05 for the form and 11:15 for the errand. The baseline has no slack before the form deadline. Record this exposure; the later buffer is not protection for every morning risk.

Use: `version | assumptions | intervals | latest starts | threatened duty | communication trigger | first optional work to move`.

### Action 2 — Update from an interruption

Write the rule before opening [later-packet.md](later-packet.md): protect the appointment, recovery and noon duty; move the optional errand when it no longer fits; communicate immediately if the form deadline becomes infeasible. Apply the two later branches separately, preserving the original plan. Do not combine a short form duration from one branch with an interruption from another.

For each branch, show occupied and unallocated minutes without overlap. Keep a buffer ledger: original reserve, minutes actually used, minutes released by moved work, remaining free time. Freed time is not proof that a moved task finished. If the interruption is before an access deadline, test intervals again rather than subtracting only from the end-of-day total.

### Action 3 — Close the planning loop honestly

Use the update to identify an estimate error, an external event and a scheduling choice; explicitly say when a category has no evidence. Write one change to the next plan, such as reserving time before the vulnerable deadline or arranging an agreed alternative before making the promise. Record any open commitment and the next useful contact. Do not rewrite a missed deadline as the original plan.

Then attempt [check-prompts.md](check-prompts.md), saving answers before opening [check-answers.md](check-answers.md). A complete source learning artifact has both schedules, feasibility arithmetic, a single-use buffer ledger, the latest-start communication rule and an honest pending-work record. These are reading/practice outputs, not new application completion conditions.

## Access, challenge and later transfer

Use durations relative to an anchor (“after the appointment”) when exact clock times are inaccessible, while retaining the actual fixed boundary. A planning aid may add and read the intervals; record that support. Variable-capacity routes may use shorter windows and explicit uncertainty rather than pretending a stable three-hour block exists. When externally imposed duties exceed capacity, retain the infeasible plan and use the appropriate scope/support channel.

For a harder supplied variation, the five-minute materials preparation is complete by 09:05, but one required form input arrives at 09:15. The form still needs 60 minutes plus the five-minute transition. Record the 10-minute shortfall before 10:10; propose an alternative without marking it accepted. An advanced live route can compare three ordinary days using actual major time ranges, with no continuous tracking. Change one estimate from those observations.

At the next suitable real day, preserve your plan, use it, and record naturally occurring changes afterward. If no interruption occurs, that day supplies no interruption-recovery evidence. Do not manufacture a disruption. Record supported use separately from unaided use and from an independent observer's actual report.

**Supportive:** Intervals and access fit; a normal interruption leaves fixed duties protected and moved work openly pending.

**Mixed:** The arithmetic fits but an omitted transition eats into recovery; revise the plan rather than calling it fully feasible.

**Contradictory:** The same buffer minutes are used twice, a deadline is silently moved, or recovery/care is removed to declare success.

**Inconclusive:** Only a task list exists, or actual start/end information is insufficient to assess the live plan.
