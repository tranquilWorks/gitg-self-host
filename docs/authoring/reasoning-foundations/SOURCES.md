# Background sources and originality boundary

Consultation date: 12 September 2026. All people, datasets, numbered cases,
record templates, prompts, and answer explanations in this cohort are original
instructional constructions. The following sources support limited background
concepts, not validation of these exercises or their proposed outcomes.

## Argument terminology

Nathan Smith, *Introduction to Philosophy*, OpenStax (2022), section 5.3,
“Arguments”: https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/5-3-arguments

The inspected section supports distinguishing an argument's reasons, conclusion,
and inferential relationship and considering the inference separately from whether
one agrees with the claims. This cohort's formal toy cases are checked independently
by complete Boolean enumeration and inspection of the stated finite inventories.
No OpenStax case or passage is reproduced. This is a background reference, not an
independent endorsement of the curriculum or a claim of validated learning effects.

## Selected bias terminology

Rose M. Spielman, William J. Jenkins, and Marilyn D. Lovett, *Psychology 2e*,
OpenStax (2020), section 7.3, “Problem Solving”:
https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/7-3-problem-solving

The inspected section provides background on heuristics and familiar vulnerabilities
such as confirmation, anchoring, and availability. It does not establish this
seven-route checklist as a validated instrument, nor demonstrate that the original
counterchecks reliably eliminate bias. The seven routes themselves come from the
project's canonical 09.03 scope; the guide explicitly permits overlap and uncertainty.

## Motivational reasoning — abstract-level consultation only

Ziva Kunda (1990), “The case for motivated reasoning,” *Psychological Bulletin*,
108(3), 480–498. PubMed record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2270237/

The bibliographic record and abstract-level search material were consulted, not the
full paper. Its relevance is the distinction between reasoning toward a preferred
outcome and reasoning constrained by available justification. No detailed effect
size, intervention claim, population generalization, or seven-part taxonomy is
attributed to this paper. Full-text scholarly review remains open.

## What these sources do not establish

There is no claim of clinical benefit, unbiased judgment, psychometric validity,
causal effectiveness, universal cultural suitability, actual learner success, or
specialist approval. 09.01's claim-attribution taxonomy is a practical instructional
convention, not an empirically validated diagnostic classification. These references
must not be copied into a registry as evidence of human acceptance or completed review.
