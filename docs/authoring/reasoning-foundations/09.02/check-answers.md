# 09.02 — Answer guidance and repair

Open only after attempting the guide's event arguments and both checks. These are authored explanations, not independent learner or formal review evidence.

## Main practice: event arguments

**Argument A is valid but not sound in the supplied inventory.** If every advertised-free event had zero total listed attendance cost, and Cedar belonged to that group, the conclusion would follow. That explains validity without assuming the premise actually holds.

The inventory shows Cedar's total listed cost as 0 + 6 = 6 units. Its advertisement is marked free. The first premise is therefore false in this fictional world, the second is true, and the conclusion is false. The false premise defeats soundness, not the all–member–property form.

A labeled repair is: “Every event advertised as free in this inventory has zero admission cost; Cedar is advertised as free; therefore Cedar has zero admission cost.” The inventory supports that narrower premise and conclusion. It does not thereby establish zero travel cost. Keep the original and repair separate.

**Argument B is invalid and therefore not sound.** The packet defines many as at least six and fully booked as reservations equal seats. Cedar has six reservations for ten seats: it has many reservations without being fully booked. Every fully booked event in the inventory still has at least six reservations. This is a counterexample with both premises true and the conclusion false.

The failed move is from “fully booked implies many” to “many implies fully booked.” The condition was reversed. The fallacy name “affirming the consequent” is optional; this explanation is the essential part. A possible repair is to obtain the actual reservation and seat totals and compare them, not to redefine “many” after the fact.

## Check A

**Voucher is invalid.** Let registered be false and stamped be true. The conditional remains true, the second premise is true, and the conclusion is false. A stamp might be applied for another reason; the premises never state that only registered vouchers receive stamps. Adding that condition is a new premise, not something already established.

**Approval is valid.** Assume approval implies a signature and that there is no signature. Approval being true would force the signature to be true, contrary to the second premise. Therefore approval must be false under those assumptions. This is the form often called modus tollens.

The table has four combinations. For Approval, the only combination satisfying both premises is approval false, signature false, and the conclusion is true there. For Voucher, the combination registered false, stamped true supplies the required counterexample.

No actual voucher or summary is inspected, so the packet does not establish the premises' truth in an external real case. It tests the forms. An invalid argument can nevertheless have a true conclusion in a particular case; invalidity says the premises do not guarantee it.

**Repair:** A counterexample that makes the conditional false is not a counterexample to validity. Mark every premise and the conclusion, then try again while holding every premise true.

## Check B

P1: Every room with enough seats is available for this group at the stated time. P2: Room M has enough seats. C: Room M is available for this group at the stated time. With “available” held fixed, this all–member–property form is valid.

The 14-seat listing supports the capacity comparison 14 >= 12, subject to the dated source's scope. The reservation confirmation is relevant positive evidence of a booking, but it does not by itself establish that every sufficiently large room is available or guarantee operational access. The broad first premise is not established. Do not label it false merely because it is unsupported; do not label the argument sound merely because there is a reservation.

The organizer making the broad availability claim bears the burden of supporting it. A critic can identify the gap without proving the room will be locked. A critic who asserts that the room definitely will be locked has made a new claim requiring support.

A bounded check is to ask the facility contact whether this group's confirmed booking will have access at the stated time and whether there are current closure conditions. Until answered, distinguish the supported capacity and recorded reservation from operational uncertainty. Neither “definitely open” nor “definitely locked” follows solely from the missing record.

The open/not-open claims conflict only with the same room, meaning, time, and conditions. Different dates may describe a change rather than a contradiction. The capacity/availability substitution is an equivocation if it is used to defend the original access conclusion. A narrower repair can establish “Room M's listed seating capacity is sufficient for 12 people”; it cannot substitute for an access check.

“Nobody proved it will be locked” supplies no positive access record by itself. It is not enough to close the missing premise. Explain that failed move instead of relying only on a fallacy name.

**Repair:** Report “valid form; broad availability premise unresolved; soundness not established; actual access unresolved.” Then identify the specific source check that could justify a narrower practical conclusion.
