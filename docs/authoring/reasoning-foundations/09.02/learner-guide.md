# 09.02 — Logic and argument structure

## What you are practicing

Reconstruct what an argument actually claims, test whether its conclusion follows, and separately check the premises. Finish with a judgment that states the reason for accepting, rejecting, or suspending it. Disliking a conclusion and finding a bad inference are different things.

**Canonical scope:** Understand premises, conclusions, validity, soundness, contradiction, inference, fallacy, and burden of proof.

**Deliverable:** Two supplied argument maps, a counterexample or explanation of entailment for each, a premise-evidence record, and one fresh application. Allow three 15-minute sessions. Paper, a text editor, dictation, or premise cards are sufficient. All required data for the fictional route are provided. No purchase, research subscription, or live dispute is required.

This is educational practice, not legal or scientific adjudication. It creates no new scoring rule. Completion is not mastery.

## Eight concepts, one procedure

A **premise** is a claim offered as a reason. A **conclusion** is the claim those reasons are meant to support. The **inference** is the move from reasons to conclusion. Write each separately before judging the whole argument.

A deductive argument is **valid** when there is no possible case in which all its premises are true and its conclusion is false. It is **sound** when it is valid and its premises are true. Whether you have established premise truth is an evidence question: use “soundness not established” when the available record leaves it unresolved. Do not use “invalid” merely to mean “I think a premise is false.”

A **contradiction** asserts a claim and its negation with the same meaning, time, and conditions. “The room was open yesterday and is not open today” is not that contradiction. An unsupported claim is also not automatically a contradiction.

A **fallacy** is a defect in reasoning. Explain the defect; naming one is not a substitute for an argument. A defective argument does not establish that its conclusion is false. A true conclusion can be offered for bad reasons.

The **burden of proof** concerns who must provide adequate support for a disputed claim. Identify the claimant and what evidence would address the claim. A critic need not establish the opposite merely to point out missing support. Conversely, a critic making a new contrary claim needs support for that claim too. Appropriate evidence depends on the kind and stakes of the claim, not a universal demand for mathematical certainty.

## A different worked example: numbered tokens

The complete toy world contains four tokens:

`T1: blue, in drawer. T2: blue, in drawer. T3: red, on desk. T4: red, in drawer.`

Argument: “Every blue token in this set is in the drawer. T1 is blue. Therefore T1 is in the drawer.”

- P1: Every blue token in this set is in the drawer.
- P2: T1 is blue.
- C: T1 is in the drawer.

If P1 and P2 hold, C cannot be false. The form is valid. The complete inventory establishes both premises within this fictional world, so the argument is sound there. This does not assert a rule about tokens outside the given set.

Notice the difference between testing a form and inspecting this inventory. If the inventory were not available, the inference could still be valid while premise truth remained unestablished. To reject an inference as invalid, supply a possible arrangement that keeps *every* stated premise true but makes its conclusion false. A case that makes a premise false does not do that job.

## Complete practice packet: event claims

Treat the following as a complete fictional inventory, not a report about real events.

| Event | Advertised as free | Admission cost | Required travel cost | Seats | Reservations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cedar | Yes | 0 units | 6 units | 10 | 6 |
| Willow | Yes | 0 units | 0 units | 10 | 10 |
| Elm | No | 4 units | 0 units | 10 | 2 |

For this packet, **total listed attendance cost** means admission plus required travel, and **fully booked** means reservations equal seats. **Many reservations** means at least six. Treat the stated definitions consistently; there are no other costs, events, or reservations in this toy world.

**Argument A:** “Every event advertised as free has zero total listed attendance cost. Cedar is advertised as free. Therefore Cedar has zero total listed attendance cost.”

**Argument B:** “Every fully booked event in this inventory has many reservations. Cedar has many reservations. Therefore Cedar is fully booked.”

Do not open the answers yet. The inventory supplies facts, not the classification of either argument.

### Action 1 — Make an argument map without silently repairing it

For each argument, write P1, P2, and C in separate lines. Define the important terms using the packet. Mark any assumption a real version would require, such as whether an advertisement's “free” refers to admission or all attendance costs. In this supplied version the explicit definition fixes the meaning; do not quietly change it mid-argument.

Preserve the original before proposing a repair. A more defensible argument is useful, but it is not evidence that the original was already defensible.

Use this record:

`Original argument | P1 | P2 | C | term definitions | unstated assumption, if any | proposed repair kept separate`

### Action 2 — Test inference and truth in separate passes

**Inference pass:** Temporarily grant every premise. Ask whether the conclusion could still be false. Try to construct a counterexample. Write all premises and the conclusion beside your candidate and explicitly mark each true or false. If one premise is false, your candidate has not refuted validity. When the inference is valid, explain the link rather than saying “it looks logical.”

**Evidence pass:** Now return to the supplied inventory and check the actual premises. Calculate any required total or comparison. Identify which row and definition support each judgment. Report the conclusion's truth separately from the validity judgment.

Use four labels, not one impression:

`Inference: valid / invalid with counterexample. Premise status: supported true / false / unresolved. Soundness: established / not sound / not established. Conclusion: true / false / unresolved in the stated world.`

For the real world, “supported true” remains a judgment based on the available evidence, not omniscience. For this closed fictional packet, the supplied facts and definitions settle the relevant values.

### Action 3 — Test a fresh argument and expose the proof burden

Complete both checks in **check-prompts.md**, save the attempts, and then open **check-answers.md**. Check B includes a complete fresh packet so no live argument is necessary.

For an optional live route, select an ordinary low-stakes recommendation, such as which room to use for a reading group. Record its actual words, premises, conclusion, and ambiguous terms. Identify a fact that would test the weakest premise. Check an appropriate source or leave it unresolved. Record who asserted each disputed claim, what support is needed, and what you actually inspected. Do not invent a source or change the claim into an easier one without saying so.

Finish with a bounded judgment: “The inference works, but premise 1 is not established,” or “This counterexample defeats the inference; I have not thereby proved the opposite conclusion.” A factual disagreement can justify suspending a decision while you obtain relevant evidence. It need not become a contest over who is smarter.

## A compact diagnostic for common errors

**The conclusion happens to be true.** That does not show the offered reasons establish it. Write a possible case that separates the reasons from the conclusion.

**A premise is false.** That can defeat soundness without defeating validity. Repeat the inference pass while granting it as an assumption.

**There is an ambiguity.** State both meanings and show where the argument changes between them. Do not conceal an equivocation by repairing the wording without keeping the original.

**Someone has not disproved the claim.** Record what positive support the claimant offers. Lack of disproof is not itself proof, and an absence claim may need a specified search method and coverage.

**A fallacy name comes to mind.** Explain the failed move using the actual premises. A disagreement or unpleasant delivery alone does not diagnose a formal fallacy.

## Accessibility and progression

Use one premise per card, spoken statements with a transcript, or arrows connecting reasons to a conclusion. A helper may transcribe or move cards at your direction, but should not choose the verdict. Keep term definitions visible. No speed requirement applies.

For a more demanding exercise, replace “all” with “some” and inspect how the conclusion must narrow. Then examine “Nine of the last ten meetings ended on time, so the next one probably will.” That is a probabilistic inference, not a guarantee. Do not call every non-guaranteeing everyday argument a deductive fallacy; first identify the type and strength of support it claims.

For later transfer, analyze one argument in a different ordinary context without reading your earlier verdicts. Preserve the original and show your counterexample or entailment explanation. Repeated success is task-specific evidence, not certification of general rationality.

## How to interpret your result

**Supportive:** The maps preserve the original claims, the counterexample grants all premises, and the final judgment distinguishes inference, premise truth, and the burden of support.

**Mixed:** A weak premise is correctly identified, but the participant calls every argument with that premise invalid or silently strengthens an argument before evaluating it.

**Contradictory:** A true conclusion is treated as proof of a good inference, or a fallacy label is used to claim the opposite conclusion without evidence.

**Inconclusive:** Only agreement or disagreement is recorded. There is no argument map, evidence record, counterexample, or explanation of why the conclusion follows.

**Final review:** Is your objection to a premise, an inference, an ambiguity, or a missing source? What precise repair would address it, and what would remain unestablished?
