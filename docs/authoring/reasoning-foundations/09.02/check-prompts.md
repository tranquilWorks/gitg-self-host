## Check A — Inference independent of premise truth

Analyze both arguments. Number the premises and conclusion. For each, either give an assignment that makes all premises true and the conclusion false, or explain why there is none. Do not infer actual premise truth merely from the form.

**Voucher:** If a voucher is registered, it is stamped. Voucher V is stamped. Therefore V is registered.

**Approval:** If a summary is approved, it carries a signature. Summary S carries no signature. Therefore S is not approved.

Use only two Boolean properties for each case. A conditional “if P then Q” is false only when P is true and Q is false. “Not P” reverses P's truth value. List the four P/Q combinations if helpful. No actual vouchers, summaries, or documents are supplied.

Explain whether the truth of either argument's conclusion in the real world has been established by this packet. Would an invalid argument's conclusion have to be false?

## Check B — Premise evidence, ambiguity, contradiction, and burden

A fictional reading group needs a room for 12 people at 18:00 on Day 5. The facility's dated room sheet states: “Room M: 14 seats. Room N: 10 seats.” A reservation confirmation identifies Room M, Day 5, 18:00. The packet contains no current opening notice, staffing record, or access confirmation.

An organizer argues: “Every room with enough seats is available for us at that time. Room M has enough seats. Therefore Room M is available for us at that time.” The organizer adds, “Nobody has proved that it will be locked.”

1. Reconstruct the argument and distinguish its deductive form from the evidence for each premise.
2. Identify what the dated sheet and reservation confirmation establish, what they do not establish, and who is responsible for supporting the broad availability claim.
3. Write a bounded question or source check addressing the missing information. State what you can conclude before receiving an answer.
4. Explain why “Room M is open at 18:00 on Day 5” and “Room M is not open at 18:00 on Day 5” conflict when meanings and conditions are held fixed, but an open notice for Day 4 and a closed notice for Day 5 need not conflict.
5. Explain the ambiguity if someone replaces “available” with “big enough” only when challenged. Preserve the original argument and give a clearly labeled narrower repair.
6. Identify why the lack-of-disproof remark does not, by itself, supply the missing positive support.
