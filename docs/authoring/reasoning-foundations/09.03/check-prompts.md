# 09.03 — Fresh checks

## Check A — Name the vulnerability and change the method

For each case, identify the most directly illustrated vulnerability, explain the mechanism, and supply a countercheck that could change the decision. Do not require every real person in that situation to have the same bias.

1. Casey wants a particular reading-group venue and reads only its favorable reviews, deliberately skipping the current access notice.
2. One striking story about a crowded session dominates Drew's judgment, although the choice concerns a different session with a different capacity and booking system.
3. Ellis sees a “10-minute task” headline and keeps estimating 12 minutes even after listing five non-overlapping required steps of 5 minutes each.
4. Fran plans to continue an unsuitable poster solely because 80 irrecoverable minutes have already been spent; an equally suitable alternative has a smaller remaining workload, and no asset or obligation would be lost.
5. Gray says “There is no possible way the outdoor event will be disrupted,” despite lacking weather, access, or cancellation information.
6. Harper demands a current access confirmation from an unfamiliar club but accepts an equally unsupported access claim from their own club; no relevant track-record difference is supplied.
7. Indigo calls their own late cancellation reasonable but another person's cancellation irresponsible, while admitting the same notice, responsibilities, and constraints applied in both cases.

State why a familiar organizer or prior effort can sometimes be relevant evidence rather than automatically something to ignore.

## Check B — A fair check can retain the initial choice

A fictional volunteer wants to participate in one session with confirmed step-free access and at most 60 minutes total future time. Both options have confirmed access and otherwise meet the stated requirements. Among feasible options, the rule is to minimize future time.

- **X:** 20 minutes round-trip travel and 30 minutes in the session.
- **Y:** 10 minutes round-trip travel and 45 minutes in the session.

The initial preference is X. The volunteer previously spent 80 irrecoverable minutes preparing specifically for Y. Nothing can be reused and there is no duty or penalty attached. X belongs to an unfamiliar group; Y belongs to a familiar group. No relevant difference in track record is supplied.

Preserve the first preference. Calculate both totals, perform a future-only and group-label countercheck, and apply the stated rule. Explain whether retaining X can count as a successful procedure check. Name one missing real-world fact that would matter outside this closed fictional packet without inventing it or overturning the packet's stipulated facts.
