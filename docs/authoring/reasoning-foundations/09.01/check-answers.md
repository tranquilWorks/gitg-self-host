# 09.01 — Answer guidance and repair

Open after making your attempts. This file is not a formal review receipt. Different wording is acceptable when the evidential distinctions are preserved.

## Check A

1. **Bounded observation:** the calendar displayed that status at the stated time. It does not by itself establish who changed it or why.
2. **Unestablished inference about the actor:** no access log or first-person account in this packet identifies the person who changed the entry. Mark the actor unknown rather than asserting the opposite.
3. **Attributed first-person report:** Robin reports embarrassment. Do not convert the feeling into proof of another person's intent or treat it as disallowed because the motive is uncertain.
4. **Attributed report containing a motive interpretation:** Pat made the allegation. The allegation's occurrence is not independent verification of its content. A bare “fact” label misses this nested distinction.
5. **Prediction:** the statement extends beyond the current room and time. The packet does not establish every future booking outcome.
6. **Conclusion:** it draws a broad practical judgment from the cancellation and prediction. Other rooms or arrangements are not addressed by the packet.

Useful unknowns include whether this was an error, which booking was cancelled, whether a replacement exists, who changed the entry, or what booking rule applied. A fair question is, “The Room C entry shows cancelled; can you clarify its current status and the available alternatives?” A question such as “Why are you excluding us?” assumes the disputed explanation.

Embarrassment is a reported experience. Its presence does not settle the coordinator's intent; uncertainty about intent does not erase the experience. The response can address both a practical booking need and how the cancellation affected Robin.

**Repair:** When you wrote a motive as an observation, rewrite it as an attributed or personally held interpretation, name the missing evidence, and keep the underlying concern. Do not replace it with an equally unsupported reassuring story.

## Check B

Initial record: the shelf was empty at 16:00 and the sheet contained no entry. “Quinn stole it” adds an actor, unauthorized taking, and intent beyond those observations.

Revision: Morgan now observes the tool bearing the matching inventory number in Cabinet D. The staff member reports moving it during cleaning. The new observation establishes the tool's observed location and identity at inspection; it supports the report's plausibility without independently witnessing every step of the move. Quinn's alleged theft remains unestablished.

A practical next step is to restore the agreed storage/checkout record and, if the accusation was communicated, correct that accusation to the relevant recipients without spreading it further. A suitable correction is, “I found the tool in Cabinet D. My earlier claim about Quinn was not supported by the evidence I had.” Do not require a final explanation of every movement before acknowledging that evidential error.

Keep the first account rather than rewriting it to “I always knew it was moved.” Finding the tool does not retroactively supply grounds for the original accusation. Equally, it is unnecessary to announce certainty about every person's innocence to retract an unsupported specific claim.

**Repair:** If your update says only “the problem is solved,” add the new observation, attribute the explanation, identify the remaining unknown, and address any harm from your earlier communication.
